## Replacement texts, ready to paste

- R1 `judea-samaria` / `full-sovereignty`: The party holds a neighbouring option, applying sovereignty to part of the area. As an Agudat Yisrael MK in United Torah Judaism, before the party existed, Meir Porush, now its leader, voted on 22 October 2025 for a bill to apply sovereignty to the settlement areas; the research found no evidence of support for dismantling the Palestinian Authority, the other element of your option.
- R2 `judea-samaria` / `one-equal-state`: The party's documented position is different. Meir Porush, now its leader, tabled a bill in March 2026 to apply Israeli law to four cities in Judea and Samaria, and on 22 October 2025, as an Agudat Yisrael MK in United Torah Judaism before the party existed, voted for a bill to apply sovereignty to the settlement areas.
- R3 `haredi-enlistment` / `anchor-torah-study-exemption`: That is the party's recorded position, shown by its leader's record as a United Torah Judaism MK before the party existed. Meir Porush voted for Basic Law: Torah Study (13 July 2026) and for a five-month law suspending arrests of yeshiva students who had not reported for enlistment (14 July 2026), which the Supreme Court struck down on 3 September 2026.
- R4 `haredi-enlistment` / `gradual-targets-by-agreement`: The party's documented approach is different. Before the party existed, Meir Porush, now its leader, voted on 14 July 2026 for a five-month law suspending arrests of yeshiva students who had not reported for enlistment, later struck down by the Supreme Court, and in December 2025 said of MK Boaz Bismuth's enlistment bill: "This is a bad draft law; it ought to be torn up."
- R5 `haredi-enlistment` / `universal-service-with-sanctions`: The party's documented approach is different. Meir Porush, now its leader, said in an August 2025 interview, "Whoever sits and studies should get a deferral," and on 14 July 2026, before the party existed, voted for a five-month law suspending arrests of yeshiva students who had not reported for enlistment, which the Supreme Court later struck down.
- R6 `haredi-enlistment` / `voluntary-service-for-all`: The party shares one element of your answer, no penalties for yeshiva students who do not serve: before the party existed, Meir Porush, now its leader, voted on 14 July 2026 for a five-month law suspending their arrests, which the Supreme Court later struck down. It differs in seeking a law that lets full-time yeshiva students keep studying, not voluntary service for everyone.
- R7 `cost-of-living` / `direct-support-for-households`: That is the party's closest documented position, though its evidence concerns Haredi families in particular. On 27 May 2026 Meir Porush, now the party's leader, voted for a United Torah Judaism (UTJ) bill to base daycare subsidies on the mother's status, and in September 2026 he criticised attempts to cancel daycare subsidies and the denial of property-tax discounts for Haredi families.
- R8 `housing` / `speed-up-planning-and-construction`: That is the party's main housing position, shown by a statement framed around the Haredi community. In September 2026 its leader Meir Porush said Haredi housing "will be at the top of the priorities" and promised to "dismantle from the foundation" the planning barriers that hold up Haredi zoning plans.
- R9 `housing` / `direct-help-for-buyers`: The research treats this as a secondary element of the party's position, whose main emphasis is building faster. On 24 May 2026 Meir Porush, now its leader, criticised criteria that excluded men who are required to enlist but have not done so from the discounted-price apartment programme, saying they discriminate against Torah learners.
- R10 `judea-samaria` / `partial-sovereignty-area-c`: That is the party's closest recorded position: in March 2026 Meir Porush, now its leader, tabled a bill to apply Israeli law to four cities, Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Ma'ale Adumim and Ariel. The research also records his votes against two narrower sovereignty bills in late 2025.
- R11 `judea-samaria` / `maintain-current-situation`: The party shares your support for strengthening settlements and your opposition to a Palestinian state: Meir Porush, now its leader, spoke as a minister in March 2023 of advancing settlement, and in September 2026 of campaigning against a Palestinian state. It differs in seeking to apply sovereignty, which your answer excludes: in March 2026 he tabled a bill applying Israeli law to four cities.
- R12 `judea-samaria` / `separation-two-states` and `two-states-pre-1967-border` (same text for both): The party's documented position is different. In March 2026 Meir Porush, now its leader, tabled a bill to apply Israeli law to four cities in Judea and Samaria, and in September 2026 he said he had "always been an active campaigner on every occasion against the establishment of a Palestinian state".
- R13 `october-7-inquiry` / `commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`: The research places the party here tentatively. Before the party existed, Ynet reported that the Haredim voted on 6 July 2026, at first reading, for MK Ariel Kallner's bill on a commission appointed through the Knesset; Meir Porush, now the party's leader, abstained at its preliminary reading on 24 December 2025, and neither he nor the party has stated a position.
