### The consistency question (amcha-yisrael and atid-echad on iran-and-regional-security)

(a) Both `ruledOut` entries for amcha-yisrael stand. The deciding words are Winter's own, general (not tied to one front or one deal) and in the negative: "We won't wait for the threat, we'll remove it. We won't end a war while the enemy has the ability to recover, we will deny it that…", with Ynet's description of him "as rejecting temporary truces as a substitute for military decision". That is a refusal of what both options propose (stopping now, before the threats are removed), not merely a different view. `end-military-operations-regional-peace` is the weaker only in coverage: nothing in the sub-section speaks to withdrawing from Lebanon or to regional peace. Its core proposal, ending all operations now, is refused in terms, and it would be incoherent to rule out the milder option and leave the stricter one open.

(b) atid-echad should stay as it is, with neither option ruled out. Its sub-section shows Strock opposing one proposed arrangement and predicting it would fail ("there will be no agreement, Hezbollah will not move beyond the Litani"), demanding retaliation when attacked ("when Israel is attacked, Israel attacks in Beirut" — which is even compatible with "using force only to respond if Israel is attacked"), and wanting to "change the border – we need to go in that direction". The Summary limits her opposition to "ceasefire arrangements with Hezbollah that she believes cannot be enforced". That is conditional, about Lebanon only ("This research found no detailed statement by her on Iran"), and "change the border" is a different view from withdrawal, not a statement refusing to end operations. Nothing there says "we will not stop" or "no ceasefire". The two rows are consistent with each other under the bar.
