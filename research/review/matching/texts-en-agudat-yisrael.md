**cost-of-living**

- R1 `open-market-competition-imports` (date only): The party's documented emphasis is different: the 28 December 2022 coalition agreement signed for UTJ budgets NIS 1 billion a year for food-purchase cards for needy families, and the party's leader promised in October 2022 to cancel the sweetened-drinks tax. The same agreement does also commit to easier import of goods approved in Europe and more competition in the food market.
- R2 `cut-taxes-and-government-spending`: The party's documented position includes tax cuts, but the research treats this as a secondary element of a mixed position. The government cancelled the taxes on sweetened drinks and disposable tableware in early January 2023 under the coalition agreement, and two of the party's MKs at the time, Eichler and Tessler, voted for the 11 March 2024 order raising VAT from 17% to 18%.
- R3 `active-state-regulation` (date only): The party's documented position includes some state price freezes, which overlap with your answer: the 28 December 2022 coalition agreement signed for UTJ commits to freezing electricity and water prices and local property taxes in 2023. The research places the party's main emphasis elsewhere, on direct help for low-income families, and notes there is no separate party economic program.
- R4 `direct-support-for-households` (date only): That is the party's closest documented position. The 28 December 2022 coalition agreement signed for UTJ budgets NIS 1 billion a year for food-purchase cards for needy families, and in July 2022 the party's leader Goldknopf called for state funding of rent for Haredi kindergartens.

**haredi-enlistment**

- R10 `anchor-torah-study-exemption`: That is the party's recorded position. Goldknopf and Tessler voted for the temporary law passed on 14 July 2026 freezing arrests of full-time yeshiva students, which the High Court of Justice struck down on 3 September 2026, and in November 2025 Goldknopf said the party would not support any bill that includes sanctions on Torah students.
- R11 `gradual-targets-by-agreement`: The party opposes enlistment targets: in January 2026 its Council of Torah Sages republished a decision saying any law setting sanctions or targets must be opposed with full force. In November 2025, after the chair of the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee presented a bill with enlistment targets and sanctions, Goldknopf said the party would not support any bill that includes sanctions.
- R12 `universal-service-with-sanctions` (date only): The party holds that full-time Torah students should be exempt without sanctions: in January 2026 its Council of Torah Sages republished a decision calling a law setting sanctions unacceptable. In November 2025 Goldknopf said the party would not support any bill that includes sanctions, and on 28 January 2026 its three MKs voted against the 2026 budget in its first reading over the issue.
- R13 `voluntary-service-for-all`: The party shares your opposition to penalties for those who do not serve: in November 2025 Goldknopf said it would not support any bill with sanctions on Torah students. Its documented position is narrower than your answer: it concerns full-time yeshiva students, for whom, the Israel Democracy Institute noted in March 2026, the party's MKs wanted the arrangement to stay voluntary and comprehensive as before.

**housing**
