Date: 2026-10-13. Scope: the 41 rows in `content/matching/positions/` whose held options are all `secondary`. For each, the party's sub-section in `content/research/en/<partyId>.md` (through its "Closest option(s)" line) was read against the issue's options. `ruledOut` was added only where the research explicitly shows the party rejecting an option it does not hold. Silence, "no position found", "no evidence links", distance from the held option, discounted votes and dated background were not counted.

### The three added rows that are judgment calls

- **chomat-torat-yisrael / october-7-inquiry.** The vote is the leader's own, from 2025, against exactly what the option proposes, and the research does not discount it. But the Closest line calls the evidence "thin and mixed" and does not say "rules out": Porush abstained on the Knesset-appointed alternative, and UTJ "used support for a state commission as a bargaining threat" (the sub-section says that threat came from Degel HaTorah's Gafni and "was a bargaining step, not a statement of preference"). Added because the same Porush vote is part of what rules the option out for Agudat Yisrael.
- **israel-first / gaza.** The line says "rejects" but concludes only "a poor fit", and hedges: "although in February 2025 Haskel said Trump's idea of temporary refuge for Gaza's residents should be seriously considered". The rejection covers the option's resettlement element; the party has not addressed "a return to full-scale fighting".
- **amcha-yisrael / iran-and-regional-security.** The rejection is in the sub-section's statements, not in the Closest line, which names no other option and says "the mapping is tentative because the evidence is general security doctrine". `end-military-operations-regional-peace` rests only on "We won't end a war while the enemy has the ability to recover"; it is the weaker of the two.

## Borderline rows decided against (left untouched)

- **amcha-yisrael / crime-in-arab-society** (`invest-in-arab-communities-first`): the line says the evidence "gives no support to putting investment in Arab communities first". That is absence of support, not a rejection.
- **amcha-yisrael / religion-and-state** (`strengthen-jewish-character`, `separate-religion-and-state`): "'invites and does not coerce' language does not fit enforcing Jewish religious law by statute" and "the party does not call for separating religion and state". "Does not fit" and "does not call for" are not rejections, and the line says "the evidence is too general".
- **degel-hatorah / gaza** (`end-war-full-withdrawal`): "No evidence links the party to a Palestinian state or to full withdrawal". Silence.
- **agudat-yisrael / gaza** (`end-war-full-withdrawal`): Goldknopf said "Giving up parts of the Land of Israel not only does not bring security…", but the line calls the record "tentative and mixed" and notes the vote for the January 2025 ceasefire deal; the statement is not about the current option.
- **eretz-yisrael-shelanu / october-7-inquiry** (`state-commission-under-existing-law`): the government Wasserlauf serves in decided against a state commission, but the sub-section says "government decisions are not personal votes" and "The candidates have not stated their own positions".
- **meretz / haredi-enlistment** (`voluntary-service-for-all`): three Meretz MKs voted in December 2021 against a bill "to abolish compulsory military service", but "Raz had himself tabled a bill with the same title", the vote was by coalition MKs against an opposition bill, and the line rests the mapping on the January 2022 votes alone.
- **raam / haredi-enlistment** (`anchor-torah-study-exemption`): all five MKs voted against Amendment 28 in July 2026, but the line says "its later votes against the government's bills do not show one [a position]". Discounted.
- **hadash, balad / haredi-enlistment**: votes against government bills are discounted in the text ("cast to bring down the government"; "does not by itself show a position").
- **taal / haredi-enlistment and religion-and-state**: Tibi's vote against Basic Law: Torah Study is not the same as the exemption option, and he "has said his votes on the enlistment law are meant to bring down the government". His 2023 vote against a civil marriage bill touches one element of `separate-religion-and-state`, but he voted for Shabbat public transport, another element of the same option.
- **israel-first / judicial-system** (`repeal-changes-protect-independence`): Haskel voted for the laws that option would reverse, but they were "cast as a coalition member" and "Israel First has not stated a position". Discounted under the stated rule.
- **new-hope / cost-of-living** (`direct-support-for-households`): MKs voted against raising allowances in 2021, but Sa'ar's quoted explanation is coalition discipline ("we would like to vote for them").
- **atid-echad / iran-and-regional-security** (`prioritize-ending-multifront-war`): Strock "has opposed ceasefire arrangements with Hezbollah that she believes cannot be enforced". Conditional and limited to Lebanon; not a rejection of ceasefires as such.
- **new-economic-party / iran-and-regional-security**: Zelekha wrote the war "ended in failure" because of the ceasefire, but also proposes covert economic means "rather than open warfare"; Iran only. No explicit rejection of an option.
- **new-economic-party / housing** (`direct-help-for-buyers`): the platform says "zero VAT" and "Mechir LaMishtaken" "failed to lower prices". Criticism of two programmes, not a rejection of the option; the line does not name it.
