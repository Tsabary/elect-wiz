## Unresolved or justified imbalances

- **Shas has no clause-8 ("coalition to the end of the term") contradiction, while agudat-yisrael and degel-hatorah do.** Justified: Shas withdrew its ministers in July 2025 but stayed in the coalition, and its May 2026 dissolution vote was cast with the coalition itself (110–0). The Shas–Likud agreement text was not read (the Knesset PDF would not download completely), so whether it has the same clause is not confirmed.
- **degel-hatorah quotes the 21 December 2022 agreement text (Mako), agudat-yisrael the signed 28 December text (Knesset).** The content described in both matches clause for clause where the agudat fact-check read the signed copy, but the clause numbers differ. Degel's quotations were not re-read against the signed scan one by one; the document now says which text it quotes.
- **haredi-public is much shorter and has seven issues without a position.** Justified by `limitedInfo: true`: a party registered in July 2026, no candidate with a Knesset record, a platform limited to Haredi community issues.
- **Crime in Arab society** is mapped differently on thin evidence: `more-police-resources-normal-law` for agudat-yisrael and degel-hatorah (a general coalition-agreement clause), `invest-in-arab-communities-first` for shas (one 2023 ministerial action). Each line says it is thin; left as is.
- **chomat-torat-yisrael keeps its override-clause `**Contradiction:**`** (November 2022 condition and June 2023 statement against staying in office to July 2025). It is Porush's own conduct as an Agudat Yisrael minister, labelled as such, and is balanced by the "Kept (eventually)" item.
- **agudat-yisrael's Eichler contradiction stays** (Council ruling of July 2025 against his January 2026 appointment), with the caveat that he is no longer its candidate.
- **yisrael-beytenu death-penalty contradiction:** no press report of the coalition's reason for the October 2021 vote was found; the text says the record gives no reasons.
- **Agudat Yisrael's Economic Efficiency Law vote (31 Dec 2024)** is linked to JDN's report of an announced vote "against the budget bill" by date only.

## For the lead reviewer (cross-group questions)

1. **Comptroller-vote affidavits (israel-first, new-hope, likud-party).** israel-first wording: Haskel "gave testimony as a witness; no report names her as a suspect"; the attorney general "reportedly instructed police on 29 July 2026 to examine" the conflicting affidavits; and "On 25 August 2026 the Times of Israel reported that the conflicting testimonies had led to a criminal investigation into possible perjury". new-hope says "We found no later report of a criminal investigation". The 25 August Times of Israel article does say the testimonies "have sparked a criminal investigation into possible perjury"; the 2 August article says the attorney general told police to "probe the conflicting affidavits and explore the possibility of launching a criminal investigation". The two documents should agree. Small wording differences too: "ruled the election invalid" here, "annulled" in new-hope; "national crimes unit" here, "major-crimes unit" there.
2. **Contradiction versus Change over time when the action comes first.** Applied here: an action in an earlier term set against a position stated later (Liberman and Ra'am, 2021–22 against 2026) is Change over time. A coalition-line vote in the same term as a continuing stated position (Haskel's 2024 tax vote; Shas's 2024 VAT vote after Azoulay's committee statement) is a Contradiction with the coalition reason. Please confirm for all groups.
3. **Hamas wording.** The glossary note for Hamas says "used plainly without adjectives in neutral text", while the Stage 2 brief says Hamas and Hezbollah are terror organizations, and several documents gloss "Hamas (a terror organization)" (degel-hatorah here; yesh-atid and the-democrats-party elsewhere). Here: standalone uncited sentences were cut, inline glosses were left. One rule is needed.
4. **Arrest-freeze law status.** The High Court struck it down on 3 September 2026 (nine justices; eight on substance, one on procedure only). Every document that mentions the law should say so.
5. **Attorney-general law tally** is 65–51 (official). Ynet's 61–51 may survive elsewhere. **Ma'ale Adumim bill** is 31–9 with one abstention (ToI: 32–9). **Death penalty law** 62–48 with one abstention, and the abstention was Porush.
6. **2025 budget final reading date.** The official record's date field reads 24 March 2025 with no time; the same bill's second-reading votes are timestamped 25 March, and the press dates passage to 25 March. degel-hatorah says 25 March; israel-first now says "March 2025"; new-hope may say 24 March.
7. **10 December 2025 crime-organization votes (44853, 44855).** israel-first, new-hope, blue-and-white, hadash and yesodot-yisrael describe two bills "titled as allowing a crime organization to be declared a terror organization". The vote-details service now titles 44853 "Fight against weapons smuggling" (rejected 26–52) and 44855 "Special powers for fighting crime organizations (temporary provisions)" (passed 54–41). Worth one check of the bill records.
8. **The July 2025 sovereignty motion.** shas quotes its operative clause ("all the areas of Jewish settlement") and maps to partial sovereignty; other groups' documents citing the same motion should use the same reading.
9. **Organization-level legal findings.** The Goldknopf State Comptroller item was kept and the civil settlement removed. If another group kept a civil settlement or an organization-level finding, the same test should apply (official finding yes; settlement without a finding no).
