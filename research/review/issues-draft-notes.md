# Stage 1 draft notes: issues and answers

Drafted 2026-10-06 for the 26th Knesset election (polling day 27 October 2026). This is a **draft**. It has not had the independent neutrality review (PRD FR7) or owner approval (FR6) yet.

Files drafted:
- `content/issues/*.json`: 10 issues, each with 3–4 options in Hebrew and English
- `content/glossary.json`: 27 approved terms with terms to avoid
- this file: why each issue was chosen and its sources, excluded issues, terminology notes, and open questions for the reviewer

How to read the sources: **[R]** means the page was fetched and read. **[S]** means only a search-result summary was seen, and the page itself was not opened (usually because it was paywalled or returned 403). [S] sources back up [R] sources and never stand alone for a key claim. Reviewers should open [S] links before the facts they support are locked.

## 1. How the 10 were chosen

Selection criteria:
1. **Importance now.** The issue shows up in recent (2025-12 to 2026-09) surveys of voting considerations or "most important issue" questions, or it is a defining dividing line in campaign coverage.
2. **Matchability.** Parties hold different stances you can tell apart, backed by platforms, questionnaire answers or actions, so the issue can separate one party from another.
3. **No overlap.** Each issue covers a distinct policy question.
4. **Whole electorate.** The set should reflect what matters to Jewish voters (secular through Haredi) and to Arab voters. Arab voters' top issue (crime) is included for that reason.

The main survey evidence behind the set:

| Survey | Date | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| IDI, voting considerations [1] | fieldwork 31 Aug–3 Sep 2026 | Jewish respondents: foreign affairs and security 39%, economy and cost of living 38%, conscription 27%, religion and state 26%, party leader 23%, judicial reform 18%. Arab respondents: crime in Arab society 53.5%, economy and cost of living 46%. 68% of Jews (32% of Arabs) say October 7 is a central factor in their vote. |
| IDI, special review [2] | 24 Dec 2025 | Jewish respondents: hostages, October 7, Haredi conscription law, cost of living and judicial overhaul roughly tied at 16–18% each. Arab respondents: economy and cost of living 33%. About 75% of Arabs say a party's anti-crime platform is a major factor. |
| JPPI Israeli Society Index [3] | 1–3 Sep 2026 | Security leads at 31% overall. Government–judiciary relations come second. On the right: security 40%, government–judiciary 35%. On the left and centre-left: "a more liberal state" 34–43%. |
| i24NEWS / Direct Polls [4] | 31 May 2026 | Judicial system 37%, conscription and burden-sharing 27%, security 21%, cost of living 10%. Coalition voters: judicial 47%. Opposition voters: conscription 44%. |

Campaign-coverage overviews [8][9][10] consistently name the same core: security and October 7, Haredi conscription, cost of living, the judicial system, religion and state, and crime in Arab society.

## 2. The 10 issues

### 2.1 `haredi-enlistment`: Haredi military enlistment (4 options)
**Why:** Ranked 2nd or 3rd in every recent survey: 27% of Jews in IDI Sep 2026 [1], 27% overall and 44% of opposition voters in i24 May 2026 [4], and tied for top in IDI Dec 2025 [2]. It brought the coalition down: the Haredi parties left in July 2025, and in May 2026 they rejected the coalition's exemption bill, which led to the Knesset dissolving [8][11]. Since 2024, 79,000+ enlistment orders have been issued and about 2,100 people have enlisted [11]. The Knesset passed Basic Law: Torah Study in July 2026 [27]. Parties' answers to ynetnews show clearly distinct positions [12]:
- Haredi parties want exemptions for yeshiva students with no sanctions. UTJ is drafting a no-sanctions red line [S, 13b].
- Coalition outline: gradual targets and delayed sanctions [13].
- Yashar!, Beyachad, Yisrael Beytenu, the Democrats and the Reservists party want universal military or civilian service with personal sanctions.
- Joint List and Ra'am oppose compulsory service for Arab citizens and reject sanctions-based enforcement.

**Options:** exemption anchored in law (Haredi parties) / gradual targets by agreement (coalition outline) / universal service with sanctions (opposition) / no compulsory service for objecting communities, voluntary civilian service only (Arab parties).

### 2.2 `cost-of-living`: Cost of living (4 options)
**Why:** IDI Sep 2026 has it at 38% for Jews (2nd, statistically tied for 1st) and 46% for Arabs (2nd) [1]. It was a top factor among right-wing voters in Dec 2025 [2]. It is a central campaign theme for Beyachad, Yashar!, Labor/Democrats and the New Economic Party [9][36]. Coverage notes Israel's price level is roughly 20% above wealthy European countries [8]. The moreInfo text deliberately avoids giving a figure.
**Options:** competition, imports and deregulation / tax and spending cuts / active state regulation and price supervision / direct support for households. Party proposals cover all four: competition authority and parallel imports (Labor) [36], free-market and lower taxes (Likud's historical platform) [37], food cards and free education from age 0 [36].

### 2.3 `housing`: Housing (4 options)
**Why:** The top issue for young voters by a wide margin: 38% of 18–35s [6][6b]. Rents rose 3.5% year on year in early 2026 [35]. Ynet's September 2026 survey of party housing plans shows clearly different approaches [34]:
- the Democrats: return the state as a central player, plus a government authority for affordable rentals
- Beyachad: a "First Million" grant for those who served, plus 100,000 rental units
- Yisrael Beytenu: mortgages up to 90% of value over 40 years
- Yesh Atid: speed up construction, urban renewal and integrated transport planning
- Degel HaTorah: change land pricing and increase support for first-time buyers

**Overlap check with cost-of-living:** IDI groups "economy and cost of living" together. Housing is kept separate because its policy levers (land, planning, public rental, mortgages) are different from consumer-price levers (imports, competition, taxes), and because young voters rank it separately. See open question Q2.

### 2.4 `judicial-system`: Judicial system (3 options)
**Why:**
- Top issue in i24 May 2026 (37%; 47% of coalition voters) [4].
- 2nd overall in JPPI Sep 2026 (35% on the right) [3].
- 18% of Jews in IDI Sep 2026 [1], and tied for top in IDI Dec 2025 [2].

Recent events: the government voted to dismiss the attorney general in August 2025, and the High Court annulled the dismissal [25]. Parties' plans as of 22 Sep 2026 [24]:
- Religious Zionism–Zehut: continue the "legal revolution"
- Yisrael Beytenu: a constitution with an override clause
- Beyachad: repeal the new judicial selection law and enact Basic Law: Legislation
- Yashar!: repeal the selection law and build broad agreement
- the Democrats: repeal the overhaul laws and entrench judicial independence
- Likud: did not answer

**Options:** continue the changes / broad agreed constitutional framework / repeal the changes and entrench independence. Three options because the field clusters into three. See Q4.

### 2.5 `october-7-inquiry`: October 7 inquiry (3 options)
**Why:** IDI Sep 2026: October 7 is a central voting factor for 68% of Jews [1]. Coverage calls it "the defining issue of the campaign" [8][9][10]. The government told the High Court there would be no inquiry before the election [14], and the Court postponed its ruling until after the election [S, 15]. Positions [16]:
- Beyachad, Yashar!, Yisrael Beytenu, the Democrats and Blue and White: a state commission under existing law
- Religious Zionism: a "national, independent" commission instead, calling a state commission "one-sided"
- the government's bill: an 80-MK supermajority, otherwise 3+3 appointments by coalition and opposition [14]
- Zehut: equal coalition/opposition appointment
- Amcha Yisrael: secret Knesset ballot

**Options:** state commission under existing law / commission appointed through the Knesset with balanced representation / broad inquiry after the fighting ends. The third option is the least well sourced for the current campaign. It reflects the earlier government line ("after the war") and the right's demand for a broad mandate. See Q5.

### 2.6 `gaza`: Gaza Strip policy (4 options)
**Why:** Security is the leading voting consideration: 39% of Jews in IDI [1], 31% overall in JPPI [3]. Current context:
- A ceasefire under a US-led plan has held since October 2025.
- The last hostage's remains were returned on 26 Jan 2026 [S, 18].
- Phases 2–3 (Hamas disarmament, the international force, IDF withdrawal) have not been implemented, and the IDF holds roughly half the Strip [17].
- In August 2026 the PM reportedly rejected the Board of Peace's 15-point roadmap [17][S, 21].
- INSS Feb 2026: 51.5% say moving to phase B does not align with Israeli interests [20].

The four options closely follow the four Mitvim categories [19]. They map onto party positions:
- resume fighting and encourage voluntary emigration: Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism (see Q6)
- hold positions, disarmament first: the stated position of the PM/government [17][21]
- international arrangement: centre/centre-left
- full withdrawal and a path to statehood: Arab parties and the left [22]

### 2.7 `judea-samaria`: Judea and Samaria and the Palestinians (4 options)
**Why:** A defining left–right line with sharp party differences [22][23]. Recent activity [17]:
- Settlement units advanced in Area C, with 4,570 units June–September 2026.
- E1 tenders close on 19 Oct 2026.
- Sovereignty bills passed preliminary readings [S, 38].
- Several countries banned settlement goods on 8 Sep 2026.
- INSS Feb 2026: only 28% support a Saudi accord that includes Palestinian statehood; 47% oppose [20].

Positions [22][23]:
- Religious Zionism and Otzma: full sovereignty and dismantling the PA
- Yisrael Beytenu: sovereignty over Area C. Beyachad sees Area C as a future sovereign part.
- Likud: no answer. Its actions: settlement support without formal sovereignty.
- the Democrats: separation with the US. Ra'am: two states. Joint List: evacuate settlements.

**Options:** full sovereignty / partial sovereignty (Area C, Jordan Valley) / maintain the current situation / separation and two states.
**Overlap with Gaza:** Gaza is about the military and governance question in the Strip after the war. This issue is about the long-term status of Judea and Samaria and the Palestinian question. Party positions differ on each, for example parties that are hawkish on Gaza but differ on sovereignty.

### 2.8 `religion-and-state`: Religion and state (4 options)
**Why:**
- 26% of Jews in IDI Sep 2026 (4th) [1].
- "A more liberal state" is the top consideration on the left and centre-left in JPPI (34–43%) [3].
- A July 2026 survey: 74% of non-Haredi Jews support some form of Shabbat public transport, while 78% of religious respondents oppose it [26].
- Party positions span Religious Zionism (no change), Likud ("preserve Shabbat"), local choice (Labor, Blue and White, Yisrael Beytenu) and full operation (the Democrats) [26].
- Basic Law: Torah Study passed 63–52 on 13 Jul 2026 [27].

**Options:** strengthen Jewish character / keep the status quo / more choice and local decisions (civil unions, local Shabbat transport) / separation of religion and state.

### 2.9 `crime-in-arab-society`: Crime in Arab society (3 options)
**Why:** The single most important issue for Arab voters: 53.5% in IDI Sep 2026 [1], and about 75% in Dec 2025 [2]. Facts:
- 252 murders in 2025, a record.
- 144–147 murders in the first half of 2026, up about 15% year on year.
- About 12% solved [28].

It is also a national issue: the largest joint Arab–Jewish protest took place on 31 Jan 2026 [S, 29b], and the President has called it a "national emergency". The policy debate is concrete:
- the government's July 2026 plan to involve the Shin Bet (about NIS 500m) [S, 30]
- the National Security Minister's push for administrative detention [S, 30]
- Arab parties' demand to reverse about $550m in cuts and end underfunding [29]

**Options:** emergency powers and the Shin Bet / more policing plus civil investment / restore budgets, close gaps and effective policing under regular law.
**Note:** Hebrew system text says "החברה הערבית" and "היישובים הערביים", per the glossary.

### 2.10 `iran-and-regional-security`: Iran, Hezbollah and regional security (4 options)
**Why:** "Foreign affairs and security" is the top consideration for Jews: 39% in IDI [1], 31% in JPPI [3]. Since 28 Feb 2026, Israel and the US have been at war with Iran [31]. Hezbollah joined on 2 Mar 2026, and the IDF entered southern Lebanon [32]. Since then:
- a Lebanon ceasefire on 16 Apr, then US-mediated arrangements
- partial IDF withdrawal from July 2026 [32]
- renewed US–Iran clashes in September 2026 [31][S, 33]

INSS Feb 2026: 50.5% support an independent Israeli strike if a US–Iran deal blocks American action [20].

**Options:** military pressure until threats are removed / force plus US-backed agreements / prioritize ending the multi-front war / end military operations and pursue a regional peace.
**Caveat:** Among Jewish parties, positions here differ in emphasis more than in direction. See Q1.

## 4. Terminology choices (see `content/glossary.json`)

Key decisions for the reviewer:
- **"Haredi" rather than "ultra-Orthodox"** in English. It is the community's own term and is standard in Israeli English outlets. "Ultra-Orthodox" is read by many in the community as implying extremism.
- **"Judea and Samaria"** in both languages, per PRD FR3 and mainstream Hebrew media usage [23]. Some Arab and left parties explicitly reject the term in favor of "occupied West Bank" [22][23]. That is a known tension: the PRD's chosen standard sides with mainstream usage. "West Bank" is not loaded in Israeli English, but it is avoided for consistency.
- **Settlements:** English "settlements" (per the PRD). In Hebrew, "יישובים" and "התיישבות" are used, not "התנחלויות". The latter is not offensive but is slightly marked. Ynet uses יישובים and התיישבות [23].
- **"Applying Israeli sovereignty"** rather than "annexation". This is the wording of the bills themselves. "Annexation" is the critics' and international term.
- **"The October 7 attack"** rather than "massacre". "Massacre" (טבח) is accurate and common in Israel; we chose "attack" only for consistent tone in neutral system text. "Failures" (כשלים) is used because every major party acknowledges failures, while "the debacle" (המחדל) implies who is to blame.
- **Inquiry models are described by appointment mechanism**: "state commission of inquiry" is the legal term, and "commission appointed through the Knesset" describes the alternative. Both camps' labels ("independent", "one-sided", "political", "cover-up", "national") are avoided in neutral text.
- **"Changes to the judicial system"** rather than "reform", "overhaul" or "coup".
- **"Arab society" and "Arab citizens"** rather than "the Arab sector", "minorities" or "Palestinian citizens of Israel".
- **"Encouraging voluntary emigration"** appears only inside the option of those who hold that view, in their wording. This is the most sensitive term choice. See Q6.
- **Option rationales intentionally use each camp's own value language**: "Torah study is a core value", "burden of service shared equally", "historic homeland", "keep Israel Jewish and democratic", "elected representatives should have the final say", "independent courts are an essential check". PRD FR4 asks that supporters recognise their option. Neutral system text (titles, descriptions, moreInfo, questions) avoids all such language.
- **Hebrew–English equivalence:** the texts were written as equivalents, not literal translations. For example, the Hebrew uses "שירות לאומי-אזרחי" (the official framework name), "בני ישיבות" and "מצטיינים בלימוד" rather than "עילויים", which can sound mocking. The English uses "civilian national service" and "outstanding scholars".
- **Gendered Hebrew:** the attorney general is referred to in the feminine form in moreInfo (the incumbent is a woman) and in the generic masculine in option text (the office). No personal names appear anywhere.

## 5. Open questions and uncertainties for the neutrality reviewer and owner

- **Q1. Is `iran-and-regional-security` matchable enough?** Most Jewish parties are hawkish. Options 1–3 differ mainly in emphasis, and option 4 is essentially the Arab parties' position. The alternative is to replace it with "Israel's international standing and relations with the US", or with "Haredi employment and budgets". It was kept because security and foreign affairs is the single highest-ranked consideration [1][3].
- **Q2. Housing alongside cost of living** means two economic issues among the 10. This is justified by the youth data [6], but the owner may prefer to merge them and free a slot.
- **Q3. Facts dated close to today:** the Gaza and Lebanon facts move quickly (Board of Peace, IDF positions, Iran fighting). Re-verify the moreInfo texts for `gaza` and `iran-and-regional-security` right before locking.
- **Q4. Judicial system has only 3 options.** A possible 4th is "keep the current system with targeted tweaks", for example narrowing the reasonableness standard (Amcha Yisrael [24]). It was judged too close to options 1–2.
- **Q5. October 7 inquiry, option 3** ("after fighting ends, broad mandate") is the weakest-sourced option. Please check whether any current list actually holds it. If not, consider replacing it with a "government/Knesset hybrid with a broad mandate" option, or leaving 2 options plus a third such as "secret Knesset ballot" (Amcha Yisrael), which is probably too niche.
- **Q6. Gaza, option 1** includes "encouraging voluntary emigration". This reflects the platforms of parties such as Otzma Yehudit [S, 38b], but it is the most contested phrase in the set. Critics call the idea "transfer". The reviewer should decide whether to keep the supporters' wording, add neutral qualification, or drop the clause.
- **Q7. Crime in Arab society** is framed around Arab communities, which is accurate to the data (Arabs are over 80% of homicide victims [S, 29c]). The reviewer should check that the title does not read as stigmatizing. An alternative is "Crime and violence in Arab communities".
- **Q8. Haredi enlistment, option 4** merges the Arab parties' opposition to compulsory service with a generic "objecting communities" framing. Haredi parties may prefer option 1, and Arab parties may want explicit mention of opposing military service on national grounds. Please check that it represents the Arab parties fairly.
- **Q9. "Judea and Samaria" in English.** The PRD settles this, but it is flagged because "West Bank" is equally mainstream in Israeli English media.
- **Q10. Likud gave no answer** to most ynet/ynetnews questionnaires [12][16][22][24]. Option design relied on government actions and statements for the coalition's positions, which is consistent with PRD FR11 (actions > statements).
