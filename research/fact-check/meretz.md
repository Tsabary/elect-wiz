# Fact-check: meretz

- Document checked: content/research/en/meretz.md (researchedAsOf 2026-10-07)
- Checked by: Stage 3 agent (not the Stage 2 author) on 2026-10-07
- Claims checked: 196 · Corrected: 32 · Removed: 1 · Sources replaced: 2 (plus 1 source removed and 8 added; the list now has 53 sources)
- Limited-information flag: false (Meretz has a full 2021–2022 Knesset voting and bill record, a detailed 2022 platform, a published April 2019 platform, a signed 2024 union agreement and broad press coverage. Method: every cited URL was opened (news sites via curl with a crawler user agent; the union-agreement PDF is a scan, so its eight pages were rendered and read as images). Bill sponsors and statuses come from KNS_Bill, KNS_BillInitiator, mk_individual and KNS_Status in the same mirror. - Dates: 30 June 2024 announcement (Maariv), 8 July 2024 signature (on the agreement's first page), 12 July 2024 Labor convention approval with one vote against (Maariv, Times of Israel), 20 Feb 2026 update (Ynet). Read all eight pages of the signed agreement: political union without merger into one corporation; Meretz remains a registered party with separate finances; standing committee headed by the serving secretary-general; one Meretz-filed candidate within the top four; no party-merger agreement so the list can't split under s.59(2) of the Knesset Law; guarantees end with the 26th Knesset. The top-four clause differs from the actual No. 6 placement; the reported Feb 2026 update (places 6, 8, 14) explains this, but its text was not found. This is stated in the document.
- Split history: Democratic Union (Sept 2019) and Labor–Gesher–Meretz (2020) from IDI profiles; the March–April 2020 faction sequence from KNS_Faction; Labor's 6 April 2020 split request from Globes.

## Notes for the consistency review
- The three Contradiction items were kept after re-verification. All three are coalition-era votes against opposition private bills (or, for Judea and Samaria, for a coalition-backed bill), with every coalition faction voting the same way. The document gives this context. Meretz's April 2019 platform establishes that the pledges predated the votes. Reviewers may want to check that other former coalition parties' documents treat coalition-discipline votes the same way.
- The Haredi enlistment, Iran, Gaza and October 7 sections rest on old (2022) evidence, the 2024 joint agreement (The Democrats' shared principles, not Meretz-only) or statements by Yair Golan (labelled as the joint list leader's). The "Closest option(s)" lines say so.
- Who formally chairs Meretz in 2026 remains unconfirmed. The party website's leadership page is stale (it lists Zehava Galon).
- The April 2019 platform PDF (IDI copy) has font-mapping errors in numbers (e.g. "29%" where the 2022 text has 80%), so only its non-numeric pledges are cited.
- The 28 June 2022 Climate Bill vote (15–0) could not be tied to a specific bill ID; the document describes it only as "a Climate Bill in a later vote".
