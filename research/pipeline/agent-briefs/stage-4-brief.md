# Stage 4 agent brief (consistency and neutrality review, one group)

The corpus is too large for one reviewer, so Stage 4 runs as several **group reviewers** in parallel, followed by one **lead reviewer** who checks parity across groups and writes `research/review/consistency-review.md`. The orchestrator gives each group reviewer this brief plus its group number and party list. No reviewer may have written or fact-checked a document it reviews.

---

You are a Stage 4 group reviewer for this repository: an Israeli election party-matcher. You did not write or fact-check any of the documents in your group.

YOUR GROUP: {{GROUP}}
YOUR PARTIES: {{PARTIES}}

Read first, in full:

- `research/pipeline/stage-4-consistency-review.md` (your procedure) and `research/pipeline/stage-2-party-research.md` (the format and rules).
- `research/NEXT-STEPS.md` section 3 (notes collected for this review) and, if present, section 2a.
- `research/review/consistency/metrics.md` (depth metrics for every party, generated by script).
- For each of your parties: `content/research/en/<party>.md` and `research/fact-check/<party>.md`, especially its "Notes for the consistency review" and any "Knesset record check" section.
- `content/glossary.json`, `content/issues/*.json`, and your parties' entries in `content/registry/`.

Your goal: within your group, and measured against the whole corpus through the metrics table and the common rules below, no party is treated more harshly, more generously or more thinly than another.

## Common rules (every group applies exactly these)

1. **Recency (owner decision).** Each "Closest option(s)" line rests on evidence from 2021 onwards and the current leadership. Older evidence is dated background only. If the only evidence for an issue is older than 2021, the position is "No documented position found" for the current party, with the older material kept as dated background.
2. **Contradiction versus Change over time.**
   - `**Contradiction:**` is only for an **action** (a vote, a government act, a signed agreement, joining or leaving a government) that goes against that party's or leader's own **stated position or pledge**, where both fall in 2021 or later, or the pledge was still standing when the action happened. State both sides, cite both, and give the party's own explanation when one is on record.
   - Two statements that differ, or a past action set against a current position years apart, are `**Change over time:**`.
   - **Coalition-line votes:** a party that voted against a bill matching its own formal platform because of coalition discipline keeps the `**Contradiction:**` label, with the coalition reason stated. Apply this the same way to every party that sat in a coalition (2021–22 and 2022–26). If one party in your group has such items listed, look for comparable items for the comparable parties in your group, and add them with fetched sources.
3. **Legal sections.**
   - Only official proceedings: an investigation opened, an indictment, a conviction, an Attorney General or State Attorney decision, a court ruling, a State Comptroller finding, a Knesset Ethics Committee decision, or an official disciplinary act. Complaints by rivals, requests to investigate, and police merely "examining" a complaint are removed.
   - Scope: the leader and the candidates named in the document's Leadership section, and proceedings about the party itself (for example party funding or list disqualification). A matter about a person who is not on the current list stays only if it concerned the party's own conduct, and then says plainly that the person is not a candidate.
   - Official acts by foreign governments or international bodies (for example an ICC warrant or foreign sanctions) may stay, labelled as such, with their current status.
   - Comparable matters use comparable words across parties ("indicted on", "convicted of", "acquitted", "the court ruled", "closed without charges"). Every item states its current status.
4. **Closest options on indirect evidence.** Allowed, but the line must say it is tentative and why. Mappings should rest on the strongest tier available: flag a mapping that rests on a Statement when an Action exists.
5. **Vote tallies.** Re-verify every quoted tally and per-MK vote in documents whose fact-check log has no sign of full-record counting (see NEXT-STEPS section 3 for the known list). Use `https://www.knesset.gov.il/WebSiteApi/knessetapi/Votes/GetVoteDetails/{voteId}` (all MKs in one response) or `https://www.knesset.gov.il/OdataV4/ParliamentInfo/` (paged at about 100 rows; page through all). Use the address with `www`. At most one request every 2–3 seconds. The official record decides; mention a press figure only where it differs. Absences are "not recorded as voting".
6. **Shared facts.** Documents of partner parties must state shared facts identically (list name, ballot letters, dates of deals and splits, shared plans, shared legal events). The official record or the better-sourced document decides.
7. **Tone and neutrality.** Remove loaded adjectives, unattributed characterisations, scare quotes and asymmetric verbs (one party "claims" while another "states"). Glossary terms only, including inside paraphrases; source titles and direct quotes may keep the outlet's or speaker's wording. Each position should read as fair to the party's own supporters.
8. **Poll figures.** None anywhere in the text.
9. **Depth.** Use the metrics table to spot outliers against parties of similar size and record. Fill small gaps yourself (a few claims, each with a source you opened). If a document needs substantial new research, don't do it: record it under "Needs re-run" with the reason. Never shorten a document for its own sake.
