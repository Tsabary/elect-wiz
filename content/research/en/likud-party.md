## Overview {#overview}

**Founding and history.** Likud was set up in 1973 as a joint list for the elections to the 8th Knesset. It brought together Herut (Menachem Begin's movement), the Liberal Party, the Free Center, the State List and the Movement for the Whole Land of Israel. The component parties formally merged into a single party in 1988 [1]. Likud first came to power in 1977 under Begin. It governed until 1984, took part in national unity governments between 1984 and 1990, and led governments again in 1996–1999, 2001–2005, 2009–2021 and from the end of 2022 [1]. Its leaders have included Menachem Begin, Yitzhak Shamir, Ariel Sharon and Benjamin Netanyahu [1].

**Ideology.** The Israel Democracy Institute (IDI), a non-partisan research body, describes Likud as a "national-liberal movement". Its founding charter combines national aims, such as the Jewish people's right to the Land of Israel and settlement throughout it, with liberal principles such as the rule of law, individual freedoms, opposition to discrimination and a free-market economy [1]. The party's registered name is "Likud – National Liberal Movement" [2]. On its website for the 2026 campaign, Likud presents "six pillars": security "without compromise", a free-market economy, Jerusalem and the Land of Israel, education and Jewish heritage, investment in society and the periphery (areas far from the centre of the country), and Israel's standing in the world [3]. The IDI's 2026 election guide links to this page as Likud's platform [4]. No longer, detailed 2026 platform document was found (see "Information availability").

**Base of support and election results.** In the November 2022 election Likud won 32 seats with 1,115,336 votes (23.4%), the largest number of any list [1]. It has 32 seats in the outgoing 25th Knesset, according to the Knesset's official member data [5, 6]. The Knesset dissolved itself on 17 July 2026 [7], and the election is set for 27 October 2026 [24].

**The list it runs on.** Likud runs on the list "Likud led by Benjamin Netanyahu for Prime Minister" (הליכוד בהנהגת בנימין נתניהו לראשות הממשלה), ballot letters **מחל**. The candidate list was submitted to the Central Elections Committee (CEC) jointly by "Likud – National Liberal Movement" and by "New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" (תקווה חדשה הימין הממלכתי), Gideon Sa'ar's party [2]. This makes it formally a **joint list**. The CEC filing names the submitting party for each candidate. Gideon Sa'ar (7) and Ze'ev Elkin (32) are listed as Likud candidates. The three candidates listed for New Hope are Talik Gvili (9), Ofir Saban (88) and Ehud Ninio (89) [2].

**The partner, New Hope, and its history with Likud.** New Hope was founded in December 2020 by Gideon Sa'ar after he left Likud, following a failed challenge to Netanyahu for the party leadership. At the time Sa'ar said Likud had become "a tool serving the personal interests of its leader" [8, 9]. The splits and reunions are, in order:

- **December 2020:** Sa'ar leaves Likud and founds New Hope, joined by other former Likud figures including Ze'ev Elkin [8].
- **2021:** New Hope runs separately, wins 6 seats and joins the Bennett–Lapid government, with Sa'ar as justice minister [8].
- **July 2022:** New Hope merges with Benny Gantz's Blue and White into the National Unity list for the 2022 election [10].
- **12 March 2024:** Sa'ar splits New Hope's 4 seats away from National Unity [10].
- **29 September 2024:** New Hope joins Netanyahu's government, bringing its four MKs into the coalition [11].
- **13 March 2025:** New Hope signs an agreement to merge into Likud ahead of the next election. Under the agreement the two run a joint slate, Sa'ar picks New Hope's first candidate, who is placed no lower than 15th, and New Hope members formally join Likud [9, 12].
- **13 August 2025:** Likud's Central Committee approves the merger. Sa'ar said it was "good to return home" [9].
- **14 July 2026:** The heads of the Likud and Statesmanlike Right (New Hope) factions agree to change the arrangement. Instead of actually merging the two factions in the outgoing 25th Knesset, they implement the agreement by running together on one candidate list for the 26th Knesset. This is how the Supreme Court described the change in its later ruling [91].

The full merger was therefore never completed in the Knesset. New Hope MK Sharren Haskel opposed the merger and refused to run on the joint list. On 19 July 2026 she petitioned the High Court of Justice to order the two parties to formally complete the merger. She argued that they had deliberately left it unfinished so that she could not split off as a one-member faction and take with her the state funding attached to her seat [13]. On 9 August 2026 the court, in a ruling written by Supreme Court President Yitzhak Amit, rejected the petition in full. It said that Haskel's delay in acting since March 2025 undermined her claim, and it accepted the factions' later choice of a joint run instead of a merger. Because the factions agreed on the joint run only after her petition, it did not order her to pay costs [91]. In August 2026 Likud's internal court likewise described Sa'ar's place on the list as "not actually a reservation, but rather a joint run of two factions within one list of candidates" [92]. The Knesset's member data still lists a separate four-member faction, "the Statesmanlike Right" (הימין הממלכתי), with Ze'ev Elkin, Akram Hasson, Michel Buskila and Sharren Haskel [5]. Haskel resigned as deputy foreign minister after voting against the July 2026 law freezing arrests of yeshiva students who did not report for service, and she now leads a separate party, "Israel First" [13, 14]. This document covers Likud. New Hope is researched separately.

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Leader: Benjamin Netanyahu** (born 1949). He served in the IDF's Sayeret Matkal unit, was Israel's ambassador to the UN (1984–1988), deputy foreign minister, foreign minister (2002–2003) and finance minister (2003–2005). He chaired Likud and led the opposition in 1993–1996 and again in 2005–2009, and has been prime minister in 1996–1999, 2009–2021 and since December 2022 [15]. Netanyahu heads the list, and for 2026 he secured the right to place eight candidates of his choice within the top 30 places [16].

**How the list was chosen.** Likud held a primary on 17 August 2026. 76,068 party members voted, a turnout of 53.5% [16]. Netanyahu said he was pleased with the result [16]. The Jerusalem Post's analysis was that members favoured experienced, loyal politicians [17]. Several sitting ministers, including May Golan, Gila Gamliel and Idit Silman, did not reach realistic places [16]. In the final CEC filing Gamliel is 25th, Golan 40th and Silman 46th [2]. Likud submitted a 35-person slate. Nine of its candidates were placed by Netanyahu, among them Defense Minister Israel Katz, Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar and minister Haim Katz, and only four are women [6].

**Top of the list (CEC filing)** [2]:

1. **Benjamin Netanyahu**, prime minister (see above).
2. **Eli (Eliyahu) Cohen**, energy and infrastructure minister; came first in the 2026 primary [2, 16].
3. **Amir Ohana**, Speaker of the 25th Knesset since December 2022. He was earlier justice minister and public security minister. Before politics he was a military police officer and worked for the Shin Bet. He chairs the Likud Pride group, which promotes LGBT equality inside the party [18].
4. **Yariv Levin**, deputy prime minister and justice minister, a lawyer and former deputy head of the Israel Bar Association. He was Knesset Speaker in 2020–2021 [19]. He led the government's changes to the judicial system (see "Judicial system").
5. **Miri Regev**, transportation minister [16].
6. **Israel Katz**, defense minister [6, 20].
7. **Gideon Sa'ar**, foreign minister and leader of New Hope, placed under the merger agreement [6, 9]. The CEC filing lists him as a Likud candidate [2]. In August 2026 Ynetnews reported that the merger agreement gave New Hope two places, one in the top five for Sa'ar and one for Michel Buskila [21]. Buskila does not appear anywhere on the 120-name CEC filing [2].
8. **Ofir Katz**, coalition chair and chair of the Knesset House Committee [22].
9. **Talik Gvili** (spelled טלי גואילי in the CEC filing), a newcomer placed by Netanyahu. She is the mother of Ran Gvili, the last hostage whose remains were returned from Gaza. The CEC filing lists her as New Hope's candidate [2, 6, 93].
10. **Yoav Kisch** [2].
11. **Jacob Bardugo**, a lawyer, former head of the national lottery (Mifal HaPais) and commentator on Channel 14, placed by Netanyahu. He has never held elected office [23].

Other names on the list include Makhlouf (Miki) Zohar (12), Amichai Chikli (14), Haim Katz (18), David Amsalem (19), Boaz Bismuth (21), Shlomo Karhi (23), Nir Barkat (24) and Ze'ev Elkin (32) [2]. Haim Katz has served as construction and housing minister [24, 25]. Bismuth chairs the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee [26].

**Departures before the election.** Two Likud MKs left over the Haredi enlistment issue. Yuli Edelstein left the party in July 2026. A year earlier he had been removed as chair of the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee for refusing to advance an enlistment bill that, in his view, lacked "significant enlistment targets, as well as personal and institutional sanctions" [27, 94]. Dan Illouz joined Yisrael Beytenu, saying it "represents the national and liberal values in which I believe" [28].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

**Formal commitment:** Likud's campaign website lists a "free economy" as one of its six pillars: "free market, less regulation, more competition", so that every citizen can "earn with dignity" [3].

**Action (imports and competition):** As economy minister, Likud's Nir Barkat led the "What's good for Europe is good for Israel" reform. The Knesset passed it in August 2024. Products that meet European Union standards can now be imported and sold without extra Israeli requirements. Barkat called it "a reform that promises generational change" [29]. In October 2025 the State Comptroller, the official watchdog of government bodies, reported that three import reforms in four years, including this one, had not lowered the cost of living. He pointed to the lack of a clear enforcement policy and to small importers not entering the market. Barkat replied that the reforms were "already proving themselves" with more competition and falling prices [30].

**Action (taxes):** On 1 January 2025 Netanyahu's government raised VAT (the tax added to almost everything people buy) from 17% to 18%, as part of tax rises to cover wartime defense costs. The Knesset passed the 2025 budget on 25 March 2025, 66 to 52 [31]. The finance ministry is held by a coalition partner, Bezalel Smotrich of Religious Zionism, not by Likud [31].

**Action (government-wide measures):** In December 2025 the government approved Smotrich's dairy reform, which would cut milk prices by 15% and remove import tariffs on dairy products. Dairy farmers protested, and in February 2026 the Knesset's legal adviser recommended taking it out of the budget's accompanying law [32]. A Times of Israel analysis in September 2026 noted that economic issues were largely absent from the campaign. It described the dairy reform as unsuccessful and mentioned a recent tax cut that lowered fuel prices [33].

**Statement:** On its website Likud highlights figures such as 12-month inflation of 1.5% and a 0.5% fall in food prices in August 2026 [3].

**Closest option(s):** The party's stated approach and its main reform favour opening the market to competition and imports (`open-market-competition-imports`). Its government raised VAT rather than cutting it, so the evidence does not support `cut-taxes-and-government-spending`.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

**Action:** On 15 July 2026 the government approved a plan, proposed by Prime Minister Netanyahu, Social Equality Minister May Golan (Likud) and National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir (Otzma Yehudit), that brings the Shin Bet (Israel's internal security service) into the fight against organized crime in Arab communities. The plan moves about NIS 497 million from "Plan 550", a five-year NIS 30 billion programme set up in 2021 to develop Arab communities. NIS 345 million goes to a Shin Bet unit on drug and weapons smuggling and 130 more staff, and NIS 132.4 million goes to a national police unit [34]. Netanyahu said that combining the Shin Bet's "intelligence, operational and technological abilities" with police action would "restore personal security to citizens" [34]. Human rights groups and Arab community organizations objected, arguing that crime among Jewish citizens is handled by ordinary policing [34].

**Action:** Earlier, on 14 December 2025, ministers moved NIS 220 million from the same development programme to the police [35]. Golan had originally asked for NIS 3.1 billion [35].

**Closest option(s):** Using extraordinary measures, including the Shin Bet, funded partly by moving money out of Arab community development (`emergency-powers-shin-bet`). No Likud position specifically on holding crime bosses without trial was found in the sources reviewed.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Action / Statement:** On 29 September 2025 Netanyahu accepted President Trump's plan to end the Gaza war, saying it "achieves our war aims": "Hamas will be disarmed. Gaza will be demilitarized." He said the Palestinian Authority "can have no role whatsoever in Gaza without undergoing a radical and genuine transformation", that Israel "will retain security responsibility, including a security perimeter for the foreseeable future", and that further withdrawals would be "linked to the extent of disarmament and demilitarization". He also said that if Hamas rejected the plan, "Israel will finish the job by itself" [36].

**Action:** On 28 May 2026 Netanyahu said: "At this point, we are fully in control of 60% of the territory of the Gaza Strip… and my directive is to get to… 70%." When an audience member called for 100%, he answered "First 70%… we'll start with that" [37]. The Times of Israel reported this as going well beyond the ceasefire lines [37]. A May 2026 letter from the US-led Board of Peace said Israel's ceasefire commitments would become "null and void" if Hamas did not accept the disarmament framework, and Hamas had largely refused to give up all its weapons [38].

**Statement:** Defense Minister Israel Katz (Likud) said on 27 May 2026 that Israel is advancing a plan for "voluntary emigration" from Gaza, "everything at the right time and in the right manner" [39]. At the state October 7 memorial on 5 October 2026 Netanyahu said "In Gaza there will not be any Hamas rule" and that everyone involved in the attack is "living on borrowed time" [40].

**Contradiction:** In September 2025 Netanyahu accepted a plan under which Israel would make further withdrawals linked to Hamas's disarmament [36]. In May 2026 he said he had ordered the IDF to expand its control of Gaza from 60% to 70%, which The Times of Israel reported as going well beyond the terms of the ceasefire [37]. On the other side, the plan linked withdrawals to disarmament [36], Hamas had largely refused to give up all its weapons, and earlier that month the Board of Peace had said Israel's truce commitments would become "null and void" in that case [38].

**Closest option(s):** Holding positions and keeping military pressure until Hamas disarms, with Israel keeping security control and not relying on foreign forces (`hold-positions-disarmament-first`). Some actions and statements, such as expanding control and the defense minister's talk of voluntary emigration, go further, towards `resume-fighting-full-control`. The evidence is mixed.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Background.** The legal exemption for yeshiva students expired in 2023, and in June 2024 the Supreme Court ruled that they must be called up like everyone else [41].

**Action (the "Bismuth outline"):** On 23 July 2025 Likud's Knesset members voted 29 to 4 to remove Yuli Edelstein as chair of the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee and replace him with Likud MK Boaz Bismuth. The IDI says Netanyahu decided on the change [26, 94]. On 28 October 2025 Bismuth published an outline for a new law. According to the IDI, it set first-year enlistment targets of 4,800, rising to 8,500 by the fifth year, before a 25% "safety net" margin is applied. It kept the exemption age at 26 and would phase sanctions in gradually while lifting the existing sanctions once the law passed [26]. The IDI argued that the bill "does not establish a general and equal enlistment duty for Haredim comparable to that applied to other sectors" [26]. Bismuth called the bill historic, and the IDI notes that Netanyahu shared his position, while some Likud MKs opposed it [41]. In March 2026 Netanyahu and Smotrich said they were setting the bill aside during the Iran war, and Bismuth said it would be completed after the war [41]. In May 2026 Netanyahu's office offered to resume committee work on the bill. The Haredi parties rejected the latest version, and it never passed [42, 43].

**Action (arrest freeze):** On 14 July 2026 the Knesset passed, 58 to 54, a temporary law freezing arrests, investigations and enforcement against yeshiva students who did not report for service [14, 44]. Several coalition MKs voted against it, including Likud's Dan Illouz and Yuli Edelstein and New Hope's Sharren Haskel. Netanyahu was in the chamber earlier but left before the vote [14]. The Supreme Court blocked the law with a temporary order on 15 July 2026 [44].

**Action (Basic Law: Torah Study):** On 13 July 2026 the coalition passed a basic law declaring Torah study a "foundational value", 63 to 52 (see "Religion and state") [45].

**Statement:** In early August 2026, according to Kan and N12 reports cited by The Jerusalem Post, Netanyahu said the arrest-freeze law had been unsuccessful and would not be continued. He said it had been meant to "lower the flames and increase the number of recruits among the haredi public", and that draft-eligible men who do not study Torah would have to "enlist or go to prison". He promised a "broad national government" that would pass a "full conscription law that meets the IDF's needs" [46]. On its website Likud says 4,298 Haredim enlisted in the latest year, up 53% from 2,811 [3].

**Contradiction:** Netanyahu says he wants a conscription law that "meets the IDF's needs" and that draft-eligible men who do not study Torah will "enlist or go to prison" [46]. But in July 2026 his coalition passed, 58 to 54, a law freezing arrests of draft-eligible yeshiva students, and the reported Likud votes against came from Dan Illouz and Yuli Edelstein [14]. The bill advanced by Likud's own committee chair is one that the IDI says "does not establish a general and equal enlistment duty for Haredim" [26].

**Closest option(s):** Gradual enlistment targets agreed with Haredi leaders, with limited and phased sanctions (`gradual-targets-by-agreement`). The arrest freeze and the Torah-study basic law also lean towards `anchor-torah-study-exemption`, while Netanyahu's campaign statements lean the other way. The evidence is mixed.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

**Formal commitment:** Likud's "society and periphery" pillar promises investment in "the periphery, housing, health and infrastructure" [3].

**Action:** Likud's Haim Katz has headed the Construction and Housing Ministry since Yitzhak Goldknopf (United Torah Judaism) resigned from it in mid-2025, first as interim minister [24, 25]. On 10 August 2026 Netanyahu, Katz and Smotrich announced "From Reserve Duty to a Key": about 20,000 discounted homes and building plots for IDF reservists in 2026–2027, including about 16,000 apartments through the "Target Price" programme (discounted apartments sold by lottery) and 4,000 plots. Reservists get priority for 75% of apartments, discounts can exceed NIS 500,000, and plots in national priority areas near the borders are exempt from land-value payments [24]. Netanyahu said reservists "give everything for the country, and they deserve everything" [24].

**Statement:** Likud's website points to a 1.2% annual fall in home prices in June–July 2026 [3].

**Closest option(s):** Direct help for buyers through discounted-price programmes and grants to those who served (`direct-help-for-buyers`), with some emphasis on the periphery (`develop-periphery-and-transport`). No specific Likud plan to speed up planning or to build state rental housing was found.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Action:** On 28 February 2026 Netanyahu announced "Operation Roaring Lion", a joint operation with the United States against Iran. He spoke of removing "the existential threat posed to Israel by the regime", said "we will strike thousands of targets belonging to the terrorist regime" and urged Iranians to seize a "once-in-a-generation chance" [47]. When the US and Iran agreed a ceasefire in April 2026, Netanyahu said it "could turn on a dime" and that Israel backed Trump's naval blockade of Iran [48]. On 11 June 2026 he said "Israel is not a party" to the US–Iran memorandum of understanding, and set out what he expected from a final deal: removal of enriched nuclear material, dismantling of enrichment infrastructure, limits on missile production and an end to Iran's support for its regional allies [49].

**Action (Lebanon):** After Hezbollah joined the war, Defense Minister Israel Katz said on 24 March 2026 that the IDF would hold a "security zone" up to the Litani River and that "where there is terror and missiles, there will be no homes and no residents" [50]. On 9 April 2026 Netanyahu announced direct negotiations with Lebanon, aimed at disarming Hezbollah and reaching peace, while saying there would be "no ceasefire in Lebanon" during the talks [51]. A US-brokered framework agreed in June 2026 provides for a gradual Israeli withdrawal, Hezbollah's disarmament and the deployment of the Lebanese army. As of late September 2026 implementation had stalled, Hezbollah had not disarmed and Israel kept positions in southern Lebanon [52].

**Statement:** On 5 October 2026 Netanyahu said Iran "is the weakest that it has been since its founding", that Israel would keep striking Hezbollah while working towards peace with Lebanon, and that Israel "still has more missions before it" [40]. Likud's "Israel in the world" pillar promises to expand "the circle of peace" and strategic alliances [3].

**Closest option(s):** Keeping up military pressure while negotiating, and turning it into US-backed deals with security guarantees before pulling back (`force-plus-us-backed-agreements`). The refusal of a ceasefire in Lebanon during talks and the open-ended security zone also reflect `military-pressure-until-threats-removed`.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Formal commitment:** Likud's "Jerusalem and the Land of Israel" pillar says "United Jerusalem is Israel's capital forever" and promises "strengthening settlement and sovereignty throughout the land" [3].

**Action (settlements):** On 11 September 2025 Netanyahu signed a framework to speed up building in Ma'ale Adumim, including the 3,412-home E1 plan east of Jerusalem, saying "There will be no Palestinian state; this place belongs to us" [53]. In June 2026 the cabinet was reported to be set to fund the de facto establishment of 61 new settlements, backed by Smotrich [54]. In August 2026 the Housing Ministry, headed by Likud's Haim Katz, published a tender for 1,234 homes in E1, which led several European countries to consider sanctions [24, 55].

**Action (applying sovereignty):** On 23 July 2025 the Knesset passed, 71 to 13, a non-binding motion calling on the government to apply Israeli sovereignty to the settlements. It was co-sponsored by Likud MK Dan Illouz, among others, and was backed across the coalition, including Likud. Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana said: "This is our land… Jews cannot be the 'occupier' of a land that for 3,000 years has been called Judea" [56]. On 22 October 2025, during US Vice President JD Vance's visit, two bills to apply sovereignty passed preliminary votes, one of them 25 to 24. Nearly all Likud MKs stayed away from the vote. The one exception, Likud MK Yuli Edelstein, voted for the broader bill and gave it its one-vote margin. Netanyahu's office called it "a deliberate political provocation by the opposition" and said "without Likud support these bills are unlikely to go anywhere" [57]. The next day, President Trump said Israel "would lose all of its support from the United States" if it annexed the territory [57].

**Statement:** Speaking about the Gaza plan in September 2025, Netanyahu said recognizing a Palestinian state would "reward terror, undermine security, and endanger Israel's very existence" [36]. In August 2026 Likud said a right-wing government was needed to keep the "historic momentum in the settlement of Judea and Samaria" [58].

**Contradiction:** Likud's 2026 platform page promises "strengthening… sovereignty throughout the land" [3], and Likud backed the July 2025 non-binding sovereignty motion [56]. But when actual sovereignty bills came to a vote in October 2025, Likud stayed away (apart from Edelstein) and Netanyahu's office called them a provocation. The votes took place during a US vice-presidential visit, and the US president came out against annexation the next day [57].

**Closest option(s):** Strengthening and expanding settlements, with no Palestinian state, while not in practice applying sovereignty (`maintain-current-situation`). The party's stated platform and its rhetoric point towards applying sovereignty (`full-sovereignty` or `partial-sovereignty-area-c`), but its votes so far have not followed through. The evidence is mixed.

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Background.** Yariv Levin, who became justice minister in December 2022, had said before the 2022 election that he wanted to "end rule by judges". He backed moving the choice of judges to elected politicians and letting a 61-MK majority re-enact laws the Supreme Court strikes down [59].

**Action:** In July 2023 the coalition passed a law barring courts from reviewing government decisions on the grounds of "reasonableness". On 1 January 2024 the Supreme Court struck it down by 8 to 7, the first time it annulled part of a basic law [60]. On 27 March 2025 the Knesset passed the Judicial Selection Law, 67 to 1, after the opposition boycotted the vote. It changes the nine-member committee that picks judges so that six members are politicians, and it requires coalition and opposition agreement on Supreme Court appointments. It takes effect when the next Knesset begins [61]. Judicial appointments were frozen for about 18 months until the High Court ordered Levin to move ahead. 79 judges and registrars were sworn in in September 2026, and Levin called it "a revolution in the human composition" of the courts [62].

**Action (attorney general):** In June 2025 the government changed the procedure for dismissing the attorney general, the government's top legal adviser, and on 4 August 2025 it voted unanimously to fire Gali Baharav-Miara. Levin said: "You cannot force the government to remain with an oppositional attorney general on procedural, technical grounds" [63] and "The government, and only the government, will decide who its legal adviser will be" [64]. On 14 December 2025 a seven-justice panel of the Supreme Court unanimously cancelled both decisions [65]. On 15 July 2026 the Knesset passed a law, sponsored by Simcha Rothman (Religious Zionism), that lets the government disregard the attorney general's legal opinions except in some criminal matters, ends her exclusive right to present the state's position in court, and gives the justice minister oversight of her work. It takes effect on 1 January 2027 [66].

**Closest option(s):** Giving elected officials more say in choosing judges, cutting the courts' power over government decisions, and ending the binding force of the attorney general's opinions (`continue-judicial-changes`).

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Statement:** Netanyahu opposes a state commission of inquiry, whose members are picked by the President of the Supreme Court. He proposes instead a "democratic and balanced" commission, with half its members chosen by the government and half by the opposition [67]. In February 2026 he published a 55-page response to the State Comptroller's October 7 investigation, saying he had repeatedly pushed to kill Hamas leaders while security chiefs had opposed it [67].

**Action:** On 16 November 2025, instead of setting up a state commission, the government formed a ministerial committee, headed by Levin, to decide on the inquiry's mandate [68]. On 19 November 2025 the High Court of Justice issued an order requiring the government to explain why it had not set up a state commission [68]. In December 2025 Likud MK Ariel Kallner, acting for Netanyahu, presented a bill for a six-member commission. The Knesset would first try to agree on all members by an 80-MK majority. If that failed, the coalition and the opposition would each pick three, with two co-chairs, and four representatives of bereaved families would oversee the work. If the opposition refused to take part, Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana would appoint its members. Kallner said "all state systems should be examined — political, security, and judicial" [69]. The Knesset's legal advisers warned that letting the government set the topics would undermine an independent investigation, and that an even number of members could lead to deadlock [70]. By mid-July 2026 the bill had passed a first reading in the Knesset [45].

**Closest option(s):** A commission picked through the Knesset, split between coalition and opposition, with bereaved families involved (`commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`).

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

**Formal commitment:** Likud's "education, identity and heritage" pillar speaks of "connection to Israel's heritage" and "pride in a Jewish democratic state" [3].

**Action (coalition agreements):** Likud's December 2022 coalition agreement with United Torah Judaism promised legislation on "the value of Torah studies" and further military exemptions for full-time religious study. It also allowed "gender-segregated public events" and set up a panel to review eligibility under the Law of Return [71].

**Action (Basic Law: Torah Study):** On 13 July 2026 the Knesset passed Basic Law: Torah Study, 63 to 52. The law, sponsored by the Haredi parties, declares Torah study "a foundational value in the heritage of the Jewish people and the state of Israel" [72, 73]. Two Likud MKs, Edelstein and Illouz, voted against it, and Netanyahu was absent [45]. Earlier wording was dropped before the final vote. Under it, those devoted to Torah study would have been "deemed… as persons who are performing meaningful service for the state of Israel and the Jewish people", and Torah study would have been "balanced with other foundational values". The Knesset's legal advisers had warned that the balancing wording could lead to benefits like those given to people who serve in the IDF [45, 73].

**Action (LGBT and family law, related):** In July 2018 Netanyahu publicly backed surrogacy rights for single men, which would have included gay couples, and then voted against the measure two days later. The Times of Israel linked this to pressure from the Haredi parties [74]. Amir Ohana, number 3 on the list, chairs the Likud Pride group [18].

No Likud bill or formal position on Shabbat public transport, civil marriage or conversion was found in the sources reviewed.

**Closest option(s):** Keeping the current arrangements mostly as they are (`keep-status-quo`). Supporting the Torah-study basic law and the coalition commitments to the Haredi parties go somewhat towards `strengthen-jewish-character`, but no Likud move to enforce Shabbat by law or add religious law to state law was found.

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Death penalty for terrorists {#other-death-penalty}

**Action:** On 30 March 2026 the Knesset passed, 62 to 47, a law sponsored by Itamar Ben Gvir (Otzma Yehudit). It makes death by hanging the default sentence for Palestinians from Judea and Samaria convicted in military courts of deadly terror attacks. Judges may give life imprisonment only in "special circumstances". For Israeli citizens it applies only to killing "with the aim of denying the existence of the State of Israel" [75]. Netanyahu, who had earlier voiced reservations and whose office pushed for softer wording, voted in favour [75].

**Action:** On 12 May 2026 the Knesset voted 93 to 0 for a law setting up a special military court to try about 300 people captured during the October 7 attack. It allows the death penalty for those convicted of genocide. Justice Minister Levin called it "one of the most important moments of the current Knesset" [76].

### Media and the public broadcaster {#other-media}

**Action:** Communications Minister Shlomo Karhi (Likud) has pushed a bill to close the public broadcaster Kan if no private buyer is found within two years. He has refused to appoint members to Kan's council, which blocked approval of its budget. The coalition also moved a bill on overseeing Kan's budget from the Economics Committee to the Finance Committee, which is headed by a Netanyahu ally. Karhi said the bill would move control from "bureaucrats and legal advisers" to elected representatives [77]. On 16 July 2026 the coalition passed Karhi's broadcasting law, 53 to 48. It expands government regulation of broadcasting, eases cross-ownership limits and removes some content requirements. Karhi said it would bring "more diversity, more freedom, more right-wing coverage and less regulation". Petitions against it were filed at the High Court [78]. Jacob Bardugo, number 11 on the list, has called for closing Kan [23].

### Free-market economy and technology {#other-economy}

**Formal commitment:** Likud presents itself as the party of a "free market, less regulation, more competition" [3].

**Statement:** On its website Likud highlights an August 2026 unemployment rate of 2.8%, tech exports of about $85 billion in 2025 and around 775 new start-ups in 2025. It also lists NIS 1.5 billion for a science and maths education initiative ("Israel Realit") and new medical and computer-science faculties in Haifa [3].

### Defense build-up and the reservists {#other-defense-reservists}

**Formal commitment:** Likud's first pillar is "security without compromise": "a strong IDF, decisive action against the enemy, and security for every citizen" [3].

**Action / Statement:** Likud points to the delivery of the Iron Beam laser air-defense system in December 2025 and to defense exports of $19.2 billion in 2025 [3]. The government's reservist housing programme gives reservists priority for 75% of discounted apartments [24]. Officials cited NIS 25 billion in earlier assistance to reservists and their families [24].

### Jerusalem {#other-jerusalem}

**Formal commitment:** "United Jerusalem is Israel's capital forever" [3]. At the September 2025 signing of the Ma'ale Adumim plan, next to Jerusalem, Netanyahu pledged to "double the city's population", referring to Ma'ale Adumim [53].

### Foreign relations and peace agreements {#other-foreign-relations}

**Formal commitment:** Likud promises to expand "the circle of peace", strengthen strategic alliances and make Israel a regional and technological power [3].

**Statement:** Netanyahu describes the alliance with the United States as being at "an all-time high" [47] and has rejected claims of a rift with the US administration [48]. He has opposed recognition of a Palestinian state [36, 53].

**Statement:** Amir Ohana, number 3 on the list, chairs the Likud Pride group, which promotes equal rights for LGBT people within the party [18].

**Contradiction:** In July 2018 Netanyahu said that denying surrogacy to single fathers "simply isn't fair", then voted against the amendment two days later [74].

### Arab communities and development funds {#other-arab-communities}

**Action:** The Likud-led government has moved a total of about NIS 717 million from the Plan 550 development programme for Arab communities to policing and the Shin Bet: NIS 220 million in December 2025 and about NIS 497 million in July 2026 [34, 35]. The scope is specific: the money is taken from a programme for Arab communities and used for law enforcement in those communities.

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Stated preferences for 2026.**

- **Statement (24 August 2026):** Netanyahu warned right-wing voters against "splinter parties that will only burn our votes", saying fragmentation "guarantees the rise of the left" [79].
- **Statement (28 August 2026):** Netanyahu called on Smotrich's Religious Zionism and Ben Gvir's Otzma Yehudit to run together "to save the right-wing bloc". When Ben Gvir refused, Likud said he had decided "to coronate a left-wing government of Gadi Eisenkot" [58].
- **Statement (early August 2026):** Netanyahu promised to form a "broad national government" after the election [46].

- **Statement (21 July 2026):** A Likud campaign ad said Eisenkot "has no government without Yair Golan and the Arab parties", presenting dependence on Arab parties as a reason to oppose him [82].

No explicit 2026 pledge by Likud ruling a specific party in or out was found in the sources reviewed.

**Track record on coalition pledges.**

- **2020:** Netanyahu signed a rotation agreement with Benny Gantz under which Gantz would become prime minister in November 2021. The government fell in December 2020 when no budget passed, before the rotation took place. Gantz accused Netanyahu of blocking the budget to avoid the rotation, and Netanyahu said Gantz had "walked back his agreements" [83].
- **2022:** After the November 2022 election, Netanyahu formed a coalition with Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism, Noam, Shas and United Torah Judaism. Otzma Yehudit was the first to sign [71, 84].
- **October 2023:** After the October 7 attack Netanyahu invited the opposition into an emergency government, and Gantz's National Unity joined [10, 85].

## Track record {#track-record}

Likud has led the government since December 2022. Item by item:

- **Judicial changes (coalition agreement commitment** [71]**):** Partly delivered. The reasonableness law was struck down [60]. The judicial selection law passed but only takes effect in the next Knesset [61]. The law weakening the attorney general's opinions passed and takes effect on 1 January 2027 [66]. - **Gaza war aims ("Hamas will be disarmed. Gaza will be demilitarized"** [36]**):** The remains of the last hostage were returned on 26 January 2026, according to Likud [3]. Hamas had not disarmed as of May 2026, and Israel controls more than half of Gaza [37, 38].
- **Iran:** Israel and the US went to war with Iran in February 2026 [47]. As of June 2026 Netanyahu was still setting conditions for a final US–Iran deal that had not yet been agreed [49].
- **Lebanon:** A framework for Hezbollah's disarmament and Israeli withdrawal was agreed in June 2026, but its implementation had stalled as of September 2026 [52].
- **Cost of living:** The import reform passed [29], but the State Comptroller found that import reforms had not lowered prices [30]. VAT rose to 18% [31].
- **Settlements:** The E1 plan was approved and tendered [53, 55]. Sovereignty, part of the 2026 platform, was not applied [57].
- **October 7 accountability:** No commission of inquiry of any kind had been set up as of mid-July 2026 [45, 68].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

**Benjamin Netanyahu: criminal trial (ongoing).** The attorney general indicted Netanyahu in three cases, and his trial in the Jerusalem District Court is ongoing [86]. The charges are fraud and breach of trust in all three cases and bribery in Case 4000 [86]:
- **Case 1000:** receiving gifts worth hundreds of thousands of shekels from Hollywood producer Arnon Milchan, allegedly in return for favours.
- **Case 2000:** alleged contacts with Yedioth Ahronoth publisher Arnon Mozes about favourable coverage in exchange for legislation to weaken the rival Israel Hayom.
- **Case 4000:** allegedly approving regulatory decisions worth hundreds of millions of shekels to Bezeq's controlling shareholder Shaul Elovitch in return for favourable coverage on the Walla news site.

Netanyahu denies all the charges. He finished his testimony on 24 June 2026 after 98 hearings that began on 10 December 2024 [86]. The trial resumed after the summer recess in early September 2026 with the remaining defense witnesses for Netanyahu and his three co-defendants, before a three-judge panel headed by Judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman. From 4 October 2026 hearings are scheduled five days a week. The judges have said they see "evidentiary difficulties in proving bribery" in Case 4000 and suggested the prosecution consider withdrawing that charge, but as of September 2026 the charge had not been changed. No verdict has been given [95].

**Pardon request (pending).** In November 2025 Netanyahu asked President Isaac Herzog for a pardon before any verdict. According to the reports, he did not admit guilt. The Justice Ministry's pardons department recommended against a pardon, while Heritage Minister Amichai Eliyahu, standing in for Justice Minister Levin, submitted a position paper in favour on 24 March 2026. The president's legal adviser is preparing a recommendation [87]. On 26 April 2026 President Herzog said that before addressing the pardon request, "efforts should first be exhausted to reach an agreement between the parties, outside the courtroom" [96]. No decision on the pardon was found in the sources reviewed as of October 2026.

**State commission on the submarine affair (ongoing).** A state commission of inquiry chaired by retired Supreme Court President Asher Grunis is examining decisions on buying submarines and naval vessels between 2009 and 2016. In June 2024 Netanyahu received a warning letter, a formal notice that he may be harmed by its findings. The commission said it was considering whether his conduct "seriously disrupted established decision-making processes and created risks to national security", and it scheduled hearings for 1 November to 31 December 2026 [88].

**International Criminal Court warrant (outstanding).** On 21 November 2024 the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant over the Gaza war, at the same time as a warrant for Hamas commander Mohammed Deif [89]. The ICC's official case page, as captured on 6 October 2026, lists the warrant as issued on 21 November 2024 and gives Netanyahu's status as "at large", meaning the warrant has not been carried out. It says the charges allege responsibility for the war crimes of starvation as a method of warfare and of intentionally directing an attack against the civilian population, and for the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution and other inhumane acts, from at least 8 October 2023 until at least 20 May 2024 [97]. When it issued the warrants, the ICC's Pre-Trial Chamber also rejected challenges by Israel to the court's jurisdiction [98].

**State Comptroller's October 7 review (frozen).** The Supreme Court froze the State Comptroller's investigation into government failures before October 7 in December 2025, six days after Netanyahu submitted his response to it. Netanyahu has called for the investigation to resume [67].

**Haim Katz (number 18; construction and housing minister): conviction (closed).** On 7 February 2022 a Jerusalem magistrates' court approved a plea agreement under which Katz was convicted of conspiracy to achieve a legitimate goal by forbidden means, over legislation he had promoted about a decade earlier. He received a six-month suspended sentence and a NIS 75,000 fine. The prosecution said the offence carried no moral turpitude and involved no corruption [90].

**Checked:** Court and prosecution reporting on the other top candidates (Eli Cohen, Amir Ohana, Yariv Levin, Miri Regev, Israel Katz, Ofir Katz, Gideon Sa'ar) in the sources reviewed. No indictments, convictions or official inquiries naming them personally were found. This was not an exhaustive search of every candidate.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

There is extensive public information about Likud. It has led the government since December 2022, so its record can be measured through Knesset votes, legislation, government decisions and court rulings, and there is wide coverage in Israeli media across the spectrum. The main gap is that **no detailed written 2026 platform was found**. The party's campaign website gives only short "six pillars" statements and lists of achievements [3], so many positions here are drawn from government actions and leaders' statements rather than from a formal platform. Positions that belong to coalition partners, such as the finance ministry's economic policy, the death penalty law and the attorney general law, are attributed to the government or to their sponsors. Likud's own role, such as its votes or its ministers' sponsorship, is stated where known. Some sources were only partly accessible: the CEC's gov.il pages could not be reached, so the IDI-hosted copy of the CEC list was used. The International Criminal Court's website could not be reached directly, so an archived copy of its official case page from 6 October 2026 was used. Several Hebrew Ynet and Kan items and some international outlets could not be opened and are not cited. Statements by individual Likud candidates on issues such as Shabbat and civil marriage were not found in the sources reviewed.
