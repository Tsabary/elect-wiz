## Overview {#overview}

**What Atid Echad is.** Atid Echad – A Good Future for Israel (עתיד אחד - עתיד טוב לישראל, "One Future") is a small registered party that, in the 2026 election, serves as the formal vehicle for a single candidate: Minister Orit Strock. In the Central Elections Committee (CEC) filing for the 26th Knesset, the joint list "Religious Zionism led by Bezalel Smotrich and Zehut led by Moshe Feiglin" was submitted by three registered parties: National Union–Tkuma, Zehut – Israeli Jewish Movement, and Atid Echad. Of the 34 candidates on the list, only one, Orit Strock at No. 3, is filed "on behalf of" Atid Echad; every other candidate is filed on behalf of National Union–Tkuma or Zehut [1]. The list runs under the ballot letter **ט** [2].

Atid Echad has no separate public platform, website, campaign or spokesperson that this research could find. In practice, it is a registered party structure used by the Religious Zionism camp, and in 2022 and 2026 by Strock personally, while she campaigns and serves as a senior member of the Religious Zionism party. In July 2026 Strock competed in the Religious Zionism party's own primaries and won first place in them, behind party chairman Smotrich, who heads the list [16, 17]. Readers should therefore treat the positions in this document as **Strock's positions and record**, which are, in practice, the positions of the Religious Zionism party she belongs to. Where evidence comes from the wider Religious Zionism party or its leader Smotrich (whose registered party is National Union–Tkuma), this document says so.

**History of the registered party.**
- **2006: founded as an Ethiopian-Israeli party.** Atid Echad was founded ahead of the March 2006 election and led by Avraham Neguise, an activist for Ethiopian Jews. It fielded 10 candidates, five of them Ethiopian immigrants [5]. It won about 0.4% of the vote and no seats [6]. Neguise later ran on the Jewish Home list in 2009 and on the Likud slate in 2015 [6, 7].
- **2015: renamed list on the same registration.** In the 2015 election, a list called "Defending Our Children – No More Feeding Them Pornography" ran on the registration that, according to The Jerusalem Post, "first ran for the Knesset in 2006" under Neguise. It was headed by Yehezkel Shtelzer, who led an anti-pornography organization [7].
- **2021: "shelf party" for a Religious Zionism MK on the Likud list.** Before the March 2021 election, Ofir Sofer of the National Union (the Religious Zionism camp) was placed 28th on the Likud list, formally as the representative of Atid Echad, which was described in the press as a "shelf party" (a registered but inactive party revived for technical use). The arrangement was part of a deal between Netanyahu and Smotrich linked to the Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit joint run, and the separate party registration let Sofer split from Likud after the election without needing a third of the faction [8, 9, 10]. Knesset faction records show that on 27 June 2021 Sofer left the Likud faction, formed a one-member "Atid Echad" faction, and on the same day joined the Religious Zionism faction [11]. He had filed the request on 14 June 2021, the day after the Bennett–Lapid government was sworn in [10].
- **2022: Strock registered under Atid Echad.** In September 2022 the Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit joint list was submitted by five registered parties, including Atid Echad; Strock, at No. 4, was the only candidate filed on its behalf [3]. Asked about it on Ynet radio on 20 September 2022, Strock said: "This option is in our hands and it allows all kinds of flexibilities. I won't detail them." Asked whether it was a technical structure used for party funding, she said that many parties had used such structures and that "you don't part so quickly" with options you have [4]. Under Israel's Party Financing Law, when two or more parties submit a joint list and sit as one Knesset faction, they are treated as separate factions for the purposes of current (non-election) funding, loans and donations, and funding is paid according to each MK's party affiliation as filed with the list [12]. This research did not find an official figure for any funding paid to Atid Echad.
- **2026: Strock again filed under Atid Echad** (No. 3) on the joint list with Zehut [1].

**Who registered and controls the party now.** This research could not open the Registrar of Parties' record for Atid Echad (government sites on gov.il were blocked from the research environment), so the identity of its current registered officers, and the date the Religious Zionism camp took control of the registration, could not be verified. The sources show only that it was used by the National Union/Religious Zionism camp from 2021 [8–10] and by Strock in 2022 and 2026 [1, 3, 4].

**Relation to the Religious Zionism party.** Strock has been an MK for the Religious Zionism party since 2021 and is a minister on its behalf [11, 18]. In the 25th Knesset she sits in the faction "Religious Zionism led by Bezalel Smotrich", not in a separate Atid Echad faction [11]. When she spoke about Atid Echad in 2022, she described it as an "option" held by her camp rather than a separate political movement [4].

**Ideology and base of support.** Because Atid Echad has no platform of its own, its ideology is Strock's: religious Zionism, settlement throughout the Land of Israel including Judea and Samaria, opposition to a Palestinian state and to the Palestinian Authority, and a stronger Jewish religious character for the state [23, 33]. Strock, a resident of the Jewish community in Hebron [22], is identified with the settlement movement, and her base is the religious-Zionist and settler public that the Religious Zionism party courts [23, 35].

**Current seats.** Atid Echad has one MK, Strock, who sits within the seven-seat Religious Zionism faction [11, 14]. In the 2022 election, the Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit joint list won 14 seats, seven of which went to the Religious Zionism party [15].

**Joint list, partners and ballot letters.** In 2026 Atid Echad runs on the list "Religious Zionism led by Bezalel Smotrich and Zehut led by Moshe Feiglin", ballot letter **ט** [1, 2]. Its partners are:
- **National Union–Tkuma**, the registered party of the Religious Zionism party led by Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich [1];
- **Zehut – Israeli Jewish Movement**, led by former MK Moshe Feiglin [1].

Smotrich and Feiglin agreed on the joint run on 1 September 2026. Ynet reported that both sides described the alliance as strictly "technical": each party runs its own campaign and they are expected to **split after the election** and negotiate on coalitions separately. Feiglin received the No. 2 slot and places 8, 10 and 11 [13].

**History of splitting after elections.** The camp Atid Echad belongs to has repeatedly run on technical joint lists that split after the vote:
- In 2021 Strock described the Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit–Noam run as a "technical bloc" and said "after the elections we can decide to separate if necessary" [21]. Knesset records show Otzma Yehudit's MK Itamar Ben Gvir left the Religious Zionism faction on 3 July 2022 [11].
- After the November 2022 election, the 14-seat joint faction split on 20 November 2022 into three factions: Religious Zionism (with Strock), Otzma Yehudit and Noam [11].
- In 2021 Atid Echad itself was used to let an MK leave Likud after the election and join Religious Zionism [8–11].
- For 2026, the partners have said in advance that the Zehut alliance is technical and that they expect to split after the election [13].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Orit Strock (No. 3 on the joint list; the only Atid Echad candidate).**
- **Background.** Strock (full name Orit Malka Strock) [11] lives in the Jewish community of Hebron. She was among those who left Yamit in Sinai when it was evacuated in 1982 [22]. Before entering the Knesset she worked for many years in settlement advocacy; in a 2021 interview she described herself as having recently served as director of the Knesset's Land of Israel Caucus [21].
- **19th Knesset (2013–2015), Jewish Home.** Knesset records list her as an MK from 5 February 2013 to 31 March 2015 in the faction "The Jewish Home, founded by the National Union and the New NRP", and as a member of the Constitution, Law and Justice Committee and the State Control Committee, and an alternate on the Finance Committee [11]. In that Knesset she blocked a conversion bill in the Constitution Committee in March 2014 (see Religion and state) [55].
- **24th Knesset (2021–2022), Religious Zionism.** She was No. 5 on the Religious Zionism list in 2021 [21] and served as an MK from 6 April 2021; Knesset records list her as chair of the Religious Zionism faction in that Knesset [11].
- **25th Knesset (2022–2026), Religious Zionism.** She was No. 4 on the 2022 joint list, filed on behalf of Atid Echad [3], and has been an MK since 15 November 2022 [11].
- **Minister.** Since 29 December 2022 she has been Minister of Settlement and National Missions in the 37th government [11, 18]. The ministry was created for her under the coalition agreement signed between Likud and the Religious Zionism party on 1 December 2022 [20]. In February 2023 the cabinet made it an independent ministry with three branches: settlement; Jewish identity and society; and pre-military academies (mechinot) and national service [19]. She also takes part in the security cabinet's discussions, where press reports quote her in 2026 [63, 64].
- **2026.** In the Religious Zionism primaries on 26 July 2026 Strock won first place among the contested slots [16]. She was then filed at No. 3 on the joint list, on behalf of Atid Echad [1].

**The rest of the top of the list** (all from the partner parties, not from Atid Echad) [1]:
1. **Bezalel Smotrich** (National Union–Tkuma), Finance Minister and chairman of the Religious Zionism party [1, 14].
2. **Moshe Feiglin** (Zehut), former MK and leader of Zehut [1, 13].
3. **Orit Strock** (Atid Echad) [1].
4. **Simcha Rothman** (National Union–Tkuma), chair of the Knesset Constitution, Law and Justice Committee, who led the 2023–2026 bills on the judicial system [1, 46, 47].
5. **Tzvi Mor** (National Union–Tkuma), father of a hostage released from Gaza, given a reserved slot by Smotrich [1, 17].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

**Summary.** Neither Atid Echad nor Strock has a published cost-of-living programme, and this research found no statement by Strock specifically on food prices or how to lower them. The evidence is limited to votes on budgets and economic laws, some from her time in the Jewish Home.

- **Action (Jewish Home period, 2013–2014):** Knesset vote records show Strock voted for the 2014 Law for the Promotion of Competition in the Food Sector, which passed its final reading on 19 March 2014 with 37 votes for and none against (counted from the raw per-MK rows) [48, 49]. She also voted for the 2013–2014 Economic Arrangements Law (the law that carries out the budget's policy changes), passed on 29 July 2013 (56 for, 41 against in the raw rows) [48, 49]. These votes were cast as a Jewish Home MK, not for Atid Echad.
- **Action (25th Knesset):** As a minister in the coalition, Strock is part of the government that passed the 2025 state budget, approved by the Knesset on 25 March 2025. Kipa reported that the budget, about NIS 620 billion, increased defence spending and funds for ministries controlled by coalition parties while cutting several civilian areas [57]. Her individual vote could not be checked because the Knesset's vote records were not reachable from the research environment.
- **Partner context (not Atid Echad):** The Jerusalem Post reports that Smotrich, as Finance Minister, pushed a dairy-market reform to lower milk and cheese prices by addressing monopolies, which did not pass because of opposition within the coalition, and that he has called for more free-market competition [15]. This is Smotrich's (National Union–Tkuma) position, not a documented Strock or Atid Echad position.

**Closest option(s):** The only documented action points toward more competition (`open-market-competition-imports`), but it is a single 2014 vote from her Jewish Home period and is weak evidence of a current position.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

**No documented position found.** This research searched in Hebrew and English for statements by Strock or Atid Echad on crime and murders in Arab communities, the Shin Bet's role, and the July 2026 plan, and found none.

- **Context (collective government action, individual vote not found):** On 16 July 2026 the government approved a plan led by Prime Minister Netanyahu, Minister May Golan and Minister Itamar Ben Gvir to bring the Shin Bet (Israel's internal security service) into the fight against crime organizations in Arab society. About NIS 497 million was moved from the five-year development plan for Arab communities (known as plan 550): about NIS 364.5 million to a dedicated Shin Bet unit against weapons smuggling and trafficking, and about NIS 132.4 million to a new national police unit [56]. Strock is a member of that government, but the report does not say how individual ministers voted, so this cannot be attributed to her as a personal position.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Summary.** Strock has consistently opposed deals that would end or pause the war before Hamas is destroyed, voted against the October 2025 deal based on the US plan, and has called for Israel to return to Gaza, including with Israeli settlement. She has also said she sees benefits in the current arrangement because the army holds more than half of Gaza.

- **Action:** Strock says she voted against stopping the war in Gaza and against the Trump plan in October 2025, and in May 2026 said she did not regret it: "Not at all. I see that every day 600 trucks enter Gaza when there is no need for that amount. And I see what the mass of released terrorists creates" [23]. The Jerusalem Post reported on 10 October 2025 that Smotrich also voted against the agreement in the government [26].
- **Action:** In January 2025 the government approved the hostage-and-ceasefire deal by 24 votes to 8; the ministers who voted against included the Religious Zionism ministers [25]. Strock was one of the party's ministers at the time [18]; her individual vote is reported only as part of that group.
- **Action:** At the cabinet meeting on 16 November 2025, during the ceasefire, Strock asked Netanyahu "How long will the ceasefire last?" and said: "We did not go to war in order to return hostages" [27].
- **Statement:** On 1 May 2024, Strock told Army Radio that a hostage deal then under discussion would "throw [the war] into the trash to save 22 or 33" hostages, and that a government that did so "has absolutely no right to exist". She said the war's written goals were to destroy Hamas's military, governmental and economic capabilities, create conditions to free the hostages, and remove the long-term threat from Gaza, and that her party had supported the first hostage deal in November 2023 and did not consider it a mistake [24].
- **Statement:** In May 2026 she said the current situation had positive elements: "We managed to return all the hostages while we control more than half of the area, are destroying the terror infrastructure there, and have international legitimacy... There is international legitimacy for disarming Hamas and removing it" [23].
- **Statement (settlement in Gaza):** On 22 March 2023, after the Knesset repealed parts of the 2005 Disengagement Law in northern Samaria, Strock said that "the sin of the disengagement will be reversed" and that a return to Gaza, which is "part of the Land of Israel", will involve many casualties [32]. Asked in May 2026 whether she sees Israeli settlement in Gaza in the coming years, she answered: "What we did in the last 3.5 years in Judea and Samaria was also considered unrealistic until we came into office. And here it happened" [23].

**Closest option(s):** Return to war and full control of Gaza with Israeli communities rebuilt there (`resume-fighting-full-control`). Her May 2026 comments, which accept the current situation in which the army holds more than half of Gaza while Hamas is disarmed, also partly match `hold-positions-disarmament-first`. Her stated long-term aim of settlement in Gaza goes beyond that option.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Summary.** Strock says Torah scholars should also serve and that Haredi students' study is not a substitute for military service. However, she has also said that Haredim cannot be enlisted through "beatings, arrests and sanctions" and has argued for a route through agreement. Her party backed the 2025–2026 coalition draft bill while asking for changes.

- **Action (Jewish Home period, 2014):** Strock voted for the Security Service Law (Amendment No. 19), the 2014 Haredi enlistment law, at its final reading on 12 March 2014. The raw per-MK records show 65 votes for and 1 against (the Knesset's summary header lists 63–1) [48, 49]. JTA reported that the law, prepared by a committee chaired by Ayelet Shaked, removed most exemptions for yeshiva students, set rising yearly enlistment targets for a transitional period, and provided for criminal sanctions against those who did not enlist, starting in 2017; the opposition boycotted the vote [50]. She also voted for the National Civilian Service Law passed the same day [48, 49]. This vote was cast as a Jewish Home MK.
- **Statement (May 2026):** "You won't hear me say that Torah learners don't need to enlist. With us, Torah learners enlist... You won't hear me say that Torah learners contribute to winning the war like soldiers. To me that is an insult to the Torah." She added: "But as a member of the government and cabinet I must give a realistic answer to this difficulty... I don't believe it is possible to enlist the Haredi public with beatings, arrests and sanctions", because, she said, such measures would not have made her own religious public act against its beliefs either. She said the way forward was the principle she had heard from Haredi MKs, that "whoever does not study Torah must enlist", while stressing that she believes Torah learners should also serve, and said the army must organize so that Haredi parents do not fear their sons will become secular, including on mixed-gender service [23].
- **Formal commitment (Religious Zionism faction, not Atid Echad alone):** On 27 November 2025, responding to the draft bill prepared by MK Boaz Bismuth, the Religious Zionism faction said: "Our principle was and remains: we will vote only for a law that will bring real and rapid enlistment of Haredim into the IDF" [51]. On 10 December 2025 Walla reported that the faction's MKs met with religious-Zionist rabbis and agreed that the bill was not good and needed changes, but that the government should not be brought down and the alliance with Likud and the Haredi parties should not be harmed [52].
- **Change over time:** In 2014 Strock voted for a law that included criminal sanctions (from 2017) [48–50]. In May 2026 she said she does not believe Haredim can be enlisted through arrests and sanctions [23]. She explained the 2026 view as a matter of realism about what works, not a change in her view that Torah learners should serve [23].

**Closest option(s):** Gradual enlistment through agreement with Haredi leadership (`gradual-targets-by-agreement`), based on her 2026 statements and her party's support for the coalition draft bill. Her 2014 vote for a law with criminal sanctions was closer to `universal-service-with-sanctions`.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

**Summary.** Strock's housing record is about settlement and rural development: bringing young families to the Negev, the Galilee and Judea and Samaria through land discounts and new communities. She has not set out a national plan for housing prices.

- **Action:** In 2023 Strock led a team of ministry directors-general on land prices in "national priority areas". On 13 September 2023 the Israel Land Authority council discussed the plan she and the Prime Minister's Office director-general had prepared. It included an extra 10% discount for reservists, raising the discount ceiling for people without a home to NIS 850,000 and linking it to the index, and removing the discount ceiling for communities with a high socio-economic ranking. The stated reason was the shortage of young couples moving to the Negev and the Galilee [58].
- **Action:** On 5 September 2024 Ynet reported that Strock wrote to Netanyahu accusing the Israel Land Authority director, Yanki Quint, of turning government decisions on land discounts for rural communities in the Galilee and the Negev into "a dead letter", and of blocking land marketing in rural communities. She also accused him of blocking the 2023 admissions-committees law, which lets communities of up to 700 families screen new residents [59].
- **Action:** The ministry's Settlement Division, under Strock, advanced the new community of Shibolet in the Lower Galilee, approved in July 2024: a rural community of 350 homes, 20% of them for people with special needs [60].
- **Action (Judea and Samaria):** Strock's ministry is a central body for building and funding settlements, including the government plan of nearly NIS 1 billion reported in August 2023, which included converting five IDF bases in Judea and Samaria into neighbourhoods and industrial zones [39] (see Judea and Samaria).

**Closest option(s):** Develop the periphery and bring families to outlying areas (`develop-periphery-and-transport`), together with discounted land for young families and reservists (`direct-help-for-buyers`). Her focus is rural and settlement areas, not the urban renewal or transport parts of the first option.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Summary.** The available evidence is about Lebanon. Strock has opposed ceasefire arrangements with Hezbollah that she believes cannot be enforced, called for harsh responses to attacks, and called for changing the border. This research found no detailed statement by her on Iran.

- **Action/Statement (security cabinet, June 2026):** At a cabinet discussion on 4 June 2026 of a proposed arrangement with Lebanon, whose precondition was that all Hezbollah operatives south of the Litani River move north, Strock said this was "like the sun rising in the west and setting in the east" and that "there will be no agreement, Hezbollah will not move beyond the Litani". She said the norm that "when Israel is attacked, Israel attacks in Beirut" must be carried out on the ground and not only on paper, warned that otherwise Hezbollah would say "a barking dog doesn't bite", and called to "change the border – we need to go in that direction" [63, 64].
- **Context:** In November 2024, the security cabinet approved the US-proposed ceasefire arrangement in Lebanon by 10 votes to 1 [65]; this research did not find whether Strock took part in that vote.

**Closest option(s):** Keep up military pressure on Hezbollah and stay in southern Lebanon without deals that cannot be enforced (`military-pressure-until-threats-removed`). The evidence is limited to Lebanon and to statements in cabinet meetings, not votes.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Summary.** This is Strock's central issue and the area where she has the most actions as minister. She supports applying Israeli sovereignty to Judea and Samaria, rejects a Palestinian state, says the Palestinian Authority should be dismantled, and as minister has funded and promoted new settlements and farms.

- **Action (ministry):** The Associated Press reported in July 2024 that Strock's ministry confirmed it had budgeted NIS 75 million in December 2023 for security equipment for "young settlements", its term for farms and outposts that the government had not authorized. Her office said the funds were coordinated with the Defense Ministry and "carried out in accordance with all laws" and that she "sees great importance in strengthening settlements" [37]. In March 2026 the Times of Israel reported a further NIS 50 million from coalition funds to the ministry for security equipment and access roads for such outposts, out of NIS 395.5 million in coalition funds for the ministry, in addition to about NIS 1.3 billion in its regular budget [38].
- **Action (government plan):** In August 2023 Calcalist, via Ynetnews, reported a government plan led by Strock of nearly NIS 1 billion (NIS 672 million confirmed at the time) for settlements in Area C (the part of Judea and Samaria under full Israeli control), covering community support, education, security, a medical centre and the conversion of five IDF bases. Parts of it came from the coalition agreement with the Religious Zionism party [39].
- **Action (government decision):** On 29 May 2025 the government approved 22 new settlements in Judea and Samaria, a joint initiative of Defense Minister Israel Katz and Smotrich, including renewed settlement in Homesh and Sa-Nur in northern Samaria. Strock wrote: "We made a promise, and we kept it. New settlements are being established in Judea and Samaria, closing a gap of decades in the settlement of the land" [36].
- **Action (2021, Religious Zionism period):** On 10 May 2021 Strock voted at the preliminary stage for a bill to repeal the evacuation clauses of the Disengagement Law for northern Samaria (42 for, 29 against in the raw rows), and for a bill to regularize settlements and neighbourhoods "in regularization processes" (45 for, 30 against in the raw rows) [48, 49].
- **Statement (sovereignty):** In a July 2024 article, Strock welcomed the Knesset's vote opposing a Palestinian state west of the Jordan River and wrote that Israel must now take "the further step: applying our own sovereignty" to Judea and Samaria. She wrote that "there is no such thing as an occupying people in its own land, and there is also no such thing as a Palestinian people", and called for settlement "in all parts of our land" and "in the end, with God's help, also applying sovereignty" [33]. In July 2025 the Knesset passed a declaratory motion calling on the government to apply sovereignty, with 71 MKs in favour [34].
- **Statement (outcome so far):** In May 2026 Strock said: "We did not apply sovereignty. That is a point where we did not succeed. It is a veto Trump placed and we did not break it." She described the farms and new settlements as "a correction of Oslo" in control of open areas, and said: "The goal was to turn back the wheel of the withdrawals of Oslo and the disengagement. We believe... that Oslo is the great disaster of the State of Israel, and we are working to fix it" [23].
- **Statement (Palestinian Authority):** "It is a hostile entity and it should not be here. It needs to be dismantled." She proposed returning to local municipal rule in Palestinian cities and villages, and said she raises changing policy toward the Palestinian Authority in almost every cabinet discussion [23].
- **Statement (Hebron and Hebron Hills):** On 7 July 2024 Army Radio broadcast a recording of Strock telling residents in the Hebron Hills: "For me it is like a time of miracle... We want to accomplish as much as possible", calling settlement there "a holy mission" [35].
- **Statement (settler violence):** In June 2023, after the IDF chief, the Shin Bet head and the police commissioner described attacks by Israelis on Palestinian villages as "nationalist terrorism", Strock said: "Who do you think you are? The Wagner Group?" and that it was "a shame and disgrace to call these events nationalist terrorism" [40].

**Closest option(s):** Full sovereignty over Judea and Samaria, expanding settlements and dismantling the Palestinian Authority (`full-sovereignty`).

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Summary.** Strock supports giving elected officials more power over the courts and the attorney general. She is a member of the government that passed and carried out these changes.

- **Statement:** At a rally on 12 March 2023 she said: "We know that the vote in the elections was over the reform, we have no intention of retreating from it... We are abolishing the dictatorship of the High Court of Justice and enabling democracy to emerge" [41].
- **Action (2021, Religious Zionism period):** On 10 May 2021 Strock voted at the preliminary stage for bills to add an "override clause" (letting the Knesset re-enact laws the Supreme Court strikes down): the proposed Basic Law: The Override, and an amendment to Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. The raw per-MK rows show 41–30 and 40–30 respectively [48, 49].
- **Action (government):** On 4 August 2025 the cabinet, of which Strock is a member, voted unanimously to dismiss Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara [44]. The Supreme Court froze the dismissal and later issued conditional orders against it [44, 45].
- **Action (coalition legislation, individual vote not checked):** The coalition passed the law cancelling the "reasonableness" ground for judicial review on 24 July 2023 by 64–0, with the opposition boycotting [42]; the Judicial Selection Law, which gives politicians more influence over appointing judges from the next Knesset, on 27 March 2025, also with an opposition boycott [43]; and on 15 July 2026 a law making the attorney general's legal opinions advisory to the government, which takes effect on 1 January 2027 [46, 47]. All three were advanced by coalition partners including Religious Zionism's Simcha Rothman [42, 47]. Strock's individual votes on these could not be checked because the Knesset vote records were not reachable.

**Closest option(s):** Give elected officials more power over the courts and end the binding force of the attorney general's opinions (`continue-judicial-changes`).

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Summary.** Strock has said she supports an investigation but is part of the government that decided not to set up a state commission of inquiry. Her comments suggest distrust of a commission formed under the existing law.

- **Action (government decision):** On 16 November 2025 the government decided not to set up a state commission of inquiry and instead to establish an "independent" commission whose composition would "reflect broad public agreement as far as possible" [28]. During that meeting Strock said: "There will be a campaign. Take that into account. Whoever managed to dull public opinion with the hostages will succeed with this too" [27]. When the cabinet secretary told her there would be a commission in the end, she replied: "I am in favour of an investigation" [29].
- **Action (coalition bill, individual vote not checked):** On 6 July 2026 the Knesset passed at first reading a coalition-backed bill for a commission whose six members would be chosen by a two-thirds Knesset majority or, failing that, three by the coalition and three by the opposition, with former hostages or bereaved families as observers. It passed 59–0 with the opposition boycotting [31]. The government told the Supreme Court it considered itself authorized to decide whether, when and with what mandate to set up an inquiry [30].

**Closest option(s):** A commission chosen through the Knesset (`commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`), based on the government decision and coalition bill she is part of. Her own statements are limited.

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

**Summary.** Strock supports a strong role for Orthodox Judaism and the Chief Rabbinate in public life, opposes recognition of alternatives in conversion, and has defended religious exemptions for service providers. She says she opposes coercion in private life but wants the state's public sphere and official procedures to follow Jewish law.

- **Statement (2022):** "We are not in favour of coercion, but we think and demand that the public sphere be run as a Jewish state. The official processes of the state should be according to the law of Moses and Israel" [4].
- **Action (Jewish Home period, 2014):** As a Constitution Committee member in March 2014, Strock filed dozens of objections that stopped a conversion bill by MK Elazar Stern, which would have let any city rabbi convert without the Chief Rabbinate's involvement. She backed instead a Jewish Home bill that kept one Chief Rabbinate member on each conversion court. She said: "The aim is not to strengthen Rabbi Yosef or Shas but to prevent harm to the status of the Chief Rabbinate", and "we are not willing to let them sell a fake product to people coming to join the Jewish people" [55].
- **Statement and coalition commitment (2022):** In December 2022 Strock said a doctor could refuse treatment that violates his religious beliefs "as long as there are enough other doctors who can give this service", in the context of an amendment to the anti-discrimination law her party was drafting and that coalition agreements provided for. After criticism she said she meant certain medical procedures, not LGBT patients; Netanyahu called her remarks "unacceptable" [54].
- **Action (ministry):** Her ministry took over the Education Ministry's Jewish Culture branch and the Religious Services Ministry's Directorate of Jewish Identity [19], and added Judaism, Zionism and heritage enrichment to national service [62].
- **Coalition context (individual vote not checked):** On 13 July 2026 the Knesset passed Basic Law: Torah Study by 63–52 [53]. This research did not find how Strock voted.

**Closest option(s):** Strengthen the Jewish character of the state and keep marriage, divorce and conversion only through the Orthodox Rabbinate (`strengthen-jewish-character`). Her statement that she opposes coercion in private life suggests she would not enforce every point of that option by law.

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Palestinian state and the Oslo Accords {#other-palestinian-state-oslo}

- **Statement:** Strock describes the Oslo Accords as "the great disaster of the State of Israel" and says her political goal is to reverse the withdrawals of Oslo and the 2005 disengagement [23].
- **Statement:** In July 2024 she wrote that promoting the idea of a Palestinian state "is a prize for terror" and that Israel must "prevent its establishment in every way" [33].

### National service and pre-military academies {#other-national-service}

- **Action:** As the minister responsible for civilian national service, Strock launched a reform in January 2025: a single national selection system in place of eight separate service organizations, professional training, new support programmes and optional evening study in Judaism, Zionism and heritage [62]. Kipa reported that the service passed 20,000 volunteers that year, including 5,782 from Arab and Druze communities and 1,535 Haredim [62].
- **Action:** Her ministry also oversees pre-military academies (mechinot) [19]. In May 2026 she said she agreed with the IDF chief of staff on setting up more academies along the eastern border corridor [23].

### Rural settlement in the Negev and Galilee {#other-rural-settlement}

- **Action:** Strock's ministry promotes new communities and land discounts in the Galilee and the Negev (see Housing) [58–60]. She said in July 2024 that settlement "in all its areas, and above all in the strategic areas" continues despite the war [60], and that alongside Judea and Samaria she is promoting communities in national priority areas, especially the evacuated areas in the south and north [35].

### Government support for settlers and relations with the United States {#other-settlers-us}

- **Statement:** In February 2023 Strock told settlers in outposts that "you went out on our mission to establish all the communities" and that governments must support them; "even the Americans can understand such a thing, although we don't need their understanding" [61].
- **Statement:** In May 2026 she said most of the major changes in Judea and Samaria, including the first new settlements and the farms, were made while Joe Biden was president, and that there had been no dramatic change under Trump [23].
- **Statement:** Asked in May 2026 about reports that the International Criminal Court might seek an arrest warrant against her, she said that she sees it as "a certificate of honour" because it would be over settlement, and that she does not travel abroad [23].

### Women in combat units and army framework for religious soldiers {#other-mixed-service}

- **Statement:** In May 2026 Strock said that she hears rabbis in her community, "even moderate ones", protesting mixed-gender service in the Armored Corps, that the army should take account of the whole spectrum of the public, and that she is not afraid to disagree with the chief of staff on this [23].

### LGBT rights and religious exemptions {#other-lgbt-religious-exemptions}

- **Statement:** See Religion and state: in December 2022 she defended letting service providers, including doctors, refuse services that violate their religious beliefs if others can provide them, then clarified she meant certain procedures rather than LGBT people [54].

### Admissions committees in small communities {#other-admissions-committees}

- **Statement/Action:** Strock supports the 2023 law that expanded admissions committees (which screen new residents) to communities of up to 700 families, and in September 2024 accused the Israel Land Authority of obstructing it [59].

### Gaza-border and northern communities {#other-border-communities}

- **Statement:** Strock says her ministry gives priority to communities in national priority areas, "with an emphasis on the evacuated areas in the south and the north" [35]. Under her ministry, national-service volunteers worked with evacuees from the Gaza-border and northern communities and helped prepare for their return [62].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Atid Echad itself** has made no separate statement about coalitions. Statements below are Strock's and her list partners'.

- **Strock, 21 February 2021:** She called on Naftali Bennett to commit not to join a government that is not right-wing: "The question is not whether the government is headed by Netanyahu or not, but whether the government includes the left or not." She said Religious Zionism was "not in Netanyahu's pocket": its agreement was to be with him if he formed a government that dealt with "Jewish identity and the Land of Israel" [21].
- **Religious Zionism, 23 February 2021:** The party refused to sign a loyalty pledge to Netanyahu, saying: "Our only loyalty is to [our] path and values, and we only be part of a government that expresses them" [66].
- **Smotrich, August 2026:** He said he remains Netanyahu's partner and that he had stayed in the government throughout the term while drawing "red lines" [14]. The Jerusalem Post reported that he has ruled out relying on Arab parties to form a government, and has said that a government with Ra'am would be worse than the October 7 attack [15].
- **Zehut:** Under the 1 September 2026 agreement, Zehut will conduct coalition negotiations separately from Religious Zionism after the election [13].

**Track record of keeping such pledges.**
- In 2021 Religious Zionism, including Strock, said it would not join a government that included the left or relied on Ra'am. It did not join the Bennett–Lapid government and went into opposition [10, 21].
- In the 2022–2026 term, Strock repeatedly warned against deals with Hamas. In May 2024 she said a government that accepted such a deal "has absolutely no right to exist" [24]. When the government approved deals in January 2025 and October 2025, the Religious Zionism ministers, including Strock, voted against but **stayed in the government** [23, 25, 26]. Smotrich has described staying as acting "responsibly" while drawing red lines [14].

This document therefore relies on her record, and notes where evidence comes from her Jewish Home period (2013–2015) or from her Religious Zionism partners rather than from Atid Echad.

Gaps: the Knesset's own website and vote database, and gov.il, were not reachable from the research environment. Votes up to 2021 were counted from the Open Knesset mirror of Knesset data. For the 25th Knesset (2022–2026) Strock's individual votes could not be checked; press reports of collective votes were used and are labelled as such. No position was found on crime in Arab society, and evidence on the cost of living and on Iran is thin.
