## Overview {#overview}

Shas is a Sephardi Haredi party. It was founded in 1984, and it grew out of complaints that Sephardi Jews faced discrimination inside the established Ashkenazi Haredi political leadership [2]. Its founder and long-time spiritual leader was Rabbi Ovadia Yosef. The list's official name still describes it as "the movement of Maran Rabbi Ovadia Yosef" [1]. A Council of Torah Sages (a body of senior rabbis) approves the party's candidate list and gives binding instructions on major decisions [4, 7].

**Ideology and base.** In September 2026 the party's Council of Torah Sages described its representatives' work as serving "the world of Torah and the yeshivas", strengthening religious affairs and helping the public [4]. Shas combines a religious agenda with an economic agenda aimed at lower-income families [2, 38]. It sits on the right of the political map and has been a long-time partner of Benjamin Netanyahu's governments [2]. In an August 2026 campaign video, the party said that more than 30,000 conscripts and career soldiers in the IDF (Israel Defense Forces) voted for Shas in the last election. The Times of Israel called the figure unsubstantiated, because it is based on "double envelope" ballots, which soldiers use but so do other groups, such as hospital patients and diplomats [73].

**Seats.** Shas won 11 seats in the 2022 election and holds 11 seats in the outgoing (25th) Knesset [2, 34].

**Status in the outgoing government.** Shas was a coalition partner in Netanyahu's government from December 2022 [31]. On July 17, 2025, after a decision by its Council of Torah Sages the day before, its six ministers and one deputy minister handed in their resignations because a law setting the status of yeshiva students had not passed. The council told its MKs to stay in the coalition for the time being [87]. Interior Minister Moshe Arbel briefly withdrew his resignation to finish urgent tasks [87], and on July 29, 2025 the government handed the party's ministries temporarily to Likud ministers [8]. In October 2025 Shas also gave up its Knesset committee posts, saying it would return "when the status of yeshiva students is resolved" [7]. Party leader Aryeh Deri, who was not a minister, kept attending security cabinet meetings (the small group of ministers that decides on security matters) [87, 13]. In May 2026, according to the Haredi news site Kikar HaShabbat, Deri told his MKs that "the coalition failed to deliver our most basic and crucial law" and that the Knesset should be dissolved and early elections held [12].

**List and ballot letters.** Shas runs alone (not on a joint list) under the list name "התאחדות הספרדים שומרי תורה תנועתו של מרן הרב עובדיה יוסף זצ"ל", submitted by the party Shas – World Union of Sephardic Torah Guardians [1]. Its ballot letters are **שס**. The Central Elections Committee (CEC) published the list on September 8, 2026 [1].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Party leadership.** Aryeh Deri chairs the party [2]. Former Sephardi Chief Rabbi Yitzhak Yosef, a son of the founder, is its spiritual leader [2]. In August 2026, after a public rift, a letter from Rabbi Moshe Maya, a senior member of the Council of Torah Sages, called for Yosef to take over "implementing the legacy" of his father, and Kan reported that Yosef had been selected to lead the party with Deri's approval. Yosef and Deri agreed to meet every week on party matters [3, 28]. The Council of Torah Sages approved the 2026 list and overruled Deri on at least one placement, keeping MK Yoav Ben-Tzur on the list [5].

**Top of the list** (CEC, September 8, 2026) [1]:

1. **Aryeh Deri.** Deri was Interior Minister from December 1988 to September 1993, and Minister of the Economy and Minister for the Development of the Periphery, the Negev and the Galilee from 2015. He was again Interior Minister from 2016 to 2021 and briefly Religious Services Minister in 2018–2019 [78]. In December 2022 he became Deputy Prime Minister, Interior Minister and Health Minister, but he left those posts on January 24, 2023, after the Supreme Court ruled his appointment unreasonable (see Legal) [74, 78]. He is not a minister now, but he has taken part in security cabinet discussions [13, 92]. In July 2026 he described this as a small, informal forum that does not vote, and said he had barely attended in recent months [30].
2. **Yinon Azoulay.** MK since March 2018 [81]. He chairs the Shas faction in the Knesset [44]. He moved up from seventh to second place on the list [5].
3. **Michael Malkieli.** Religious Services Minister from December 2022 until July 2025. He was also acting Interior Minister from January to April 2023 [82].
4. **Yoav Ben-Tzur.** Deputy Interior Minister in 2020–2021, Labor Minister from January 2023 to July 2025, and acting Health Minister in early 2023 [79]. Deri reportedly tried to drop him from the list, but the Council of Torah Sages kept him [5].
5. **Haim Biton.** Minister in the Education Ministry from December 2022 to July 2025 [80]. In 2022 he was the party's CEO [38].
6. **Dror Amos.** A new candidate. He is a reserve officer and chairman of the Haifa Religious Council. Media described his placement as part of an effort to show openness to IDF service [5]. A request to remove him from the list was rejected by the CEC chair (see Legal) [76].
7. **Moshe Abutbul**, 8. **Uriel Busso**, 9. **Yosef Taieb**, 10. **Yonatan Mishriki**, 11. **Yosef Elnatanov** (new candidate from the Bukharan Jewish community), 12. **Erez Malul**, 13. **Simon Moshiashvili** [1, 4, 5].

Long-serving MKs Yaakov Margi and Moshe Arbel are not running again [4].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

Shas's central demand in this term was a law that lets full-time yeshiva students keep studying instead of serving, without penalties.

- **Action:** In July 2025 Shas ministers left the government because a law setting the status of yeshiva students had not passed. In October 2025 the party gave up its Knesset committee posts, saying it would return when "the status of yeshiva students is resolved" [7, 87].
- **Action:** In November 2025 Netanyahu reportedly moved forward with MK Boaz Bismuth's enlistment bill after getting the go-ahead from Shas and Degel HaTorah. The bill reportedly reduced penalties, and Bismuth had said full-time yeshiva students should be allowed to keep studying while those not studying should be drafted [10]. Shas MKs repeatedly voiced support for the bill, which kept exemptions for full-time yeshiva students while, according to its supporters, increasing enlistment among other Haredi men [34]. In May 2026 Shas and United Torah Judaism rejected a later version of the bill. After Netanyahu told the Haredi parties there was no majority to pass it, Deri called for early elections [11, 12].
- **Action:** Shas backed a law, passed 58–54 on July 14, 2026, that banned arrests, investigations and other enforcement against yeshiva students who had not reported for service. Kikar HaShabbat described Deri as the law's initiator [30]. The law's text ran until November 30, 2026, but the Times of Israel reported that, for legal reasons tied to the election, it would in practice run into early 2027 (one report said late January, another February) [13, 14]. The Supreme Court (sitting as the High Court of Justice) froze it a day later [13, 14]. Deri said the IDF chief of staff, who opposed the law, was "trying to help the left-wing bloc" [13]. He also said the party should tell the court "we do not recognise your authority" and should not send lawyers to defend the law there [30]. In an October 2026 party bulletin he wrote that parents who send a son to yeshiva fear "that the day will come when he is arrested" [17].
- **Action:** Shas and Degel HaTorah pushed for **Basic Law: Torah Study** [2, 6]. A basic law is a law with constitutional status. The Knesset passed it 63–52 on July 13, 2026. It declares Torah study "a fundamental value" of the Jewish people and the State of Israel. A clause meant to balance Torah study against other values such as military service was removed before the final vote [6]. On June 1, 2026, Shas had told Netanyahu it would stop voting for government bills unless this law advanced [19]. Deri pressed for a vote in June 2026 even as Iranian missile attacks were under way, calling the law a declaration of the "contribution of Torah scholars to the people of Israel and its security" [21]. In July 2026 Rabbi Yitzhak Yosef told the party to back coalition bills in exchange for advancing Haredi-backed bills, writing that "under no circumstances should the passage of the Torah Study Law be put at risk" [22].
- **Action:** In August 2024 Labor Minister Ben-Tzur asked to delay by a year the cancellation of daycare subsidies for families where the father is a yeshiva student of military age [70].
- **Statement:** During a June 7, 2026 visit to a military prison holding yeshiva students, Deri said: "stop treating Torah learners as criminals" and called them "the glory of the Jewish people" [9]. In September 2026 he said he does "not feel obliged to urge Haredim to enlist until there are real tracks that will allow them to maintain their strictly observant lifestyle," and that "the army doesn't want Haredi soldiers" [15].
- **Statement:** In October 2026 Deri wrote that Shas would "not enter any government before the status of Torah students is settled and guaranteed" [17].
- **Statement:** Ynet reported in September 2026 that Shas now acknowledges that drafting young men who have left yeshiva studies cannot be avoided, and that MK Azoulay said the military would need to adapt itself [16].

**Closest option(s):** A law that lets full-time yeshiva students keep studying instead of serving, with no criminal or financial penalties (`anchor-torah-study-exemption`). The party has accepted that men who leave yeshiva may be drafted, under conditions suited to a Haredi lifestyle [16].

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

- **Action:** Shas pushed for Basic Law: Torah Study, passed in July 2026 [2, 6].
- **Action:** The "Rabbis Law", sponsored by Shas MK Erez Malul, passed 46–35 on January 15, 2025. It lets the Religious Services Minister, a post Shas then held, allocate state funds to religious councils to pay local rabbis and ritual-bath attendants. Critics said it increased the minister's influence over local religious services [68].
- **Formal commitment:** In its December 2022 coalition agreement with Likud, Shas secured a promise to reverse the 2021 kashrut reform. That reform had allowed kosher certification by bodies other than the Chief Rabbinate. Shas also sought to cancel a directive that had changed how city rabbis are appointed, and secured NIS 220 million for building and renovating synagogues, ritual baths and cemeteries [31]. In 2026 the Jerusalem Post reported that the Haredi parties had sponsored legislation, since passed, repealing the kashrut reform [2].
- **Statement:** In February 2026 Shas, United Torah Judaism and Otzma Yehudit said in a joint statement that they would vote for a bill giving the Chief Rabbinate full control over prayer at all parts of the Western Wall in its preliminary reading. The bill was a response to a Supreme Court ruling that the state must go ahead with upgrading the egalitarian prayer area [69].
- **Statement:** According to the Jerusalem Post, Shas and United Torah Judaism oppose civil marriage and other alternatives to religious law and want marriages to stay with the Rabbinate. They favour Orthodox control of conversion, have historically opposed expanding public transport on Shabbat, and oppose expanding non-Orthodox prayer at the Western Wall [2]. A Ynet review of party positions on Shabbat public transport in August 2026 approached only the non-Haredi parties, so it does not include Shas [85].

**Closest option(s):** Give Jewish religious law a bigger role, keeping marriage, divorce and conversion with the Orthodox Rabbinate and strengthening religious institutions (`strengthen-jewish-character`). Much of the record is about keeping existing Rabbinate powers, which overlaps with `keep-status-quo`.

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

- **Action:** All 64 coalition MKs, then including Shas's 11, voted for the July 2023 "reasonableness" law. It stopped courts from reviewing whether government and ministerial decisions are reasonable. The opposition boycotted the vote [58]. The Supreme Court later cancelled this law (see the issue description).
- **Action:** The law changing the committee that chooses judges passed 67–1 in March 2025, while Shas was part of the coalition. The opposition boycotted the vote. The report does not list votes by party [59]. The law takes effect when the next Knesset begins [59].
- **Action:** On March 20, 2023 the coalition passed in first reading, 63–55, a basic law that would stop the Supreme Court from reviewing the appointment of ministers. It followed the court's ruling against Deri's appointment and was known as "Deri Law 2" [61].
- **Statement:** In April 2023 Deri said the coalition could pass its judicial bills "whenever we want" if talks with the opposition failed [60]. In September 2026, after the court struck out a Shas petition, he said the decision "strengthens our determination to fundamentally fix the judicial system and return power to the people and their elected representatives" [57]. Ynet reported that Shas was not originally a leading force behind the changes to the judicial system, and that as enforcement against yeshiva students grew, the party began criticising the justice system, which it says is harming the Haredi public in order to bring down the coalition [57].
- **Statement:** In July 2026 Deri said he would not recognise the Supreme Court's authority over the law on arrests of yeshiva students [30].

**Closest option(s):** Give elected officials more say in choosing judges and limit judicial review (`continue-judicial-changes`).

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

The October 7 attack was carried out by Hamas, a terror organization, in 2023.

- **Action:** On October 22, 2025, Shas members of the Knesset's State Control Committee voted with Likud and United Torah Judaism to reject a proposal to establish a state commission of inquiry into the October 7 attack [65].
- **Action:** On July 6, 2026 a coalition bill for an inquiry commission whose members would be picked through the Knesset passed its first reading, 59–0, with the votes of coalition MKs while the opposition boycotted the vote. Ynet reported that the bill was advanced "as part of the deal between Netanyahu and the Haredi parties" [66]. In June 2026 Shas and United Torah Judaism had denied that there was a "deal", after Ynet reported that they had agreed to back this bill in return for their own bills. They said Netanyahu had committed to advance their own bills [23].
- **Statement:** On January 8, 2025, Shas MK Yinon Azoulay said a state commission of inquiry "should be established… as soon as possible." MK Yosef Taieb said one "is needed," but that the party would follow the Council of Torah Sages. Interior Minister Moshe Arbel (Shas) had backed a state commission in a letter to the attorney general in August 2024 [62]. The same evening, the two MKs clarified that the decision on who would head an inquiry was up to Deri [63]. Kikar HaShabbat reported that they did so after Deri, who opposes a state commission, rebuked them [64].
- **Statement:** In October 2026 Shas declined to tell Ynet which inquiry model it supports [67].
- **Contradiction:** In January 2025 two Shas MKs and a Shas minister publicly backed a state commission of inquiry [62] (the MKs then left the question of who would head it to Deri [63]). In October 2025 the party's members of the State Control Committee voted against a proposal to establish one [65].

**Closest option(s):** Based on its committee vote and the reported deal on the coalition bill, a commission picked through the Knesset (`commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`). Some Shas MKs' January 2025 statements supported `state-commission-under-existing-law`.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

- **Action:** Labor Minister Ben-Tzur voted in the government for the January 2025 hostage and ceasefire deal, saying afterwards that his "hands trembled" when he signed in favour [51].
- **Statement:** On January 28, 2025 Ben-Tzur told Netanyahu that "the six Shas ministers, led by the chairman, will support any deal you make," and urged him to move to the deal's second phase [51]. In July 2025 Welfare Minister Yaakov Margi said a hostage deal was needed "even at the cost of ending the war" [52].
- **Statement:** In October 2025 Deri welcomed the US-brokered ceasefire and hostage deal as "a great miracle." He stressed that Israel would get all the hostages back while IDF soldiers stayed in control of more than half of Gaza "until Hamas is dismantled and another force enters." He contrasted it with earlier proposals that, he said, involved full withdrawal and leaving Hamas in power [49, 50].
- **Statement:** The Jerusalem Post's 2026 election guide says Shas supports dismantling Hamas and demilitarizing Gaza, while opposing indefinite Israeli control [2].

No published Shas plan for Gaza's future was found. In its 2022 coalition agreement Shas made no independent security or foreign-policy demands [31]. Searches covered party statements, the Knesset record and news coverage.

**Closest option(s):** Move forward with the US plan: an international force and gradual disarmament of Hamas, with the army withdrawing gradually (`advance-international-arrangement`). Deri's emphasis on the army staying until Hamas is dismantled also overlaps with `hold-positions-disarmament-first`. Apart from a Shas minister's vote for the January 2025 deal, the evidence is statements.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

Hezbollah is the Iran-backed terror organization in Lebanon.

- **Action:** Although not a minister, Deri took part in the security cabinet's discussions during the wars with Iran [13, 92]. In June 2025 he described the "very hard" deliberations before the strike on Iran [93]. In July 2026 he said this was a small, informal forum that does not vote, and that he had barely attended in recent months [30].
- **Statement:** On April 8, 2026, Deri defended Netanyahu's decision to accept a ceasefire with Iran. He called the campaign "a war of no choice" that had achieved major results, and stressed the importance of the alliance with the United States. He accused former defence ministers and chiefs of staff who attacked the ceasefire of acting against the state for political gain [53].
- **Statement:** In an Israel Hayom interview published on April 9, 2026, Deri called the ceasefire with Iran welcome, because it made it more likely that the regime would fall from within. He said the aim of the campaign had been to create the conditions for the regime's fall. On Lebanon he said the army was now several kilometres inside the country and "will not leave until Hezbollah is disarmed… until the agreement is fulfilled." He also said the period of the Trump administration was a great opportunity for Israel to establish its regional standing [92].

No formal Shas platform on Iran or Lebanon was found. In its 2022 coalition agreement Shas made no independent security or foreign-policy demands [31]. Searches covered Hebrew and English news, party statements and the Knesset record.

**Closest option(s):** Keep up military pressure while reaching US-backed agreements, and pull back from Lebanon only once a deal is fulfilled (`force-plus-us-backed-agreements`), based on Deri's support for the US-coordinated campaign, the ceasefire, and staying in Lebanon until Hezbollah is disarmed. The evidence is the leader's statements and his part in security cabinet discussions.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

- **Action:** In July 2025 Shas was among the parties that submitted a Knesset declaration supporting applying Israeli sovereignty to Judea and Samaria. It passed 71–13 with the votes of all coalition parties, which then included Shas, and has no legal force [55]. Its operative clause called on the government to apply Israeli sovereignty to "all the areas of Jewish settlement of all kinds in Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley" [56].
- **Formal commitment:** The 2022 coalition agreement included a commitment to advance a "humanitarian solution" for connecting unauthorised outposts in Judea and Samaria to electricity [31].
- **Statement:** In September 2026 Shas told Ynet that, as "founders of the right-wing bloc," it is a partner to "every right-wing initiative" of Religious Zionism and Otzma Yehudit. It said it had voted for initiatives of Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich and opposes a Palestinian state [54]. Ynet noted that the party does not campaign on settlement, has no detailed plan on the issue, and focuses on tradition and yeshivas. As Interior Minister, Deri supported the local councils in Judea and Samaria [54].

**Closest option(s):** The party co-submitted a declaration calling for sovereignty over the areas of Jewish settlement, and opposes a Palestinian state. This is closest to `partial-sovereignty-area-c`, although the declaration does not rule out going further (`full-sovereignty`). The party has no detailed plan and does not campaign on the issue, so its practical position may be closer to `maintain-current-situation`.

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

- **Action:** Shas's December 2022 coalition agreement guaranteed "at least" NIS 1 billion for pre-loaded food cards for needy families [31]. The scheme was run through the Shas-led Interior Ministry. In September 2023 the Supreme Court allowed a NIS 400 million round of the scheme to go ahead, and ordered the government to review eligibility rules after petitions argued they favoured Haredi families [32]. In December 2025 Deri said Shas would not support the 2026 budget until a NIS 277 million food-card payment, which he said had been promised to Shas, was funded [33].
- **Action:** The 2022 agreement also included freezing utility prices and municipal property taxes and cancelling the taxes on single-use tableware and sugary drinks [31]. Finance Minister Smotrich ordered both taxes cancelled on January 1, 2023, in line with demands by Haredi parties [36].
- **Action:** In February 2023 Shas MKs Azoulay, Arbel, Malul, Moshiashvili and Taieb were among the sponsors of a bill to apply the Food Competition Law fully to pharmacy chains, to increase competition and lower consumer prices [39]. The bill became the Food Competition Law (Amendment No. 8), which passed its final reading and was published in July 2024 [40].
- **Action:** Welfare Minister Margi (Shas) backed a law, passed on March 31, 2025, setting up a national authority to fight poverty. MKs from Shas, the Democrats and Hadash-Ta'al supported it [46].
- **Action:** In March 2024 Labor Minister Ben-Tzur (Shas) signed the order raising the minimum wage to NIS 5,880.02 a month [89].
- **Action:** In January 2024 the government, in which Shas then held ministries, decided to raise VAT from 17% to 18% from January 2025. The Knesset Finance Committee approved the order in February 2024 and the Knesset plenum in March 2024 [88, 89]. At the committee, Shas MK Azoulay said he was "in essence against raising VAT" and agreed that it hurts the weakest [88]. No breakdown of how Shas MKs voted was found. The rise took effect on January 1, 2025, along with other tax and price increases approved to cover war costs [37].
- **Formal commitment:** In 2015, in an earlier term, Shas demanded removing VAT from basic products as a condition for joining the government [86].
- **Statement:** The party's 2022 campaign used the slogan "Only Shas will lower the cost of living" [38]. The Jerusalem Post's 2026 guide says Shas calls for help to lower-income families and opposes measures that hurt poorer households [2].

**Closest option(s):** Direct help for the households hit hardest, such as food subsidies for low-income families (`direct-support-for-households`). The party has also pushed for removing some consumer taxes (`cut-taxes-and-government-spending`), and its MKs sponsored more competition in the pharmacy market (`open-market-competition-imports`).

### Housing {#issue-housing}

- **Action:** Deri was among the sponsors of a Public Housing (Purchase Rights) bill [42]. A committee version published in November 2025 would re-enact an expired law. It lets tenants who have lived in public housing for at least five years buy their apartment at a subsidised price, with the proceeds going to a fund for buying new public housing [41].
- **Formal commitment:** Globes reported that the December 2022 coalition agreements contained concrete housing measures mainly for the Haredi public: a new Haredi city near employment centres, a plan for 200,000 homes for the Haredi population by 2035, and grants of NIS 100,000 for buyers in outlying areas. For the general public they used general language [43].
- **Statement:** In May 2026 the Israel Land Authority made settling one's military status a condition for entering "discounted apartment" lotteries, and reserved half of the apartments for reservists [45]. Shas faction chair Azoulay attacked the decision: "Families of terrorists will receive a 'discounted apartment,' but Torah students will not" [44].

**Closest option(s):** Help people buy directly, through discounted purchases and grants (`direct-help-for-buyers`). Much of the party's housing record targets the Haredi community specifically.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

The record is thin. No Shas platform, official statement or recorded party vote was found on bringing in the Shin Bet, special detention powers, or more police under normal law.

- **Action:** In August 2023 Shas Interior Minister Moshe Arbel pressed Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich to release about NIS 300 million in grants to Arab local authorities that Smotrich had frozen. Smotrich said such money often reached criminal organisations. Arbel warned that freezing the funds could harm the authorities' ability to balance their books [90]. After a meeting with Arbel, the heads of the Shin Bet and the police and Arab local leaders, Smotrich agreed to transfer NIS 200 million, through supervision mechanisms meant to keep the money from crime organisations, and to fund technology for the police [91].
- **Statement:** In January 2025 then-Shas MK Avraham Betzalel, who is not on the 2026 list, said crime in Arab society is "a strategic threat to the State of Israel" that requires "mobilization of all state systems" against criminal organisations' finances, protection rackets, money laundering and arms trading [47]. He did not specify which measures.

The government approved the Shin Bet plan on July 15, 2026, after Shas had left the government. Coverage of the decision does not mention Shas [48]. Searches covered Hebrew and English news, Haredi news sites and the Knesset bills database.

**Closest option(s):** The limited record leans toward keeping funding flowing to Arab local authorities, with safeguards against crime organisations (`invest-in-arab-communities-first`). This rests on one minister's action in 2023, and no Shas position on the Shin Bet or special powers was found.

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Torah study and Haredi institutions funding {#other-haredi-institutions}

- **Formal commitment:** In the 2022 coalition agreement, Shas and the other religious parties secured promises to raise funding for schools that do not teach the full core curriculum (55% of the public-school level for exempt schools and 75% for recognised "unofficial" schools), and more funding for yeshivas and kollels (yeshivas for married men) [31].
- **Action:** Shas backed the 2026 budget, passed on March 30, 2026. A last-minute coalition change to the budget added about NIS 800 million for Haredi programmes and institutions, and the attorney general froze the funds [35].
- **Statement:** At the end of May 2026 Deri warned that the attorney general's move to cancel tax benefits for yeshivas serving students who had not enlisted could lead to "a tax revolt" [20]. In July 2026 MK Malkieli sought a Knesset debate against restricting tax benefits for Torah institutions with students who had not served. The motion failed on a tied vote [83].

### Welfare and poverty {#other-welfare-poverty}

- **Action:** Shas held the Welfare and Labor ministries in 2022–2025 [31, 79]. It backed the March 2025 law creating a national anti-poverty authority [46]. Its food-card programme is described under Cost of living [32, 33].

### Health and the periphery {#other-health-periphery}

- **Formal commitment:** The 2022 coalition agreement allocated NIS 5.85 billion to the health system, focused on "disadvantaged and far-flung communities." It also promised more transport subsidies for the periphery and a fast rail line from Kiryat Shmona to Eilat [31].

### Religious services and the legacy of Rabbi Ovadia Yosef {#other-religious-services}

- **Action:** A law was passed providing for state-funded events to commemorate Rabbi Ovadia Yosef. In September 2026 the Supreme Court rejected as moot a Shas petition against the attorney general's decision to block a state-funded memorial event within three weeks of the election. Deri said that "neither the High Court nor the attorney general will succeed in stopping the legacy of Maran" [71].

### Sephardi identity and representation {#other-sephardi-identity}

- **Statement:** Shas was founded to represent Sephardi Jews [2]. Its 2026 list includes a candidate from the Bukharan Jewish community [5].

### Hostages {#other-hostages}

- **Statement:** Shas ministers said they would support deals to bring back hostages, and one said a deal was needed even at the cost of ending the war [51, 52]. Deri called the October 2025 deal "a great miracle" [49].

### Environment {#other-environment}

- **Action:** The party's 2022 coalition agreement required cancelling the tax on single-use tableware, which had been brought in for environmental reasons. The tax was cancelled in January 2023 [31, 36]. Research reported by the Times of Israel found that the Haredi public saw the tax as an attack on its way of life [84].
- **Formal commitment:** In the same agreement Shas signed on to passing a stalled climate bill, including a promise to cut Israel's greenhouse gas emissions by 50% of 2015 levels by 2030 [31].

### Temple Mount {#other-temple-mount}

- **Statement:** In September 2026 spiritual leader Rabbi Yitzhak Yosef said it is forbidden under Jewish religious law to vote for "someone who ascends the Temple Mount." The remark was understood as aimed at National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir [72].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

- **April 9, 2026 (Deri):** In an Israel Hayom interview on the next government, Deri answered "Netanyahu" and said he would be glad if it were a broader government, and if Benny Gantz's party passed the electoral threshold [92].
- **July 2026 (party newspaper):** An editorial in Shas's official newspaper, Haderech, said that "without a common denominator, there is no common camp," and accused figures in Netanyahu's bloc of retreating on the Torah study and arrests laws. Ynetnews described it as the first sign that the party might not see itself as automatically bound to the right-wing bloc [25].
- **July 16–17, 2026 (Deri):** "We support Netanyahu, period." Deri ruled out cooperating after the election with Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party, describing his camp as one "of hatred against everything Judaism stands for." He said there was "no alternative" to Netanyahu's bloc and that he would prefer to broaden a Netanyahu-led government [24, 30]. In the same interview he said that what Eisenkot might do after a win was a matter for the Torah sages to decide on its merits [30].
- **October 2026 (Deri):** Deri wrote that Shas would not enter any government before the status of Torah students is settled and guaranteed [17]. Ynet reported in September 2026 that Likud has said it would pass the enlistment law that failed in the outgoing Knesset right after the election, before the next government is formed, in line with Shas's demand [16].
- **October 6, 2026:** After Naftali Bennett said he would join the next government only if a law barred anyone who avoids military service or encourages others to avoid it from the security cabinet, Shas MK Malkieli said that "in the next government" Shas would pass a "Bennett Law" aimed at him [18].

**Mixed messages from the spiritual leader.** On July 12, 2026 Army Radio reported that, in a closed conversation, Rabbi Yitzhak Yosef had called Netanyahu "a liar who cannot be trusted" over the enlistment law and said Shas might back Eisenkot for prime minister [25, 26]. - **Track record of keeping pledges:** In 2022 Shas stood by Netanyahu as it had said it would, and joined his government [38, 31]. But it did not carry out its January 2026 threat to vote against the 2026 budget unless that law passed, and supported the budget in March 2026 [34, 35] (see Track record).

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

- **Aryeh Deri, 2000 conviction.** Deri served 22 months in prison (2000–2002) after being convicted of bribery while he was Interior Minister. The conviction carried moral turpitude (a legal finding that bars a person from public office for several years) [75].
- **Aryeh Deri, 2022 tax conviction.** On January 25, 2022, the Jerusalem Magistrate's Court convicted Deri, under a plea deal, of five income-tax offences and one land-tax offence. His resignation from the Knesset took effect beforehand, so the conviction did not carry moral turpitude [75]. On February 1, 2022 the court sentenced him to a one-year suspended sentence and a fine of NIS 180,000 [94]. **Status:** final.
- **Supreme Court ruling, 2023.** On January 18, 2023, the Supreme Court ruled 10–1 that Deri's appointment as Interior and Health Minister was "extremely unreasonable." It cited his convictions and his statement to the court during the plea deal that he was leaving politics. The court ordered Netanyahu to remove him [74]. He left office on January 24, 2023 [78]. **Status:** final. Deri is not a minister.
- **Election-law order, 2026.** On August 23, 2026, after a complaint by the Yashar party, CEC chair Justice Noam Sohlberg issued an interim order requiring Deri to take down a Shas campaign video featuring a soldier, which seemingly breached the ban on using images of security forces in election material. Deri was given two days to respond. Shas removed the video the same day [73]. **Status:** video removed; no final decision was found.
- **Candidate challenge, 2026.** On September 23, 2026, the CEC chair rejected a request by the group Itim to strike candidate #6, Dror Amos, from the list over the cooling-off rules for public officials [76, 77]. **Status:** rejected.

No other indictments, convictions or official inquiries involving the current top candidates were found. Searches covered Hebrew and English news, CEC decisions and court coverage.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

There is enough public information on Shas. The record includes a long Knesset and government history (including ministries held from 2022 to 2025), signed coalition agreements, laws it sponsored, and wide coverage in Hebrew, English and Haredi media.

There are gaps:

- **No published 2026 platform.** No formal 2026 platform (מצע) was found, and the party website could not be reached. The party's positions come from its actions, its coalition agreements, and statements by the leader and MKs.
- **Thin evidence on some issues.** Gaza, Iran and Lebanon, and Judea and Samaria rest mainly on statements, a declaration vote and the leader's part in security cabinet discussions. Crime in Arab society rests on a single 2023 action by a Shas minister on funding for Arab local authorities.
- **Votes by party.** Knesset open data for the 25th Knesset does not include individual votes. Several votes are therefore described from news reports that do not always break them down by party (for example the March 2025 law on choosing judges).
- **Leadership messages.** The party chairman and its spiritual leader have sent mixed messages on coalition partners.
- **Sources that could not be opened.** Several i24NEWS and Kan reports could not be opened for citation, so they are not used.
