## Overview {#overview}

**New Hope** (תקווה חדשה) is a right-wing party. Its registered name is now "New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" (תקווה חדשה הימין הממלכתי) [1]. Gideon Sa'ar founded it in December 2020, after he left the Likud. Sa'ar said at the time that the Likud "had become a tool serving the personal interests of its leader", meaning Benjamin Netanyahu [3, 4]. Several sitting lawmakers joined him, among them Ze'ev Elkin, Sharren Haskel, Michal Shir and Yifat Shasha-Biton. Two MKs from the Derech Eretz party also joined [3].

**Ideology and base.** The party describes itself as "the statesmanlike right" (הימין הממלכתי). When Sa'ar split from Benny Gantz in March 2024, he said "the voice of the statesmanlike right is more essential today than ever" [5]. The Israel Democracy Institute (IDI) places it on the right of Israeli politics [4]. In practice its record combines security positions on the right with some liberal positions on personal issues, such as LGBT rights in 2021 and medical cannabis in 2025 (see "Other notable positions") [9, 39, 55]. The documents reviewed for this research don't describe a specific demographic or community base.

**Election history and splits.**
- **2021 (24th Knesset):** New Hope ran alone. It won 209,161 votes and 6 seats [3, 4], and joined the government of Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid. Sa'ar became justice minister, Ze'ev Elkin housing minister and Yifat Shasha-Biton education minister [24].
- **2022 (25th Knesset):** In July 2022 New Hope joined Benny Gantz's Blue and White on a joint list called National Unity (HaMachane HaMamlachti). New Hope brought 4 seats to the merger and Blue and White brought 8 [5]. New Hope won 4 of the list's seats [3].
- **October 2023:** After the October 7 attack, two New Hope ministers joined Benjamin Netanyahu's emergency government as part of National Unity. They later resigned [4].
- **Split from National Unity, March 2024:** On 12 March 2024 Sa'ar took New Hope out of National Unity and demanded a seat in the war cabinet [5]. In the Knesset the party has sat as a separate faction, "the Statesmanlike Right", since 13 March 2024 [21, 22]. On 25 March 2024 Sa'ar left the coalition after he wasn't given a war-cabinet seat [6].
- **Rejoining the government, September 2024:** Sa'ar and Netanyahu announced on 29 September 2024 that New Hope would rejoin the government [7]. In November 2024 Sa'ar became foreign minister [9].
- **Merger agreement with the Likud, March 2025:** On 13 March 2025 New Hope signed an agreement to dissolve into the Likud before the next election [10]. The Likud Central Committee approved the merger on 13 August 2025 [11].
- **From merger to joint list, July 2026:** The merger was never completed in the Knesset, because the parties never sent it to the Knesset House Committee for approval [14, 15]. According to a Likud filing quoted by Srugim, on 14 July 2026 the two factions agreed to run on a joint candidate list instead of fully merging [16]. Also in July 2026, New Hope MK Sharren Haskel resigned as deputy foreign minister and launched a new party, Israel First. On 19 July 2026 she petitioned the High Court of Justice to force the merger to be completed, so that she could formally split away from the faction [13, 14, 15]. On 9 August 2026 the court, in a ruling written by Supreme Court President Yitzhak Amit, rejected the petition in full. It said that her delay in acting since March 2025 undermined her claim, and it accepted the factions' later choice of a joint run instead of a merger. The ruling records that on 14 July 2026 the heads of the two factions agreed to carry out the agreement by running together on one candidate list for the 26th Knesset, instead of merging the factions in the outgoing Knesset. Because the factions agreed on the joint run only after her petition, the court did not order her to pay costs [67].

**Current seats.** In the outgoing 25th Knesset the New Hope faction formally holds 4 seats: Ze'ev Elkin, Sharren Haskel, Michel Buskila and Akram Hasson [21, 22]. Sa'ar left the Knesset in July 2025 but remains foreign minister [20]. Haskel now leads a separate party [13, 14].

**The list it runs on (joint list).** For the October 2026 election New Hope runs on the Likud's joint list, "The Likud led by Benjamin Netanyahu for Prime Minister" (הליכוד בהנהגת בנימין נתניהו לראשות הממשלה), with the ballot letters **מחל** [2]. According to the Central Elections Committee (CEC) filing, two parties submitted the list: "Likud – National Liberal Movement" and "New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" [1]. Its partner is therefore the Likud. The history of splits between these two parties is set out above. Sa'ar left the Likud in December 2020 to found New Hope [3]. The parties then signed a merger agreement in March 2025, and in July 2026 they turned it into a joint run [10, 16].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Gideon Sa'ar (party leader).** Sa'ar is foreign minister and New Hope's chairman [60]. According to the Knesset's records, he was an MK in the 16th to 19th Knessets and the 21st to 25th Knessets. He served as education minister (2009–2013), interior minister (2013–2014) and justice minister (2021–2022), and became foreign minister in November 2024 [23, 9]. His 2021–2022 justice-ministry term was in the Bennett–Lapid government [24], and the Knesset's records also list him as deputy prime minister in that government [23]. He was also a minister without portfolio from October 2023 to March 2024 and again from September to November 2024 [23]. In January 2025, as foreign minister, he and Justice Minister Yariv Levin presented a compromise on how judges are chosen. It became law in March 2025 (see "Judicial system") [27, 28]. Sa'ar resigned from the Knesset under the "Norwegian Law" in July 2025. This law lets a minister give up a Knesset seat to the next person on the list while staying in government. His seat went to Akram Hasson [20].

**Position on the 2026 list.** The Likud secretariat approved a reserved slot on the Likud list for Sa'ar in August 2026 [17]. A reserved slot is a place on the list that the party leader assigns directly, outside the primary. The March 2025 agreement also let Sa'ar pick one more candidate for a reserved slot no lower than 15th place [10]. In August 2026 Ynetnews reported that the agreement gave New Hope two slots: one in the top five for Sa'ar and one for Michel Buskila [19]. When the list was presented on 8 September 2026, Sa'ar was 7th, Talik Gvili 9th and Bardugo 11th [18]. Gvili is the mother of Ran Gvili, a police officer who fell in battle on October 7; his body was taken to Gaza, and he was the last hostage whose remains were returned [18, 80]. Kipa gives her first name as Talik; the CEC page lists her as Tali (טלי גואילי) [1, 18].

**Who was filed on New Hope's behalf.** The CEC candidate page lists each candidate's party. It shows that **Sa'ar (#7) and Ze'ev Elkin (#32) were filed on behalf of the Likud**, not New Hope. Only **Tali Gvili (#9), Ofir Saban (#88) and Ehud Ninio (#89)** are listed "on behalf of New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" [1]. Before the primary, the Likud had shortened its membership requirements so that Elkin could run in it [17]. Bardugo (#11) is listed on behalf of the Likud [1].

**Other figures.**
- **Ze'ev Elkin** was housing minister and minister for Jerusalem affairs in the 2021–2022 government [24]. Since late 2024 he has been the minister in the Finance Ministry responsible for rebuilding the north and the communities near Gaza [57, 58, 59]. The 2025 agreement expected him to receive a high reserved slot [10]. On the CEC list he is 32nd [1].
- **Michel Buskila** has sat in the Knesset for the faction since 5 July 2024 [21, 22]. He is the faction's whip [60].
- **Akram Hasson** is a Druze politician. He entered the Knesset in July 2025 in Sa'ar's place [20, 21].
- **Sharren Haskel** was New Hope's third-ranking member and deputy foreign minister. She resigned in July 2026 and set up Israel First [10, 13, 14].
- **Yifat Shasha-Biton** was New Hope's education minister in the 2021–2022 government [24]. On 2 July 2024 she announced that she was leaving politics and taking "a time-out from political life" [120]; Michel Buskila has held the seat since 5 July 2024 [21, 22]. She is not among the candidates filed on behalf of New Hope for 2026 [1]. Her record at the Education Ministry is therefore not used in this document to describe the party's current positions.
- **Yoaz Hendel**, communications minister in the 2021–2022 government, was listed then as a New Hope minister [24]. He is not among the candidates filed on behalf of New Hope for 2026 [1], and his record is not covered here.
- The New Hope leader on the joint list's top line is Sa'ar. The list itself is headed by Netanyahu (#1), Eli Cohen (#2), Amir Ohana (#3), Yariv Levin (#4) and Miri Regev (#5), all filed for the Likud [1].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

New Hope has no platform for 2026, because it runs on the Likud's list, and no statement by Sa'ar or Elkin on how to lower prices was found from the period since the party rejoined the government in September 2024. The search covered a party platform, news coverage of Sa'ar and Elkin, and the Knesset voting database. The documented position comes from three earlier sources inside the 2021–2026 window: the plans the party published for the March 2021 election (they survive only in archived copies of its website), its votes in the 2021–2022 government, and the principles of the National Unity list, which New Hope co-wrote for the November 2022 election. All three point the same way: more competition and easier imports.

- **Action:** On 4 November 2021 the Knesset passed the Economic Plan Law that accompanied the 2021–2022 budget (this kind of law is known as the Economic Arrangements Law) by 61 votes to 57, with one MK present but not voting. New Hope's six MKs who voted (Benny Begin, Zvi Hauser, Sharren Haskel, Meir Yitzhak-Halevi, Yifat Shasha-Biton and Michal Shir) voted for it [116]. According to Globes, the law included rules that bring import requirements into line with European Union standards and a change that opened kosher certification to competition, together with a higher retirement age for women and a congestion charge for the Tel Aviv area from 2025 [115]. The same MKs voted for the 2021 budget (61 to 59) on 4 November 2021, and five of them for the 2022 budget (59 to 56) on 5 November 2021 [116]. Sa'ar and Elkin were ministers in the government that proposed these laws [24]. Neither voted: the Knesset's record shows that Elkin's membership of the Knesset ended on 15 June 2021 and Sa'ar's on 9 July 2021, after they became ministers [23, 121].
- **Action:** On 24 November and 1 December 2021 the party's MKs who voted opposed opposition bills to raise child allowances and to raise the disability allowance to the level of the minimum wage (details under Track record) [82, 84]. In June 2022 Sa'ar said of such votes: "There have been a lot of well-intentioned social bills that benefit the citizens and we would like to vote for them, but there is coalition discipline and there are budget constraints, so we could not" [89].
- **Formal commitment (National Unity principles, 2022; written jointly with Blue and White before New Hope left the list on 12 March 2024):** "We will advance a comprehensive economic plan that combines a free economy with social justice and includes reforms to open the Israeli market to competition and imports, removing barriers and reducing unnecessary protective walls, in order to lower the cost of living" [117, 5].
- **Formal commitment (plan for the March 2021 election, archived copy):** The party's plan "Hope for lowering the cost of living" listed nine steps. The first was to "reduce the power of the big monopolies in the food market" and to let any business import goods that meet European standards. Others were to close the local water corporations, which the plan said would cut water prices by about 17%; to set up a single food and import authority; to increase competition among banks; to cut the price of electricity by 25% within four years through new gas agreements and competition among private suppliers; to open postal services to competitors; to update the basket of price-controlled foods so that it favours healthier products; to give the Consumer Protection Authority more power to punish businesses; and to teach financial and consumer education in schools [83]. Its five-point economic plan promised to "open the Israeli market to competition by lowering import barriers" and to cut bureaucracy, in order to "increase competition and lower the cost of living for the Israeli consumer" [83].
- **Action:** The faction's MKs, Sa'ar included, voted for the Economic Efficiency Law for the 2025 budget year, which froze tax updates and set a surtax. It passed its final reading on 24 December 2024 [42, 43]. All four voted for the 2025 state budget, which passed by 66 to 52 on 25 March 2025 (the Knesset record files the vote under the sitting of 24 March) [39, 40, 76]. The faction's MKs also voted for the 2026 budget and the 2026 Economic Efficiency Law on 30 March 2026 [39, 40, 42, 43].
- **Statement:** In September 2025 MK Sharren Haskel said she supports capitalism, lower taxes and deregulation. She was then New Hope's third-ranking member [10, 12]. She has since left to found Israel First [13], so this is not a current party position.

**Closest option(s):** Tentative: opening the market to more competition and easier imports (`open-market-competition-imports`). This rests on the party's 2021 plan, its MKs' vote for the November 2021 import reform and the 2022 National Unity principles [83, 115, 116, 117]. None of this evidence dates from the period since September 2024, when the party's recorded actions on the economy are its votes for the government's budgets [39, 42]. The 2021 plan's wider powers for the Consumer Protection Authority also touch on state regulation (`active-state-regulation`), but that is a small part of the plan.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

No current New Hope platform or leadership statement was found on the Shin Bet plan, on holding crime suspects without trial, or on funding for Arab communities. The search covered news coverage of Sa'ar, Elkin, Buskila and Hasson and the Knesset voting database for crime-related bills between 2021 and 2026. The party's record has two parts: what Sa'ar did as justice minister in 2021–2022, and a few Knesset votes since 2024.

**As justice minister, 2021–2022.** Sa'ar's measures were changes to criminal law and police powers. The laws apply to everyone in Israel; the government presented them as part of its response to crime in Arab society [94, 96, 98].

- **Action:** On 7 December 2021 the Knesset passed a temporary amendment to the Penal Law (Amendment No. 140) that sets a minimum sentence, a quarter of the maximum sentence, for holding, carrying or trading in illegal weapons, except in special cases [94, 95]. The bill was proposed by New Hope MK Sharren Haskel [94]. Sa'ar welcomed its passage and said the party was fulfilling an election promise to deal with illegal weapons: "Punishments that deter are needed to deal with the national scourge of illegal weapons" [94].
- **Action:** On 17 October 2021 the government approved a bill, tabled by Sa'ar, to let the police search a home without a court warrant where there is probable cause of a serious crime, such as extortion or the use of illegal weapons, and a concern that evidence will be destroyed. It was drafted as a temporary measure. Sa'ar said: "The police must be given the best possible tools to succeed in their mission to end the violence. The right to life is also a human right and I will bring more legislation until we end the crime wave" [96]. The bill passed its first reading on 13 December 2021 by 60 votes to 58, with all six New Hope MKs who voted in favour [97]. It did not become law: the Knesset's record shows it still in committee, being prepared for its final readings, when the 24th Knesset ended [93].
- **Action:** On 9 May 2022 a government bill promoted by Sa'ar passed its first reading by 18 votes to none. According to the Times of Israel, it would raise the prison term for offences involving a weapon that contains substantial parts of a conventional weapon from three to five years, and for buying a weapon from seven to ten years. Sa'ar said that "one of the characteristics and causes of the crime phenomenon in Arab society is the availability of weapons of various kinds on a large scale" [97, 98]. The Knesset's record shows the government bill still in committee when the 24th Knesset ended [93]. The law that was published in July 2022 under the name "Penal Law (Amendment No. 143 and Temporary Provision)" is a different one: it grew out of a private member's bill and deals with offences against children and helpless persons in educational and care settings, not with weapons [128].
- **Action:** On 11 May 2022 the six New Hope MKs who voted opposed an opposition bill to set minimum sentences for weapons offences, which was defeated 53–61 [82]. The party's own minimum-sentence law had been in force since December 2021 [94, 95].
- **Formal commitment (National Unity principles, 2022; written jointly with Blue and White before the split of 12 March 2024):** the list pledged to "fight consistently and systematically against crime organizations, protection rackets and the holding of illegal weapons", and to fight violence in Arab society [117].
- No statement by Sa'ar or Elkin was found on whether the Shin Bet should investigate crime organizations, or on funding for Arab communities as a way to reduce crime.

**Knesset votes since 2024.**

- **Action:** On 10 December 2025 the Knesset held preliminary readings on two crime bills. Elkin, Haskel and Hasson voted **against** a bill by MK Merav Cohen to combat weapons smuggling, which was rejected by 52 votes to 26 with four MKs present but not voting. They voted **for** the Special Powers to Combat Crime Organizations (Temporary Provisions) bill by Tzvika Fogel, chair of the Knesset's National Security Committee, which passed by 54 votes to 41 [39, 40, 65, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74]. Maariv described Fogel's bill as resting on declaring crime organizations to be terror organizations, after which the police and the Shin Bet could use technological and investigative tools that only the Shin Bet now has [64].
- **Action:** In May 2024, while New Hope was in opposition, Haskel voted in a preliminary reading for the bill on protecting the public from crime organizations [39, 40]. The law that passed lets courts restrict the movement and contacts of members of crime organizations, at the police's request and with the attorney general's approval [44]. No New Hope vote is recorded on its final reading in December 2024, or on a July 2026 crime-organization bill [39, 40].
- As ministers, Sa'ar and Elkin belong to the government that runs crime policy. No source was found that records how they voted on any specific crime-related government decision.

**Closest option(s):** Tentative. The whole record is about enforcement: tougher sentences and wider powers for the police. As justice minister in 2021–2022 Sa'ar worked through criminal law and police powers, and none of the sources opened shows him proposing a role for the Shin Bet or detention without trial. That is closest to `more-police-resources-normal-law`, although his bill on searches without a warrant went further than that option's "no special powers" [94, 96, 97]. The most recent action, the December 2025 vote for a bill giving the police and the Shin Bet counter-terrorism tools against crime organizations, is closest to using extraordinary measures (`emergency-powers-shin-bet`) [64, 71]. No party statement explains that vote. No evidence was found that the party puts investment in Arab communities first.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Statement:** Sa'ar's line as foreign minister has been to support US President Donald Trump's plan for Gaza while insisting that Hamas disarm first. On 23 October 2025 he said "Israel is committed to working towards the success of President Trump's plan". He also said "Hamas and Islamic Jihad must lay down their arms. Gaza must be demilitarized… This is at the heart of the Trump plan. We will not compromise on it" and warned that attacks on Israeli forces "will be answered forcefully" [48].

**Statement:** On 14 July 2025 Sa'ar told Euronews that Israel has no intention of controlling Gaza in the long term and has "only security concerns" there. He said Hamas "cannot be part of the future of Gaza", but that "if Hamas is ready to lay down its arms… we can do it on a political path". He described the Palestinian Authority as still sponsoring terrorism and incitement [47].

**Statement:** In September 2026, at a press conference in Ljubljana, Sa'ar said: "We are not planning to expel or to deport anyone from [the] Gaza Strip. If someone wants to immigrate of his own will, and there is a country which is ready to accept him… we will not oppose that. But we will not force anyone against his will." [49] According to the Times of Israel, he spoke after National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir had presented a plan to encourage voluntary emigration from Gaza [49].

**Action:** In January 2025 the government approved the hostage-release and ceasefire deal by 24 votes to 8. The ministers who voted against came from Religious Zionism and Otzma Yehudit, plus Likud ministers David Amsalem and Amichai Chikli [50]. Sa'ar, then foreign minister, is not among the ministers the report names as voting against.

**Earlier statement:** When Sa'ar left the coalition in March 2024, he argued that Hamas's military power should have been destroyed "on a faster schedule". He also said that Hamas's control of aid distribution undermined the effort to bring it down [6].

**Statement:** In July 2025 Elkin did not sign a letter by 22 ministers and MKs that asked for a settlement group to be allowed to tour northern Gaza. He told Kan news that "the most painful thing for our enemy is losing land", and that making clear to Hamas that "they will lose land that they will never get back" would be "a significant pressure tool" [56]. The search found no statement by Sa'ar or Elkin calling for settlement in Gaza.

**Closest option(s):** Move forward with the US plan, with an international arrangement, while Hamas is disarmed (`advance-international-arrangement`). The repeated condition that Hamas disarm before anything else, and the refusal to give the Palestinian Authority a role, also overlap with keeping pressure until disarmament (`hold-positions-disarmament-first`). The evidence is mostly statements.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Statement:** In June 2024, while in opposition, Sa'ar said of the government's enlistment bill: "if we cannot legislate a good law, it's better not to legislate a law at all". According to the Times of Israel, he had repeatedly promised to oppose bills on Haredi enlistment that he considered exemption measures [9].

**Formal commitment / Action:** The November 2024 coalition agreement between New Hope and the Likud lets the party vote differently from the coalition on only two subjects: medical cannabis and changes to the judicial system. Bills on Haredi enlistment are **not** on that list [9].

**Action:** On 14 July 2026 the Knesset passed, 58 to 54, a temporary amendment to the Security Service Law (Amendment 28). It suspends arrest, investigation and enforcement against yeshiva students required to serve, until 30 November 2026. This applies on condition that they study at least 45 hours a week (40 for kollel students) [31]. A kollel is a yeshiva for married men. In the final reading Ze'ev Elkin, Michel Buskila and Akram Hasson voted **for** and Sharren Haskel voted **against** [29, 30]. Haskel then resigned as deputy foreign minister, saying she "can no longer support a government that harms the security of the country during wartime" [13]. The High Court of Justice froze the law the next day and struck it down on 3 September 2026 [79].

**Action:** The New Hope MKs (Elkin, Buskila, Hasson and Haskel) voted for the temporary law that extends men's mandatory service from 30 to 32 months, which passed in July 2026 [29, 30, 31].

**Action:** Elkin, Buskila and Hasson voted for "Basic Law: Torah Study", which passed its final reading on 13 July 2026. It declares Torah study "a foundational value" of the Jewish people and the state. Haskel voted against it in earlier readings [29, 30, 35].

**Change over time:** In January 2022, when New Hope sat in the Bennett–Lapid coalition and Sa'ar was justice minister, the government's enlistment bill passed its first reading 51–48 with the votes of New Hope MKs, among them Haskel; Sa'ar and Elkin did not vote, because both had left the Knesset after becoming ministers [23, 121]. On 11 June 2024, when the party was in the opposition, Sa'ar, Elkin and Haskel voted against reviving the same bill, which the Knesset did by 63–57 [81]. The two votes were cast in different Knessets, the first as part of the coalition and the second in the opposition, with the October 7 attack and the war in between. Sa'ar's stated view in June 2024 was that it is better to pass no law than a bad one [9].

**Change over time:** In June 2024, in opposition, Sa'ar promised to oppose enlistment bills he saw as exemption measures and said a bad law was worse than none [9]. In the November 2024 coalition agreement the party gave up the right to vote differently from the coalition on enlistment bills [9]. In July 2026 the New Hope MKs who remained with him voted for the law suspending arrests of yeshiva students who were required to serve [29, 30, 31]. Sa'ar himself was no longer an MK and didn't vote [20]. The votes followed the coalition line set in that agreement, 20 months after the party had publicly given up its earlier position.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. Sa'ar's 2024 statements are closest to requiring service with sanctions (`universal-service-with-sanctions`). The faction's 2026 votes, for suspending enforcement against yeshiva students and for the Torah-study basic law, are closer to `anchor-torah-study-exemption`. Per the evidence ranking, the more recent actions carry more weight.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

No current New Hope platform or party decision on how to make housing cheaper was found. The search covered a platform, news coverage of Elkin and Sa'ar, and the Knesset voting database. The documented record comes from Ze'ev Elkin, who was construction and housing minister from June 2021 to December 2022 [24, 111], and from his later work as a minister in the Finance Ministry.

**What the party promised before the 2021 election.**
- **Formal commitment (plan for the March 2021 election, archived copy):** "New Hope's Plan for Housing" had five points: a single "National Housing Ministry" bringing together the planning, land and housing bodies; urban renewal of 5,000 buildings in five years, with an emphasis on the periphery; 100,000 rental apartments within ten years through an organised long-term rental market; cutting the time from marketing land to occupancy by 30% within four years; and a national plan for a first apartment for young couples and families [83].

**What Elkin said on taking office.**
- **Statement:** In June 2021, shortly after becoming housing minister, Elkin warned that housing prices would keep rising. He said "we must get back to a higher rate of housing unit planning", backed extending the work of the National Committee for Planning and Building Preferred Housing Sites (a fast-track planning committee), and supported continuing National Outline Plan 38, which allows extra floors on buildings reinforced against earthquakes. He also said local authorities needed to know that promised transport projects would be built. According to Globes, the government had committed in the coalition talks to approving the planning of 300,000 housing units [62].

**What he did as housing minister, 2021–2022.**
- **Action (fast-track planning):** In August 2021 the Knesset extended the fast-track planning route for preferred housing sites by four more years. The law also widened the committee's powers over urban-renewal sites, over planning in Arab, Druze and Circassian communities and in housing sites meant for the Haredi public, and over sites near mass-transit stations [95].
- **Action (discounted homes for first-time buyers):** On 9 September 2021 the Israel Land Council approved Elkin's program "Target Price 2.1". Under it, state land is sold to builders on condition that a set share of the homes is sold to eligible buyers, such as first-time buyers and young families, at a 20% discount of up to NIS 300,000. Buyers in national priority areas get a further grant of NIS 40,000, and local residents get priority for a share of the homes. According to Globes, land at the reduced price is sold only in cities where the average price is up to NIS 20,000 per square metre and in cities that rank four or lower on the Central Bureau of Statistics' socio-economic scale [106, 107]. The first lottery opened on 20 March 2022 with 10,028 homes in 31 cities, and the ministry said about 30,000 would be offered during the year [107]. Elkin said in 2023 that almost 30,000 homes were drawn in 2022, against about 3,000 a year before he took office [111].
- **Action (the government housing plan for 2022–2025):** On 31 October 2021 Elkin, Finance Minister Avigdor Liberman and Interior Minister Ayelet Shaked presented a joint plan. Its targets for the four years were 280,000 housing starts, planning for 500,000 new homes, and tenders for 300,000 homes on state land. It set aside about NIS 8 billion for infrastructure that supports housing, about NIS 5.5 billion for kindergartens and schools, and more than NIS 2 billion for planning and development in Arab society. Its reforms included extending the fast-track committee, faster urban renewal, converting offices into homes, quicker permits, and raising the purchase tax on an additional home to 8%. Elkin called it "a comprehensive plan and not a magic solution of one step", and said the state would give up between NIS 12 and 20 billion in land income for Target Price [105]. The purchase tax on second and further homes rose from 5% to 8% in late November 2021 [113].
- **Action (public housing):** In November 2021 Elkin and the chair of the Knesset Economic Affairs Committee presented an arrangement under which no forced evictions from public housing would be carried out until a team had finished setting criteria for them [112]. In the same month the party's MKs who voted opposed an opposition bill to enlarge the public-housing stock (see Track record) [82].
- **Statement (Elkin's account of his rental policy):** Elkin said in 2023 that in his term the ministry promoted long-term rental homes at a fixed, index-linked rent, both through the government company Dira Lehaskir ("Apartment for Rent") and on private land through tax benefits passed in the Economic Arrangements Law. He said a second reform, a tax benefit for private owners who let a home for at least five years at a fixed, index-linked rent, was at an advanced stage when he left office and was not adopted by his successor [111]. This is Elkin's own account; it was not checked against an official record.

**Results in his term.**
- Housing starts reached 63,300 in 2021, the highest figure since 1995, according to the Central Bureau of Statistics; 12,760 of them were in the government's discounted-price program [109]. In the twelve months to September 2022 there were 70,250 starts, slightly below a peak of 72,540 in the twelve months to June 2022 [110].
- Prices kept rising. Home prices rose 13% in 2021 [107], and according to Nadlan Center, a real-estate news site, the rise reached a rate of about 20% a year at its peak during his term [111]. The Times of Israel reported in April 2022 that the coalition had committed to limiting the rise in 2022 to 6% [113].
- **Statement:** In March 2022 Elkin said: "The government does not have control over housing prices but it does have control of certain elements of the market." He said the state was now marketing land for 100,000 homes a year, against 50,000 in the year before, and that several such years were needed to close the gap [108]. In 2023 he attributed the price rise to the low level of land marketing in 2019–2020 and described the increase in supply as the main achievement of his term [111].

**Since 2023.**
- **Statement:** In September 2023 Elkin said the root of the solution is to increase supply dramatically. He supported keeping Target Price and objected to a decision taken under his successor that made the discounts smaller. He also proposed that the state spread developers' payments for land, because many land tenders were failing, and called for a working model of long-term rental [111].
- **Action:** As the Finance Ministry minister for rebuilding the north and south, Elkin led a government plan approved on 16 April 2026. It invests nearly NIS 1 billion over five years in the Golan Heights, including housing construction, and turns Katzrin into a city. He said its aim was "to bring in thousands of new families" [59]. He also heads the directorate for rebuilding the north after the war [57]. In March 2025 he backed the Tekuma law, which commits state support to the Gaza border area through 2028 [58]. These steps develop outlying regions, but they aren't presented as a housing-price policy.

**Closest option(s):** Tentative for the party today: building much more, faster (`speed-up-planning-and-construction`), together with direct help for buyers through discounted homes (`direct-help-for-buyers`). Both rest on the party's 2021 plan, on what Elkin did as housing minister in 2021–2022 and on what he said in 2023, not on a current party platform [83, 105, 106, 107, 111]. His 2026 Golan plan also fits developing the periphery (`develop-periphery-and-transport`) [59]. His steps on long-term rental at index-linked rents overlap in part with `state-led-affordable-rental`, but no proposal by him to limit rents in the private market was found [111].

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Action:** Sa'ar co-authored the compromise with Justice Minister Yariv Levin on the Judicial Selection Committee, the body that picks judges. It was presented on 9 January 2025 [28]. Under it, the committee is made up of three coalition representatives, one opposition representative, three judges and two lawyers, one chosen by the coalition and one by the opposition. According to Globes, a Supreme Court appointment needs support from both the coalition and the opposition [28]. The Knesset passed it on 27 March 2025, after the opposition boycotted the vote, and it takes effect when the next Knesset begins [27]. It was passed as two laws: the amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary (Amendment No. 4), by 67 votes to 1 with two MKs present but not voting, and the Courts Law (Amendment No. 105), by 64 votes to none with one MK present but not voting. Sa'ar, Elkin, Haskel and Buskila voted for both [26, 68, 69]. The Times of Israel reported that the March 2025 merger agreement cost Sa'ar his freedom to vote differently from the coalition on judicial legislation [10].

**Action:** On 15 July 2026 the Knesset passed the Government Legal Adviser Law (Legal Opinions, Representation and Supervision). Under this law the government may decide that the attorney general's opinions are not binding, except in criminal matters. It may also hire other lawyers to represent its position in court. The law takes effect on 1 January 2027 [34]. Elkin, Hasson, Buskila and Haskel voted for it in the final reading [32, 33].

**Action:** In October 2025 all four New Hope MKs voted in a preliminary reading for a bill to split the attorney general's role [32, 33]. On 2 June 2026 they voted for the Attorney General and Chief Prosecutor bill in its first reading [32, 33].

**Action (as justice minister, 2021–2022):** Sa'ar's term at the Justice Ministry is set out item by item under Track record. In short: four Supreme Court justices were appointed in February 2022, two of them described by the Jerusalem Post as moderate conservatives [99, 100]; the government appointed Gali Baharav-Miara, Sa'ar's preferred candidate, as attorney general [101]; Sa'ar said he intended to split the attorney general's role, but no government bill to do so was found in that Knesset's records [101, 102]; and a law letting courts reject evidence that was obtained illegally was passed [95, 103].

**Statement:** According to the Jerusalem Post, Sa'ar and Levin pitched their 2025 compromise as "evolutionary and not revolutionary" [63].

**Closest option(s):** The party's votes since 2025 support continuing the changes, giving elected officials more say over judicial appointments and making the attorney general's opinions non-binding (`continue-judicial-changes`). The judicial-selection law itself was framed as a compromise with an opposition veto over Supreme Court appointments [27, 28].

**Action (in government, 2025–2026):** After rejoining the coalition, every New Hope MK who voted on similar bills voted **against** them (not every MK voted on every bill):

If the opposition does not cooperate, the Knesset speaker picks them [38]. On 24 December 2025 Buskila voted for this bill in its preliminary reading. He warned that if the opposition boycotted, "the coalition would appoint all members of the commission of inquiry, which… would be no different than establishing a government commission of inquiry" [38].

**Contradiction:** In July 2024, in opposition, the party's MKs (Sa'ar included) voted for state commission of inquiry bills [36, 37]. From 2025 they voted against such bills, and one of them (Buskila) voted for the coalition's commission appointed through the Knesset [29, 30, 36, 37]. The change followed the party's move from the opposition into the coalition in September 2024 [7]; no party statement explaining it was found.

**Disagreement inside the party:** Elkin opposed the coalition's Knesset-appointed commission bill [38], while Buskila voted for it [29, 30].

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. The party's votes since 2025 are closest to `commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`, although Elkin objected to a design that lets the coalition pick all members. Its 2024 votes in opposition matched `state-commission-under-existing-law`.

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Current stance.** New Hope runs on the Likud's joint list, whose name presents Benjamin Netanyahu as the candidate for prime minister [2]. Its leader holds a reserved slot on that list [17, 18]. No separate New Hope statement was found about which other parties it would or wouldn't sit with after the 2026 election. When the merger was signed in March 2025, Sa'ar said his faction "never abandoned the right-wing worldview" [10].

**Record of keeping coalition pledges.**
- **2021:** On election night in March 2021, Sa'ar said he would keep working "to establish a government of change". He had refused to join any government led by Netanyahu [25]. He kept this pledge after the 2021 election: New Hope joined the Bennett–Lapid government [24]. At the start of that campaign, in December 2020, he had also said: "I will not sit under Lapid as prime minister" [124] (see the Contradiction below). On 25 March 2021, two days after the election, Yifat Shasha-Biton, then second on New Hope's list, said the party would not form a government propped up by the Joint List, the alliance of Arab-majority parties, and was aiming for a "center-right government made up of Zionist parties" [123].
- **2022:** For the November 2022 election New Hope ran with Blue and White as National Unity. The list's principles called for "a broad and stable government, a national unity government that does not rely on the extremes" [117]. On 18 September 2022 Sa'ar said National Unity would not join a narrow government that relied on the Arab-majority list Hadash–Ta'al: "We won't agree to a government that rests" on it, "period" [118].
- **2023–2024:** After the October 7 attack, New Hope's ministers joined Netanyahu's emergency government as part of National Unity [4]. On 29 September 2024 Sa'ar rejoined Netanyahu's government. He called it "the patriotic and right thing to do now" and said opposition parties' positions on the war were "different, even far from my own" [7]. Netanyahu said that "since October 7 we have put all the grudges of the past behind us" [7].
- **2025:** In March 2025 New Hope agreed to merge back into the Likud, five years after Sa'ar left it [10].

**Arab parties: Ra'am in 2021–2022, and the list's stance in 2026.** The Bennett–Lapid government that New Hope joined in June 2021 included the Arab party Ra'am, which was to receive a deputy minister in the Prime Minister's Office [24]. The Times of Israel described it as the first major Arab party to join a governing coalition, and noted that Sa'ar partnered with Ra'am in that government [89, 118]. In June 2022, after the bill on emergency regulations in Judea and Samaria had failed, Sa'ar was asked whether a political partnership with an Arab party was not possible. He answered: "It will be very sad, but that may be the conclusion if we do not succeed in passing this bill", and added: "I know that Mansour Abbas is trying to get order in Ra'am" [89]. In September 2022 he ruled out a government that relied on Hadash–Ta'al, as described above [118]. For the 2026 election New Hope runs on the Likud's list [1, 2]. On 14 September 2026 the Likud petitioned the Central Elections Committee to disqualify Ra'am and its leader Mansour Abbas from the election [119]. On 23 September 2026 the committee voted 18 to 5 to bar Ra'am, accepting the petitions of the Likud, Otzma Yehudit and a victims' organization [125]. On 2 October 2026 the Supreme Court unanimously overturned that decision, so Ra'am is running [126]. Sa'ar gave his own account on 22 August 2026, in an interview on N12's "Meet the Press". Asked about having sat with Mansour Abbas, he said: "The experiment of 2021 failed. I was part of it, and lessons have to be drawn from what happened." He said a government formed with the Joint List abstaining and with Ra'am's active support "cannot be repeated" and is "not relevant to the reality after October 7", and that the 2021 government had failed over the regulations for Judea and Samaria and the family-unification law [122].

**Change over time:** In 2021–2022 New Hope sat in a coalition that included Ra'am [24]. In 2026 it runs on a list that asked for Ra'am to be barred from the election, and Sa'ar says the 2021 arrangement cannot be repeated [1, 119, 122]. His explanation is that the 2021 government failed and that October 7 changed the situation [122]. No pledge by Sa'ar or New Hope from before the June 2021 government not to sit with Ra'am, or not to rely on it, was found in Hebrew and English searches of the 2021 campaign coverage. The one dated statement found, Shasha-Biton's of 25 March 2021, was about the Joint List, which was not part of the 2021 government; Ra'am is a separate party [123, 24, 118]. The 2021 plans that were read do not deal with coalition partners [83]. This is therefore recorded as a change over time and not as a broken pledge.

**Bills about the prime minister's office.** As justice minister in 2021–2022, Sa'ar sponsored a bill to limit a prime minister to eight years in office and drafted another to bar a person indicted for a serious crime from forming a government. The Times of Israel reported that both were seen as aimed at Netanyahu, who was then on trial. Sa'ar said the term-limit bill was not aimed at him personally, and it would not have applied to his past terms [85, 86, 90, 92]. In 2026 Sa'ar runs on a list whose name presents Netanyahu as its candidate for prime minister [2]. Neither bill became law (see Track record). No statement was found in which Sa'ar withdrew his support for either idea, so this is not labelled as a change of position; his move back to Netanyahu's side is covered by the Contradiction below.

**Contradiction:** In 2021 Sa'ar campaigned on refusing to sit in a government led by Netanyahu [25]. From September 2024 he served in Netanyahu's government, and in 2026 he runs on Netanyahu's list [7, 1, 2]. Sa'ar's explanation in September 2024 was that joining was "the patriotic and right thing to do now" and that the opposition parties' positions on the war were far from his own [7].

**Contradiction:** In December 2020, at the start of the campaign for the March 2021 election, Sa'ar told Channel 13: "I will not sit under Lapid as prime minister. He cannot unite the nation, and there is no chance I will join a government whose path is completely different than my path" [124]. In June 2021 New Hope joined a government built on a rotation agreement between Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid, in which Bennett served first as prime minister and Lapid was alternate prime minister [24]. By September 2022 Lapid was prime minister and Sa'ar was serving as justice minister in his government [118]; the Knesset's record lists Sa'ar as justice minister until the end of the 24th Knesset [23]. No statement by Sa'ar addressing the December 2020 pledge was found. In June 2022 he said that during the 2021 campaign he had spoken of "a 'change government' and a coalition composition that is different from what we were used to", and that he would make the same decision again [89].

## Track record {#track-record}

New Hope has been in government twice: in the Bennett–Lapid government (2021–2022) and in the Netanyahu government (briefly in 2023–2024 as part of National Unity, and again from September 2024) [4, 24, 7].

**The 2021–2022 government: promises and delivery.** New Hope held three ministries in the Bennett–Lapid government: Sa'ar was justice minister and deputy prime minister, Elkin was construction and housing minister and minister for Jerusalem affairs, and Yifat Shasha-Biton was education minister [23, 24]. Shasha-Biton left politics in July 2024 and is not a New Hope candidate, so her ministry is not reviewed here [1, 120]. On 20 June 2022 Bennett and Lapid announced that they would dissolve the Knesset, which left only days to finish pending bills [90]. The items below compare what the party promised with what was done.

*Justice Ministry (Sa'ar)*

- **A term limit for prime ministers. Not delivered.** In December 2020 Sa'ar said a law limiting prime ministers to eight years would be the first law a government led by him would pass (dated background: the statement was made in the campaign for the March 2021 election) [88]. Interior Minister Ayelet Shaked said in November 2021 that the bill was "included in the coalition agreement, agreed upon by all the coalition factions" [86]. Ministers approved Sa'ar's bill on 14 November 2021. It would have made a prime minister step down after eight consecutive years in office, and it did not apply to terms already served [85]. It passed its first reading on 22 November 2021 by 66 votes to 48; all six New Hope MKs who voted were in favour [87]. The Knesset's record shows that it was prepared for its final readings, but it never passed them; the Times of Israel reported that the coalition failed to muster a majority [90, 93]. On 22 June 2022 Sa'ar announced a last attempt to pass a term limit before the Knesset dissolved, and the record shows a new version tabled for a first reading [90, 93]. Sa'ar said that month: "The party leaders showed no commitment even though we were very close. We missed an opportunity for a bill that was a cornerstone for this government" [89]. The 2022 National Unity principles repeated the pledge [117].
- **Barring a person under indictment from forming a government. Not delivered.** In October 2021 Sa'ar published a draft bill under which an MK charged with an offence punishable by more than three years in prison could not be asked to form a government or serve as alternate prime minister. It was meant to take effect from the next government [92]. He said in November 2021 that he needed to convince "my friends, and especially Prime Minister Bennett"; Shaked opposed the idea, saying the attorney general "should not be allowed to decide who will be prime minister" [86]. According to the Times of Israel, Bennett blocked the bill throughout his year in office, and on 22 June 2022 he said he would vote against it [90]. On 26 June 2022 the Ministerial Committee for Legislation backed two private members' versions, and a Knesset legal adviser said that passing such a law so close to an election was "very problematic" [91]. Neither this bill nor the term limit became law: both would have amended Basic Law: The Government, and the Knesset's bill record lists only one amendment to that Basic Law enacted by the 24th Knesset, Amendment No. 10 of July 2021 [127].
- **Illegal weapons. Delivered in part.** Sa'ar described the December 2021 minimum-sentence law, proposed by New Hope's Sharren Haskel, as the fulfilment of an election promise [94]. It was passed as a temporary provision [95]. His two other crime bills, on searches without a warrant and on longer sentences for weapons offences, passed a first reading only [93, 97, 98]. Details are under "Crime in Arab society".
- **Splitting the attorney general's role. Not delivered in that term.** The Times of Israel reported in February 2022 that Sa'ar had "expressed his intention to split the attorney general role". The attorney general both advises the government and heads the state prosecution [101]. A search of the Knesset's bill database found no government bill of the 24th Knesset (2021–2022) with the attorney general in its title [102]. In September 2022 Sa'ar told Makor Rishon, when asked why he had not split the role: "in one year I did not manage to carry out my whole platform, but I managed quite a lot" [129]. In October 2025 and June 2026 the party's MKs voted for bills that split the role (see "Judicial system") [32, 33].
- **Appointments.** On 7 February 2022 the government appointed Gali Baharav-Miara as attorney general; she had Sa'ar's backing [101]. On 21 February 2022 the Judicial Selection Committee appointed four Supreme Court justices: Ruth Ronen, Khaled Kabub, Gila Kanfi-Steinitz and Yechiel Kasher [99, 100]. The Jerusalem Post described Kanfi-Steinitz and Kasher as moderate conservatives and wrote that Sa'ar had "placed his stamp on the judiciary"; Kabub is the first Muslim justice appointed permanently to the court [99]. Religious Zionism MK Simcha Rothman voted against the appointments, and Shaked voted against Ronen and abstained on Kabub [99, 100].
- **Rights of suspects and defendants. Delivered in part.** A government amendment to the Evidence Ordinance passed its final reading on 30 June 2022. It lets a court in a criminal trial reject evidence that was obtained illegally, if admitting it would substantially harm the right to a fair trial [95, 103]. A government bill for a Basic Law on rights in criminal proceedings passed its first reading on 8 March 2022 by 34 votes to 25, with the six New Hope MKs who voted in favour, and went no further [93, 103]. The 2022 National Unity principles pledged to complete it [117].
- **Emergency regulations in Judea and Samaria. Not passed.** Sa'ar's bill to renew them was rejected on 6 June 2022 by 58 votes to 52 (see "Judea and Samaria") [104].

*Construction and Housing Ministry and Jerusalem Affairs (Elkin)*

- **More planning and more land for building. Largely delivered; prices still rose.** Elkin said in June 2021 that planning had to speed up and that the fast-track planning committee should continue [62]. The committee's term was extended by law in August 2021 [95]. The October 2021 plan set targets of 280,000 housing starts and planning for 500,000 homes in 2022–2025 [105]. Starts rose to 63,300 in 2021 and to about 70,000 in the twelve months to September 2022 [109, 110]. Elkin left office in December 2022, in the first year of the plan, so the four-year targets cannot be measured against his term [111]. Home prices rose 13% in 2021, and the rise later reached a rate of about 20% a year; the Times of Israel reported that the coalition had committed to holding the 2022 rise to 6% [107, 111, 113].
- **Discounted homes. Delivered.** The 2021 plan promised a national plan for a first apartment [83]. Target Price was approved in September 2021 and its lotteries began in March 2022 [106, 107].
- **Long-term rental. Delivered in part, by Elkin's own account.** The 2021 plan promised 100,000 rental apartments within ten years [83]. Elkin says the ministry promoted long-term rental projects and that a wider reform was not completed (see "Housing") [111].
- **Other points of the 2021 housing plan.** No record was found of a single national housing ministry being set up in that term, or of how many buildings went through urban renewal [83].
- **Public housing.** Forced evictions were frozen in November 2021 while criteria were set [112]. The party's MKs voted against an opposition bill to enlarge the public-housing stock (see "Votes with the coalition" below) [82].
- **Jerusalem.** On 14 November 2021 the government approved sanctions on ministries that do not move their national units to Jerusalem, such as a ban on renovations and on buying new furniture. Elkin, as minister for Jerusalem affairs, said the decision came "at last" with timetables and sanctions [114]. No record of how many units moved afterwards was found.

*The party's 2021 plans*

- **Lower import barriers. Delivered in law.** The 2021 plans promised to let businesses import goods that meet European standards and to lower import barriers [83]. The Economic Plan Law of November 2021, which the party's MKs supported, brought import rules into line with European Union standards [115, 116]. The other steps in the cost-of-living plan (closing water corporations, a single food authority, a 25% cut in electricity prices) were not found among that government's measures in the sources reviewed.

*Votes with the coalition*

- **Coalition-line votes, 2021–2022.** While the party sat in the Bennett–Lapid coalition, its MKs who voted, among them Sharren Haskel (who has since left the party) and Yifat Shasha-Biton, voted with the coalition against opposition private bills at their preliminary readings: to increase the public-housing stock (24 November 2021, defeated 40–44), to raise child allowances (24 November 2021, defeated 38–43), to set minimum sentences for weapons offences (11 May 2022, defeated 53–61) and, in Shasha-Biton's case, on public-transport discounts for people with disabilities (15 June 2022, defeated 47–52) [82]. Sa'ar and Elkin did not vote in any of the four; they were ministers and no longer MKs [23, 121]. On 1 December 2021 New Hope MK Zvi Hauser also voted against a bill to equalise the disability allowance with the minimum wage (defeated 20–22) [84]. The plans New Hope published for the March 2021 election hold no pledge to enlarge the public-housing stock, raise child allowances, set minimum sentences for weapons offences or give such a transport discount; its housing plan deals with supply, rental and first homes [83]. Its disability plan pledged to raise disability allowances to the level set in the 2018 law and link them to the average wage, which is a smaller rise than the December 2021 bill proposed [83]. With no matching pledge, these are recorded as votes with the coalition and not as contradictions. On minimum sentences, the party's own law had been in force since December 2021, five months before it voted against the opposition's bill [94, 95]. Sa'ar gave the coalition's reason in June 2022: "We had to vote against our wishes in many cases, mostly on privately initiated bills. There have been a lot of well-intentioned social bills that benefit the citizens and we would like to vote for them, but there is coalition discipline and there are budget constraints, so we could not" [89].
- **Other votes with the coalition, 2021–2022.** The party's MKs who voted supported the 2021 and 2022 budgets and the Economic Plan Law in November 2021 [116], the first reading of the bill on searches without a warrant in December 2021 [97], and the temporary Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law, which passed its final reading on 10 March 2022 by 45 votes to 15 [104]. An earlier government request to extend the previous version of that law had failed on a tied vote of 59 to 59 on 5 July 2021, with six New Hope MKs, Sa'ar among them, voting for the extension [104]. The party's remaining votes of that term were not reviewed one by one.

**Since 2024.**

- **Judicial selection compromise.** Sa'ar co-authored the January 2025 compromise on choosing judges, and it became law on 27 March 2025 [26, 27, 28]. The commitment was delivered as law. It takes effect only when the next Knesset begins [27].
- **Coalition agreement, November 2024.** The deal made Sa'ar foreign minister, Haskel deputy foreign minister and Elkin a minister in the Finance Ministry. It also increased the Foreign Ministry's budget for public diplomacy [9]. These appointments were carried out: Sa'ar has served as foreign minister since then [23], and Elkin has headed the rebuilding of the north and south [57, 59].
- **Haredi enlistment.** Sa'ar had promised to oppose bills on Haredi enlistment that he saw as exemption measures [9]. In July 2026 the faction's remaining MKs voted for the law suspending enforcement against yeshiva students [29, 30, 31]. This led Haskel to resign from the government [13].
- **October 7 inquiry.** In opposition in 2024 the party voted for a state commission of inquiry. In government it voted against such bills in 2025 and 2026 [36, 37].
- **Party structure.** The 2025 promise to merge into the Likud was not completed. In July 2026 it was replaced by a joint run [14, 16].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

No indictments, convictions or official inquiries against Gideon Sa'ar or Ze'ev Elkin personally were found in the sources reviewed. The check covered news coverage of the party and its leaders from 2020 to 2026 and the Knesset records cited here.

**State Comptroller vote (court ruling; police investigation of affidavits).** This matter concerns the conduct of the party's Knesset faction. Of the people involved, only Sa'ar is a candidate on the 2026 list: Haskel now leads a separate party, and Buskila is not on the list [1, 13].

In June 2026 the Knesset elected Michael Rabello as state comptroller. In July 2026 the High Court of Justice annulled the election, ruling that coalition MKs who recorded their votes had breached the requirement of a secret ballot, and ordered a new vote. Rabello has not taken office [60, 66]. In an affidavit to the High Court, New Hope MK Sharren Haskel stated that the faction's MKs were told to film themselves voting for Rabello and show the videos to the prime minister. This contradicted earlier affidavits from Likud MKs, who said no such instruction was given [60, 61]. According to The Times of Israel, citing Kan, she told police that the instruction came from the party's whip, Michel Buskila. Sa'ar submitted an affidavit to the High Court calling Haskel's account "baseless" and saying he had told party members not to film their votes [60]. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara directed the police's Lahav 433 major-crimes unit to examine the conflicting affidavits for possible perjury [60]. On 25 August 2026 The Times of Israel reported that the conflicting testimonies had led to a criminal investigation into possible perjury [75]. No report names a suspect. As of 19 August 2026 the Knesset had not held the new vote ordered by the court, and the Knesset speaker said there would be no repeat election [66]. **Current status:** the police investigation of the conflicting affidavits is the latest reported step; no later report of a suspect being named, an indictment, a closed case or a new vote was found as of 12 October 2026.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Enough public information exists to describe New Hope's record. The party has sat in the Knesset since 2021 and has served in two governments. Its leader has been foreign minister since November 2024, and the Knesset's voting database records how its MKs voted [21, 29, 32, 36, 39].

Gaps:
- **No current platform.** New Hope did not publish a separate 2026 platform, because it runs on the Likud's list. The plans it published for the March 2021 election survive only in archived copies of its 2021 website, which is offline [83]. The cost-of-living plan, the five-point economic plan, the housing plan and the disability plan were read in full from copies saved from the Internet Archive on 12 October 2026; the archive itself was offline for part of that day. Later formal commitments are the principles of the National Unity list for the 2022 election, which New Hope shared with Blue and White until March 2024 [117], the 2024 coalition agreement and the 2025 merger agreement [9, 10].
- **The 2021–2022 government.** The party's record in that government is taken from Knesset vote and bill records and from news reports [87, 93, 95, 97, 103, 104, 116]. Government decision texts on gov.il could not be opened (the site refused every request on 12 October 2026), so the housing plan and the Jerusalem decision are cited from press reports [105, 114]. Elkin's account of his rental policy and of the number of homes drawn in lotteries is his own and was not checked against official figures [111]. Only the votes of that term that are named in this document were read.
- **Sources lean on the leader.** Many positions come from Sa'ar's statements as foreign minister, which may reflect government policy as much as party policy.
- **Older evidence on some issues.** On the cost of living, the evidence is the 2021 plan, a 2021 vote and the 2022 National Unity principles; no statement on prices since September 2024 was found. On housing the evidence is the 2021 plan, Elkin's record as a minister and his statements, and on crime in Arab society it is Sa'ar's record as justice minister in 2021–2022 and a few later votes.
- **Party identity on the list.** The CEC filing lists Sa'ar and Elkin on behalf of the Likud. Only three candidates are listed on behalf of New Hope [1]. One of the party's four MKs, Haskel, now leads a separate party [13].
