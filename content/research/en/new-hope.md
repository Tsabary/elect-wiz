## Overview {#overview}

**New Hope** (תקווה חדשה) is a right-wing party. Its registered name is now "New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" (תקווה חדשה הימין הממלכתי) [1]. Gideon Sa'ar founded it in December 2020, after he left the Likud. Sa'ar said at the time that the Likud "had become a tool serving the personal interests of its leader", meaning Benjamin Netanyahu [3, 4]. Several sitting lawmakers joined him, among them Ze'ev Elkin, Sharren Haskel, Michal Shir and Yifat Shasha-Biton. Two MKs from the Derech Eretz party also joined [3].

**Ideology and base.** The party describes itself as "the statesmanlike right" (הימין הממלכתי). When Sa'ar split from Benny Gantz in March 2024, he said "the voice of the statesmanlike right is more essential today than ever" [5]. The Israel Democracy Institute (IDI) places it on the right of Israeli politics [4]. In practice its record combines security positions on the right with some liberal positions on personal issues, such as LGBT rights in 2021 and medical cannabis in 2025 (see "Other notable positions") [9, 39, 55]. The documents reviewed for this research don't describe a specific demographic or community base.

**Election history and splits.**
- **2021 (24th Knesset):** New Hope ran alone. It won 209,161 votes and 6 seats [3, 4], and joined the government of Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid. Sa'ar became justice minister, Ze'ev Elkin housing minister and Yifat Shasha-Biton education minister [24].
- **2022 (25th Knesset):** In July 2022 New Hope joined Benny Gantz's Blue and White on a joint list called National Unity (HaMachane HaMamlachti). New Hope brought 4 seats to the merger and Blue and White brought 8 [5]. New Hope won 4 of the list's seats [3].
- **October 2023:** After the October 7 attack, two New Hope ministers joined Benjamin Netanyahu's emergency government as part of National Unity. They later resigned [4].
- **Split from National Unity, March 2024:** On 12 March 2024 Sa'ar took New Hope out of National Unity and demanded a seat in the war cabinet [5]. In the Knesset the party has sat as a separate faction, "the Statesmanlike Right", since 13 March 2024 [21, 22]. On 25 March 2024 Sa'ar left the coalition after he wasn't given a war-cabinet seat [6].
- **Rejoining the government, September 2024:** Sa'ar and Netanyahu announced on 29 September 2024 that New Hope would rejoin the government [7]. In November 2024 Sa'ar became foreign minister [9].
- **Merger agreement with the Likud, March 2025:** On 13 March 2025 New Hope signed an agreement to dissolve into the Likud before the next election [10]. The Likud Central Committee approved the merger on 13 August 2025 [11].
- **From merger to joint list, July 2026:** The merger was never completed in the Knesset, because the parties never sent it to the Knesset House Committee for approval [14, 15]. According to a Likud filing quoted by Srugim, on 14 July 2026 the two factions agreed to run on a joint candidate list instead of fully merging [16]. Also in July 2026, New Hope MK Sharren Haskel resigned as deputy foreign minister and launched a new party, Israel First. On 19 July 2026 she petitioned the High Court of Justice to force the merger to be completed, so that she could formally split away from the faction [13, 14, 15]. On 9 August 2026 the court, in a ruling written by Supreme Court President Yitzhak Amit, rejected the petition in full. It said that her delay in acting since March 2025 undermined her claim, and it accepted the factions' later choice of a joint run instead of a merger. The ruling records that on 14 July 2026 the heads of the two factions agreed to carry out the agreement by running together on one candidate list for the 26th Knesset, instead of merging the factions in the outgoing Knesset. Because the factions agreed on the joint run only after her petition, the court did not order her to pay costs [67].

**Current seats.** In the outgoing 25th Knesset the New Hope faction formally holds 4 seats: Ze'ev Elkin, Sharren Haskel, Michel Buskila and Akram Hasson [21, 22]. Sa'ar left the Knesset in July 2025 but remains foreign minister [20]. Haskel now leads a separate party [13, 14].

**The list it runs on (joint list).** For the October 2026 election New Hope runs on the Likud's joint list, "The Likud led by Benjamin Netanyahu for Prime Minister" (הליכוד בהנהגת בנימין נתניהו לראשות הממשלה), with the ballot letters **מחל** [2]. According to the Central Elections Committee (CEC) filing, two parties submitted the list: "Likud – National Liberal Movement" and "New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" [1]. Its partner is therefore the Likud. The history of splits between these two parties is set out above. Sa'ar left the Likud in December 2020 to found New Hope [3]. The parties then signed a merger agreement in March 2025, and in July 2026 they turned it into a joint run [10, 16].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Gideon Sa'ar (party leader).** Sa'ar is foreign minister and New Hope's chairman [60]. According to the Knesset's records, he was an MK in the 16th to 19th Knessets and the 21st to 25th Knessets. He served as education minister (2009–2013), interior minister (2013–2014) and justice minister (2021–2022), and became foreign minister in November 2024 [23, 9]. His 2021–2022 justice-ministry term was in the Bennett–Lapid government [24], and the Knesset's records also list him as deputy prime minister in that government [23]. He was also a minister without portfolio from October 2023 to March 2024 and again from September to November 2024 [23]. In January 2025, as foreign minister, he and Justice Minister Yariv Levin presented a compromise on how judges are chosen. It became law in March 2025 (see "Judicial system") [27, 28]. Sa'ar resigned from the Knesset under the "Norwegian Law" in July 2025. This law lets a minister give up a Knesset seat to the next person on the list while staying in government. His seat went to Akram Hasson [20].

**Position on the 2026 list.** The Likud secretariat approved a reserved slot on the Likud list for Sa'ar in August 2026 [17]. A reserved slot is a place on the list that the party leader assigns directly, outside the primary. The March 2025 agreement also let Sa'ar pick one more candidate for a reserved slot no lower than 15th place [10]. In August 2026 Ynetnews reported that the agreement gave New Hope two slots: one in the top five for Sa'ar and one for Michel Buskila [19]. When the list was presented on 8 September 2026, Sa'ar was 7th, Talik Gvili 9th and Bardugo 11th [18]. Gvili is the mother of Ran Gvili, a police officer who fell in battle on October 7; his body was taken to Gaza, and he was the last hostage whose remains were returned [18, 80]. Kipa gives her first name as Talik; the CEC page lists her as Tali (טלי גואילי) [1, 18].

**Who was filed on New Hope's behalf.** The CEC candidate page lists each candidate's party. It shows that **Sa'ar (#7) and Ze'ev Elkin (#32) were filed on behalf of the Likud**, not New Hope. Only **Tali Gvili (#9), Ofir Saban (#88) and Ehud Ninio (#89)** are listed "on behalf of New Hope – the Statesmanlike Right" [1]. Before the primary, the Likud had shortened its membership requirements so that Elkin could run in it [17]. Bardugo (#11) is listed on behalf of the Likud [1].

**Other figures.**
- **Ze'ev Elkin** was housing minister and minister for Jerusalem affairs in the 2021–2022 government [24]. Since late 2024 he has been the minister in the Finance Ministry responsible for rebuilding the north and the communities near Gaza [57, 58, 59]. The 2025 agreement expected him to receive a high reserved slot [10]. On the CEC list he is 32nd [1].
- **Michel Buskila** has sat in the Knesset for the faction since 5 July 2024 [21, 22]. He is the faction's whip [60].
- **Akram Hasson** is a Druze politician. He entered the Knesset in July 2025 in Sa'ar's place [20, 21].
- **Sharren Haskel** was New Hope's third-ranking member and deputy foreign minister. She resigned in July 2026 and set up Israel First [10, 13, 14].
- The New Hope leader on the joint list's top line is Sa'ar. The list itself is headed by Netanyahu (#1), Eli Cohen (#2), Amir Ohana (#3), Yariv Levin (#4) and Miri Regev (#5), all filed for the Likud [1].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

Related actions:

- **Statement:** In September 2025 MK Sharren Haskel said she supports capitalism, lower taxes and deregulation. She was then New Hope's third-ranking member [10, 12]. She has since left to found Israel First [13], so this is not a current party position.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

No New Hope platform, official decision or leadership statement was found on crime in Arab society, the Shin Bet plan, holding crime suspects without trial, or funding for Arab communities. The search covered news coverage of Sa'ar, Elkin, Buskila and Hasson and the Knesset voting database for crime-related bills between 2024 and 2026. The party's position can be read only from a few Knesset votes:

- **Action:** On 10 December 2025 the Knesset held preliminary readings on two crime bills. Elkin, Haskel and Hasson voted **against** a bill by MK Merav Cohen to combat weapons smuggling, which was rejected by 52 votes to 26 with four MKs present but not voting. They voted **for** the Special Powers to Combat Crime Organizations (Temporary Provisions) bill by Tzvika Fogel, chair of the Knesset's National Security Committee, which passed by 54 votes to 41 [39, 40, 65, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74]. Maariv described Fogel's bill as resting on declaring crime organizations to be terror organizations, after which the police and the Shin Bet could use technological and investigative tools that only the Shin Bet now has [64].
- **Action:** In May 2024, while New Hope was in opposition, Haskel voted in a preliminary reading for the bill on protecting the public from crime organizations [39, 40]. The law that passed lets courts restrict the movement and contacts of members of crime organizations, at the police's request and with the attorney general's approval [44]. No New Hope vote is recorded on its final reading in December 2024, or on a July 2026 crime-organization bill [39, 40].
- As ministers, Sa'ar and Elkin belong to the government that runs crime policy. No source was found that records how they voted on any specific crime-related government decision.

**Closest option(s):** Tentative: the evidence is thin and consists of votes only. The December 2025 vote for a bill giving the police and the Shin Bet counter-terrorism tools against crime organizations is closest to using extraordinary measures (`emergency-powers-shin-bet`). No party statement explains the vote.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Statement:** Sa'ar's line as foreign minister has been to support US President Donald Trump's plan for Gaza while insisting that Hamas disarm first. On 23 October 2025 he said "Israel is committed to working towards the success of President Trump's plan". He also said "Hamas and Islamic Jihad must lay down their arms. Gaza must be demilitarized… This is at the heart of the Trump plan. We will not compromise on it" and warned that attacks on Israeli forces "will be answered forcefully" [48].

**Statement:** On 14 July 2025 Sa'ar told Euronews that Israel has no intention of controlling Gaza in the long term and has "only security concerns" there. He said Hamas "cannot be part of the future of Gaza", but that "if Hamas is ready to lay down its arms… we can do it on a political path". He described the Palestinian Authority as still sponsoring terrorism and incitement [47].

**Statement:** In September 2026, at a press conference in Ljubljana, Sa'ar said: "We are not planning to expel or to deport anyone from [the] Gaza Strip. If someone wants to immigrate of his own will, and there is a country which is ready to accept him… we will not oppose that. But we will not force anyone against his will." [49] According to the Times of Israel, he spoke after National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir had presented a plan to encourage voluntary emigration from Gaza [49].

**Action:** In January 2025 the government approved the hostage-release and ceasefire deal by 24 votes to 8. The ministers who voted against came from Religious Zionism and Otzma Yehudit, plus Likud ministers David Amsalem and Amichai Chikli [50]. Sa'ar, then foreign minister, is not among the ministers the report names as voting against.

**Earlier statement:** When Sa'ar left the coalition in March 2024, he argued that Hamas's military power should have been destroyed "on a faster schedule". He also said that Hamas's control of aid distribution undermined the effort to bring it down [6].

**Statement:** In July 2025 Elkin did not sign a letter by 22 ministers and MKs that asked for a settlement group to be allowed to tour northern Gaza. He told Kan news that "the most painful thing for our enemy is losing land", and that making clear to Hamas that "they will lose land that they will never get back" would be "a significant pressure tool" [56]. The search found no statement by Sa'ar or Elkin calling for settlement in Gaza.

**Closest option(s):** Move forward with the US plan, with an international arrangement, while Hamas is disarmed (`advance-international-arrangement`). The repeated condition that Hamas disarm before anything else, and the refusal to give the Palestinian Authority a role, also overlap with keeping pressure until disarmament (`hold-positions-disarmament-first`). The evidence is mostly statements.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Statement:** In June 2024, while in opposition, Sa'ar said of the government's enlistment bill: "if we cannot legislate a good law, it's better not to legislate a law at all". According to the Times of Israel, he had repeatedly promised to oppose bills on Haredi enlistment that he considered exemption measures [9].

**Formal commitment / Action:** The November 2024 coalition agreement between New Hope and the Likud lets the party vote differently from the coalition on only two subjects: medical cannabis and changes to the judicial system. Bills on Haredi enlistment are **not** on that list [9].

**Action:** On 14 July 2026 the Knesset passed, 58 to 54, a temporary amendment to the Security Service Law (Amendment 28). It suspends arrest, investigation and enforcement against yeshiva students required to serve, until 30 November 2026. This applies on condition that they study at least 45 hours a week (40 for kollel students) [31]. A kollel is a yeshiva for married men. In the final reading Ze'ev Elkin, Michel Buskila and Akram Hasson voted **for** and Sharren Haskel voted **against** [29, 30]. Haskel then resigned as deputy foreign minister, saying she "can no longer support a government that harms the security of the country during wartime" [13]. The High Court of Justice froze the law the next day and struck it down on 3 September 2026 [79].

**Action:** The New Hope MKs (Elkin, Buskila, Hasson and Haskel) voted for the temporary law that extends men's mandatory service from 30 to 32 months, which passed in July 2026 [29, 30, 31].

**Action:** Elkin, Buskila and Hasson voted for "Basic Law: Torah Study", which passed its final reading on 13 July 2026. It declares Torah study "a foundational value" of the Jewish people and the state. Haskel voted against it in earlier readings [29, 30, 35].

**Change over time:** In January 2022, when New Hope sat in the Bennett–Lapid coalition and Sa'ar was justice minister, the government's enlistment bill passed its first reading 51–48 with the votes of New Hope MKs, among them Haskel; Sa'ar and Elkin are not recorded as voting. On 11 June 2024, when the party was in the opposition, Sa'ar, Elkin and Haskel voted against reviving the same bill, which the Knesset did by 63–57 [81]. The two votes were cast in different Knessets, the first as part of the coalition and the second in the opposition, with the October 7 attack and the war in between. Sa'ar's stated view in June 2024 was that it is better to pass no law than a bad one [9].

**Change over time:** In June 2024, in opposition, Sa'ar promised to oppose enlistment bills he saw as exemption measures and said a bad law was worse than none [9]. In the November 2024 coalition agreement the party gave up the right to vote differently from the coalition on enlistment bills [9]. In July 2026 the New Hope MKs who remained with him voted for the law suspending arrests of yeshiva students who were required to serve [29, 30, 31]. Sa'ar himself was no longer an MK and didn't vote [20]. The votes followed the coalition line set in that agreement, 20 months after the party had publicly given up its earlier position.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. Sa'ar's 2024 statements are closest to requiring service with sanctions (`universal-service-with-sanctions`). The faction's 2026 votes, for suspending enforcement against yeshiva students and for the Torah-study basic law, are closer to `anchor-torah-study-exemption`. Per the evidence ranking, the more recent actions carry more weight.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

No current New Hope platform or party decision on how to make housing cheaper was found. The search covered a platform, news coverage of Elkin (housing minister in 2021–2022) and Sa'ar, and the Knesset voting database. The documented record comes from Elkin's time as a minister:

- **Statement:** In June 2021, shortly after becoming housing minister, Elkin warned that housing prices would keep rising. He said "we must get back to a higher rate of housing unit planning", backed extending the work of the National Committee for Planning and Building Preferred Housing Sites (a fast-track planning committee), and supported continuing National Outline Plan 38, which allows extra floors on buildings reinforced against earthquakes. He also said local authorities needed to know that promised transport projects would be built. According to Globes, the government had committed in the coalition talks to approving the planning of 300,000 housing units [62].
- **Action:** Ze'ev Elkin was housing minister in 2021–2022 [24]. The sources reviewed don't document which housing programs he carried out.
- **Action:** As the Finance Ministry minister for rebuilding the north and south, Elkin led a government plan approved on 16 April 2026. It invests nearly NIS 1 billion over five years in the Golan Heights, including housing construction, and turns Katzrin into a city. He said its aim was "to bring in thousands of new families" [59]. He also heads the directorate for rebuilding the north after the war [57]. In March 2025 he backed the Tekuma law, which commits state support to the Gaza border area through 2028 [58]. These steps develop outlying regions, but they aren't presented as a housing-price policy.

**Closest option(s):** Tentative: the evidence is thin and comes from Elkin as a minister, not from a party platform. His 2021 statements are closest to speeding up planning and construction (`speed-up-planning-and-construction`). His 2026 Golan plan also fits developing the periphery (`develop-periphery-and-transport`).

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Statement:** On 10 March 2026, during the war with Iran, Sa'ar said: "We are not looking for an endless war". He also said Israel would continue "until the minute that we and our partners think that is appropriate to stop", in coordination with the United States. He described the aim as removing "existential threats from Iran to Israel" for the long term by targeting Iran's nuclear and missile capabilities, and, according to Al-Monitor, creating conditions for regime change [51].

**Statement:** On 2 September 2026 Sa'ar told France 24 that Israel "will not allow" Iran to achieve nuclear weapons. He warned that an Iranian attack would bring a "severe" response [52].

**Statement:** On Lebanon, Sa'ar said in January 2026 that the Lebanese government's and army's efforts to disarm Hezbollah were "far from sufficient… in light of Hezbollah's efforts to rearm and rebuild, with Iranian support" [53]. In July 2026 he announced a further round of talks with Lebanon in Rome. It continued a US-brokered framework agreement that provides for disarming Hezbollah and a gradual Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon. Israel ties its withdrawal to progress in dismantling Hezbollah's military capabilities [54].

**Statement:** On 30 September 2024 Sa'ar defended the government's escalation against Hezbollah in a Knesset speech. He told the opposition that if its position had been accepted, "Hassan Nasrallah would be alive, and with him, all of Hezbollah's command" [8].

**Closest option(s):** Keep up military pressure while negotiating US-backed deals, such as disarming Hezbollah, and withdraw only once security guarantees are in place (`force-plus-us-backed-agreements`).

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Statement:** On 30 April 2026 Sa'ar said Israel does not intend to apply sovereignty in Judea and Samaria "at least in the coming months — it is against the path of President Trump's plan". He added that "implementing sovereignty, we will need to do only with the coordination with the United States of America", and that "for now… President Trump doesn't support that". In the same briefing he said the government opposes a Palestinian state and is advancing measures on the ground in the settlements [45].

**Statement:** In September 2026, after the UK moved toward sanctions over the E1 settlement project, Sa'ar warned: "If Britain acts against Israel, Israel will act against Britain." [46] E1 is a plan of about 3,000 homes east of Jerusalem.

**Statement:** In July 2025 Sa'ar described the Palestinian Authority as still sponsoring terrorism and incitement [47].

**Action (Knesset votes):**
- On 23 July 2025 all four New Hope MKs (Elkin, Haskel, Buskila and Hasson) voted for the summary motion of coalition whip Ofir Katz, which was adopted, in a Knesset debate titled "Applying sovereignty in Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley" [32, 33].
- On 22 January 2025 Buskila voted **against** a preliminary reading of a bill to apply Israeli sovereignty in the Jordan Valley [32, 33].
- On 31 December 2025 Elkin and Hasson voted **against** a preliminary reading of a bill to apply sovereignty to the settlement of Beitar Illit [32, 33].
- No New Hope MK's vote is recorded on the 22 October 2025 preliminary readings of bills to apply sovereignty to Judea and Samaria and to Ma'ale Adumim [32, 33].

**Contradiction:** The New Hope MKs voted in July 2025 for the motion adopted in the debate on applying sovereignty in Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley [32, 33]. But in December 2025 they voted against a bill to apply sovereignty to Beitar Illit [32, 33]. It was a bill by Yisrael Beytenu MKs, which was in the opposition, and the New Hope MKs voted with the coalition [77, 78, 74]. Sa'ar has said sovereignty would be applied only in coordination with the United States and not in the coming months [45].

**Change over time:** In January 2025, before the July 2025 motion, the New Hope MKs had voted with the coalition against a Yisrael Beytenu bill to apply sovereignty to the Jordan Valley [32, 33, 77, 78, 74].

**Closest option(s):** Keep the current situation, with no Palestinian state and continued settlement, and no sovereignty for now (`maintain-current-situation`). Sa'ar describes sovereignty as a possible later step, coordinated with the United States [45].

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Action:** Sa'ar co-authored the compromise with Justice Minister Yariv Levin on the Judicial Selection Committee, the body that picks judges. It was presented on 9 January 2025 [28]. Under it, the committee is made up of three coalition representatives, one opposition representative, three judges and two lawyers, one chosen by the coalition and one by the opposition. According to Globes, a Supreme Court appointment needs support from both the coalition and the opposition [28]. The Knesset passed it on 27 March 2025, after the opposition boycotted the vote, and it takes effect when the next Knesset begins [27]. It was passed as two laws: the amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary (Amendment No. 4), by 67 votes to 1 with two MKs present but not voting, and the Courts Law (Amendment No. 105), by 64 votes to none with one MK present but not voting. Sa'ar, Elkin, Haskel and Buskila voted for both [26, 68, 69]. The Times of Israel reported that the March 2025 merger agreement cost Sa'ar his freedom to vote differently from the coalition on judicial legislation [10].

**Action:** On 15 July 2026 the Knesset passed the Government Legal Adviser Law (Legal Opinions, Representation and Supervision). Under this law the government may decide that the attorney general's opinions are not binding, except in criminal matters. It may also hire other lawyers to represent its position in court. The law takes effect on 1 January 2027 [34]. Elkin, Hasson, Buskila and Haskel voted for it in the final reading [32, 33].

**Action:** In October 2025 all four New Hope MKs voted in a preliminary reading for a bill to split the attorney general's role [32, 33]. On 2 June 2026 they voted for the Attorney General and Chief Prosecutor bill in its first reading [32, 33].

**Statement:** According to the Jerusalem Post, Sa'ar and Levin pitched their 2025 compromise as "evolutionary and not revolutionary" [63].

**Closest option(s):** The party's votes since 2025 support continuing the changes, giving elected officials more say over judicial appointments and making the attorney general's opinions non-binding (`continue-judicial-changes`). The judicial-selection law itself was framed as a compromise with an opposition veto over Supreme Court appointments [27, 28].

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Action (in opposition, 2024):** On 17 July 2024, while New Hope was in opposition, Sa'ar, Elkin, Haskel and Buskila voted **for** two bills to set up a state commission of inquiry into the October 7 attack. A state commission is the type whose members are picked by the President of the Supreme Court [36, 37].

**Action (in government, 2025–2026):** After rejoining the coalition, every New Hope MK who voted on similar bills voted **against** them (not every MK voted on every bill):
- a bill on when a state commission of inquiry must be set up, on 19 March 2025;
- three state-commission bills on 12 November 2025;
- three more on 1 July 2026 [36, 37].

**Action:** The coalition's bill, by Likud MK Ariel Kallner, proposes a commission whose members are picked through the Knesset. If the opposition does not cooperate, the Knesset speaker picks them [38]. On 24 December 2025 Buskila voted for this bill in its preliminary reading. No vote by Elkin, Haskel or Hasson is recorded [29, 30].

**Statement:** According to the Times of Israel, Ze'ev Elkin cast the only vote against the Kallner bill when the Ministerial Committee for Legislation backed it in December 2025. He warned that if the opposition boycotted, "the coalition would appoint all members of the commission of inquiry, which… would be no different than establishing a government commission of inquiry" [38].

**Contradiction:** In July 2024, in opposition, the party's MKs (Sa'ar included) voted for state commission of inquiry bills [36, 37]. From 2025 they voted against such bills, and one of them (Buskila) voted for the coalition's commission appointed through the Knesset [29, 30, 36, 37]. The change followed the party's move from the opposition into the coalition in September 2024 [7]; no party statement explaining it was found.

**Disagreement inside the party:** Elkin opposed the coalition's Knesset-appointed commission bill [38], while Buskila voted for it [29, 30].

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. The party's votes since 2025 are closest to `commission-appointed-by-knesset-balance`, although Elkin objected to a design that lets the coalition pick all members. Its 2024 votes in opposition matched `state-commission-under-existing-law`.

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

**Action:** Elkin, Buskila and Hasson voted for "Basic Law: Torah Study", which declares Torah study a foundational value. It passed its final reading on 13 July 2026. Haskel voted against it in earlier readings [29, 30, 35].

**Action:** On 19 November 2025 Elkin, Haskel and Hasson voted **against** two bills to run public transport on Shabbat, in their preliminary reading [39, 40].

**Action:** On 24 March 2026 Elkin, Hasson and Buskila voted for the Religious Courts (Arbitration) Law in its final reading [39, 40]. The law lets rabbinical and Sharia courts act as arbitrators in civil disputes under religious law, when all parties agree in writing. Family, criminal and administrative matters are excluded. Arbitration decisions cannot override laws protecting fundamental rights such as gender equality [41].

**Statement:** In January 2021 New Hope said it would act for LGBT rights "in deeds, as part of a liberal worldview". On marriage equality, Sa'ar said the party would "hold discussions" before announcing a position [55]. No later position on civil marriage was found.

**Closest option(s):** Mostly keeping the current arrangements (`keep-status-quo`). The 2026 votes for the Torah-study basic law and religious-court arbitration lean toward a larger role for religion (`strengthen-jewish-character`). The evidence is mixed.

### Rebuilding the north and the Gaza border area {#other-north-south-rehabilitation}

**Action:** Ze'ev Elkin, as Finance Ministry minister, heads the directorate for rebuilding the north. He said "everything that existed prior to October 7 must be restored", and proposed using the moment to expand the region, for example with universities [57]. He backed the Tekuma law, which commits state support to the Gaza border area through 2028 [58], and led the April 2026 Golan development plan [59].

### Length of military service {#other-military-service-length}

**Action:** The New Hope MKs voted for the July 2026 temporary law extending men's regular service from 30 to 32 months through June 2029, with extra payments for affected soldiers [29, 30, 31].

### Druze representation {#other-druze-community}

**Statement:** When Sa'ar left the Knesset in July 2025 so that Akram Hasson could take his seat, he said it was "very important" to him "to strengthen the representation in the Knesset of the loyal and courageous Druze community, with whom we share a destiny" [20].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Current stance.** New Hope runs on the Likud's joint list, whose name presents Benjamin Netanyahu as the candidate for prime minister [2]. Its leader holds a reserved slot on that list [17, 18]. No separate New Hope statement was found about which other parties it would or wouldn't sit with after the 2026 election. When the merger was signed in March 2025, Sa'ar said his faction "never abandoned the right-wing worldview" [10].

**Record of keeping coalition pledges.**
- **2021:** On election night in March 2021, Sa'ar said he would keep working "to establish a government of change". He had refused to join any government led by Netanyahu [25]. He kept this pledge after the 2021 election: New Hope joined the Bennett–Lapid government [24].
- **2023–2024:** After the October 7 attack, New Hope's ministers joined Netanyahu's emergency government as part of National Unity [4]. On 29 September 2024 Sa'ar rejoined Netanyahu's government. He called it "the patriotic and right thing to do now" and said opposition parties' positions on the war were "different, even far from my own" [7]. Netanyahu said that "since October 7 we have put all the grudges of the past behind us" [7].
- **2025:** In March 2025 New Hope agreed to merge back into the Likud, five years after Sa'ar left it [10].

**Contradiction:** In 2021 Sa'ar campaigned on refusing to sit in a government led by Netanyahu [25]. From September 2024 he served in Netanyahu's government, and in 2026 he runs on Netanyahu's list [7, 1, 2]. Sa'ar's explanation in September 2024 was that joining was "the patriotic and right thing to do now" and that the opposition parties' positions on the war were far from his own [7].

## Track record {#track-record}

New Hope has been in government twice: in the Bennett–Lapid government (2021–2022) and in the Netanyahu government (briefly in 2023–2024 as part of National Unity, and again from September 2024) [4, 24, 7].

- **Justice ministry, 2021–2022.** Sa'ar served as justice minister [24]. The sources reviewed didn't allow a reliable item-by-item comparison of his 2021 promises with what was delivered in that period.
- **Coalition-line votes, 2021–2022.** While the party sat in the Bennett–Lapid coalition, its MKs who voted, among them Sharren Haskel (who has since left the party) and Yifat Shasha-Biton, voted with the coalition against opposition private bills at their preliminary readings: to increase the public-housing stock (24 November 2021, defeated 40–44), to raise child allowances (24 November 2021, defeated 38–43), to set minimum sentences for weapons offences (11 May 2022, defeated 53–61) and, in Shasha-Biton's case, on public-transport discounts for people with disabilities (15 June 2022, defeated 47–52) [82]. Sa'ar and Elkin are not recorded as voting in any of the four. On 1 December 2021 New Hope MK Zvi Hauser also voted against a bill to equalise the disability allowance with the minimum wage (defeated 20–22) [84]. The plans New Hope published for the March 2021 election hold no pledge to enlarge the public-housing stock, raise child allowances, set minimum sentences for weapons offences or give such a transport discount; its housing plan deals with supply, rental and first homes [83]. Its disability plan pledged to raise disability allowances to the level set in the 2018 law and link them to the average wage, which is a smaller rise than the December 2021 bill proposed [83]. With no matching pledge, these are recorded as votes with the coalition and not as contradictions. The party's other 2021–2022 votes were not reviewed.
- **Judicial selection compromise.** Sa'ar co-authored the January 2025 compromise on choosing judges, and it became law on 27 March 2025 [26, 27, 28]. The commitment was delivered as law. It takes effect only when the next Knesset begins [27].
- **Coalition agreement, November 2024.** The deal made Sa'ar foreign minister, Haskel deputy foreign minister and Elkin a minister in the Finance Ministry. It also increased the Foreign Ministry's budget for public diplomacy [9]. These appointments were carried out: Sa'ar has served as foreign minister since then [23], and Elkin has headed the rebuilding of the north and south [57, 59].
- **Haredi enlistment.** Sa'ar had promised to oppose bills on Haredi enlistment that he saw as exemption measures [9]. In July 2026 the faction's remaining MKs voted for the law suspending enforcement against yeshiva students [29, 30, 31]. This led Haskel to resign from the government [13].
- **October 7 inquiry.** In opposition in 2024 the party voted for a state commission of inquiry. In government it voted against such bills in 2025 and 2026 [36, 37].
- **Party structure.** The 2025 promise to merge into the Likud was not completed. In July 2026 it was replaced by a joint run [14, 16].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

No indictments, convictions or official inquiries against Gideon Sa'ar or Ze'ev Elkin personally were found in the sources reviewed. The check covered news coverage of the party and its leaders from 2020 to 2026 and the Knesset records cited here.

**State Comptroller vote (court ruling; police investigation of affidavits).** This matter concerns the conduct of the party's Knesset faction. Of the people involved, only Sa'ar is a candidate on the 2026 list: Haskel now leads a separate party, and Buskila is not on the list [1, 13].

In June 2026 the Knesset elected Michael Rabello as state comptroller. In July 2026 the High Court of Justice annulled the election, ruling that coalition MKs who recorded their votes had breached the requirement of a secret ballot, and ordered a new vote. Rabello has not taken office [60, 66]. In an affidavit to the High Court, New Hope MK Sharren Haskel stated that the faction's MKs were told to film themselves voting for Rabello and show the videos to the prime minister. This contradicted earlier affidavits from Likud MKs, who said no such instruction was given [60, 61]. According to The Times of Israel, citing Kan, she told police that the instruction came from the party's whip, Michel Buskila. Sa'ar submitted an affidavit to the High Court calling Haskel's account "baseless" and saying he had told party members not to film their votes [60]. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara directed the police's Lahav 433 major-crimes unit to examine the conflicting affidavits for possible perjury [60]. On 25 August 2026 The Times of Israel reported that the conflicting testimonies had led to a criminal investigation into possible perjury [75]. No report names a suspect. As of 19 August 2026 the Knesset had not held the new vote ordered by the court, and the Knesset speaker said there would be no repeat election [66]. **Current status:** the police investigation of the conflicting affidavits is the latest reported step; no later report of a suspect being named, an indictment, a closed case or a new vote was found as of 12 October 2026.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Enough public information exists to describe New Hope's record. The party has sat in the Knesset since 2021 and has served in two governments. Its leader has been foreign minister since November 2024, and the Knesset's voting database records how its MKs voted [21, 29, 32, 36, 39].

Gaps:
- **No current platform.** New Hope did not publish a separate 2026 platform, because it runs on the Likud's list. The plans it published for the March 2021 election survive only in archived copies of its 2021 website, which is offline [83]; they are used here only to check its 2021–2022 votes. Formal commitments are therefore limited to the 2024 coalition agreement and the 2025 merger agreement [9, 10].
- **Sources lean on the leader.** Many positions come from Sa'ar's statements as foreign minister, which may reflect government policy as much as party policy.
- **Few documented positions on some issues.** No documented party position on the cost of living was found. On housing and crime in Arab society the evidence is limited to a few votes and to Elkin's statements and actions as a minister.
- **Party identity on the list.** The CEC filing lists Sa'ar and Elkin on behalf of the Likud. Only three candidates are listed on behalf of New Hope [1]. One of the party's four MKs, Haskel, now leads a separate party [13].
