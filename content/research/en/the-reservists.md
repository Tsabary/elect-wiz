## Overview {#overview}

**Founding.** The Reservists (HaMiluimnikim, המילואימניקים) is a party founded in 2025 and led by former communications minister Yoaz Hendel [1]. The Registrar of Parties announced its registration on September 18, 2025 [2, 3]. It grew out of a civic movement of the same name that Hendel set up after leaving politics, during the war that began on October 7, 2023, which campaigned for military service by everyone and penalties for draft evasion [3, 66]. Among its founders were leading figures in the serving organizations' campaign against the bill to exempt yeshiva students, which the party calls an "evasion law" [2]. The party says its members are reservists, reservists' families, wounded soldiers, bereaved families and civilian volunteers [2]. Its website says the movement helped found a "coalition of serving organizations" that campaigned in and outside the Knesset against a bill to regulate the military service of yeshiva students, which the party calls an "evasion law" [4]. Likud asked the Registrar of Parties to bar the name "The Reservists", arguing that it could mislead the public; on December 4, 2025 the Registrar rejected the objection and approved the name [5].

**Stated goals.** The goals the party registered with the Registrar of Parties are: promoting Zionist values (settlement, absorbing immigrants and defending the homeland); military or national service for every citizen; balance between the branches of government, including changing the system of government and promoting a constitution; national unity and respectful discussion; and making the Knesset worthy of the spirit of the people and its great sacrifice [1]. At its founding the party named three conditions it would demand before any government is formed: a "Zionist government", "service for all", and a commission of inquiry into the events of October 7 [2].

**Ideology and base.** Hendel describes himself and the party as right-wing. He defines "right-wing" as serving in the army, defending and holding the land, and supporting a free economy and competition, not as loyalty to one leader [6]. The party opposes a Palestinian state and supports settlement in Judea and Samaria [6, 7]. It wants a government made up only of "Zionist" parties, meaning one without Haredi parties and without Arab parties [8, 9]. When it was founded in 2025, The Times of Israel described it as a "right-of-center party" [3]. In August 2026 the Jerusalem Post wrote that the alliance Hendel then led with Chili Tropper had "not identified itself as Left or Right but has aligned itself with center-right-leaning policies" [7]. Its appeal is aimed at people who serve in the army and reserves and their families [2, 4].

**Current seats.** The Reservists holds no Knesset seats. It was founded in 2025 and is running in a Knesset election for the first time in October 2026 [1, 2].

**Joint list.** The party runs on a joint list with Yaron Zelekha's New Economic Party (הכלכלית החדשה), called "The Reservists and the Economic Party led by Yoaz Hendel and Yaron Zelekha" (המילואימניקים והכלכלית בראשות יועז הנדל וירון זליכה). The list was submitted to the Central Elections Committee (CEC) by both parties [10] and runs under the ballot letters די [11]. Hendel and Zelekha announced the joint run on September 6, 2026, a few days before the deadline for submitting lists. They agreed on two basic principles: joining only a "Zionist government", and demanding that a law requiring military service for all be passed before a government is formed [9, 12]. Hendel said the partnership rests mainly on two issues: service for all (his focus) and lowering the cost of living (Zelekha's focus) [9]. Zelekha, a former accountant general at the Finance Ministry, focuses on economic reforms [13]. Zelekha described the arrangement as "a technical bloc between two parties", but said the connection between them is "not technical at all" [14].

**Split history.** The Reservists and the New Economic Party have not run together before, so there is no history of the two splitting up after an election. Before this partnership, the Reservists had a different short-lived alliance. On July 7, 2026, Hendel and former minister Chili Tropper announced a joint run under the name "Zionist Home – The Reservists" [12, 15]. On September 6, 2026, two months later and before any election, the two announced they would go "separate political paths"; Tropper joined Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar list, and Hendel teamed up with Zelekha the same day [12, 16]. Hendel himself has been part of several political alliances that split up (see [Leadership](#leadership) and [Coalition stance](#coalition)).

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

The list's top candidates, in CEC order, are: 1. Yoaz Hendel (The Reservists), 2. Yaron Zelekha (New Economic Party), 3. Einat Wilf (The Reservists), 4. Haviv Volibovich (New Economic Party), 5. Yoav Shlomo Adomi (The Reservists), 6. Ofir Shai Langman (New Economic Party), 7. Shlomi Damari (The Reservists), 8. Lior Gilboa (New Economic Party), 9. Tehila Peretz (The Reservists), 10. Shalom Biton (New Economic Party), 11. Amalia Keinan (The Reservists). The places alternate between the two parties [10].

**Yoaz Hendel (No. 1, party leader).**
- **Background.** Hendel grew up in the religious-Zionist community and lives in Nes Harim, a moshav where religious and secular residents live together [21]. He is a battalion commander in the reserves [18]. In 2024 he said he had returned from more than 120 days of reserve duty in Gaza [19], and by late 2025 he said he had served more than 500 days of reserve duty since October 2023 [20]. Earlier in his career he headed public diplomacy (hasbara) in the Prime Minister's Office under Benjamin Netanyahu [21, 22].
- **Knesset and government.** Knesset records show Hendel was a Knesset member in the 21st, 22nd, 23rd and 24th Knessets (April 2019 to June 2021). He was elected on the Blue and White list, sat in the Derekh Eretz faction from March 29, 2020, and in the New Hope faction in 2021. He served as communications minister from May 17 to December 16, 2020, and again from June 13, 2021 to December 29, 2022 [23]. His Knesset membership ended on June 15, 2021, while he continued as a minister [23].
- **Party history.** In March 2020 Hendel and Zvi Hauser, both elected on the Blue and White list, refused to back a minority government that would have relied on outside support from the Joint List, and set up the Derekh Eretz faction [23, 24, 25]. In December 2020 Derekh Eretz joined Gideon Sa'ar's new party, New Hope, and backed Sa'ar for prime minister; Benny Gantz then removed Hendel as communications minister [26, 27]. In 2022 Hendel formed the Zionist Spirit alliance with Ayelet Shaked. It broke up on September 11, 2022, after Hendel said he would not support a narrow Netanyahu-led government [28, 29]. Two days later Hendel announced he was taking a break from political life, and he did not run in that election [66]. Walla described the 2026 alliance with Tropper as his sixth political partnership [15]. Hendel has said a party is "only a platform" for his views and that he is "proud of [his] ideological stubbornness" [22].
- **Notable work as minister.** In September 2020 the government approved his plan for building fiber-optic internet networks, including a fund paid for by the phone and internet companies to finance networks in outlying areas [30]. In April 2022 he ordered that users of "kosher phone" lines (phones approved by a Haredi rabbinic committee) could keep their numbers when switching companies or moving to a smartphone; Haredi leaders opposed the move [31].
- **Legislation.** Knesset records list him as the first sponsor of eight private bills in the 22nd to 24th Knessets (some of them the same bill re-submitted in a new Knesset). They include bills on information-sharing between security agencies and terror victims, a special exam date for students on reserve duty, the status of mixed religious-secular state schools, and adding serious human-rights violations as a consideration in licensing defense exports [32]. None of them became law: the records list each one as tabled for a preliminary debate or as stopped [32].
- **Party leadership.** On June 8, 2026 the party's elected leadership of about 40 people elected Hendel as party chair, and party members chose the Knesset list in primaries on June 16, 2026 [33, 34].

**Yaron Zelekha (No. 2, New Economic Party leader).** Zelekha is a former accountant general at the Finance Ministry who focuses on economic reform and the cost of living [13]. He is the joint-list partner and leads a separate party; his own record is covered in that party's research.

**Einat Wilf (No. 3, The Reservists).** Wilf was a Knesset member from 2010 to 2013 for Labor and then the Independence party [35]. She joined the Reservists on September 7, 2026, and was given third place on the joint list [35, 36, 37]. Kipa described her as the chair of the "Oz" party, known in recent years for her work presenting Israel's case abroad and her views on security, Zionism and the Israeli-Palestinian conflict [36]. In October 2025 the Reservists had proposed her as a possible member of an October 7 commission of inquiry [38].

**Yoav Shlomo Adomi (No. 5, The Reservists).** A founder of the party and one of the leaders of the serving organizations' campaign. The party's page describes him as a deputy battalion commander in Battalion 8111 of the 5th Brigade who has served about 550 reserve days since October 7, 2023, and as a vice president for business development and innovation [39].

**Shlomi Damari (No. 7, The Reservists).** A reservist who says he served about 500 days during the war. His younger brother Dan was killed at the Nova festival on October 7, 2023. He has been active in the Knesset on behalf of reservists and wounded soldiers [34, 40].

**Tehila Peretz (No. 9, The Reservists).** A former casualty officer who accompanied wounded soldiers and their families for about 700 days during the war; she leads the party's work on mental health and wounded soldiers [41].

**Amalia Keinan (No. 11, The Reservists).** Former head of the emergency response team in Yonatan, a community in the Golan Heights, and a supply-chain manager at a startup [42].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

**Formal commitment:** The party's platform says Israel's strength rests on a free economy and on encouraging competition. It promises to fight monopolies "by breaking up or limiting the power of dominant bodies", to set up a civilian task force with legal powers to remove barriers to doing business, and to have the government announce three measurable economic reforms each year and report on them to the Knesset Economy Committee [8]. It also proposes shifting budgets "from the public that evades service to the public that serves", and using military or national service as a main criterion for discounts on daycare, mortgages, municipal tax (arnona), the subsidized-housing program and income-tax credits [8].

**Formal commitment:** The August 2026 "Shield of David" (Magen David) plan, presented with former partner Chili Tropper, committed to lowering the cost of living by encouraging competition, breaking up monopolies and concentration, giving targeted support to working parents and reservists, reducing regulation, opening the economy to imports and removing trade barriers, and cutting insurance premiums by fighting car theft [43, 44, 45].

**Statement:** On forming the joint list in September 2026, Hendel said his partnership with Zelekha joins "Yaron Zelekha, who comes to break the monopolies that make it expensive to live here," with "the Reservists, who fight the political monopolies" [46]. Zelekha, the joint-list partner, said on September 16, 2026 that the list would demand the Finance Ministry, and that his first steps would be cancelling customs duties and import quotas, barring local monopolies from controlling imports, and re-enacting a law against monopolies charging excessive prices [14]. In October 2026 Hendel tied the cost of living to reservists, saying the people who serve hundreds of reserve days are the same people struggling with mortgages, housing and supermarket prices [6].

**Closest option(s):** Open the market to more competition and imports, and break up dominant companies (`open-market-competition-imports`), with targeted help for people who serve (an element of `direct-support-for-households` aimed only at those who serve).

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

**Formal commitment:** The party calls crime in Arab society "the eighth front" and published a "10-point plan" for it [47]. The plan includes: a new Shin Bet division to fight crime in Arab society, making this one of the Shin Bet's core missions with intelligence and operational units; local police forces under mayors, supervised by the national police and with full policing powers, including the power to demolish illegal buildings; minimum sentences and treating shootings, illegal weapons and agricultural crime as serious offenses; a new authority in the National Security Ministry to coordinate the police, Shin Bet, Finance and Interior ministries against economic crime, money laundering, illegal construction and illegal weapons; more judges in local courts and special courts for weapons and agricultural crime; a NIS 50,000 grant for discharged soldiers who serve two years in the police; an amendment to the "Dromi law" (Israel's law on self-defense against intruders) stating that any intrusion at night into the private property "of a citizen carrying a weapon" counts as a danger to life, allowing action in self-defense; enforcement against polygamy; and a national goal of settlement and a Jewish majority in the Negev and Galilee, including new Druze communities [47]. The general platform also says the Shin Bet should lead enforcement "until the police are rebuilt", calls for treating shooting incidents, illegal weapons and agricultural crime "as terror", and calls for more police stations in the Negev, the Galilee and Arab society [8].

**Formal commitment:** The August 2026 plan likewise promised a Shin Bet division against crime in Arab society and a governance authority under the National Security Ministry [43, 45]. In August 2026 the party, then allied with Tropper, said it would demand the National Security Ministry for Hendel [7, 44].

**Statement:** Hendel said in August 2026 that "there is a Wild West in the Arab sector" and that one of his main goals is "to dismantle the autonomies" [44]. In July 2026 he said his biggest fear is Israel breaking up into "autonomies without governance", pointing to the Negev and the Galilee [22].

The party's published plans do not mention holding suspects without trial, and they do not include a separate development-investment plan for Arab communities.

**Closest option(s):** Use extraordinary measures, bringing the Shin Bet into the fight against crime organizations (`emergency-powers-shin-bet`). The party's plan also adds many police resources, but it has not addressed detention without trial.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Formal commitment:** The party's platform says "a terror state will not rise again on our doorstep" and calls for: full Israeli control along the perimeter, on the high ground overlooking Israeli communities and on the Philadelphi corridor (the strip along the Gaza-Egypt border); a "Judea and Samaria model" of freedom of action, meaning the army keeps the right to make arrests, destroy infrastructure and stop attacks inside Gaza because "our enemies' nature is to break agreements"; "exacting a price" in the perimeter up to the "yellow line", where the land would be farmed by the Israeli communities near Gaza; and a government office for voluntary emigration "to allow Gaza residents who want to, to emigrate to other countries" [8]. The platform says Israel should rely only on itself for security [8].

**Statement:** In March 2024, during the war, Hendel proposed moving Gaza's civilian population into a secured, closed tent city with screening, so that the army could fight in Rafah; Walla described the plan as moving about 1.5 million Gaza residents into a secured compound inside Israeli territory, in the south of the country. He said the goal was "destroying Hamas's center of mass and demilitarizing the Strip" [19]. In April 2025 he wrote that to prevent another massacre Israel must "sit there, create a buffer and exact a price", and "take territory" [48]. In October 2026 he said Israel must keep holding strategic points and high ground, which requires enough soldiers [6].

The Jerusalem Post reported in September 2026 that no detailed Gaza policy had been published for the joint list [49]. The party's platform does not address the US plan's international force or a new Palestinian administration, and does not call for rebuilding Israeli communities inside Gaza.

**Closest option(s):** Keep Israeli forces in place and keep security in Israel's own hands, without relying on foreign forces (`hold-positions-disarmament-first`), combined with encouraging voluntary emigration, an element of `resume-fighting-full-control`. The evidence is mixed, because the party has not said whether it supports going back to full-scale war.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

This is the party's central issue.

**Formal commitment:** The platform says every Israeli citizen ("secular and religious, Jews and Arabs") will serve at 18, in the army or in civilian service, under "an equal enlistment law for all" [8]. It calls for one enlistment authority that sorts people into army or civilian service, service frameworks suited to the Haredi way of life, yeshivas on the front lines, more national-service tracks for Arab society, and temporary exemptions only after a test for outstanding Torah scholars, outstanding academics and top athletes [8]. The sanctions it lists for people who do not serve are: losing the right to vote and to be elected, losing tax and arnona discounts, losing state allowances, losing a driver's license, a ban on leaving the country and exclusion from public-sector jobs; education budgets would go only to institutions whose students serve [8]. In September 2026 the joint list told Ynet that every citizen would report to the enlistment office, with army service given priority, and that, apart from medical exemptions, those who do not serve would face sanctions and lose state benefits [50].

**Formal commitment:** Passing a law requiring service for all before a government is formed is one of the two founding conditions of the joint list [12, 46]. The party also limits party membership to people who have done military or national service [5, 51].

**Statement:** Hendel said in November 2025 that whoever chooses not to serve "chooses to be a second-class citizen" and would get nothing from the state and could not run for the Knesset [5, 51]. He called the exemption bill advanced in 2025 by Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee chair Boaz Bismuth "a catastrophe" and "an evasion law" [5, 20]. In October 2026 he said that institutions that teach students not to serve "should not get a shekel from the state", and suggested yeshivas in the Jordan Valley and on the Egyptian border that combine Torah study with service [6]. In August 2026 he pointed to separate frameworks for Haredi soldiers, such as the Netzah Yehuda battalion and the Hashmonaim Brigade, as working solutions [52]. Zelekha, the joint-list partner, said Jews should serve in the army except for a few hundred genuine Torah prodigies, and Arab citizens should do national service in public services [14].

**Action:** In May 2026 the party set up a "Council of Torah Sages" of rabbis who served in the army or reserves, which issued a religious ruling calling for enlistment as part of a "commanded war" [53].

**Closest option(s):** Require every 18-year-old, including Haredim, to serve in the army or civilian national service, with exemptions only for a small number of outstanding Torah scholars and loss of state benefits for those who refuse (`universal-service-with-sanctions`). The party's proposed sanctions go further than that option, including loss of the right to vote.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

The party has not published a dedicated housing plan. Its housing-related commitments are mostly benefits for people who serve and moving population out of the center.

**Formal commitment:** The platform proposes giving land to reservists in national-priority areas, using service as a main criterion for mortgage discounts and for the subsidized-housing program (Mechir LaMishtaken), and subsidizing rent and insurance for reservists during and around their reserve service [8]. The August 2026 plan promised significant subsidies for buying land for those who serve [43, 45]. The platform also offers large corporate-tax breaks to technology companies that employ about 70% of their staff in the Negev, the Galilee or the Jordan Valley [8].

**Statement:** In October 2026 Hendel said he wants to build new communities, at first for reservists, and to "move the population from the center to the Negev, the Galilee and the Jordan Valley, as far east as possible, as a huge national project" [6].

**Closest option(s):** Direct help for buyers, aimed at people who served (`direct-help-for-buyers`), combined with developing the periphery (`develop-periphery-and-transport`). The evidence is limited, because the party has not addressed building permits, rental regulation or overall housing supply.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Formal commitment:** The platform calls for an "aggressive security policy that moves the decision to enemy territory", replacing containment with initiative and offense [8]. On Iran, it says the earlier Operation Rising Lion against Iran "proved intelligence and air superiority but left Iran with offensive capabilities. The mission is not finished. Israel cannot contain Iran with the means and intention over time" [8]. On Hezbollah, it says that if Hezbollah is not disarmed under the agreement, a campaign should be opened to disarm it and take the commanding ridges in Lebanon; it calls for full control of the high ridges in Lebanon and the Hermon peak, and for regional cooperation to weaken the Iranian axis [8]. It also calls for strengthening Israel's own arms industry to reduce dependence on others [8].

**Statement:** On March 2, 2026, during Operation Roaring Lion against Iran, Hendel said: "We are in a war to remove an existential threat… Israel needs to destroy Iran's military capabilities and nuclear infrastructure and do everything possible to stabilize an alternative regime." He said Israel had for years "preferred quiet over military initiative", which led to October 7 [54]. On May 7, 2026 he said "we have not finished the job in Lebanon" [55]. In October 2026 he praised operations in Lebanon and holding strategic positions, and said Israel should keep holding the high ridges [6].

**Action:** As a minister in October 2022, Hendel abstained in the government vote on the US-brokered maritime border agreement with Lebanon, after earlier saying he would not vote for it. He said he had concluded the deal did not harm Israeli sovereignty in its territorial waters and had US guarantees [56, 57].

**Closest option(s):** Keep attacking Iran, Hezbollah and their allies until they can no longer threaten Israel, and stay on the high ground in Lebanon (`military-pressure-until-threats-removed`). The platform's call for regional cooperation is a smaller element of `force-plus-us-backed-agreements`.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Formal commitment:** The platform promises to "act to prevent a Palestinian state in diplomatic channels" and to strengthen settlement on the commanding ridges [8]. Under the heading "sovereignty through deeds", it calls for strengthening settlement in Judea and Samaria and for "winning the struggle" over land in Area C (the part of Judea and Samaria under full Israeli control), making the Jordan Valley a national settlement goal, and using individual farms and grazing land to hold territory [8].

**Formal commitment:** In September 2026 the joint list told Ynet: "We support settlement in all the areas of the State of Israel. The strategy is taking commanding areas and agricultural land." On future evacuation as part of an agreement: "No. Everything that went up with the approval of the state and the army will stay." On sovereignty: "The party supports sovereignty through deeds, not words. If it is possible to apply law, for example to the Jordan Valley, the [settlement] bloc or another strategic area… we will support it." On illegal Palestinian building in Area C: "Strict enforcement, full removal." On illegal outposts: those approved by the army stay; those built without approval on private land are removed [58].

**Statement:** Hendel told the Jerusalem Post in 2026: "We will not allow a Palestinian state to be established," and that Israel must control the high ground and ensure settlement in Judea and Samaria [7]. In October 2026 he said he supports settlement in Judea and Samaria and the "farms project" (individual farms on open land) [6]. In January 2022, as a minister, he said "you don't build settlements as a response to attacks; you build settlements as a strategy" [21].

**Closest option(s):** Apply Israeli sovereignty to key areas such as the Jordan Valley (`partial-sovereignty-area-c`), together with continued settlement and army freedom of action, which are elements of `maintain-current-situation`. The party opposes a Palestinian state.

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Formal commitment:** The platform promises "corrections in the justice system under broad agreements and in a joint public process", and to set out each branch's status and responsibility in law [8]. It lists: external, independent review of the justice system; splitting the role of the attorney general (who is currently both the government's legal adviser and head of public prosecution); a "Basic Law: Legislation" to regulate relations between the government, the Knesset and the courts; a term limit for prime ministers; and fewer government ministries [8]. When the party was founded in September 2025 it described its approach as changes to the judicial system by broad agreement rather than imposed changes [3].

**Formal commitment:** In September 2026 the joint list told Ynet: "We are in favor of changes in the justice system." It said the first step is a Basic Law: Legislation; Knesset members should set a "balanced" make-up for the committee that selects judges; the Knesset should be able to override a Supreme Court ruling that cancels a law only with a special majority of more than 72 of its 120 members; the "reasonableness" ground (the courts' power to cancel government decisions they find extremely unreasonable) should be narrowed but not cancelled; and Basic Laws should need at least 61 votes to pass, as part of an overall reform [59]. The August 2026 plan with Tropper spoke of a "Zionist constitution", changing the system of government, "responsible correction" of the justice system and strengthening the rule of law [43, 45].

**Statement:** In September 2022 Hendel wrote that there is "a normal right in Israel that does not seek to destroy the justice system but to fix it" [67]. Ynetnews described him at the time as positioning himself on the "liberal right-wing" and rejecting Likud's combative stance against the legal system [29].

**Closest option(s):** Set the powers of each branch through a broadly agreed basic law, with a limited Knesset override (`broad-agreed-constitutional-framework`). Some elements, such as splitting the attorney general's role and giving the Knesset a say in the judges' committee, overlap with `continue-judicial-changes`.

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Formal commitment:** A commission of inquiry into October 7 is one of the party's three founding conditions [2]. The platform says Israel must investigate "all the failures" that led to the October 7 attack, and adds: "The word 'state' [mamlachtit] has become an excuse not to investigate. It is time to investigate" [8]. In November 2025 Hendel called for a state commission of inquiry into the October 7 failures [5, 51].

**Action:** On October 15, 2025 the party called for a state commission of inquiry to be set up immediately and proposed nine possible members, saying an agreed commission could be formed "within a day": Justice Noam Sohlberg, Maj. Gen. (res.) Doron Almog, Prof. Suzie Navot, former minister Yuval Steinitz, former MK Einat Wilf, Prof. Yuval Elbashan, Miriam Peretz, Maj. Gen. (res.) Yoram Yair and former justice minister Prof. Daniel Friedmann [38, 60]. On October 16, 2025 it wrote to President Isaac Herzog asking him to host talks with the justice system and all parties to agree on the commission's make-up, so that it would win broad public trust [38, 61].

**Formal commitment:** In October 2026 the joint list told Ynet: "In favor of a state commission of inquiry. It is preferable to reach a composition by broad agreement. Who will appoint it? The Supreme Court, preferably Sohlberg" [62], referring to Justice Noam Sohlberg, whom the party had also proposed as a commission member in 2025 [38].

**Closest option(s):** A state commission of inquiry with members picked through the Supreme Court, as the law requires (`state-commission-under-existing-law`). The party's emphasis on a broadly agreed make-up is an element of `commission-chosen-by-neutral-mechanism`.

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

The party's platform and its 2026 statements do not address marriage, conversion, kosher certification or Shabbat. The evidence below is mostly from Hendel's time as a minister.

**Statement:** In September 2021, as communications minister in a government that was reforming kosher certification, Hendel said: "No one should have a monopoly on Judaism. Not Haredi, not religious, not religious-Zionist, not 'light', not Reform or Conservative." He said mayors should decide how public transport works in their cities on Shabbat, and that after the budget passed the government could deal with conversion and civil marriage: "We need to provide an alternative for those who cannot marry" [63, 64].

**Formal commitment:** The August 2026 plan said public funding would go only to schools that teach core subjects (such as math and English), which would affect Haredi schools that do not teach them [43, 45]. As a Knesset member Hendel sponsored a bill on the status of mixed religious-secular state schools, which did not advance [32].

**Statement:** In July 2026 Hendel criticized the coalition's exemption bills and what he called "a law that disgraces the Torah" (in another speech, "a law called the Torah law"), and said Haredi political leaders treat the state as "the rule of heretics" [22, 65]. In May 2026 the party set up its own "Council of Torah Sages" of rabbis who serve in the army, which Hendel called "the true Council of Torah Sages" [53].

**Closest option(s):** Keep the Rabbinate but allow alternatives, such as civil marriage, and let each city decide on Shabbat transport (`more-choice-local-decisions`). This is based on Hendel's 2021 statements; the party has no current formal position on these questions.

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Benefits for reservists and people who serve {#other-reservist-benefits}

**Formal commitment:** The platform proposes: priority for reservists in public-sector jobs and for reservists' businesses in state tenders; compensation for employers of reservists who serve more than 15 days a year; support for small businesses run by reservists; land for reservists in national-priority areas; and subsidized rent and insurance during and around reserve service [8]. The August 2026 plan added full funding of bachelor's and master's degrees for those who serve, arnona and National Insurance benefits for businesses that employ active reservists, and funded workshops to help soldiers process combat experience and return to civilian life [43, 45].

### Wounded soldiers, bereaved families and mental health {#other-wounded-and-mental-health}

**Formal commitment:** The party lists a dedicated "PTSD plan" among its work plans [8]. The August 2026 plan promised to expand rehabilitation and mental-health services in outlying and war-affected areas, support bereaved families, and create special tracks back to study and work for those hurt in the fighting [44, 45]. Several of its candidates are bereaved relatives or have worked with wounded soldiers [4, 34, 41].

### Rebuilding border and frontline communities {#other-frontline-communities}

**Formal commitment:** The August 2026 plan promised, within 90 days of a government being formed, a government plan for northern residents to return home, a dedicated budget to rebuild communities, and a plan to strengthen Kiryat Shmona, the Galilee, the communities near Gaza, the Negev and the communities along the eastern border [43, 45].

### Education {#other-education}

**Formal commitment:** The August 2026 plan promised to strengthen public education, fund only schools that teach core subjects, give school principals more autonomy, invest heavily in early childhood, raise young teachers' pay and change how teachers are employed and promoted [43, 45]. The platform says education budgets should go only to institutions whose students serve the state [8].

### System of government {#other-system-of-government}

**Formal commitment:** The platform proposes limiting a prime minister to eight years or two terms, and cutting the number of government ministries to those that "change reality" [8]. The August 2026 plan added a "Zionist constitution", changing the system of government, handing more powers to local government, cutting bureaucracy, and term limits for elected officials [43, 44].

### Settlement, agriculture and the Negev and Galilee {#other-settlement-and-agriculture}

**Formal commitment:** The platform calls for strengthening settlement in Judea and Samaria, the Negev, the Galilee and along the borders; "restoring the Judaization of the Negev and Galilee as a national mission" (meaning more Jewish communities there); individual farms and grazing land to hold open areas; a civilian guard model for farmland ("the new HaShomer"); permanent local emergency squads with policing powers; advanced infrastructure (education, health, communications) in border communities; and support for agriculture, research and efficiency, which it calls necessary for holding land and for food security [8].

### Defense industry and army size {#other-defense-industry}

**Formal commitment:** The platform calls for strengthening Israeli defense industries and producing weapons in Israel, and for enlarging the regular army, career army and reserves [8]. **Statement:** Hendel wrote in May 2025 that Israel must not depend on foreign defense industries, "not even the United States" [48]. He has argued that a small army forces repeated reserve call-ups and harms security [22, 52].

### Women in combat roles {#other-women-in-combat}

**Statement:** In March 2024, after reserve duty in Gaza, Hendel said that doubts about whether women can be combat soldiers "have disappeared" and that "the army needs people who hold weapons, men or women" [19]. In August 2026 he said that with a larger army, women paramedics could be assigned to units other than Haredi ones [52].

### Arab citizens and national service {#other-arab-citizens}

**Formal commitment:** The platform's service-for-all principle applies to Arab citizens too, through more national-service tracks in Arab society [8]. **Statement:** Hendel said in July 2026 that he hopes "an Arab and Zionist party" will one day join a coalition, "but not a Palestinian-Israeli party" [22]. Zelekha, the joint-list partner, said in September 2026 that "most Arab citizens of Israel are loyal citizens" and that there should be no incitement against them [14].

### Communications {#other-communications}

**Action:** As communications minister Hendel advanced the 2020 government plan for nationwide fiber-optic networks, including in outlying and border areas [30], and in 2022 ordered full number portability for kosher phone lines [31]. In 2024 he named the fiber-optic reform as the change he made as minister [19].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Statements on who the party will and won't sit with:**
- **At founding.** When the party was registered in September 2025 it said it would "deal with principles and not political boycotts", and The Times of Israel reported that the party said it was "not boycotting anyone, including Netanyahu" [2, 3]. From November 2025 Hendel has ruled out Haredi and Arab parties (below).
- **No Haredi or Arab parties.** The platform says "a government that relies on non-Zionist parties (Haredi or Arab) is pressured into making non-Zionist decisions" [8]. On November 19, 2025 Hendel called for "a Zionist unity government, without Arabs and Haredim" [5, 51]. On November 28, 2025 he said: "I will not sit with Haredim and Arabs" [20]. On September 6, 2026, announcing the joint list, he said: "We need to form a Zionist government. Without Haredi and Arab parties" [9]. On October 1, 2026 he said of the Haredi parties: "I won't bring them, I don't want to. I think they need to sit on the side for a few years" [6].
- **Enlistment law before a government.** The joint list's founding principles include demanding that a law requiring service for all be passed before a government is formed [12, 46].
- **Netanyahu.** Hendel has said Netanyahu "should go home" and is responsible for October 7, but he has not ruled out sitting with him [5, 20]. On July 10, 2026 he pledged: "I will never agree to complete 61 seats for him", meaning he would not be the deciding partner in a narrow Netanyahu government; he said he would join a Netanyahu-led government only as part of a broad unity government of "70 to 80 Zionist seats" [22]. He told the Jerusalem Post: "Netanyahu is not my red line. My red line is … whether it is a Zionist government" [7]. On October 1, 2026 he said: "I am not in the bloc of Netanyahu and the Haredim", and also that he does not want "a bloc of Eisenkot and the Arab parties" [6].
- **Who should lead.** Hendel has said the government should be formed by whoever brings the most "Zionist seats" [6, 22]. Zelekha said the list is "not in anyone's pocket, not Bibi's and certainly not Eisenkot's" [14].
- **The Democrats.** Hendel said in October 2026 that Yair Golan "is not for settlement in Judea and Samaria, he is for a Palestinian state, I am against", and that he does not see how they could sit together in a coalition based on his principles [6].
- **Demands.** In August 2026, when it was allied with Tropper, the party said it would seek the National Security Ministry for Hendel [7, 44]. Zelekha has said the list will demand the Finance Ministry [14].
- **Another election.** Hendel has said that if the alternative is a government that passes an exemption law, he prefers another election [22].

**Track record of keeping such pledges:**
- **2020.** Hendel and Hauser said in March 2020 that they would not support a minority government relying on the Joint List, and they kept that position despite pressure from their own list [24, 25]. Hendel later said Blue and White "broke up over my refusal to go with the Joint List" [19]. He then served as communications minister from May 2020 in the government formed under the coalition agreement between Likud and Blue and White [23, 27].
- **2020–2021.** In December 2020 Hendel joined Gideon Sa'ar's New Hope, backing Sa'ar for prime minister and saying Netanyahu "chose to keep dividing" [26]. After the March 2021 election he became communications minister in the government formed in June 2021 [23].
- **Contradiction:** Hendel now rules out governing with Arab parties [8, 9], but from June 2021 to December 2022 he served as a minister [23] in a government whose coalition included Mansour Abbas's Ra'am [22]. Asked about this in July 2026, Hendel said he had also thought the partnership "could work", but concluded it could not after Ra'am froze its coalition membership during a military operation [22].
- **2022.** Hendel said he would not support a narrow Netanyahu-led government. He kept that position: his alliance with Ayelet Shaked broke up over it in September 2022 [28, 29], and he then took a break from politics and did not run [66].
- **2026 alliances.** Hendel's talks to join Benny Gantz's Blue and White did not produce an agreement. Reports said the main gap was Hendel's insistence on ruling out Haredi parties, which Gantz and Dedi Simchi would not do [15, 17, 22]. His alliance with Tropper ended on September 6, 2026 [12].

## Track record {#track-record}

The Reservists has never been in the Knesset or in government; this is its first election [1, 2]. Its record so far is as a movement and party outside the Knesset:
- It campaigned with other "serving organizations" against the bill to regulate yeshiva students' military service, and says it helped stop it [4, 34].
- In October 2025 it published proposed members for an October 7 commission of inquiry and asked the President to broker agreement on one [38, 60].
- It defeated Likud's objection to its name before the Registrar of Parties in December 2025 [5].
- It held a vote for party chair on June 8, 2026 and primaries for its list on June 16, 2026 [33, 34].
- It set up a "Council of Torah Sages" of rabbis who serve in the army in May 2026 [53].

**Hendel's record in government (2020–2022), compared with the positions he now holds:**
- **Communications reforms.** He promoted the fiber-optic plan, which the government approved in September 2020 [30], and ordered number portability for kosher phone lines in April 2022 [31].
- **Religion and state.** In 2021 he said the government would move on to civil marriage and conversion after passing the budget [63, 64].
- **Lebanon.** He abstained on the 2022 maritime agreement with Lebanon [56, 57].
- **Coalition partners.** See the contradiction noted in [Coalition stance](#coalition) about serving in a government that included Ra'am.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Enough public information exists to describe the party's positions on most issues. The party has a detailed published platform and governance plan on its website [8, 47], answered Ynet's issue-by-issue questionnaires on enlistment, the judicial system, Judea and Samaria and the October 7 inquiry in September and October 2026 [50, 58, 59, 62], and its leader has a public record as a Knesset member and minister [23]. The gaps are: the party itself has no Knesset voting record, because it has never been in the Knesset; the joint list with the New Economic Party has not published a combined platform, so some economic positions come from the partner party's leader; the party has no published position on marriage, conversion or Shabbat; its Gaza position does not address the US plan's international force; it has no detailed housing plan; and several commitments come from the August 2026 "Shield of David" plan, published with former partner Chili Tropper before that alliance ended.
