## Overview {#overview}

**What the party is.** Ta'al (התנועה הערבית להתחדשות, the Arab Movement for Renewal) is a secular Arab party founded by Ahmad Tibi ahead of the 1996 Knesset election. It first ran under the name "Arab Union" (האיחוד הערבי), did not pass the threshold, and took its current name before the 1999 election [6]. According to Tibi's official Knesset biography, he founded the party "with a group of Arab academics", withdrew from the 1996 race shortly before the vote and asked his supporters to vote for the other Arab parties [7]. The Israel Democracy Institute (IDI) describes Ta'al as supporting an Israeli-Palestinian peace process leading to an independent Palestinian state and a "just solution" to the Palestinian refugee issue, as working to close gaps between Arab citizens and the Jewish majority and achieve full civil equality, and as "secular in character" [6]. The Jerusalem Post describes it as occupying a position between Hadash (socialist, Jewish-Arab) and Balad (Arab nationalist), "balancing participation in Israeli political institutions with protecting the Palestinian cause" [28]. +972 Magazine describes the party as built mainly around its long-time leader rather than broad internal structures [29].

**History of alliances.** Since the 1999 election Ta'al has never run on its own list; it has always joined other parties [6]. Tibi entered the Knesset in 1999 on the Balad list and broke away in December 1999 to form a Ta'al faction; in 2003 Ta'al ran with Hadash; from 2006 to 2013 it ran with Ra'am as "Ra'am–Ta'al" [7]. In 2015 it joined the original Joint List (Hadash, Ra'am, Ta'al and Balad), which won 13 seats [7]. The alliance has split and re-formed several times (see below).

**Base of support.** Ta'al's base is among Arab citizens; the party is headquartered in Tayibe, in the "Triangle" area of central Israel, where its central committee met to approve the 2026 alliance [26]. Its second MK in the outgoing Knesset, Samir Bin Said, was a high-school teacher and head of the education department of the Segev Shalom local council [8].

**Current Knesset seats.** In the 2022 election Ta'al ran with Hadash as Hadash–Ta'al, which won 5 seats [13]. Tibi was Ta'al's only MK at first; Samir Bin Said (Ta'al) entered the Knesset on 20 June 2025 under a rotation agreement, replacing Hadash's Youssef Atauna [10, 104]. In June 2026 Bin Said announced he would challenge Tibi for the party leadership [9]. On 14 July 2026 Kikar HaShabbat reported that Bin Said's resignation from the Knesset would take effect the next day [12]; on 15 July 2026 Kan's Arabic service reported that he had withdrawn the resignation within the legal window, announced he was leaving Ta'al and joining Hadash, and accused Tibi of running the party "in an individual manner" [11]. Knesset records still list Bin Said as a serving MK in the Hadash–Ta'al faction [104]. As a result, Ta'al is now represented by **one MK, Ahmad Tibi**, inside the five-member Hadash–Ta'al Knesset faction, which Tibi has chaired since November 2022 [11, 104].

**The 2026 list: a joint list.** Ta'al runs in the 26th Knesset election (27 October 2026) on **the Joint List (הרשימה המשותפת), ballot letters ודם**, together with **Hadash** (the Israeli Communist Party, led by Yousef Jabareen) and **Balad** (National Democratic Assembly) [1, 2]. The Central Elections Committee (CEC) list, published on 9 September 2026, places Tibi at No. 2, behind Jabareen; Ta'al's other filed candidates include lawyer Ahmad Darawshe and Haitham Zahalka [1]. After Balad chair Sami Abu Shehadeh (originally No. 3) withdrew his candidacy on 2 October 2026, every candidate below him moved up one place [3]. Ta'al's central committee approved its candidates in this order: Ahmad Tibi, Ahmad Darawshe, Haitham Zahalka, Ghassan Abdallah, Itimad Qaadan and Laith Kabha [25].

**Terms of the 2026 agreement.** Under the agreement signed on 19 August 2026, Tibi was expected to chair the Joint List's Knesset faction and was expected to receive a deputy Speaker post [24]. Al-Quds Al-Arabi reported that Ta'al's second candidate (No. 9 on the filed list) is to rotate with the No. 2 slot after 20 months, that Balad gave up half of its faction-chair term to Ta'al, and that Hadash gave up half of its deputy-Speaker term [25]. Ta'al's central committee said it accepted terms it considered "less than we deserve" in order to "give unity a real chance", raise turnout and help bring down what it called "the government of fascism, destruction and racism" [26]. Tibi also invited Ra'am to join and turn the three-party list into a four-party one [27].

**History of splits among these partners (with dates).**
- **January 2019:** Tibi asked the Knesset to split Ta'al from the Joint List faction on 8 January 2019 [17]. Hadash and Ta'al ran together in April 2019 and won 6 seats, while Ra'am and Balad ran together on a separate list that won 4 seats [18].
- **July 2019:** the four parties, including Ta'al, agreed to run together again as the Joint List for the September 2019 election [18].
- **February 2021:** Ra'am left the Joint List and filed its own list on 4 February 2021; Hadash, Ta'al and Balad stayed together. Tibi publicly offered to give up his No. 2 slot to Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas to keep the list united [19].
- **September 2022:** one day after agreeing to run together, Balad split from Hadash and Ta'al about an hour before the 15 September 2022 filing deadline, citing a dispute over rotating a slot with Ta'al [14]. Al-Quds Al-Arabi reported on 14 September 2022 that Balad had also demanded that Ta'al sign a commitment not to recommend any candidate for prime minister, a commitment Hadash had signed, and that Ta'al wanted to keep room to manoeuvre [15]. Hadash–Ta'al won 5 seats; Balad failed to pass the threshold [13].
- **January–August 2026:** the four parties signed a document in Sakhnin in January 2026 to work toward a joint list [20, 30]. Ra'am later stayed out after Abbas sought the right to split off and join a coalition [28, 29]. On 9 August 2026 Tibi rejected the slate proposed by the parties' "Reconciliation Committee" because Ta'al's second representative was placed ninth rather than sixth or seventh, and froze talks [22, 23]. He reversed course and the three parties signed on 19 August 2026 [24].

**Legal challenge in 2026.** On 23 September 2026 the CEC voted 18-5 to disqualify the Joint List, accepting a request by Likud under the Basic Law provision that bars lists supporting armed struggle against Israel [5]. On 2 October 2026 the Supreme Court unanimously overturned that decision [4].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Ahmad Tibi (leader; Joint List No. 2).** Born on 19 December 1958 in Tayibe, where he lives; a physician specialising in gynaecology [7]. According to his Knesset biography, in the 1980s he took part in early contacts between Israel and the PLO; after the 1993 Oslo Accord Yasser Arafat appointed him as a special adviser, and in 1998 he was spokesman of the Palestinian delegation to the talks that led to the Wye River Memorandum. He resigned as Arafat's adviser in March 1999 [7]. He has served in the Knesset continuously since 1999 and is its longest-serving Arab member [7]. He served as Deputy Speaker in several Knessets, chaired the Ra'am–Ta'al faction from the 17th to the 19th Knesset, sat on the Finance Committee, and in 2008 headed a parliamentary inquiry committee on the employment of Arab citizens in the civil service [7]. His Knesset biography says he has passed about 12 laws, "almost all on social, economic and consumer matters", and that in 2010 he gave a Knesset speech on the Holocaust that then-Speaker Reuven Rivlin called "the best speech in the history of the Knesset" [7]. Knesset records show he has chaired the Hadash–Ta'al Knesset faction since November 2022 and sits on the Finance Committee [104].

**Ahmad Darawshe (Ta'al's second candidate).** A lawyer and Ta'al official; filed at No. 9 on the CEC list and No. 8 after Abu Shehadeh's withdrawal [1, 3, 25]. Under the agreement he is to rotate into the No. 2 slot after 20 months [25].

**Haitham Zahalka (Ta'al's third candidate)** is filed at No. 12 (No. 11 after Abu Shehadeh's withdrawal) [1, 3, 25]. No further public role was found.

**Osama Saadi** (former Ta'al MK, described by The Jerusalem Post as a close associate of Tibi) is not a candidate in 2026 but led Ta'al's side of the final negotiations with Hadash in August 2026 [24, 25].

**Top of the Joint List (partners).** After Abu Shehadeh's withdrawal the list is headed by Yousef Jabareen (Hadash), then Tibi, Faten Ghattas (Hadash), Bakr Awawdeh (Balad) and Ofer Cassif (Hadash) [1, 3].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

**Formal commitment:** The Joint List's ten-point economic and social plan, published on its website and reported on 30 September 2026 by Zo Haderech, the Communist Party's weekly, under the title "Investing in life, not in wars" [105, 86], calls for deep cuts in military spending, public housing for all who lack housing, rent capped at 20% of household income, raising the minimum wage to at least 70% of the average wage, free education from birth to doctorate, raising the basic allowance to 25% of the average wage, ending tax benefits for financial corporations and large companies, raising tax on financial gains (including stock-market gains) to 30%, exempting wage earners and self-employed people earning up to the average wage from income tax, cutting VAT to 10%, and cancelling privatisation programmes [105]. This is a plan of the joint list; no separate Ta'al economic platform was found.

**Action:** On 22 June 2026 Hadash–Ta'al and Ra'am jointly filed a no-confidence motion on "the economic situation, the cost of living and its effect on Arab society"; Tibi voted for it [68]. Tibi voted against the coalition's 2025 budget-arrangements laws that froze tax-bracket updates and taxed undistributed company profits (December 2024; passed 55-52 and 59-58), and against the 2026 economic arrangements law (March 2026; passed 62-55) [69, 70, 76].

**Action:** The Aviation Services Law of 2012, known as "the Tibi law", requires airlines to compensate and assist passengers whose flights are cancelled or long delayed [96]. In June 2025 El Al asked the Transport Ministry to amend the law so that airlines would not have to pay compensation for flights cancelled during the June 2025 war with Iran ("Operation Rising Lion") [97].

**Statement:** In February 2026 Tibi said that if the state employed Arab university graduates equally in the civil service and the economy, it would gain workers and slow the drift of young people into crime; he noted that only 13% of Arab society is in the civil service [30].

**Closest option(s):** Direct help for households, through higher wages and allowances and subsidised basics (`direct-support-for-households`); the joint list's plan also includes state intervention such as higher taxes on large companies and financial gains (`active-state-regulation`). The evidence is a joint-list plan and opposition votes, not Ta'al-specific policy.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

**Formal commitment:** The Joint List's "plan to eradicate organised crime", published on its website, says a "warlike approach" fuels violence and that crime cannot be fought "while weakening democracy" or the independence of the courts [106]. It proposes a new five-year development plan for Arab society (jobs, education, planning, business and infrastructure), support for victims of crime, stronger police investigation units, a dedicated organised-crime unit in the State Attorney's office, a national authority to coordinate police, tax and anti-money-laundering bodies, wider confiscation of criminal assets, action against illegal weapons and protection rackets, and protection for local councils, witnesses and officials threatened by crime groups [106].

**Statement:** In a February 2026 interview Tibi said most murders in Arab society are committed by crime organisations, and that fighting them is the responsibility of the state, meaning the police and the prosecution. He said community violence (such as murders of women and school violence) is the responsibility of Arab leaders, clergy, families and schools [30]. He said he had called "for 20 years" for the collection of illegal weapons, and that a state that "can bomb a weapons truck in Sudan" should be able to stop armed vehicles entering Arab towns [30]. He blamed National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and the government for the rise in crime, and said he would like Yoav Segalovitz or Yair Golan as public security minister in the next government [30]. He described local initiatives such as the volunteer guard in Kafr Qasim as a success but "a handful against the huge threat" [30].

**Statement:** In August 2023 Tibi said: "We supported the protection [racketeering] law but we will oppose administrative detentions, especially those Ben-Gvir is behind. I have always opposed administrative detentions, of Arabs and of Jews alike." Administrative detention means holding someone without trial [88].

**Action:** In February 2024 Tibi voted against the preliminary reading of the government-backed bill to expand measures against crime organisations, which passed 46-28 [64]. Hadash–Ta'al filed a no-confidence motion in July 2023 accusing the government of policies that strengthen crime in Arab society; Tibi voted for it [65]. On 6 July 2026 Ra'am and Hadash–Ta'al filed another no-confidence motion over "the continuing failure in the fight against crime"; Tibi voted for it [66].

**Statement:** In February 2026 Tibi said the Arab parties planned to escalate protests against crime, up to a three-day general strike of Arab workers [30]. **Formal commitment:** The Joint List's economic plan also calls for programmes to close gaps between Arab society and the rest of the country in education, housing, welfare, infrastructure, public transport and industrial zones, and for recognising Arab towns as development areas [105].

No statement by Tibi or Ta'al specifically on the July 2026 government decision to bring the Shin Bet (Israel's internal security service) into the fight against crime was found (searched Hebrew, Arabic and English news, July–October 2026); the Joint List's crime plan does not mention the Shin Bet [106].

**Closest option(s):** Invest in Arab communities first (`invest-in-arab-communities-first`) together with a stronger police and prosecution effort under normal law, including weapons collection and no detention without trial (`more-police-resources-normal-law`). The Joint List's crime plan and Tibi's statements combine both; Tibi explicitly opposes administrative detention.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Formal commitment:** According to a 7 October 2026 review of party platforms by Zo Haderech (the weekly of the Israeli Communist Party, which is Hadash's registered party within the Joint List), the Joint List demands an immediate and complete halt to military operations in Gaza, full withdrawal of all troops and lifting of the blockade, and its member parties called for a deal releasing all Israeli hostages in exchange for all Palestinian prisoners. It rejects Israeli control, applying sovereignty or renewed settlement in Gaza, and says Gaza's future should be decided only by the Palestinian people and its leadership, as part of a Palestinian state [85].

**Statement:** In February 2024 Tibi told The Jerusalem Post that the Arab public "opposed the killing of Israeli civilians on October 7 and said so", while describing Israel's bombing of Gaza as "war crimes" [78]. He said Gaza should be rebuilt with international and Arab help, that only Palestinians should choose their leaders, preferably by election, and that Hamas and Islamic Jihad could join the PLO and take part in elections, which, the Post noted, would require recognising Israel and rejecting violence [78]. He argued that only negotiations, not military pressure, had brought hostages back alive [78]. In a February 2024 interview with Newsweek he called on the US to stop the war and criticised President Biden for not doing so [79]. In June 2024 he said "we are against targeting civilians… in the south of Israel — any child, any woman" [80].

**Statement:** In February 2025 Tibi called President Trump's proposal to move Gaza's population out of the Strip "dangerous delusions" and said "the Palestinian people will remain in its homeland" [82]. In October 2025, when the last living hostages were released and Trump addressed the Knesset, Hadash–Ta'al MKs, led by Ayman Odeh and Tibi, planned to attend the session rather than boycott it [81]. In February 2026 he said "a child is a child" and that kidnapping and murdering children and killing them in air strikes on populated buildings are "both criminal"; he said 13 of his relatives, including two children, had been killed in Gaza [30].

**Closest option(s):** End the war completely, withdraw the army and hand control to a Palestinian government as a step toward a Palestinian state (`end-war-full-withdrawal`).

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Statement:** In May 2024 Tibi said he did "not want to interfere in Jewish wars" over the enlistment law: "Let them settle it among themselves." He added that if the law's failure depended on Arab votes, "we will vote against and bring down the government" [44]. In December 2025 he told his faction: "We are mobilised to bring down the government; we will not vote for the enlistment law" [45]. In the same report a Hadash–Ta'al source told Israel Hayom that voting for the law was unlikely but that abstaining was "certainly possible" if the coalition lacked a majority [45].

**Action:** On 10 June 2026 the Knesset passed in a preliminary reading, 56-43, a Basic Law declaring Torah study a foundational value [46, 71]. The Times of Israel described the bill as designed to anchor Torah study in law as equal to military service, after Haredi parties sought to preserve yeshiva students' exemptions following a 2024 High Court ruling [46]; according to Ynet, a clause explicitly equating Torah students with soldiers had been removed from the text after criticism [47]. The official vote record shows no Hadash–Ta'al or Ra'am MK voting [71]. According to opposition sources quoted by Ynet and Hebrew media reports cited by The Times of Israel, the Arab MKs stayed away as part of a "deal" with United Torah Judaism, which in return would help block a bill restricting mosque loudspeakers (the "muezzin law") [46, 47]; Walla reported that those involved denied any such understanding, and later reported that the parties had denied a formal deal [48, 49]. The Times of Israel wrote that the bill "might not have advanced" without the Arab MKs' absence, while Ynet noted that it would still have passed had the absent MKs voted against [46, 47]; the official tally (56-43, with the ten Hadash–Ta'al and Ra'am MKs absent) is consistent with Ynet's arithmetic [71]. Tibi did not vote at the first reading on 1 July 2026 (passed 63-53), when Bin Said and the Hadash MKs voted against [72]. Tibi voted **against** the law at its final reading on 13 July 2026, when it passed 63-52 [73, 52].

**Note on consistency:** Critics, including Deputy Foreign Minister Sharren Haskel, accused Tibi of helping the Haredi parties pass the bill [47]. The record does not show a direct contradiction of his stated position: the Torah-study Basic Law is a separate bill from the enlistment law he said he would not vote for, Hadash–Ta'al MKs did not vote for it at any stage, his May 2024 pledge to vote against if the outcome depended on Arab votes was not tested at the preliminary reading on the official tally, and he voted against it at the final reading [44, 45, 71, 72, 73].

**Statement:** In February 2026 Tibi said he had sponsored a bill on exemption from service for reasons of conscience and that he "appreciates conscientious objectors" [30].

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. Tibi describes the Haredi enlistment dispute as an internal Jewish matter and supports exemptions on grounds of conscience, which is closest to voluntary service (`voluntary-service-for-all`), but no Ta'al position on how Haredi yeshiva students should be treated was found.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

**Formal commitment:** The Joint List's September 2026 plan calls for public housing for all who lack housing, rent capped at 20% of household income, restoring the Public Housing Law, large-scale building of rental or discounted homes by government companies, a state mortgage bank, and cancelling mortgage interest for young people and first-time buyers [105, 86].

**Statement:** In February 2026 Tibi said planning authorities "choke" Arab towns and do not allow them to expand, so families build on private land without permits, and thousands of homes in the Negev, the Galilee and the Triangle are demolished [30]. He called for repealing the Kaminitz Law, which he described as allowing administrative enforcement without a court process and harsher penalties for building violations, calling it one of "the most terrible racist laws" [30]. He said Arab citizens moving to mixed cities such as Haifa, Karmiel, Lod and Ramla for safety was "their full right" [30].

**Action:** On 15 June 2026 Hadash–Ta'al and Ra'am filed a no-confidence motion over "the policy of demolition, exclusion and planning suffocation against Arab citizens"; Tibi voted for it [67].

**Closest option(s):** State-built affordable rental housing and rent regulation (`state-led-affordable-rental`), together with more planning and land for Arab towns. Ta'al's own emphasis is on planning and demolitions in Arab communities, which none of the options addresses directly.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Statement:** Asked in February 2026 whether he supported an attack on Iran and a change of its regime, Tibi said: "I'm against wars. The war in Iran will destroy the whole Middle East… A diplomatic solution is better." He said he rejects "war for regime change", called the Iranian regime "totalitarian" and said it shoots protesters, but opposed what he called the "neighbourhood bully" policy he attributed to Netanyahu [30]. On 15 June 2026, after a US–Iran agreement reopened the Strait of Hormuz, he said Trump's "greatest achievement is to open the Strait of Hormuz, which was open before the war began" [84, 87].

**Statement:** At the Joint List's Hebrew campaign launch on 5 October 2026 Tibi presented the list as "the address for left-wing, liberal democratic Jews who want a party that opposes war and war crimes" [36].

No Ta'al statement specifically on Hezbollah or the fighting in Lebanon in 2026 was found (searched Hebrew, Arabic and English news).

**Closest option(s):** End military operations and seek regional peace through diplomacy (`end-military-operations-regional-peace`); the evidence is statements only.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Formal commitment:** The IDI describes Ta'al as supporting an independent Palestinian state and a just solution to the refugee issue [6]. According to Zo Haderech, the Joint List supports a sovereign Palestinian state alongside Israel on the 4 June 1967 lines (the pre-1967 border) with East Jerusalem as its capital, the removal of checkpoints and army infrastructure, and the evacuation of all settlements and outposts, which it calls the main obstacle to peace [85]. Joint List chair Jabareen has said the list would push for renewed negotiations with the Palestinian leadership toward a Palestinian state alongside Israel [28].

**Action:** On 22 October 2025 Tibi voted against the preliminary reading of the bill to apply Israeli sovereignty to the settlements in Judea and Samaria (it passed 25-24) and against the bill to apply sovereignty to Ma'ale Adumim [55, 56]. On 18 July 2024 he voted against a Knesset resolution opposing a Palestinian state (adopted 68-9) [57]. On 21 February 2024 the nine votes against the government's declaration rejecting unilateral recognition of a Palestinian state came from the two Arab parties; during the debate Tibi said a Palestinian state "will be established" despite the government's opposition [77]. On 30 October 2024 he voted for a proposed Basic Law to recognise a Palestinian state, which was rejected 9-47 [58].

**Statement:** In September 2026 Tibi welcomed a British decision to impose sanctions and a ban on trade in goods from Judea and Samaria, saying "better late than never" and calling the decision "brave and necessary" in view of what he called the escalating "ethnic cleansing" of Palestinians there [83]. In February 2024 he said Israel had tried "settlements, occupation, destruction… there is only one thing it has not tried – ending the occupation and forming a Palestinian state" [78].

**Statement:** On the character of the state itself, Tibi opposes defining Israel as "Jewish and democratic", calling it "an oxymoron", and supports a model of "a state of all its nationalities" in which Arab citizens are recognised as a national minority [30].

**Closest option(s):** Two states on the pre-1967 border with East Jerusalem as the Palestinian capital and evacuation of all settlements (`two-states-pre-1967-border`).

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Action:** Tibi voted against the first reading of the change to how judges are chosen (20 February 2023, passed 63-47) and against the first reading of the law limiting the "reasonableness" standard (11 July 2023, passed 64-56) [53, 54]. He voted against the law changing the attorney general's powers at its final reading on 15 July 2026 (passed 65-51) [74].

**Statement:** In March 2023 Tibi said "there is no doubt we firmly oppose the regime overhaul [changes to the judicial system] and will continue to oppose it" [89]. He also said the Supreme Court had "usually not given relief to Arabs and Palestinians", citing its rulings on the Nation-State Law, admissions committees, settlements, land expropriation and home demolitions, and that it was natural that Arab citizens did not flock to the protests [89, 40]. He said in March 2023 that he admired the protesters' persistence and would join them, and warned that if the legislation passed, "Arabs will be hurt" [40].

**Closest option(s):** Reverse the recent changes and protect the independence of the courts and the attorney general (`repeal-changes-protect-independence`). Tibi combines this with criticism of the Supreme Court's past record on Arab citizens' rights.

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Action:** On 12 November 2025 Tibi voted for two private bills to set up a state commission of inquiry into the events of October 7, which were rejected 45-53 and 44-52; Samir Bin Said voted for the first [60, 61]. On 24 December 2025, during the debate before the coalition's bill for an alternative, Knesset-based inquiry passed its preliminary reading 53-48, Tibi told the Speaker "you are a disgrace" [102].

**Statement:** At the 5 October 2026 campaign launch Tibi said the Joint List would vote immediately after the election to set up a state commission of inquiry into the failures that led to October 7 [34, 36]. In February 2024 he said Netanyahu "is responsible for the Israeli failure of October 7" [79].

**Closest option(s):** A state commission of inquiry whose members are chosen by the President of the Supreme Court under the existing law (`state-commission-under-existing-law`).

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Equality for Arab citizens {#other-arab-citizens-equality}

**Statement:** In February 2026 Tibi said he wants to legislate a Basic Law on equality, repeal the Kaminitz Law, "eradicate crime organisations", and open a university and a government hospital in an Arab town [30]. He has criticised the Supreme Court for not intervening against the Nation-State Law (2018), which defines Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people [89, 80], and said in 2024 that after October 7 the country became "ethnocracy, only for Jews" [80]. He said Arab citizens make up a quarter of Israel's doctors and nearly half of its pharmacists, but thousands of Arab doctors and teachers cannot find jobs [30]. Tibi refers to Arab citizens as "Palestinian citizens of Israel" [78].

### Wartime speech and civil liberties {#other-wartime-speech}

**Statement:** Tibi says Arab citizens have been persecuted for expressing sympathy with Gaza civilians since October 7. In June 2024 he said "hundreds of Arab citizens were hunted down, chased by the Israeli police for writing a post or a story empathising with the children of Gaza or saying no to the war" [80]. In February 2026 he said that after October 7 people in the Knesset called him a "terror supporter", and that he and Ayman Odeh had attended the funeral of Vivian Silver, who was killed at Kibbutz Be'eri, and met hostage families [30].

**Action:** Tibi voted against the April 2024 law allowing the government to shut down foreign broadcasters considered a security risk (used against Al Jazeera), which passed 71-10 [63]. In June 2025 Hadash–Ta'al and Ra'am announced they would boycott a meeting of opposition leaders in protest at opposition parties' support for impeaching Hadash's Ayman Odeh over a social-media post [103].

### Palestinian identity and the Arab parties' role {#other-palestinian-identity}

**Statement:** Tibi describes Arab MKs as "Arab members of Knesset, not Zionists" and says the parties can cooperate "not as Zionists but as proud Arabs" [30]. He has said "the Arab public supports the Palestinian people, because it is a part of it" [78]. He has long called for all four Arab parties to run together, saying in August 2025 that "the four Arab lists must run together on a joint slate" to raise turnout and "ensure the removal of Netanyahu, Smotrich and Ben-Gvir" [21].

### Security prisoners and Marwan Barghouti {#other-security-prisoners}

**Action:** Tibi, represented by the legal aid group Adalah, petitioned the High Court of Justice against the policy restricting MKs' visits to Palestinian security prisoners, after his requests to visit Walid Daqqa and Fatah leader Marwan Barghouti, who is serving five life sentences for murder, went unanswered for months [92, 93]. The Shin Bet opposed a Tibi–Barghouti meeting, saying it was not based on anything Tibi had done but on concern that it would raise Barghouti's status [93]. On 12 April 2026 the court struck down the faction-based visit framework but unanimously rejected Tibi's specific request to meet Barghouti, citing classified security material; one justice wrote that Tibi appeared to have been treated unequally compared with other MKs [92]. In June 2026 Tibi voted against a bill to bar Red Cross visits to security prisoners, which was rejected 36-41 [75].

### Death penalty and counter-terror legislation {#other-death-penalty}

**Action:** On 10 November 2025 Tibi voted against the first reading of the bill imposing the death penalty on terrorists (passed 39-16) [59]. After the law passed on 30 March 2026 (62-47), Hadash–Ta'al called it "an official declaration of the institutionalisation of apartheid and racism" and said it would petition the High Court [100]. Tibi voted against the October 2024 law ending UNRWA's operations in Israel (passed 92-10) [62]. In an October 2024 committee debate on a bill to deport relatives of terrorists, Tibi asked whether the law would also apply to Jewish terrorists [101].

### LGBT rights {#other-lgbt-rights}

**Statement:** In March 2021 Tibi said in an interview with an Arabic channel: "We are against promoting the LGBT phenomenon", that he and Ta'al's MKs would vote against any pro-LGBT law, that he opposed a proposal to replace "father and mother" on official forms, and that "I oppose and Ta'al opposes" taking part in LGBT demonstrations [95].

### Holy places and the muezzin bill {#other-holy-places}

**Statement:** In October 2023 Tibi proposed raising penalties for desecration of the religious symbols of all three monotheistic faiths, saying attacks on mosques and other religious property had gone without arrests or enforcement [94]. He opposed the bill restricting mosque loudspeakers; in June 2026 Israel Hayom reported that Moshe Gafni (United Torah Judaism) had called Tibi to tell him the coalition had pulled the bill and that his party would not support it; Gafni's office said it does not comment on his personal conversations [50]. The bill later passed a preliminary reading on 1 July 2026, 50-36, with United Torah Judaism absent [51].

### Consumer protection {#other-consumer-protection}

**Action:** Tibi's Knesset biography says most of the roughly 12 laws he has passed concern social, economic and consumer matters [7]. The best known is the Aviation Services Law of 2012, known as "the Tibi law", which sets compensation and assistance for passengers whose flights are cancelled [96, 97].

### International pressure and sanctions {#other-international-pressure}

**Statement:** Tibi has called on foreign governments to act against Israeli government policy. In February 2024 he said the US could stop the war in Gaza and should not veto a UN Security Council resolution to end it [78, 79]. In September 2026 he welcomed British sanctions and a trade ban on goods from Judea and Samaria [83].

### Parliamentary plans after the election {#other-parliamentary-plans}

**Statement:** In September 2026 Tibi said the Arab parties would act immediately after the election, even without joining a government, to replace Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana, saying that electing the Speaker right after the election means "total control of the Knesset" [33]. On 5 October 2026 Jabareen said the Joint List would vote, even "without a government being formed", for a law barring people indicted for serious crimes from forming a government [36].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**2026 statements (with dates).**
- **20 February 2026:** Asked whether Ta'al would join the government, Tibi said: "That's not on the table." He said there are "all kinds of ways" to have influence, including "a blocking bloc", recalling the 1992 outside support for Yitzhak Rabin, and that "we will do everything possible to remove [Netanyahu], including things we have not done in the past" [30]. He said Naftali Bennett was "not my cup of tea", that Yair Lapid was "more tolerable" and had worked with him when Lapid was prime minister, and that Yair Golan was "braver" than other leaders [30].
- **10 September 2026:** Tibi told Army Radio "we will do things we have not done in the past… without us there is no change of government", without saying whether this meant outside support for a government [31]. In a Kan Reshet Bet interview he said "we will do things we haven't done so that there won't be a transition prime minister… there are parliamentary moves and contacts, everything can be done" [32].
- **19 September 2026:** Tibi said the Arab parties would vote right after the election to replace the Knesset Speaker, even without entering a government [33].
- **5 October 2026:** Asked whether the Joint List would provide a "safety net" for a minority government, Jabareen did not commit, saying "after the election, we'll know to decide responsibly" [36]. Tibi said the list would "send home" Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir and Smotrich and called the Joint List "the home of all liberal and democratic people, Jews and Arabs" [34, 35]. On 4 October 2026 he criticised the Democrats for supporting Ben-Gvir's request to disqualify Balad's Abu Shehadeh, calling it "a serious mistake" [37].
- **27 August 2026:** Hadash MK Ofer Cassif, also on the Joint List, said the list would "most likely" recommend an alternative candidate such as Gadi Eisenkot if removing Netanyahu depended on it [38].
- The Jerusalem Post summarises the Joint List's position as not committed to any particular coalition, with support for an alternative government depending on negotiations over its demands (Palestinian statehood, equality, action on crime and budgets for Arab communities) [28].

**Other parties' positions toward Ta'al.** Most opposition parties seeking to replace Netanyahu have said they would not form a government with Arab parties [28]. In September 2026 Gadi Eisenkot said any partner must recognise Hamas as a terrorist organisation that must be dismantled, and said of Tibi, "he helped them bring down the government"; Avigdor Liberman ruled out a minority government relying on outside Arab support [39].

**Track record on such pledges.**
- **September 2019 and March 2020:** the Joint List, including Ta'al, recommended Benny Gantz for prime minister (in September 2019 without Balad) [43, 16].
- **2021:** Tibi said in March 2023 that Hadash–Ta'al had recommended Lapid, and that when the Bennett–Lapid government was sworn in, "we did not vote against it. We went outside", and that Lapid thanked him [40].
- **September 2022:** Hadash committed in its agreement with Balad to stay out of any coalition "that promotes a policy of occupation and racism", while leaving open a recommendation of Lapid or Gantz on conditions [16]. After the November 2022 election Hadash–Ta'al recommended no one [41, 42].
- **2023:** Ta'al's MKs never joined a coalition. In January 2023 The Jerusalem Post reported that Tibi had reached an agreement with Likud to give up a Finance Committee seat in exchange for a deputy Speaker post; Tibi denied this [98]. In July 2023 coalition sources said Shas and United Torah Judaism leaders told Ben-Gvir that Tibi had helped the coalition bring down the previous government [99].

Ta'al's record is consistent with its stated position of influencing government formation from outside rather than joining a coalition; no case was found in which Ta'al broke an explicit pledge on whom it would sit with.

## Track record {#track-record}

**Never in government.** Ta'al has never been part of a governing coalition [7, 28]. Its record is as an opposition faction.

**Parliamentary roles.** Tibi has been a Knesset member continuously since 1999, served as Deputy Speaker in several Knessets, chaired the Ra'am–Ta'al faction and now chairs the Hadash–Ta'al faction, and sat on the Finance Committee [7]. He reports passing about 12 laws, mostly on social, economic and consumer issues, including the 2012 Aviation Services Law [7, 96].

**Voting record in the 25th Knesset (2022–2026), from official Knesset vote records:**
- Judicial system: against the first readings of the judicial-selection change (February 2023) and the reasonableness law (July 2023), and against the attorney-general law (July 2026) [53, 54, 74].
- Judea and Samaria: against the sovereignty bills (October 2025) and the July 2024 resolution against a Palestinian state; for a Basic Law to recognise a Palestinian state (October 2024) [55, 56, 57, 58].
- October 7 inquiry: for bills to set up a state commission of inquiry (November 2025) [60].
- Security legislation: against the death-penalty bill (November 2025), the UNRWA law and the foreign-broadcaster law [59, 62, 63].
- Crime: against the bill expanding measures against crime organisations (February 2024); filed or backed no-confidence motions on crime in 2023 and 2026 [64, 65, 66].
- Economy: against the coalition's budget-arrangements laws for 2025 and 2026 [69, 70, 76].
- Torah-study Basic Law: absent from the preliminary and first readings, against at the final reading (see the Haredi enlistment section) [71, 72, 73].

**Promises and delivery.** As an opposition party Ta'al's main stated goals (Palestinian statehood, equality for Arab citizens, reducing crime, repealing the Kaminitz Law) have not been achieved; it has not held the executive power needed to deliver them. Its own claimed achievements are mainly consumer and social legislation and the formation of joint Arab lists [7, 26].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

- **2003 disqualification overturned:** Before the 2003 election the CEC disqualified Tibi from running; the Supreme Court cancelled the disqualification, and he was elected [7].
- **2012 Ethics Committee sanction:** On 17 January 2012 the Knesset Ethics Committee barred Tibi from plenum debates and committee meetings for one week (he could still vote), following a complaint by MK Anastasia Michaeli (Yisrael Beytenu) over a speech in verse in which he mocked her [91]. The sanction has long since ended.
- **2026 request to investigate declined:** On 6 January 2026 the police asked the State Attorney's office for approval to open a criminal investigation against Tibi on suspicion of insulting a public servant, after he called the police spokesman "zero" and other insults in a television debate. In February 2026 Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara, following the recommendation of the State Attorney and his deputy, declined to approve an investigation, ruling that the remarks were made as part of his public activity as an MK and are covered by substantive immunity [90]. No investigation was opened.
- **2026 list disqualification overturned:** The CEC voted 18-5 on 23 September 2026 to disqualify the Joint List on a Likud request under the Basic Law provision barring lists that support armed struggle against Israel; on 2 October 2026 the Supreme Court unanimously overturned the decision, finding the evidence did not meet the legal standard [5, 4]. The proceedings against individual candidates concerned Hadash's Ofer Cassif (allowed to run, 7-2) and Balad's Sami Abu Shehadeh (withdrew), not Ta'al's candidates [4].

No indictments, convictions, State Comptroller findings or other official inquiries against Ta'al's leaders were found. ## Information availability {#information-availability}

Enough public information exists. Ta'al's leader has served in the Knesset since 1999, so there is an official biography and a full per-MK voting record for the 25th Knesset (2022–2026), and Tibi gives frequent interviews in Hebrew, Arabic and English.

Gaps a reader should know about:
- **No separate Ta'al platform was found.** Ta'al runs only on joint lists. The 2026 programme documents found are those of the Joint List as a whole: an economic plan and a crime plan published on its website. Its positions on Gaza and on a Palestinian state are known only from a review of party platforms by Zo Haderech, the weekly of the Communist Party (Hadash's registered party), which is a partisan source.
- **Ta'al's own record is mostly Tibi's record.** Ta'al has had one or two MKs, and from July 2026 only Tibi, as Samir Bin Said moved to Hadash (he remains an MK in the joint Hadash–Ta'al faction). Positions of Ta'al's other 2026 candidates (Ahmad Darawshe, Haitham Zahalka) are almost undocumented.
- **No documented Ta'al position** on Jewish religion-and-state arrangements (marriage, conversion, Shabbat, the Chief Rabbinate), on how Haredi yeshiva students should be treated beyond Tibi calling it a "Jewish" dispute, or on Hezbollah and the 2026 fighting in Lebanon.
- No Ta'al reaction was found specifically to the July 2026 government decision to bring the Shin Bet into the fight against crime.
