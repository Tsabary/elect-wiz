## Overview {#overview}

**Yashar LeYisrael with Eisenkot** (ישר לישראל עם איזנקוט, usually called "Yashar!", which means "straight" or "honest") is a party founded by Gadi Eisenkot, a former IDF Chief of Staff. Eisenkot announced the party, its name and its founding group on September 16, 2025 [2]. The founders named at the launch included Yoav Horowitz, a former director-general of the Prime Minister's Office; Matan Kahana, a former minister; Tal Russo, a former head of the IDF Southern Command and former MK; economist Manuel Trajtenberg, who led the government committee set up after the 2011 social protests; Shir Siegel, daughter of former hostages Keith and Aviva Siegel; Wix president Nir Zohar; and Inbar Harush-Giti, a former director of the "Acharai!" youth organization and former head of the Defense Ministry's national-service outline directorate [2]. The party described its mission as "repair, healing and hope for Israeli society", putting Israel's security and national interests first, building a broad governing alternative and keeping Israel "Jewish, democratic and strong" [2].

**Predecessor.** Yashar has no formal predecessor party. Eisenkot entered the Knesset in November 2022 for the National Unity party (המחנה הממלכתי), led by Benny Gantz [4]. He announced on June 30, 2025 that he was leaving National Unity and the Knesset, and handed his resignation to the Knesset Speaker on July 2, 2025. His seat went to Eitan Ginzburg [3]. When he resigned he said he would work to "replace this bad government and establish a Zionist, patriotic, statesmanlike, democratic alternative" [3].

**Ideology and base.** The party describes itself as statist (ממלכתי) and Zionist. Its platform calls for Israel to be "Jewish, democratic, liberal and Zionist" [22], and builds most of its programmes around one principle: "whoever serves and works is prioritised and rewarded" [17]. Its central themes are a law requiring military or civilian service from everyone, including Haredim and Arab citizens [16]; state education with a shared core curriculum [19]; a state commission of inquiry into October 7 [9, 28]; and a security-first foreign policy that rejects a Palestinian state while opposing some settlement-expansion decisions [9, 12]. The Jewish Chronicle describes Yashar as generally seen as to the left of Likud and to the right of the centre-left parties, with a strong security focus [15]. Its candidate list is a mix of former security chiefs, former MKs from National Unity and Yamina, educators, local leaders and bereaved families; i24NEWS described it as focused on security, with members drawn from different sectors of Israeli society [46]. Eisenkot said the list reflects the party's commitment to equality between men and women [111].

**Knesset seats.** Yashar did not exist at the 2022 election and holds no Knesset seats as a party [15]. Some of its candidates were elected in 2022 on other lists (see Leadership).

**List and ballot letters.** Yashar runs on the joint list **"ישר! עם איזנקוט לראשות הממשלה מאחדים את ישראל"** ("Yashar! with Eisenkot for Prime Minister – Uniting Israel"), ballot letters **דרך**. The Central Elections Committee (CEC) candidate page records that the list was submitted by two registered parties: **Yashar LeYisrael with Eisenkot** and **Yesodot Yisrael** (יסודות ישראל, "Foundations of Israel") [1].

**Joint list and partner.** The partner is Yesodot Yisrael, the party of MK Chili Tropper. Tropper is No. 6 on the list, and his allies Shira Shapira and Elyasaf Peretz are Nos. 10 and 20 [1]. Ynetnews reported on September 6, 2026, two days before lists closed, that Tropper had split with Yoaz Hendel (their "Zionist Home–Reservists" alliance) and was moving toward an alliance with Eisenkot. It reported that the arrangement under discussion would **not** be a technical bloc, so Tropper could not split off into a separate faction after the election, and that Tropper would get first choice of a ministerial portfolio if Yashar enters government [44]. The Jerusalem Post reported the same day that Tropper was weighing this move together with Shapira and Peretz as a group [45]. **Split history:** this is the first election the two parties have run together, so they have no history of splitting after an election. Tropper sat in the Knesset for Blue and White/National Unity, not for Yashar [40, 44, 49].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Gadi Eisenkot (No. 1, party leader and candidate for prime minister).** Eisenkot was born in Tiberias to parents who had immigrated from Morocco. He enlisted in the Golani Brigade at 18 and served 41 years in the IDF [9]. He was sworn in as the IDF's 21st Chief of Staff on February 16, 2015, succeeding Benny Gantz [47]. Earlier posts included Golani Brigade commander, military secretary to Prime Minister Ehud Barak (2000), commander of the Judea and Samaria Division (2003), head of the General Staff Operations Directorate and head of the Northern Command around the 2006 Lebanon war, and Deputy Chief of Staff [47]. He was the first IDF Chief of Staff of Moroccan descent [92]. Aviv Kochavi replaced him on January 15, 2019 [52]. He entered politics in August 2022 with National Unity [9]. Knesset records show he was an MK from November 15, 2022 to July 4, 2025, and a **minister without portfolio from October 12, 2023 to June 13, 2024** [4]. That was the emergency government formed after the October 7 attack, in which he sat as an observer in the war cabinet [9, 53]. He left the government with Benny Gantz, who announced on June 9, 2024 that they were leaving because "Netanyahu is preventing us from progressing to a real victory" [53]. His son Gal Eisenkot, a soldier in the Maglan commando unit, was killed in combat in northern Gaza on December 7, 2023 [9]. As an MK he was lead sponsor of the **National Security Strategy Law, 2025**, which passed its final reading [5, 6] (see Track record).

**Yoram Cohen (No. 2).** Cohen headed the Shin Bet (Israel's internal security service) from 2011 to 2016, under Prime Minister Netanyahu, after more than 35 years in the security services [37, 38]. He grew up in the religious Zionist movement. He worked with Eisenkot against terrorism during the Second Intifada (2003–2005) and joined Yashar on May 5, 2026, after talks with Naftali Bennett's party [37, 38]. He leads the security part of Yashar's plan for Arab society [33].

**Orit Farkash-Hacohen (No. 3).** Knesset records show that Farkash-Hacohen was elected for Blue and White in April 2019 and has sat in every Knesset since. She was Minister of Strategic Affairs (May–November 2020), Minister of Tourism (October 2020–June 2021) and Minister of Innovation, Science and Technology (June 2021–December 2022), and was a Deputy Speaker of the Knesset from October 2023 to October 2025 [41]. She sat in the National Unity faction until July 2025 and then in the "Blue and White – National Unity" faction, where Knesset records still list her as an MK [41]. On July 24, 2025 she told Gantz she wished to end her membership of the faction in order to join Eisenkot, and said she would not resign from the Knesset so as not to give the coalition an extra seat [110]. Before politics she chaired the Electricity Authority. Al-Monitor reported in August 2015 that a session was convened on August 6, 2015 to remove her, after she warned that the government's natural-gas plan would raise household electricity bills and refused to approve gas contracts with the Tamar field partners [50]. In May 2026, after she moved to Yashar, Gantz asked her to resign her Knesset seat [40].

**Adi Altschuler (No. 4).** Altschuler is an educator and social entrepreneur. She founded the Krembo Wings youth movement for young people with and without disabilities, inclusive schools and kindergartens, and the "Zikaron BaSalon" Holocaust remembrance initiative. She is an Israel Prize laureate. Eisenkot announced on September 6, 2026 that she is the party's candidate for Minister of Education [39].

**Matan Kahana (No. 5).** Kahana is a former Air Force pilot [51]. Knesset records show he was elected in October 2019 for the Yamina alliance (New Right), served as **Minister of Religious Services from June 2021 to May 2022** and then as Deputy Minister of Religious Services until August 2022, moved to the National Unity faction in August 2022, and left the Knesset on July 8, 2025 [42]. He left National Unity together with Eisenkot [51] and was a founder of Yashar in September 2025 [2]. As Minister of Religious Services he passed a reform that lets private bodies supervise kashrut (kosher certification), over the opposition of the Chief Rabbinate, and he advanced a conversion reform [43, 87] (see Religion and state and Track record).

**Chili Tropper (No. 6, Yesodot Yisrael).** He leads the partner party (see Overview) [1, 44]. Knesset records show he was elected for Blue and White in April 2019, was **Minister of Culture and Sport from May 2020 to December 2022** (and briefly also Minister of Science and Technology in 2021), and served as a **minister without portfolio in the emergency government from October 12, 2023 to June 13, 2024** [49]. He sat in the National Unity faction until July 2025 and since then in the "Blue and White – National Unity" faction, where he remains an MK [49].

**Further down the list** (CEC order [1]): No. 7 Shaul Meridor, an economist [109]; No. 10 Shira Shapira (Yesodot Yisrael), an architect whose son Aner was killed on October 7 [46]; No. 11 Inbar Harush-Giti, a party founder who leads its work on national service [2, 33]; No. 12 Elazar Stern; and No. 13 Kamil Abu Rokan, a retired IDF major general from the Druze community [46].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar's economic programme promises an "uncompromising fight against the cost of living". It would cancel import barriers and Israel-only standards "that have no justification", open the market to parallel imports, break the power of concentrated groups in the food and consumer markets while protecting Israeli agriculture, reform kashrut rules and the import of kosher goods, cut red tape for producers and importers, and remove barriers to foreign companies and new competitors [17]. The youth programme repeats the same competition measures [20].

**Formal commitment:** On taxes, the programme proposes lowering income tax and paying for it with taxes on products with "clear negative effects", such as disposable tableware and sweetened drinks. Property-tax discounts, tax exemptions and allowances would be given by socio-economic need, measured after the person's earning capacity has been used, and not by sector. It also promises a campaign against the black economy: real-time digital reporting of large transactions, much less use of cash, and AI tools to find fake invoices [17]. Tax credits would be reshaped to reward those who carry the economic and security burden, so that "work always pays more than an allowance" [17].

**Formal commitment:** The party also offers targeted help, mostly for people who serve: a 15% raise for all conscripts and a 50% raise for combat soldiers in their third year [20, 21, 30], priority for working and serving families in daycare subsidies and housing benefits [17], and higher old-age allowances for low-income pensioners, Holocaust survivors and immigrants with small pensions [25]. **Statement:** In August 2026 Farkash-Hacohen (No. 3) told The Media Line that Yashar would close ministries it regards as unnecessary, reduce government spending tied to coalition politics, and review subsidies it believes discourage work [14].

**Action (bills by top candidates):** In June 2023 Farkash-Hacohen, Tropper and Kahana were among dozens of MKs from across the Knesset who co-sponsored a bill to cut VAT to 8% for two years on price-controlled products and basic foods such as bread, milk, meat, eggs, flour and oil. It did not advance past the preliminary stage [75]. In June 2025 Farkash-Hacohen and Kahana co-sponsored a bill to give tax benefits to factories that compete in highly concentrated markets, citing the 2011 Trajtenberg committee's finding that concentration is a main cause of high prices. It was stopped [83]. Trajtenberg, who led that committee, is a Yashar founder [2].

**Action (in government, 2021):** Kahana, Tropper and Farkash-Hacohen were ministers in the government that passed the 2021 Arrangements Law. Besides the kashrut reform, it included an import reform that Srugim reported was expected to save consumers about NIS 6 billion a year [87, 41, 42, 49].

**Action (budget votes):** Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against the 2023–2024 state budget and the accompanying economic arrangements law in May 2023 [58, 93], and against the 2025 budget at first reading in December 2024 [91] and at its final reading on March 24, 2025 [106]. Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against the 2026 budget in its final reading on March 30, 2026 [102].

**Closest option(s):** Open the market to more competition and imports (`open-market-competition-imports`). The platform also includes targeted support for people who serve and low-income pensioners (`direct-support-for-households`), and an income-tax cut paired with new consumption taxes.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar's internal-security programme promises a "national emergency plan" against crime. It includes a national cabinet against organized crime, headed by the prime minister with a dedicated government project manager; a multi-year plan to strengthen the police, with a four-year emergency budget for staff, investigators, intelligence and technology; a dedicated enforcement system for organized crime with its own investigation and intelligence units, a dedicated prosecution unit and specialised courts; and wider use of asset forfeiture and administrative tools [18]. It promises that police independence "will be anchored in legislation" and that politicians will stop dealing with police appointments and investigations [18].

**Formal commitment:** A specific section on crime in Arab society calls for "joint action with the leadership of Arab society and the community", with Arab society actively taking part. This starts with **compulsory civilian service for young Arab citizens**, partly directed at fighting crime, alongside targeted enforcement and more technology [18]. On June 24, 2026 the party presented a plan for Arab society covering personal security, state education and civilian service. Yoram Cohen, a former Shin Bet head, leads its security part. Inbar Harush-Giti said that bringing young Arab citizens into civilian service "can help deal with the challenges of education, violence and crime" [33].

**What wasn't found:** No Yashar statement was found for or against the government's July 2026 decision to bring the Shin Bet into the fight against crime organizations, or for or against holding suspects without trial. Searches covered Hebrew and English news and the party's programmes.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is mixed. The core is a large police and enforcement build-up under normal law, with new dedicated units, prosecutors and courts (`more-police-resources-normal-law`). It is combined with community partnership, civilian service and state education for young Arab citizens, which overlaps with `invest-in-arab-communities-first`. The party has not taken a documented position on Shin Bet involvement or detention without trial.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

**Statement:** In an interview published on October 6, 2026, Eisenkot said he would advance "President Trump's 20-point postwar plan, but strictly from a position of strength". He said Hamas's "total disarmament" is a condition before governance can be restored and Gaza rebuilt [9]. In September 2026 he said only one of the war's goals, the return of the hostages, had been achieved [10].

**Statement:** The Jerusalem Post reported in early September 2026 that Eisenkot is open to the Palestinian Authority or another replacement taking over governance from Hamas. It also reported that he supports rebuilding in stages, tied to how much disarmament has actually happened, and that he accuses Prime Minister Netanyahu of having "kept Hamas in power in the Strip" [11]. Eisenkot says he agrees with the majority view in the defense establishment that, if the choice is between the PA and Hamas, "Hamas is much worse" [11].

**Statement (about his role as a minister, 2023):** Eisenkot says that during the November 2023 hostage negotiations he was "the lone voice" in the war cabinet arguing for continuing the deal and releasing hostages in stages before fighting resumed [9].

**Action (Knesset votes):** On October 28, 2024 Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper all voted for the law ending the activity of UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, in Israeli sovereign territory. It passed 92–10 [64]. On December 6, 2023, while National Unity was part of the emergency government, all four voted against the wartime supplementary budget at first reading, which passed 62–53 [59]. The Times of Israel reported that the faction opposed the budget because Gantz's public request to remove discretionary coalition funds from it was ignored; at the final vote on December 14, 2023 its MKs were not in the hall, though their opposition was recorded in the protocol [60].

**Closest option(s):** Move forward with the US plan, with gradual disarmament and withdrawal (`advance-international-arrangement`). Eisenkot puts strong emphasis on Hamas disarming before reconstruction, which overlaps with `hold-positions-disarmament-first`. No statement was found supporting renewed full-scale war, encouraging emigration from Gaza, or Israeli settlement in Gaza.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar promises to pass a **"State Service for All" law**. Every citizen would be required to serve, with the IDF first and civilian or national service for those the IDF doesn't take. The IDF would choose conscripts by its needs "while preserving their way of life" [16]. A new independent national service administration would summon all young people for screening, with no "sectoral, communal or party-political hand" in the process [16]. Each year **up to 3%** of the age group (the exact share set by the defense establishment's needs) could defer service by one year at a time to study Torah. Selection would be open, with external and equal criteria, and those deferred would still be drafted and do basic training. Deferrals would be renewed yearly under supervision, and false reporting would cancel the deferral and lead to criminal proceedings [16].

**Formal commitment:** Those who are not exempt and don't serve would lose a long list of state benefits. These include state tenders, scholarships, property-tax discounts, priority in housing programmes, National Insurance benefits, transport discounts, mortgage and rent aid, long-term rental eligibility, land allocations, purchase-tax and capital-gains exemptions on a single home, support for Torah institutions, and even a driving licence and the right to leave the country. They would also face criminal law [16]. People who serve would get priority in benefits, ranked: combat veterans first, then other military veterans, then civilian-service veterans [16].

**Statement:** Eisenkot told the Jerusalem Post in September 2026 that he proposes a "universal service law. Everyone must serve: haredim and Arabs", with about 3% of Haredim eligible for exemptions [10]. In October 2026 he called the Haredi slogan "we will die rather than enlist" "un-Jewish behavior" [9]. In May 2026 he said the army must grow and repeated his call to draft Haredim into the IDF and Arab citizens into national service [36].

**Action (2023 bill):** On May 9, 2023 Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper co-sponsored, with Benny Gantz and others, the **"Israeli Service" bill** [70]. It would have required every citizen and permanent resident to do at least 24 months of service in the IDF, the rescue services or community service, with the IDF choosing first. Christian and Muslim Arab citizens would serve in the civilian tracks [70]. Graduates of Haredi schools could ask to fulfil their duty through **a track that combines study with service**, provided they study at least 45 hours a week in a Haredi yeshiva and don't work; the bill set no numerical cap on this track [70]. The bill would also have abolished yearly enlistment targets and set the exemption age at 24. Sanctions for those who don't comply were administrative fines and a ban on state employment and state tenders, and up to two years in prison for breaching a court order to report or knowingly giving false information [70]. The bill did not pass.

**Statement (2024, as a minister):** On February 26, 2024, while in the emergency government, Gantz and Eisenkot presented an enlistment outline. Under it most young people would serve, and a new administration would decide each person's type of service [8].

**Action (Knesset vote, 2024):** On June 11, 2024 Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against reviving a 2022 enlistment bill from the previous Knesset. The motion passed 63–57 [61]. The Jerusalem Post reported that the bill would change the age of exemption for yeshiva students, offer short or alternative service tracks, and cut funding to yeshivas that miss enlistment quotas [62].

**Change over time (not a contradiction):** Both the 2023 bill and the 2026 platform require universal service and include criminal penalties, so the earlier bill does not contradict the current position. But the 2026 platform is stricter on Torah study. The 2023 "Israeli Service" bill that Eisenkot and the other top candidates co-sponsored would have let any Haredi school graduate who studies full time in a yeshiva ask to fulfil his duty through a track combining study with service, with no cap on numbers [70]. Yashar's 2026 platform instead caps Torah-study deferrals at up to 3% of each age group, for one year at a time, and requires those deferred to do basic training [16].

**Closest option(s):** Require everyone, including Haredim, to serve in the army or civilian service, with a small quota of Torah scholars and loss of benefits for those who refuse (`universal-service-with-sanctions`).

### Housing {#issue-housing}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar's economic programme says it will "flood the market with new apartments", tax land that is not being advanced for construction, and use the need for bomb shelters as an engine of urban renewal (rebuilding older buildings) [17]. It ties housing to transport: finishing a fast rail network linking the periphery to the centre, and speeding up metro and light-rail lines in the big cities [17]. **Statement:** In August 2026 Farkash-Hacohen told The Media Line that there are no "magic solutions" for housing, and that better transport and infrastructure could widen the realistic housing market instead of relying on construction policy alone [14].

**Formal commitment:** For renters, the youth programme promises to stop rents rising and bring them down, to publish anonymised rental and sale prices for each street, to make landlords rather than tenants pay brokerage fees, backed by a new enforcement unit, and to create subsidised legal aid and fast-track dispute resolution for renters [20]. It also promises help for working and serving young couples buying a first home [20], and reservists would get dedicated land allocations and long-term rental projects [21].

**Formal commitment:** The northern recovery plan includes urban renewal in Kiryat Shmona, a framework agreement for thousands of new homes, and rent-to-own and rental tracks for people in essential professions who move north [22].

**Closest option(s):** Build much more, faster (`speed-up-planning-and-construction`), combined with investment in transport and the periphery (`develop-periphery-and-transport`). There are secondary elements of rental-market regulation and help for buyers who serve.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

**Statement:** Eisenkot calls Iran Israel's most dangerous enemy and says it could reach nuclear capability "within several months" [32]. In October 2026 he cited Iran's 440 kg of enriched uranium and proposed a "comprehensive plan… to act to destroy the capability", coordinated with the United States and including an "intrusive inspection regime" [9]. The Jerusalem Post reported in September 2026 that he wants a US commitment to strike any movement of Iran's enriched uranium, and wants covert Mossad operations widened to cover Iran's ballistic missiles [11].

**Statement:** On Lebanon, the Jerusalem Post reported that Eisenkot is worried about some 2,000 Hezbollah fighters still in southern Lebanon. He doubts the Lebanese army will act alone, would be willing in some circumstances to go back to an eight-kilometre security zone, and wants US and French backing to push the Lebanese army to confront Hezbollah [11]. In October 2026 he said there "could also be an opportunity with Lebanon", and that improving the 1974 separation agreement with Syria would require "the total removal of the Turks and the Russians" [9].

**Formal commitment:** The party's ten-point plan calls for a new national security doctrine and for Israel to become a regional power, while working to expand "the circle of peace" [28]. **Statement:** Eisenkot proposes a "multi-theater initiative with the American administration", strengthening ties with Egypt, Jordan and the Abraham Accords countries, and actively bringing in more states, including building toward a "NATO of the Sunni states" [9].

**Closest option(s):** Keep up military pressure and turn it into US-backed agreements (`force-plus-us-backed-agreements`).

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

**Statement:** Eisenkot rejects a Palestinian state. In August 2026, after the Ra'am leader called for one, he pledged there would be no Palestinian state, "not now, not in the next government" [13]. He said "anyone who is thinking about a two-state solution after October 7 is just delusional" [12]. In October 2026 he described the arrangement he has in mind as "autonomy or a state-minus" that "certainly will not have an army, and will be under overriding Israeli security control" [9].

**Statement:** He also says "the Palestinian Authority needs to be allowed to govern the 2.8 million Palestinians. I do not want to govern them", and proposes improving living conditions with investment from wealthy Sunni states [9].

**Statement (settlements):** "I support settlements provided they conform strictly to the map of Israel's security interests and to the law." He says illegal outposts in Areas A and B would be "evacuated immediately" and that everything legally approved in Area C "will remain" [9]. On building in the E1 area near Jerusalem he said "Not now. This is a mistake that costs us an international price" [12].

**Statement (security and law enforcement):** He says that giving the police to Minister Ben Gvir and the Civil Administration (COGAT) to Minister Smotrich cost the IDF its control in the area [12]. He would dismantle Smotrich's powers in the Defense Ministry, return civil affairs to the military system, and restore administrative detention orders against Jewish extremists who commit violence [9, 10, 11].

**Action (Knesset votes by top candidates):**
- On July 18, 2024 the Knesset adopted a resolution, 68–9, at the end of a debate on international recognition of a Palestinian state. Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted for it. Eisenkot and Kahana are not recorded as voting [63]. The resolution stated that the Knesset "adamantly" opposes the establishment of a Palestinian state west of the Jordan River, arguing such a state could serve as a base for terrorist groups [95].
- On October 22, 2025, after Eisenkot and Kahana had left the Knesset, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted for a bill applying Israeli sovereignty to Ma'ale Adumim, a large settlement east of Jerusalem. It passed its preliminary reading 31–9 [65, 66]. Neither of them is recorded as voting on the same day's broader bill to apply sovereignty to all of Judea and Samaria, which passed 25–24 [94].

**Statement (reported):** The Jewish Chronicle reports that Eisenkot opposes a formal annexation of the West Bank, arguing that Israel must preserve a Jewish majority and avoid a single state [15].

**What wasn't found:** No direct statement by Eisenkot or the party, and no platform text, was found supporting or opposing applying Israeli sovereignty to all or part of Judea and Samaria, including the Jordan Valley. Besides the Jewish Chronicle's summary [15], the only record found is the Ma'ale Adumim vote by Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper [65].

**Closest option(s):** Keep the current situation, with no Palestinian state and no applied sovereignty, settlements kept within security needs, and Israeli security control (`maintain-current-situation`). Eisenkot differs from that option's wording in promising to evacuate illegal outposts and to oppose E1 construction for now.

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

**Statement:** Eisenkot said in October 2026 that his government would immediately cancel the law changing the Judicial Selection Committee, cancel "measures aimed at weakening the attorney general", and repeal the law that moved the department for investigating judges. It would also pass a law preventing a criminal defendant from forming a government, limit a prime minister's tenure to eight years, and cap the cabinet at 24 ministers [9].

**Formal commitment:** The economic programme promises legislation to "ensure the independence of the justice system", presented as a way to restore the confidence of the high-tech sector and foreign investors [17]. Under the heading of strengthening democracy, the ten-point plan commits to an independent judicial system and a free press, a Basic Law: Legislation that would strengthen the Knesset's independence and authority, other laws setting agreed "rules of the game", a limit of two terms for a prime minister, and an end to political appointments in the civil service [28]. **Statement (reported):** The Jewish Chronicle reports that Yashar opposes changes that limit the Supreme Court's powers and supports a written constitution that would bring the Basic Laws together in one document [15].

**Action (Knesset votes, 2023):** Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against the government's 2023 changes to the judicial system in both first-reading votes found:
- the first reading of an amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary on February 20, 2023, passed 63–47 [54];
- the first reading of the "reasonableness" amendment on July 11, 2023, passed 64–56. That amendment limits the courts' power to cancel government and ministerial decisions as "unreasonable" [55, 56].

The opposition boycotted the final vote on the reasonableness law on July 24, 2023, which passed 64–0 [56]. It also boycotted the final vote on the law changing the Judicial Selection Committee on March 27, 2025 [57], which passed 67–1. None of the four is recorded as voting [107]. That law was the same Basic Law amendment whose first reading they had opposed in February 2023 [54, 107]. Eisenkot was still an MK on both dates [4].

**Action (bills):**
- In December 2024 Tropper, Eisenkot, Kahana and Farkash-Hacohen co-sponsored a Basic Law amendment. It would make the most senior Supreme Court justice automatically become court president if the Judicial Selection Committee doesn't choose one within 60 days. The sponsors' explanation says a permanent, independent president is needed to protect the judiciary's independence [72].
- The same group co-sponsored a Basic Law amendment limiting a prime minister to eight consecutive years [71].
- In May 2023 Eisenkot, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper co-sponsored, with Gantz and others, a bill writing the right to equality into Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. It bans discrimination on grounds including religion, sex, sexual orientation, origin, age and disability [76].
- Eisenkot co-sponsored a bill to convene the Knesset Ethics Committee over ministers giving information to the Knesset [48].
None of these bills passed.

**Action (votes by top candidates after Eisenkot left):** On October 29, 2025 Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against bills to split the role of the attorney general. They passed their preliminary reading 61–46 [67]. On June 2, 2026 they also voted against the first reading of the Attorney General (Opinions, Representation and Oversight) Law, which passed 65–47 [105].

**Consistency check:** No vote or bill was found in which the leaders supported the government's changes to the judicial system. Their record matches Eisenkot's stated plan to reverse them [9].

**Closest option(s):** Reverse the recent changes and protect the independence of the courts and attorney general (`repeal-changes-protect-independence`). The reported support for a written constitution [15] and the planned Basic Law: Legislation [28] overlap with `broad-agreed-constitutional-framework`.

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

**Formal commitment:** A state commission of inquiry into October 7 is the first item of Yashar's ten-point plan [28]. It was also presented as a core pillar at the party's launch conference on June 30, 2026 [31].

**Formal commitment / Statement:** In August 2026, at Kibbutz Nir Oz and together with former hostage Gadi Moses, Eisenkot presented a draft framework for the commission. Under the draft, the government would recommend that the **President of the Supreme Court** decide the commission's members, after consulting the deputy president, the President of Israel and the Knesset Speaker. The commission would examine the events and decisions of the political leadership, the military and security establishment, and the relevant civilian state bodies before October 7, 2023, starting from the decade after the 2014 Gaza operation (Protective Edge). The draft was published for public comment until October 7, 2026 [32]. Eisenkot committed to set up the commission "in the first government session" [32] and said the inquiry should cover "the last 10 years" [10]. In October 2026 he said: "We will immediately pass a government decision establishing a formal state commission of inquiry" [9].

**Action (bills by Farkash-Hacohen, No. 3):** Farkash-Hacohen sponsored three bills to set up the commission:
- In June 2024 she sponsored a bill to set up a five-member state commission of inquiry into October 7 and the war, with the **President of the Supreme Court appointing its chair and members** within 30 days [73]. She resubmitted it in February 2025 [96].
- In February 2025 she sponsored a bill to make a state commission of inquiry **mandatory**, without a government decision, after "exceptional events": a war lasting over three months, a surprise attack in which enemy forces held Israeli territory for over 24 hours, or an event in which more than 30 people were killed or taken hostage. The Supreme Court president or deputy president would head it and choose members after consulting the President of Israel. At least two members would be women and one would represent bereaved or affected families [74].
None of these bills passed.

**Action (vote):** On December 24, 2025 Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against a bill for a "state-national" commission of inquiry into October 7. It passed its preliminary reading 53–48 [68].

**Closest option(s):** A state commission of inquiry whose members are chosen by the President of the Supreme Court, as the law requires (`state-commission-under-existing-law`). Yashar adds a consultation step with the President of Israel and the Knesset Speaker.

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar's immigration programme commits to "inclusive and respectful Judaism… while widening the independence of local authorities and communities to shape Shabbat in the public sphere according to their character and values." On personal status it says: "We will work to institutionalise civil unions in Israel, promote respectful conversion processes", and allow burial of terror victims in the IDF's manner [23].

**Formal commitment:** In education, Yashar would make the State Education Law and core studies a condition for full public funding. It would require non-state institutions, especially Haredi ones, to join a Haredi state stream with full core studies and state supervision, and would significantly cut funding for institutions that don't meet the state standard [19]. The national core would include "Jewish-Israeli identity", Hebrew, English, mathematics, science, AI, critical thinking, and citizenship and democracy [19]. Eisenkot said in October 2026 that "any institution that does not enter the state education system within four years will simply not receive state funding" [9], and in September 2026 that "whoever is not in state education… will not receive funding from the state" [10]. **Statement:** In December 2025 he called state education, in its Hebrew, religious and Haredi streams, "the ticket into Israeliness" [29].

**Action (by a top candidate, in government):** As Minister of Religious Services, Matan Kahana (No. 5) led a kashrut reform that passed as part of the Arrangements Law on November 4, 2021 [87]. It opened kosher certification to competition: private certification bodies, alongside local rabbinates, could certify businesses under Chief Rabbinate standards, with the Chief Rabbinate supervising [87]. Maariv reported that the reform formally took effect in January 2023, but successive religious services ministers kept the previous arrangements in place through interim orders for three years, so private certification was hardly implemented. On July 15, 2026 the Knesset passed a government amendment (Amendment No. 5) returning certification authority only to public bodies, the Chief Rabbinate and local rabbis and religious councils [88]. Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper voted against that amendment at its first reading on June 2, 2026 [104] and at its final reading on July 15, 2026, which passed 46–41 [108]. On July 13, 2026 they also voted against Basic Law: Torah Study, which passed 63–52 [103]. In February 2022 the cabinet's legislative committee approved Kahana's conversion reform for a first reading [43]. Knesset records show that the government's 2022 conversion bill was waiting for a first reading when that Knesset ended, and it went no further [97].

**Action (bills by top candidates):**
- In November 2024 Kahana sponsored a bill requiring that women make up about 47% of the body that elects the Chief Rabbis and the Chief Rabbinate Council, without changing its rabbinic majority [78].
- In January 2023 Tropper co-sponsored a bill to strengthen the status of "integrated" state schools, which teach religious and secular pupils together [79].
- The 2023 bill writing equality into a Basic Law, co-sponsored by Eisenkot, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper, includes religion and sexual orientation among banned grounds of discrimination [76].
None of these bills passed.

**Statement (reported):** The Jewish Chronicle reports that Yashar favours civil marriage, same-sex marriage and letting each municipality decide on Shabbat [15]. The civil-union and local-Shabbat points match the party's own platform [23]. No platform text on same-sex marriage was found on the party's site.

**Closest option(s):** Keep the Rabbinate but allow alternatives: civil unions and local decisions on Shabbat (`more-choice-local-decisions`).

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Reservists and soldiers {#other-reservists-and-soldiers}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar promises to invest **NIS 20 billion over three years** in reservists, their families, livelihoods and employers [21]. It would cap reserve duty during a prolonged "routine of fighting" at **50 days a year and 150 days over three years**, as a four-year temporary measure, and require at least six months' notice of reserve duty [21]. Other measures include full compensation to employers from National Insurance for social contributions during reserve duty, grants for reservists' families, land and long-term rental housing for combat reservists, and expanding the "Mimadim LeLimudim" scholarship so combat soldiers get 100% of their degree paid [21]. Conscripts would get a 15% pay rise, and combat soldiers in their third year 50% [21, 30].

**Action (2024–2025, as an MK):** Eisenkot was lead sponsor of a bill to make all IDF servicemembers eligible for the "Mimadim LeLimudim" fund. The coalition voted it down on November 20, 2024 [7], and Knesset records list it as stopped [48]. He also co-sponsored a bill exempting reserve-duty pay from income tax and adding tax credits for active reservists, which also did not advance [48].

### National security strategy {#other-national-security-strategy}

**Action:** In July 2023 Eisenkot sponsored a "National Security Concept" bill. Its explanatory notes argue that Israel's security doctrine has been informal since David Ben-Gurion, that the state should have a formal one, and that such a document would be "a compass" for strengthening national security and a tool for the country's leaders [80]. That bill was stopped [98], but the idea returned in a later bill. The National Security Strategy Law, 2025, sponsored by Eisenkot and Yuli Edelstein, passed its second and third readings on June 10, 2025 by 19–0 [5, 6]. It requires the National Security Council to prepare a national security strategy for each new government, which the government must approve within about five months. Only a summary is published [6]. Eisenkot said that after October 7 the law reflects shared lessons and "is meant to serve as a compass" for long-term planning [6].

### Northern Israel recovery {#other-north-recovery}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar promises about **NIS 15 billion over five years** for northern Israel, including about **NIS 6 billion for bomb shelters** [22]. It would complete shelters within 9 km of the northern border by 2030 instead of 2035: detached homes 1–5 km from the border by the end of 2028, those 5–9 km away by the end of 2029, and urban housing by the end of 2030 [22]. The plan includes a 300-officer district police unit against serious crime and protection rackets, lower corporate tax rates in the border area (2.5%) and the northern district (5%), business property-tax cuts in 2026–2028, a forward emergency medical centre in Kiryat Shmona, more informal-education funding per pupil, and childcare subsidies for working families near the border who have served [22]. The Media Line also reported special tax benefits for Kiryat Shmona and incentives for young families to move north [14].

### Education reform {#other-education}

**Formal commitment:** Beyond the core-curriculum rules (see Religion and state), Yashar would set up a statutory **national education council**, similar to the National Security Council, to set a five-year plan [19]. It would give more powers to local authorities, give principals autonomy over a flexible budget and a separate pay scale, and create a new optional teacher employment track based on quality rather than seniority [19]. Other points: fewer external matriculation (bagrut) exams, replaced by skills-based assessment; AI taught as part of the curriculum; all care for children from birth to age 3 under the Education Ministry, with national licensing; and a higher pay scale for kindergarten teachers [19]. Adi Altschuler is the party's candidate for Education Minister [39].

### Immigration and emigration {#other-aliyah-and-emigration}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar sets a target of **one million new immigrants in the programme's first decade and two million by 2048**, Israel's centenary [23, 24]. It cites more than 130,000 Israelis leaving in 2023–2025 [24]. It proposes a one-stop absorption body that starts working with immigrants abroad, fast-track recognition of degrees and professional licences, removal of banking barriers, a "hybrid residency" status for Israelis returning gradually, visa tracks for top international talent, and hundreds of new research positions in academia [23, 24]. It also proposes tax relief for older Jews with assets abroad who want to immigrate [24], and completing the immigration of communities already approved for aliyah [23].

### Senior citizens {#other-senior-citizens}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar promises higher old-age allowances for low-income pensioners, including Holocaust survivors and immigrants with small pensions, and a permanent mechanism to protect allowances from erosion [25]. It would consider mandatory long-term-care insurance for the whole population, run a national programme against loneliness through local authorities, set up a national council for senior citizens, consider ways for people over 67 to keep working, and impose tougher penalties for fraud against older people [25].

### Mental health and post-trauma {#other-mental-health}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar proposes national rehabilitation administrations, focused on the north and south, as "a single address" for people affected by trauma. A minister would be responsible for rehabilitation. Every discharged soldier would be entitled to continuing psychological care for as long as needed. Support for bereaved families would be extended to siblings and close circles, and patients would get a choice of treatment track [27, 20].

### Death penalty for terrorists {#other-death-penalty}

**Action (votes by top candidates):** On March 30, 2026 the Knesset passed the Death Penalty for Terrorists Law 62–48. Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper, then MKs in the Blue and White – National Unity faction, voted against it [101]. JTA reported that the law makes hanging the default sentence for Palestinians from Judea and Samaria convicted by military courts of deadly attacks on Israelis. Judges may impose life imprisonment in "special circumstances". For Israeli citizens tried in civilian courts, it applies to those who sought to "negate the existence of the State of Israel" [100]. None of the four leaders is recorded as voting on the bill's first reading on November 10, 2025, by which time Eisenkot and Kahana had left the Knesset [69]. No statement by Eisenkot or by Yashar on the death penalty was found.

### Disability, inclusion and victims of terror {#other-disability-and-inclusion}

**Action (laws passed):** Tropper (No. 6) co-sponsored the **Israeli Sign Language Recognition Law**, passed in 2026 [85, 98], and a law creating a national day honouring soldiers and civilians wounded in Israel's wars and in terror attacks, passed in January 2026 [85, 98]. He also co-sponsored a 2025 law giving crime victims the right to state their position before a prisoner's parole [85, 98]. Kahana (No. 5) and Farkash-Hacohen (No. 3) co-sponsored the 2023 law on payments to families of hostages and missing persons, and a 2024 law on aid for university students who served in the reserves [84, 86, 98]. Kahana also co-sponsored a 2024 law barring state funding of legal representation for people suspected, accused or convicted of security offences in the war [84, 98].

**Formal commitment / Candidate:** Adi Altschuler (No. 4), the party's candidate for Education Minister, founded the Krembo Wings youth movement for young people with and without disabilities and inclusive schools and kindergartens [39].

### Women's representation {#other-womens-representation}

**Action (bills):** Eisenkot co-sponsored a bill setting quotas for women on the boards of public companies; it was stopped [48]. Kahana sponsored a bill to make women about 47% of the body that elects the Chief Rabbis [78]. Farkash-Hacohen's bills on state commissions of inquiry require women to be represented on them [73, 74]. **Statement:** Eisenkot said Yashar's candidate list reflects the party's commitment to equality between men and women [111].

### Culture, sport and science {#other-culture-sport-science}

**Statement (about his record in government):** As outgoing Minister of Culture and Sport (2020–2022), Tropper told Walla in November 2022 that the sports budget rose by about 30% in the last budget. He said a ten-year, NIS 3 billion plan for sports facilities had been agreed, and that he left "an effective tool in legislation" against violence at sports grounds, though he still saw serious violence [89]. **Statement:** As Minister of Innovation, Science and Technology, Farkash-Hacohen was presented in May 2022 with the Israel Space Agency's NIS 600 million, five-year programme to grow the civilian space industry, and said the sector had "great trading and economic potential" [90].

### Agriculture and food security {#other-agriculture}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar treats agriculture as part of national security. It proposes a multi-year national food-security plan with production targets for essential products [26]. It would gradually replace "inefficient protections" with transparent direct support for farmers, anchor agriculture's status in law, break up concentration in the food market, support direct sales from farmers, and encourage agrivoltaic fields that combine farming with solar power [26]. The economic programme says import competition should go together with "preserving and encouraging Israeli agriculture" [17].

### Governance and public service {#other-governance}

**Formal commitment:** Yashar promises to clear the public service of political appointments, set measurable targets, close unnecessary ministries, and use AI to digitise services [17]. Eisenkot proposes capping the cabinet at 24 ministers, an eight-year limit for prime ministers, and a law barring a criminal defendant from forming a government [9]. The internal-security programme proposes a ministerial committee against public corruption [18].

**Action (bills):**
- In December 2024 Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper co-sponsored the Basic Law amendment for an eight-year limit on a prime minister's consecutive time in office [71]. Kahana had earlier tabled an identical bill in the same Knesset (P/315/25), which was removed from the agenda in March 2023 [71, 84, 98].
- In November 2023, a month into the war, Farkash-Hacohen co-sponsored two bills. One would abolish the government's newly created ministries and move their budgets to the war effort [81]. The other would freeze coalition-agreement funds during the emergency [82]. On January 31, 2024 she voted for the ministries bill, which was voted down 44–30 [99].
None of these bills passed.

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Stated partners.** Eisenkot named Naftali Bennett's Together (B'Yachad), Avigdor Liberman's Yisrael Beytenu and Yair Golan's Democrats as partners [9, 35]. In September 2026 he said he did not rule out any Zionist party and would see as a partner anyone who "accepts Israel as a Jewish democratic state, the values of the Declaration of Independence, and the obligation of service" [10].

**Conditions for other partners.** Eisenkot sets four conditions for any coalition partner: accepting Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, recognising the founding values of the Declaration of Independence, mandatory national service, and recognising Hamas as a terrorist organization [9, 35]. He repeated the conditions about the Declaration of Independence and Israel as a Jewish state in May 2026 [36].

**Haredi parties.** In July 2026 Eisenkot said "I am not flirting with them" about the Haredi parties, but said any party accepting "the obligation to serve – whether in the IDF or through National Service" could join: "I say this both to the haredim and to the Arabs" [34]. In October 2026 he said: "If only. It would be enormously significant if a Haredi party accepted those conditions" [9].

**Arab parties.** In July 2026, when asked three times whether he would form a government that depends on Arab parties, Eisenkot did not answer directly. He said "We will establish a Zionist, statesmanlike government" [34]. By September 2026 he said he did not see "an Arab party that accepts my principles", including on Hamas being a terrorist organization [10]. Jüdische Allgemeine reported on September 25, 2026 that he excluded Ra'am and the Joint List [35]. In October 2026 he said: "I do not want the support of the Arab parties, and I am not calling on them to support me" [9]. In September 2026 Ynetnews described Eisenkot as willing to accept Arab parties abstaining in a vote to form a coalition [44]. This is different from relying on their support, which he rejects.

**Arab parties in Knesset legislation.** In November 2024 Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper co-sponsored, with Gantz, a Basic Law amendment to widen the grounds for barring candidates and parties from running. Supporting a lone attacker would count as supporting terror, and a party running inside a joint list could be disqualified separately. The sponsors argued that, as a lesson of October 7, those who support or encourage terror must not be elected, while keeping judicial review [77]. The bill had not passed by the end of the Knesset [98].

**Likud and Netanyahu.** In July 2026 Eisenkot said Netanyahu and "all the other members of his security cabinet" are "not fit to hold any leadership position", but called a direct question on ruling out Netanyahu one he didn't "even need to answer" [34]. In October 2026 he said he would not accept Netanyahu staying on as caretaker prime minister and wants him to retire from political life [9]. No statement was found on sitting with a Likud led by someone else.

**Track record of keeping pledges.** Yashar has not run in an election before, so there is no record of keeping coalition pledges as a party. In his own career, Eisenkot was elected in 2022 as an opposition MK and joined Netanyahu's emergency government on October 12, 2023, after the October 7 attack [4, 9]. He left on June 13, 2024 [4], after Gantz said Netanyahu was "preventing us from progressing to a real victory" [53]. While National Unity was in the emergency government, its MKs, including Eisenkot, voted against the December 2023 supplementary budget at first reading, objecting to the coalition funds it kept [59, 60]. No pre-2023 pledge by Eisenkot personally about joining such a government was found. The partner, Tropper, agreed not to split from the joint faction after the election [44].

## Track record {#track-record}

**Yashar as a party** has never been in government or in the Knesset [15]. Its leaders' record comes from their earlier roles.

**Eisenkot in government (October 2023–June 2024).** He served as minister without portfolio and war cabinet observer from October 12, 2023 to June 13, 2024 [4, 9]. Tropper served in the same government as a minister without portfolio over the same period [49].
- **Hostage deal:** He says that in November 2023 he was "the lone voice" in the war cabinet arguing for continuing the hostage-release deal in stages [9].
- **Budget:** While in the government, he and the other National Unity MKs voted against the December 2023 wartime supplementary budget at first reading, objecting to the coalition funds it contained [59, 60].
- **Haredi enlistment:** In February 2024 he and Gantz presented an outline under which most young people would serve, with a new administration deciding each person's type of service [8]. It was not adopted. Two years later his party's platform includes a fuller "State Service for All" law [16].
- **Leaving government:** Gantz announced on June 9, 2024 that he and Eisenkot were leaving the emergency government [53]. Eisenkot's term as minister ended on June 13, 2024 [4].

**Eisenkot as an MK (November 2022–July 2025).**
- **Passed:** the National Security Strategy Law, 2025, which became law [5, 6].
- **Stopped (Knesset initiator records):** the "Mimadim LeLimudim" eligibility bill for IDF servicemembers, voted down by the coalition on November 20, 2024 [7, 48]; an income-tax exemption on reserve-duty pay with tax credits for active reservists [48]; quotas for women on the boards of public companies [48]; and convening the Ethics Committee over ministers' disclosure of information to the Knesset [48].
- **His own National Security Concept bill (2023), which was stopped** [80, 98].
- **Bills he co-sponsored that did not pass:** the "Israeli Service" universal-service bill (2023) [70]; the Basic Law right to equality (2023) [76]; the eight-year limit on a prime minister's term (2024) [71]; automatic appointment of the most senior justice as Supreme Court president (2024) [72]; and wider grounds for barring candidates who support terror (2024) [77].
- **Key votes:** against the 2023 changes to the judicial system [54, 55]; against the 2023–2024 and 2025 budgets [58, 91, 93]; against reviving the 2022 enlistment bill in June 2024 [61]; and for the UNRWA law in October 2024 [64]. He is not recorded as voting on the July 2024 resolution against a Palestinian state [63].
- Several of these causes, reservist benefits and degree funding for soldiers, are now part of Yashar's platform [21].

**Promises compared with delivery.** As Yashar has never governed, there is no record of its platform promises being carried out. Comparing its leaders' earlier actions with today's platform:
- **Kept:** Eisenkot said in July 2025 he would build a "Zionist, patriotic, statesmanlike, democratic alternative" [3], and he founded Yashar in September 2025 [2]. His votes and bills on the judicial system, prime-ministerial term limits and reservist benefits match the platform and his stated plans [9, 21, 28, 54, 55, 71, 72]. Bills for a state commission of inquiry into October 7 were sponsored by Farkash-Hacohen, not by Eisenkot [73, 74, 96].
- **Changed:** on Haredi enlistment the 2023 bill he co-sponsored had no cap on the combined study-and-service track for yeshiva students, while the 2026 platform caps Torah-study deferrals at up to 3% (see Haredi military enlistment) [16, 70].

**Top candidates' government records.**
- **Matan Kahana**, Minister of Religious Services from June 2021 to May 2022 [42]: led the 2021 kashrut reform, passed in the Arrangements Law [87]. The Knesset reversed it on July 15, 2026 [88]. His conversion reform did not reach a first reading [43, 97].
- **Chili Tropper** (Yesodot Yisrael), Minister of Culture and Sport from May 2020 to December 2022 [49]: said in a November 2022 interview that the sports budget rose by about 30%, that a NIS 3 billion facilities plan was agreed, and that he left legislation against violence at sports grounds [89].
- **Orit Farkash-Hacohen**, minister of Strategic Affairs, Tourism, and Innovation, Science and Technology between 2020 and 2022 [41]: as minister she was presented with the Israel Space Agency's NIS 600 million space-industry programme in May 2022 [90].
- **Yoram Cohen** headed the Shin Bet from 2011 to 2016 [37, 38].

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Plenty of public information is available. Yashar has published detailed policy programmes on its website covering national service, the economy, internal security, education, young people, reservists, northern Israel, immigration, senior citizens, agriculture and mental health [16–27]. Its leader gave long interviews in September and October 2026 on security, the judicial system and coalitions [9, 10].

The Knesset's open data and vote records document the parliamentary actions of the four top candidates who sat in the 25th Knesset: Eisenkot, Kahana, Farkash-Hacohen and Tropper. This includes their roles, the bills they sponsored and their individual votes on the judicial changes, budgets, Haredi enlistment, UNRWA, sovereignty, the death penalty and the October 7 inquiry [4, 41, 42, 49, 54–69, 84–86, 91, 93, 94, 98, 99, 101, 102].

The gaps are these:
- The party itself has never been in government or had its own Knesset faction. Its "Action" evidence comes from its leaders' records as National Unity MKs and ministers, and from Farkash-Hacohen's and Tropper's votes after Eisenkot and Kahana left the Knesset in July 2025.
- The four leaders' individual votes were checked across the 25th Knesset's plenum vote records (votes from January 2023 to July 2026) for the main relevant bills. The other candidates were not MKs, so they have no voting record. The final 2025 votes on the Judicial Selection Committee law were boycotted by the opposition [57].
- Its positions on Gaza, Iran and Judea and Samaria come mainly from the leader's interviews, not from a published platform chapter. Some site pages mention plans for housing, transport, the periphery and a constitution that are not yet published.
- No statement by the party was found on applying Israeli sovereignty in Judea and Samaria, on Shin Bet involvement in fighting crime in Arab society, or on the death penalty for terrorists. For sovereignty and the death penalty, only the votes of two top candidates were found.
