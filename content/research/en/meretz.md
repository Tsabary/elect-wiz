## Overview {#overview}

**Founding and history.** Meretz was founded before the 1992 Knesset election as a joint list of three parties: Ratz (led by Shulamit Aloni), Mapam (led by Yair Tzaban) and Shinui (led by Amnon Rubinstein) [14, 15, 16]. According to the Israel Democracy Institute (IDI), the three parties kept operating as separate units until they fully merged into a single party in 1997 [15]. In 1992 Meretz won 12 seats and joined Yitzhak Rabin's government [14, 15]. It later sat in the governments of Shimon Peres (1995–1996) and Ehud Barak (1999–2000), and then spent two decades in opposition [15]. After the 2003 election it briefly ran under the name Yachad, led by Yossi Beilin, and returned to the Meretz name for the 2006 election [14, 15]. Its leaders over the years included Shulamit Aloni, Yossi Sarid, Yossi Beilin, Haim Oron, Zehava Galon, Tamar Zandberg and Nitzan Horowitz [15, 16].

**Ideology.** The party describes itself as "the left-wing party of Israel" and says it promotes human and civil rights, the fight against racism, a social-democratic economic policy, the separation of religion and state, and peace based on a two-state solution [16]. The IDI places Meretz on the Zionist left and describes it as supporting social equality, equal rights for Arab citizens, two states for two peoples, a halt to settlement building and the separation of religion and state [15]. Its last full platform, published for the November 2022 election, opens by defining Israel as "the national home of the Jewish people" and also "a state that belongs equally to all its citizens" [17].

**Base of support.** The party says it has Jewish and Arab municipal representatives in dozens of local councils, and is represented in the Histadrut labor federation, the Zionist institutions and the kibbutz movement [16]. In the 2026 primaries, press reports described its activists as coming from the political left, human-rights groups and Jewish–Arab partnership circles [10].

**Election results.** Meretz won 6 seats with 202,218 votes (4.6%) in 2021, and 4 seats in April 2019 [15]. In the November 2022 election it received 150,793 votes (3.2%) and fell just below the electoral threshold, about 3,800 votes short, so for the first time since 1992 it had no Knesset members [14, 15]. Knesset faction records show the Meretz faction of the 24th Knesset ending on 15 November 2022 [19].

**Current Knesset seats: none.** Meretz has had no members in the 25th Knesset (elected 2022) [14, 19].

**The joint list for 2026.** Meretz runs in the October 2026 election on **"The Democrats led by Yair Golan" (הדמוקרטים בראשות יאיר גולן), ballot letters אמת** [1, 2]. The Central Elections Committee (CEC) filing states that the list was submitted by two registered parties: "The Democrats, founded by the Labor movement" (הדמוקרטים מיסודה של תנועת העבודה, i.e. the former Labor Party, renamed) and Meretz [1]. Of the 120 candidates on the filed list, only one, No. 6 Gabriela (Gaby) Lasky-Shutz, is marked as filed on behalf of Meretz. All the others are marked as filed on behalf of The Democrats [1].

**How the partnership came about (dated).**
- **30 June 2024:** Labor and Meretz announced an agreement to unite into one party called "The Democrats", led by Labor chairman Yair Golan (see the partner's document), together with Meretz secretary-general Tomer Reznik [4]. Under the announced terms, the new party's Knesset list would be chosen in open primaries, and Meretz members would be guaranteed at least one place in each block of four places up to place 16 [4].
- **8 July 2024:** the "political union agreement" (הסכם איחוד פוליטי) between the Israeli Labor Party and Meretz was signed. Meretz published it on its website [3]. It defines the union as a political union of the two parties "without this constituting a merger of the two parties into one corporation" [3]. Its terms include:
  - Meretz members join the party formerly called Labor, which is renamed The Democrats [3].
  - There is no transfer of assets or debts. Each party keeps its own separate finances, and Meretz "will continue to exist as a registered party" [3].
  - Meretz's own institutions are replaced by a standing committee headed by Meretz's serving secretary-general [3].
  - For the 26th Knesset, the parties submit a joint list on which one representative is filed on Meretz's behalf, so that Meretz stays eligible for party funding. That person was to be placed within the top four [3].
  - **No "party merger" agreement would be filed, so that the joint list cannot later split into separate Knesset factions under section 59(2) of the Knesset Law** [3].
- **12 July 2024:** Labor's convention approved the agreement with one vote against [5, 6].
- **Late 2025 to February 2026 (dispute and update):** Ynet reported that in the preceding months Golan had sought to change the 2024 agreement so that Meretz representatives would not receive realistic places on the list [7]. On **20 February 2026** the two parties signed an update. It set Meretz's guaranteed places at **6, 8 and 14** on the list, extended the guarantee to future elections (cancellable only by a special majority of a joint convention), and added Meretz representatives to coalition-negotiation teams [7].
- **20 July 2026:** The Democrats' primaries were held. Lasky placed sixth and Michal Rozin eighth. Reports said that Meretz's guaranteed places (6, 8 and 14) were applied [8, 9, 11].

**History of splits after past elections.** Meretz has run on joint lists several times:
- **2019–2020:** In September 2019 Meretz ran within the Democratic Union, together with Ehud Barak's Israel Democratic Party and the Green Movement [15]. For the March 2020 election it ran on a joint list with Labor and Gesher [15, 18].
- **The March–April 2020 split:** After the March 2020 election, Knesset records show the joint faction "Labor–Gesher–Meretz" (16 to 23 March 2020) becoming "Labor–Meretz" (23 March to 6 April 2020), and then a separate "Meretz" faction from 6 April 2020 [19]. On 6 April 2020 Labor chairman Amir Peretz formally asked the Knesset to split from Meretz so that Labor could join the government being formed with Blue and White. He said: "We unified because there was a risk that Meretz would not pass the minimum threshold. Now we are separating with mixed feelings" [18].
- **The current partnership:** The 2024 agreement's stated aim is to prevent a similar post-election split of The Democrats' list into separate factions [3]. No split has taken place so far.

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Who leads Meretz.** The 2024 union agreement replaced Meretz's own institutions with a standing committee headed by the party's serving secretary-general [3]. Tomer Reznik has been Meretz's secretary-general throughout: he was named as such when the union was announced in June 2024 and when the update was signed in February 2026 [4, 7]. The registry lists **Gaby Lasky** as the party's leader for this election because she is the only candidate filed on Meretz's behalf (No. 6) [1]. No source found in this research names her as formal party chair.

**Gaby (Gabriela) Lasky-Shutz, No. 6 (filed on behalf of Meretz).**
- **Profession:** a lawyer who specialises in freedom of expression and the right to protest. She is a former secretary-general of Peace Now and a former member of the Tel Aviv-Yafo city council [11, 31].
- **Knesset service:** a Meretz MK in the 24th Knesset, from 16 June 2021 to 15 November 2022 [20]. She entered when Meretz ministers Nitzan Horowitz and Tamar Zandberg resigned their seats under the "Norwegian Law", which lets ministers give up their Knesset seats to the next candidates on the list [25]. The Times of Israel described her at the time as a "human rights lawyer" who had represented Ahed Tamimi, a Palestinian teenager tried in a military court [25].
- **Legal aid for protesters:** she co-founded "Otef Atzurim" (Detainee Support Network), a group of about 50 volunteer lawyers who give free legal aid to people arrested at protests against the government's changes to the judicial system and protests for the hostages' return. Calcalist reported in October 2024 that the group had handled more than 1,800 arrests since 2023 [30].
- **2026 primaries:** she placed sixth in The Democrats' primaries on 20 July 2026, within the places guaranteed to Meretz [8, 11].

**Other Meretz figures on the list.**
- **Michal Rozin (No. 8):** a Meretz MK in the 20th, 21st and 24th Knessets [20]. She was one of the former Meretz MKs competing for the places guaranteed to Meretz [7, 10].
- **Mossi Raz:** a former Meretz MK [20] who also competed for Meretz's places [7, 10]. He does not appear among the top 22 places of the published results [8].

**Top of the joint list.** The top five places on the filed list are all held by The Democrats (the former Labor Party) [1]:
1. Yair Golan
2. Naama Lazimi
3. Gilad Kariv
4. Efrat Rayten
5. Yair (Yaya) Fink

Yair Golan, chairman of The Democrats, sat in the Meretz Knesset faction from April 2020 to November 2022 [20]. He served as Deputy Economy Minister in the 2021–2022 government and lost the 2022 Meretz leadership primary to Zehava Galon [23]. The partner party's leaders and record are covered in The Democrats' own research document.

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

Meretz has had no Knesset members since November 2022, so its most recent **actions** are its MKs' votes in the 24th Knesset (2021–2022) and its record in the 2021–2022 government. Its most recent **formal commitments** are its 2022 election platform [17] and the July 2024 union agreement it signed with Labor [3]. The agreement lists shared "founding principles" for The Democrats [3]. Statements by Gaby Lasky, the candidate filed on Meretz's behalf, are given where found.

Knesset per-MK votes below were counted from the raw rows of the Knesset's open-data vote tables (KNS_PlenumVote and KNS_PlenumVoteResult), as mirrored by the Open Knesset project, because the Knesset website itself could not be reached from the research environment [21, 22].

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

- **Formal commitment:** Meretz's 2022 platform calls for "a broad and up-to-date welfare state", "a strong public sector", reducing inequality, "raising taxes on capital and high earners", "reducing indirect taxes", and "regulation and supervision" to prevent the exploitation of workers and public money [17].
- **Formal commitment, taxes:** the platform proposes:
  - exempting basic food products, medicines and medical services from VAT (the tax added to almost everything you buy) [17];
  - a reduced VAT rate on books, newspapers, and cultural and sports events [17];
  - a new 55% income-tax bracket for monthly earnings above NIS 60,000, and a top rate of 70% [17];
  - a progressive inheritance tax of 10–30% on estates above NIS 5 million, excluding the family home [17];
  - raising corporate tax to 30% for companies with taxable income above NIS 1.2 million a year [17].
- **Formal commitment, market concentration:** the platform calls for:
  - breaking up "control pyramids" (chains of holding companies through which a few groups control many firms) by limiting them to two layers [17];
  - regulatory limits on holding companies in several sectors, and breaking up multi-sector business groups "for reasons of competition" [17];
  - requiring private monopolies to publish financial reports [17].

  It says concentration "leads to excessive price rises" [17].
- **Formal commitment, privatization:** the platform calls for a complete halt to privatization and a gradual return of public services to public ownership [17].
- **Formal commitment, direct support:** the platform calls for:
  - equalising the universal disability and old-age allowances with the minimum wage in the first budget year [17];
  - raising child allowances, and restoring allowances for single-parent families [17];
  - subsidising public transport by 80% for senior citizens, children, students and people with disabilities [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** the agreement Meretz signed commits The Democrats to "social-democratic economic policy". It calls for a strong welfare state "as opposed to an ultra-capitalist approach", "shrinking social and economic gaps", and investment in the periphery [3].
- **Action:** in November 2021 all six Meretz MKs present voted for the 2021 and 2022 state budgets and the accompanying Economic Arrangements Law. The final votes passed 61–59 (2021 budget, 4 November 2021), 59–56 (2022 budget, 5 November 2021) and 61–57 (Arrangements Law, 4 November 2021) [21, 22].
- **Contradiction:** the 2022 platform pledges to raise child allowances and greatly expand public housing [17], and Meretz's website says the party fights for raising disability allowances [16]. But while Meretz was in the governing coalition, all six of its MKs present voted against two opposition private bills at their preliminary readings on 24 November 2021:
  - a bill to raise child allowances, defeated 38–43;
  - a bill to increase the public-housing stock, defeated 40–44 [21, 22].

  On 1 December 2021, four Meretz MKs (Lasky, Rozin, Raz and Rinawie Zoabi) voted against a private bill to equalise the disability allowance with the minimum wage, which was defeated 20–22 [21, 22]. No statement by Meretz explaining these votes was found.

**Closest option(s):** Active state regulation, with taxes on high incomes and action against concentration (`active-state-regulation`), combined with direct support for households through higher allowances and subsidies (`direct-support-for-households`). The formal commitments fit both. The 2021 coalition-era votes against some opposition allowance bills are noted above.

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

- **Formal commitment:** Meretz's 2022 platform says the causes of violence and crime in Arab society include a lack of policing and enforcement, poverty and unemployment, poor treatment of disconnected youth, and social alienation [17]. It says the government must give the problem "full attention and the resources required". This includes strengthening police presence in Arab communities "while fundamentally changing the ethos of police enforcement towards Arab citizens", alongside more resources for community programmes and welfare services in Arab society [17].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform also calls for:
  - a broad affirmative-action programme for Arab citizens "in all areas of life" [17];
  - better police services for all citizens, with clear targets to reduce illegal weapons and raise the number of solved crimes [17];
  - a ban on using Arab nationality as a criterion for security checks ("profiling") [17].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform states that holding people without trial (administrative detention) violates the right to a fair trial [17].
- **Action (government, 2021):** while Meretz was in the coalition, the government adopted Government Decision 550 in October 2021. This is the "Takadum" five-year plan for the economic and social development of Arab society, worth about NIS 30 billion. Government Decision 549, a separate plan to fight crime and violence in Arab society, was adopted at the same time [41].
- **Action (vote, 2021):** in October 2021 the government approved a bill to temporarily widen the grounds on which police may search without a court warrant. The IDI described it as part of the government's push against serious crime in Arab society [39]. On 13 December 2021 all six Meretz MKs voted to send this bill to committee for its final readings. The vote passed 60–58 [21, 22].
- **Action (vote, 2022):** on 11 May 2022 all six Meretz MKs voted against an opposition private bill setting minimum sentences for weapons offences. It was defeated 53–61 at its preliminary reading [21, 22].
- **Not found:** no statement by Meretz or Lasky was found on bringing the Shin Bet (Israel's internal security service) into the fight against crime, or on the July 2026 proposal to move about NIS 532 million from the Takadum plan to the Shin Bet and the police [41]. Searches covered Hebrew and English news and Meretz's website.

**Closest option(s):** Invest in Arab communities first (`invest-in-arab-communities-first`). The platform links crime to long neglect, calls for development funding, and opposes holding people without trial. It also has elements of more police resources under normal law (`more-police-resources-normal-law`): stronger police presence and support for a temporary widening of search powers in 2021.

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** the agreement Meretz signed in July 2024 says that rebuilding the Gaza Strip and its economy is an Israeli interest. It says Israel should work to hand control of Gaza "to moderate Palestinian actors" able to ensure stable government, "while preserving Israeli security interests" [3]. It also commits to "fight for the return of the hostages" and to restoring full security to residents of the south and north who were hit by the October 7 attack and the war [3].
- **Formal commitment (2022 platform, before the war):**
  - Gaza is part of the territory of a future Palestinian state [17].
  - Meretz will act to lift the closure of Gaza and ease restrictions on movement, goods and building materials, "while preserving the security of residents of the south" [17].
  - Israel should cooperate in practical ways with whoever controls Gaza, "regardless of who governs it", so that the Strip can develop [17].
  - Gradual rebuilding followed by a political arrangement is "the only way" to stop cycles of fighting [17].
- **Statement (Gaby Lasky, undated video, reported October 2026):** in a video circulated in October 2026, Lasky said that on International Women's Day "we must stand in solidarity with the women in Gaza, with the women in the West Bank, with the women in Iran", and that "if the world were led by women there would be fewer wars" [11, 12].
- **Statement (Gaby Lasky, September 2026):** in a post about the makers of "Naza", a film about IDF activity in Gaza that alleges war crimes, Lasky wrote that "the violent discourse against the creators must stop". She said such works "raise moral questions that, precisely out of the pain, must be asked". Ice reported that the post was later deleted [11, 12].
- **Not found:** no Meretz statement was found on the October 2025 US-plan ceasefire or on its next stages, after searches of Hebrew and English news and Meretz's website.

**Closest option(s):** Advance an international arrangement with a Palestinian administration without Hamas and a gradual withdrawal (`advance-international-arrangement`). This is closest to the 2024 agreement's call to hand Gaza to moderate Palestinian actors while keeping Israeli security interests. The 2022 platform's call to lift the closure and treat Gaza as part of a future Palestinian state also has elements of `end-war-full-withdrawal`.

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

- **Action (vote, January 2022):** on 17 January 2022, five Meretz MKs voted for the government's Security Service Law (Amendment 25) on the integration of yeshiva students. The bill was defeated in a 54–54 tie after Meretz MK Ghaida Rinawie Zoabi broke coalition discipline and voted against [21, 22, 42]. According to Srugim, the bill was meant to:
  - set new enlistment targets for yeshiva students and graduates of Haredi schools, with a yearly increase target and financial consequences if targets were missed [42];
  - lower the age at which those who had deferred service become exempt [42];
  - allow shortened service combined with job training or civilian national service [42].
- **Action (vote, January 2022):** on 31 January 2022, all six Meretz MKs voted for the resubmitted version (Amendment 26). It passed its first reading 51–48 [21, 22, 43].
- **Action (votes):**
  - On 22 December 2021, Meretz MKs Raz, Golan and Rozin voted against a private bill to abolish compulsory military service. It was defeated 18–32 [21, 22].
  - On 2 February 2022, all six Meretz MKs voted against an opposition private bill to require the army to call up those obliged to serve. It was defeated 30–50 [21, 22].
- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform calls for "conditioning all budgets for Haredi education on core studies" (mathematics, English and similar subjects) [17]. The platform has no section on Haredi enlistment as such [17].
- **Statement (joint list leader):** at The Democrats' convention on 31 May 2026, its leader Yair Golan promised to legislate an enlistment law. He said the party would not sit in government with parties he blamed for, among other things, large-scale non-enlistment of yeshiva students during the war [45].
- **Not found:** no Meretz-specific statement on Haredi enlistment after 2022 was found.

**Closest option(s):** Gradual enlistment targets with incentives and budget consequences (`gradual-targets-by-agreement`). This is the bill Meretz's MKs voted for in 2022. The evidence is limited: no later Meretz position on universal service with sanctions was found.

### Housing {#issue-housing}

- **Formal commitment:** Meretz's 2022 platform says "every resident has a basic right to housing". It blames high prices on government reliance on the free market [17]. It proposes a "national emergency plan" that includes:
  - publicly funded construction of "hundreds of thousands" of homes [17];
  - affordable-housing clauses in every new plan [17];
  - long-term rental models with "protection mechanisms for tenants and supervision of rent" [17];
  - leasing public land by tender and ending "maximum price" tenders [17];
  - government-financed long-term rental housing that the government builds and maintains [17];
  - making approval of new building conditional on a share of public and affordable housing [17].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform also proposes:
  - adding about 15,000 public-housing apartments within a decade, and ending the waiting list for public housing [17];
  - a 30% "property tax" on owners of second and further homes, unless the homes are placed under a "fair rent authority" that sets the rent [17];
  - a national urban-renewal plan for 100,000 homes in city centres [17];
  - removing barriers that cause inequality between Jews and Arabs in the allocation of land [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** the agreement Meretz signed calls for "various solutions for affordable housing, including public housing" [3].
- **Formal commitment:** Meretz says its MKs initiated the Public Housing Law [16].
- **Action (vote):** see the 24 November 2021 vote against an opposition bill to increase the public-housing stock, noted under Cost of living [21, 22].

**Closest option(s):** State-led affordable rental with rent regulation (`state-led-affordable-rental`). The platform's urban-renewal and public-transport proposals also touch on `develop-periphery-and-transport`.

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

- **Formal commitment (2022 platform):** on Iran, the platform says Iran's nuclear ambitions threaten "the whole world". It says Israel should "focus on the diplomatic path" and a return to the 2015 nuclear agreement, which it says keeps Iran from nuclear weapons and "distances an unwanted war" [17]. On Lebanon, it says the threat from Hezbollah requires "a careful policy combining deterrence and restraint" until a basic change of government in Lebanon allows peace [17]. On security in general, it says Israel's security should not rest only on military deterrence, but also on regional and international political alliances and on taking up the Arab Peace Initiative [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** the agreement Meretz signed says:
  - Israel's security rests on "advanced security capabilities, political arrangements and regional alliances" [3];
  - reconciliation with the Palestinians on a two-state basis would help create "a strong regional and international front against the rising Iranian threat" [3];
  - Israel should work for a regional alliance including the moderate Arab states and Saudi Arabia "against the axis of evil led by Russia and Iran" [3].
- **Statement (Gaby Lasky):** see her undated remark about solidarity with women in Iran, reported in October 2026 [11, 12].
- **Not found:** no Meretz or Lasky statement was found on the June 2025 or February 2026 wars with Iran, or on the 2026 fighting in Lebanon. Searches covered Hebrew and English news and Meretz's website.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence predates the current wars and is mixed. The 2024 agreement's emphasis on alliances and US- and Arab-backed arrangements is closest to `force-plus-us-backed-agreements`. The 2022 platform's preference for diplomacy and "restraint" leans towards `prioritize-ending-multifront-war`.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

- **Formal commitment (2022 platform):** the platform supports a Palestinian state in Judea and Samaria and Gaza, with these terms:
  - a border based on the pre-1967 line (the "Green Line"), with agreed land swaps at a 1:1 ratio [17];
  - shared sovereignty in Jerusalem so that it is the capital of both states. Jewish neighbourhoods would go to Israel and Palestinian neighbourhoods to Palestine, with special arrangements in the "Holy Basin" [17];
  - an agreed solution to the refugee question [17].

  It says that "most settlements will be evacuated, but most Israeli citizens living beyond the Green Line will be annexed to Israel within agreed land swaps" [17]. It also calls for:
  - stopping all settlement construction regardless of negotiations [17];
  - a law to compensate settlers who leave voluntarily before an agreement [17];
  - evacuating isolated settlements "with or without an agreement" [17];
  - repealing the 2017 Settlement Regularisation Law [17];
  - opposing "any legislation that applies Israeli law in the occupied territories" [17].

  The security terms it describes include a demilitarised Palestinian state, IDF elements on the ground for an agreed period, international guarantees and a long-term international military presence [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** preserving Israel's "Jewish and democratic character requires a solid Jewish majority". The parties will "strive for separation between us and the Palestinians" and resolve the conflict "on the basis of two states for two peoples: the State of Israel and alongside it a demilitarised Palestinian state", with Israel's security "a condition for any arrangement" [3].
- **Action (votes):** all six Meretz MKs present voted against bills to apply Israeli sovereignty to Judea and Samaria:
  - 28 July 2021, defeated 47–63 at its preliminary reading [21, 22];
  - 22 June 2022, defeated 46–52 [21, 22].
- **Action (letter):** in November 2021, Meretz MKs Mossi Raz and Gaby Lasky wrote to Housing Minister Ze'ev Elkin. They demanded that he "immediately stop" his plan to double the number of Israeli families in the Jordan Valley, and warned that it endangered the coalition [26].
- **Contradiction:** the 2022 platform says Meretz will oppose any legislation applying Israeli law in the territories [17]. But on 6 June 2022, five of Meretz's six MKs (Golan, Lasky, Salalha, Rozin and Raz) voted for the bill to extend the emergency regulations that apply Israeli criminal law, and in some cases civil law, to Israelis living in Judea and Samaria. Meretz MK Ghaida Rinawie Zoabi voted against, and the bill was defeated 52–58 [21, 22, 44]. The Media Line reported that the regulations were "part of the legal status quo the coalition agreed upon" [44]. The vote came four months before the platform was published.
- **Statement (Gaby Lasky, July 2026):** at a faction meeting of The Democrats, Lasky told Golan not to say "Judea and Samaria" but "the West Bank". Golan replied that he rejects the idea that "Judea and Samaria" is a right-wing term [13].

**Closest option(s):** Two states through a negotiated agreement with land swaps (`separation-two-states`). The platform keeps most settlers in Israel through swaps while evacuating most settlements. Its call for a shared Jerusalem capital and a full settlement freeze also contains elements of `two-states-pre-1967-border`.

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

- **Formal commitment (2022 platform):** Meretz "will fight to prevent unnecessary political interference in the legal system and political influence on judges", and will fight the politicisation of legal advice in government ministries and the Knesset [17]. It opposed a bill to change the status of ministries' legal advisers, and says that subordinating legal advisers to ministers "harms citizens' freedom" [17]. It calls for completing a constitution through Basic Laws, including a Basic Law on social rights, and opposes "any compromise on constitutional matters" that would weaken basic democratic values [17]. It notes that Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty and Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation were passed "at Meretz's initiative" [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** the agreement commits The Democrats to:
  - "full separation of powers" [3];
  - "curbing government institutions to prevent harm to individual rights" [3];
  - protecting the independence of the legal system and "the gatekeepers", meaning the attorney general and other legal advisers [3];
  - completing a Basic Law: Legislation [3];
  - limits on power, such as limiting the prime minister's terms [3].
- **Action (votes, 2021):**
  - On 10 May 2021, all six Meretz MKs voted against referring a proposed "Basic Law: Override" to committee. It would let the Knesset re-pass laws struck down by the Supreme Court. The vote passed 41–30 [21, 22].
  - On 14 July 2021, all six Meretz MKs voted against two opposition bills to amend Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty so that a law could stay valid despite a court ruling. They were defeated 50–62 and 50–64 [21, 22].
- **Action (legislation, 2022):** in June 2022 the Ministerial Committee for Legislation backed a bill sponsored by Lasky. It would amend Basic Law: The Government to bar a person indicted for a serious crime from being tasked with forming a government. The Knesset's legal adviser called legislating it on the eve of elections "problematic", and the bill did not reach a vote before the Knesset dissolved [27].
- **Action (protest legal aid, 2023–2024):** Lasky co-founded the volunteer legal-aid network for those arrested at protests against the government's changes to the judicial system. She said organising began "the day after Yariv Levin's speech" announcing the plan in January 2023 [30].
- **Statement (Gaby Lasky, March 2026):** in an op-ed, Lasky said a police commissioner who breaches the attorney general's directives "is not a policeman but a politician in uniform". She called for the dismissal of Police Commissioner Danny Levy and the dismantling of a new police "incitement department", which she described as bypassing prosecutors [31].

**Closest option(s):** Reverse the recent changes and protect the independence of the courts and the attorney general in a basic law (`repeal-changes-protect-independence`).

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

- **Statement (joint list leader, 20 July 2026):** on the night of the primaries, Yair Golan said: "The first decision we will pass in the government, as soon as we sit around the table, will be establishing a state commission of inquiry" [8].
- **Statement (Meretz secretary-general, February 2026):** when the updated union agreement was signed, Reznik described the partnership's goal as "replacing the October 7 government headed by Netanyahu" [7].
- **Action (related, 2022):** in January 2022, Lasky submitted a bill to set up a state commission of inquiry into the submarines and naval vessels affair. Ice reported that she did so because not all ministers supported a government decision to set up such a commission [29]. This shows a preference for the state-commission mechanism under existing law, but it concerns a different matter.
- **Not found:** no Meretz-specific statement or document on how the October 7 commission's members should be chosen was found. Searches covered Hebrew and English news, Meretz's website and the 2024 agreement.

**Closest option(s):** A state commission whose members are chosen by the President of the Supreme Court under existing law (`state-commission-under-existing-law`). This rests mainly on the joint list leader's statement and on Lasky's earlier use of the state-commission route.

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

- **Formal commitment (2022 platform):** Meretz supports "freedom of religion and freedom from religion". It says these "require separating religion from politics and religion from the state" [17]. It calls for:
  - **marriage:** civil marriage and divorce, including for same-sex couples, a state marriage registrar, and family courts with authority over all personal-status matters. Rabbinical and Sharia courts would hear such cases only if both sides agree [17];
  - **conversion:** freedom to choose Orthodox, Reform, Conservative or other conversion [17];
  - **the Chief Rabbinate:** it "will cease to exist as a state institution funded by the state", and religious councils will be abolished [17];
  - **religious services:** provided equally to all religions and all streams of Judaism according to their share of the population [17];
  - **Shabbat:** keeping Shabbat as the general day of rest, while running public transport on Shabbat and holidays, including intercity trains and buses, "taking into account the character of the local population" [17]. It also calls for allowing cultural and leisure activity on Shabbat and repealing the law against displaying leavened bread (hametz) in public on Passover [17];
  - **women at the Western Wall:** egalitarian prayer at holy sites, including the Western Wall [17].
- **Formal commitment (coalition agreement, June 2021):** Meretz's coalition agreement with Yesh Atid included a clause on promoting public transport on Shabbat. It also included a commitment to find a solution for the marital status of couples who cannot marry in Israel, "including same-sex couples, so that they are recognised as married" [24].
- **Track record:** Globes reported in November 2021 that Transport Minister Merav Michaeli's initiative to allow local Shabbat bus services lacked coalition consensus, and that Yamina held an effective veto over religion-and-state changes [38]. No law or government decision allowing Shabbat public transport, and no civil-marriage law, was found to have passed before the Knesset voted to dissolve in June 2022 [21, 22, 38].

**Closest option(s):** Separate religion and state, with civil marriage and divorce, Shabbat transport and equal treatment of all streams (`separate-religion-and-state`).

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### LGBT rights {#other-lgbt-rights}

- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform has a detailed chapter on LGBT rights, based on the demands of the community's organisations. It includes:
  - equal recognition of same-sex partnerships and identical registration of parenthood for LGBT couples [17];
  - adoption rights in Israel and abroad [17];
  - amending the surrogacy law [17];
  - recognition of gender identity [17];
  - health-funding equality for transgender people [17];
  - educational content on LGBT history [17].

  Meretz's website also says it fights to end conversion therapy for LGBT people [16].
- **Action (government):** on taking office in June 2021, Health Minister Nitzan Horowitz (Meretz) announced plans to remove the question that barred gay men who had had sex with men in the previous 12 months from donating blood [33]. The Times of Israel later reported that he had ruled all such restrictions would be lifted, and that he defended updated blood-donation forms in February 2022 [34].
- **Formal commitment (coalition agreement, 2021):** see the clause on recognising same-sex couples as married, under Religion and state [24].

### Women's rights and abortion {#other-womens-rights-abortion}

- **Action (government):**
  - On 1 December 2021, Health Minister Horowitz announced plans to ease Israel's abortion rules, calling the existing committee process "ridiculously outdated" and "chauvinistic" [35].
  - On 27 June 2022, the Knesset Labor, Welfare and Health Committee approved his new regulations [36]. They allow early drug-induced abortions in clinics run by the health funds, remove intrusive questions, and end the requirement to appear in person before the committee [36].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform calls for criminal and civil sanctions against excluding women from the public sphere, and for enforcing the ban on gender segregation on buses [17].

### Environment and climate {#other-environment-climate}

- **Action (government):** in November 2021, Environmental Protection Minister Tamar Zandberg (Meretz) introduced a tax on disposable plastic tableware. It raised the tax by NIS 11 per kilogram, with the aim of doubling consumer prices and cutting purchases by 40% [37]. In January 2023 the new finance minister, Bezalel Smotrich, ordered the tax cancelled [40].
- **Action (votes):** on 6 October 2021, all six Meretz MKs voted against an opposition Climate Bill, which was defeated 47–55 [21, 22]. On 28 June 2022, Meretz MKs Salalha, Rozin and Raz voted for the Climate Bill in a later vote [21, 22].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform calls for:
  - green taxation and incentives for cutting emissions [17];
  - expanding solar energy, including on the roofs of apartment buildings [17];
  - protecting open spaces, the coast and nature [17];
  - environmental-justice measures [17].

### Public transport {#other-public-transport}

- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform calls for:
  - public transport "seven days a week, 24 hours a day", including intercity trains and buses [17];
  - at least 50 km of new public-transport lanes a year [17];
  - metropolitan transport authorities [17];
  - a government target for shifting travel from private cars to public transport [17].

### Cannabis {#other-cannabis}

- **Formal commitment:** the platform supports "full and supervised" legalisation of cannabis, including home growing, taxation and sale, and erasing criminal records for past personal use [17].
- **Action (votes):** on 13 October 2021, all six Meretz MKs voted for a bill regulating medical cannabis, which passed its preliminary reading 54–42 [21, 22]. On 22 June 2022, Lasky and Raz voted for a later version, which was defeated 20–29 [21, 22].

### Arab citizens' equality and the Nation-State Law {#other-arab-equality-nation-state-law}

- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform pledges to repeal Basic Law: Israel – the Nation State of the Jewish People (2018). It says the law excludes the Arab minority, including Muslims, Druze, Christians and Circassians [17]. It also pledges to:
  - give the Declaration of Independence binding legal status in a basic law [17];
  - restore Arabic as an official language [17];
  - recognise the Arab minority as a national minority with collective rights [17];
  - add Muslim, Christian and Druze holidays to the state calendar [17];
  - repeal the "Nakba Law", the "Boycott Law" and the "admissions committees law" [17].
- **Formal commitment (2024 union agreement):** "We will strive to repeal the Nation-State Law and anchor the principle of equality and social rights in basic legislation" [3].

### Citizenship law and family unification {#other-citizenship-law}

The Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law (Temporary Order) restricts residency and citizenship for Palestinians from Judea and Samaria and Gaza who marry Israeli citizens.

- **Change over time:**
  - On 5 July 2021, as part of the coalition, all six Meretz MKs voted for the government's request to extend the law. The motion was also declared a confidence vote and ended in a 59–59 tie [21, 22].
  - In 2022 a new version advanced with opposition support. In votes on 7 February 2022, Meretz MKs voted against it. On 10 March 2022, five Meretz MKs (Rozin, Salalha, Rinawie Zoabi, Lasky and Raz) voted against it at its final reading, where it passed 45–15 [21, 22].
  - On 10 March 2022, Meretz's MKs also voted with the Joint List and Ra'am for a series of reservations (proposed amendments) to the bill, all of which were defeated [21, 22].

  No explanation from Meretz for the change was found. Its 2022 platform says Israel is responsible for the rights of "Palestinians married to Israelis" [17].

### Protest rights and policing {#other-protest-policing}

- **Action:** Lasky voted for, and the Knesset passed, the Detainee and Prisoner Shackling (Duty to Inform) Law on 31 May 2022. It requires police and prison guards to tell a judge when a shackled detainee or prisoner is in the courtroom. It was published on 1 June 2022 [21, 22, 46].
- **Action (votes):** in May and July 2022, Meretz MKs voted for a bill requiring video documentation of police use of water-cannon vehicles [21, 22].
- **Action:** see Lasky's legal-aid network for arrested protesters [30].
- **Statement:** see her March 2026 op-ed calling for the police commissioner's dismissal [31].
- **Formal commitment:** the platform has a section on police violence and freedom of expression, and opposes the biometric database [17].

### Asylum seekers and migrant workers {#other-asylum-seekers}

- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform calls for:
  - ending the policy of deporting and detaining asylum seekers [17];
  - a "fair and functioning" refugee-status process [17];
  - work permits until status is decided [17];
  - access to health and welfare services [17];
  - promoting the UN-brokered outline for resettling half of the asylum seekers in Western countries and giving status to the rest [17].

### Disability rights and welfare {#other-disability-welfare}

- **Formal commitment:** the platform says the Equal Rights for People with Disabilities Law was passed at Meretz's initiative. It calls for:
  - applying that law to all ministries, including Education and Defence [17];
  - a rehabilitation law and a national rehabilitation authority [17];
  - raising the disability allowance to the level of the minimum wage [17];
  - a Basic Law: Social Rights covering health, education, housing, employment, dignified subsistence, organising and striking [17].
- **Contradiction:** see the 1 December 2021 vote against a bill to equalise the disability allowance with the minimum wage, under Cost of living [21, 22]. On 15 June 2022, all six Meretz MKs also voted against an opposition bill on public-transport discounts for people with disabilities, which was defeated 47–52 [21, 22].

### Workers' rights {#other-workers-rights}

- **Formal commitment:** the platform calls for:
  - "a revolution in the labour market" to secure fair wages and job security [17];
  - ending indirect employment through contractors in the welfare and education ministries [17];
  - state guarantees for pension savings [17].

  The 2024 union agreement commits The Democrats to protecting workers' rights, strengthening enforcement of labour laws and strengthening workers' organisations [3].

### Animal welfare {#other-animal-welfare}

- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform calls for:
  - moving all animal-protection powers to the Environmental Protection Ministry [17];
  - funding enforcement of the Animal Welfare Law [17];
  - a ban on the fur trade [17];
  - stricter criteria and outside representation on committees that approve animal experiments [17].

### Security policy and human rights in Judea and Samaria {#other-security-human-rights}

- **Statement (Gaby Lasky, April 2022):** after the 7 April 2022 shooting attack in Tel Aviv, Lasky said in a radio interview that some children of attackers "are left without a livelihood". She said that to prevent them being exploited or turning to terror "they need a livelihood", and that there should be no "collective punishment" of families, such as house demolitions or administrative detention [28]. Coalition partners criticised the remarks; Justice Minister Gideon Sa'ar called them "contrary to the government's position" [28]. Hours later Lasky apologised: "my statement was out of place and did not for a moment justify the criminal terror". She said she unequivocally condemns the attack and that terror must be fought [28].
- **Formal commitment:** the 2022 platform calls for:
  - bringing protections for minors in the military court system into line with Israeli juvenile law [17];
  - ending the eviction of Palestinian residents in East Jerusalem [17];
  - offering Israeli citizenship to East Jerusalem residents who want it [17];
  - strict control over arms exports to regimes that violate human rights [17].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

- **Current stance (joint list):**
  - **31 May 2026:** at The Democrats' convention, Golan said the party "will not sit in a government with those who were partners in the government of October 7". He named parties that, in his words, abandoned Israel's security, promoted the changes to the judicial system and led large-scale non-enlistment during the war, and said "not only Netanyahu must go, but also all his partners" [45]. He also said he would not commit to recommending the leader of the largest party in the opposition bloc, "but whoever agrees to our values" [45].
  - **20 February 2026:** Meretz's secretary-general Tomer Reznik said "the task before us is clear: replacing the October 7 government headed by Netanyahu" [7]. The updated union agreement signed that day gives Meretz representatives places on the coalition-negotiation teams [7].
- **Formal commitment (2024):** the union agreement states the parties' shared aim of offering "a governing alternative" to replace the government [3]. When announcing the union on 30 June 2024, Golan described its purpose as "replacing the worst government since the founding of the state" [4].
- **No statement by Lasky** on specific coalition partners was found.

**Track record of keeping such pledges:**
- **2021:** in June 2021 Meretz signed a coalition agreement with Yesh Atid and joined the government led by Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid [24]. This was its first government since 2000 [14, 15]. The coalition also included the right-wing Yamina and New Hope parties, Yisrael Beytenu and the Arab party Ra'am [24]. Meretz's agreement gave it a veto over adding any further faction to the government [24]. Meretz's conference approved the agreement unanimously [24].
- **2021–2022, threats over settlement policy:** while in the coalition, Meretz MKs warned that settlement plans endangered the government [26]. They nevertheless stayed in the coalition until it dissolved itself in June 2022 [21, 22].
- **2022:** Meretz won no seats, so its 2022 pre-election coalition position was not tested [14].
- **2020:** in its earlier joint-list experience, the partner (Labor) left the joint faction to join a government Meretz opposed [18].

## Track record {#track-record}

**Meretz in government, June 2021 to December 2022 (Bennett–Lapid government).**

- **Ministries held:** Health (Nitzan Horowitz), Environmental Protection (Tamar Zandberg) and Regional Cooperation (Issawi Frej) [24]. Yair Golan was Deputy Economy Minister [23].
- **Coalition-agreement items and what happened:**
  - **Public transport on Shabbat** (coalition clause [24]): not delivered. Globes reported that the initiative lacked coalition consensus and that Yamina held a veto [38].
  - **Recognising couples who cannot marry in Israel, including same-sex couples, as married** (coalition clause [24]): no such law was passed in the sources reviewed.
  - **LGBT equality in health:** delivered in part. Horowitz moved to lift restrictions on blood donations by gay men [33, 34].
  - **Abortion access:** delivered. New regulations were approved by the Knesset committee on 27 June 2022 [36].
  - **Environmental taxation:** the disposable-plastics tax took effect in November 2021 [37], and the next government ordered it cancelled in January 2023 [40].
  - **Arab society:** the government in which Meretz sat adopted the Takadum five-year plan (Decision 550) and a crime-fighting plan (Decision 549) in October 2021 [41].
- **Budget:** Meretz MKs voted for the 2021 and 2022 state budgets in November 2021 [21, 22].

**Knesset record, 24th Knesset (2021–2022), from Knesset vote data [19–22]:**
- Meretz had six MKs at a time, with members rotating through the Norwegian Law [20, 25].
- Its MKs voted against bills to apply Israeli sovereignty in Judea and Samaria, and against bills to let the Knesset override Supreme Court rulings [21, 22].
- They voted for the government's Haredi enlistment bill at first reading [21, 22].
- They voted against the 2022 Citizenship Law after voting to extend its predecessor in 2021 [21, 22].
- Several of the votes on welfare and housing bills described above conflict with the party's later platform [16, 17, 21, 22].

**Notable internal events:**
- MK Ghaida Rinawie Zoabi broke coalition discipline in January 2022, bringing down the first vote on the enlistment bill [42].
- She voted against the Judea and Samaria regulations bill in June 2022 [44].
- The Knesset voted to dissolve itself on 22 June 2022, with Meretz MKs voting in favour [21, 22].

**Since November 2022:** Meretz has had no Knesset members and therefore no voting record [14, 19]. Its main actions have been:
- the July 2024 union with Labor [3, 4, 5];
- the February 2026 update to that agreement [7];
- the participation of its candidates in The Democrats' July 2026 primaries [8, 9, 10].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

No indictments, convictions or official inquiries involving Meretz's current leading figures (Gaby Lasky, secretary-general Tomer Reznik and Michal Rozin) were found.

**One reported administrative matter.** In August 2022 Ynet reported that Regional Cooperation Minister Issawi Frej (Meretz) had appointed Lasky's partner, Tamir Ben Dov Krispin, to a political-trust post in his office. According to the report, a ministry appointments committee then picked Ben Dov for a temporary professional post, in breach of civil-service rules, and the appointment was frozen [32]. The Civil Service Commission told Ynet that the ministry had not placed Ben Dov in the senior post and that he remained in a trust post, "so we see no reason to address the matter" [32]. Lasky did not respond to the report [32]. No finding against Lasky was reported.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Enough public information exists to describe Meretz's positions, but with clear limits.

**Strong evidence:**
- A detailed 76-page 2022 platform [17].
- A signed 2024 union agreement that sets out shared principles [3].
- A full per-MK voting record for the 24th Knesset (2021–2022) [21, 22].
- Press coverage of its 2021–2022 ministers [33–38].

**Limitations:**
- Meretz has had no Knesset members since November 2022, so there is no voting record after that date [14, 19].
- Since July 2024 Meretz operates mainly inside The Democrats. Its own institutions were replaced by a standing committee, and the 2026 campaign platform could not be retrieved because The Democrats' website blocked automated access [3].
- Few Meretz-specific statements were found on the 2025–2026 issues: the Iran wars, the Gaza ceasefire plan, Haredi enlistment after the 2024 Supreme Court ruling, and how to choose an October 7 commission. Several issue sections therefore rely on the 2022 platform, the 2024 agreement or statements by the joint list's leader, and say so.
- The Knesset website could not be reached from the research environment. Votes were counted from the official Knesset open-data tables as mirrored by Open Knesset [21, 22]. Bill sponsorship could not be confirmed from Knesset pages; where it is stated, it comes from press reports [27, 29].
- No source was found confirming who formally chairs Meretz in 2026. Lasky is the only candidate filed on Meretz's behalf [1], and the secretary-general heads its standing committee under the 2024 agreement [3, 7].
