## Overview {#overview}

**Amcha Yisrael** (Hebrew: עמך ישראל, roughly "Your People, Israel" or "People of Israel") is a new right-wing party founded and led by Brigadier General (res.) Ofer Winter, a former IDF commander. It is running in its first election, the 26th Knesset election on 27 October 2026.

**Founding.** Winter formally launched the party on 25 August 2026 at an event at the Shalva Center in Jerusalem, about two weeks before the deadline for submitting candidate lists [7, 8, 9]. Before the launch he had turned down offers of a reserved place on the Likud list and early mergers with existing right-wing parties, and chose to run independently [9, 14]. The party submitted its list on 7 September 2026. This closed the door on a merger with Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionist Party, which Smotrich had called for, and went against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's push for small right-wing parties to merge [1, 25]. Officially, the list was submitted by a registered party called **Ami Chai La'ad** (עמי חי לעד), and the CEC published it under the name "עמך ישראל" on 7 September 2026 [1]. No further public information about the history of this registered party was found. The ballot reads "Amcha Yisrael led by Ofer Winter" (עמך ישראל בראשות עופר וינטר).

**Predecessors and mergers.** The party has no predecessor in the Knesset. Its No. 2 candidate, Yoseph Haddad, had registered a political party of his own before he joined Winter's list on 25 August 2026 [7, 10, 12]. Amcha Yisrael is **not a joint list**: it runs alone, with a single member party [1].

**Ideology.** The party describes itself as "a new right-wing national movement" [5]. Its founding document (מסמך אבני היסוד, "foundation stones") calls for a state that is "Jewish, Zionist and sovereign", a security doctrine that moves "from containment to decisive victory", universal service, changes to the justice system, a fight against organized crime, free-market economics, and the decentralization of power to local government [2]. Winter frames the 2026 election as "a referendum" on whether the army should act with "initiative, deception and decisive victory, or containment", and on whether the same politicians who sat in the Knesset on 7 October 2023 should stay [9]. The Israel Democracy Institute's profile likewise describes the party as right-wing, calling for voluntary emigration from Gaza, opposing a Palestinian state, and supporting changes to the judicial system and Haredi enlistment [6]. Its campaign slogans include "Less politics, more leadership" and "What was will not be" [9, 15].

**Base of support.** The party appeals mainly to right-wing voters disappointed with the current coalition [10]. Winter names younger voters and reservists as his core constituency and says he hopes to draw support from Likud, other right-wing parties, the center and first-time voters [28]. Political sources quoted by the Jerusalem Post said part of its support comes from former Likud and Yisrael Beytenu voters [48]. Winter comes from the national-religious community, but the people around him say the list has a secular majority, which they present as a difference from existing religious-Zionist parties [12, 25]. The list presents itself as coming "from Metula in the north to Be'eri in the Gaza border area, from Nazareth to Jerusalem" [4], and includes an Arab Christian (Haddad), immigrants, bereaved family members, reservists and residents of settlements [4, 28]. Winter describes the list as "religious, nonreligious, Jews, Arabs, Druze" [28].

**Current seats.** The party holds **no seats** in the outgoing (25th) Knesset; it was founded after the 2022 election and has never run before [7, 8].

**Ballot letter.** The CEC approved the list and assigned it the ballot letter **ך** (final kaf) on 27 September 2026, as reported by Ynet, which reproduced the CEC announcement [71].

## Leadership and key candidates {#leadership}

**Ofer Winter (No. 1, party chair).** Winter, who was 53 in June 2024 [64], lives in Mitzpe Netofa in the Galilee and is married with eight children [4]. He studied at the Or Etzion military boarding school and the Bnei David pre-military academy in Eli [4]. He enlisted in 1990 and served in the elite Sayeret Matkal and Maglan units [4, 64]. During the Second Intifada he commanded the Givati reconnaissance company and later its reconnaissance battalion. The party says the battalion received a Chief of Staff's citation (עיטור מופת) for recovering six Givati soldiers killed in the armored personnel carrier disaster; Jewish Insider describes it as a decoration for his battalion's fighting in Gaza in 2004 [4, 64]. He later served as IDF attaché to the US Marine Corps, commanded the Duvdevan commando unit and the Gaza Division's northern brigade [4]. As Givati Brigade commander he led the brigade in the 2014 Gaza operation (Operation Protective Edge) [4, 61]. He was promoted to brigadier general in 2015 and served as chief of staff of Central Command [61], and then as military secretary to four defense ministers, including Benjamin Netanyahu during his time as defense minister [4, 64]. His last post was commander of the 98th "Fire Formation" Division, until September 2022 [4, 61].

Several episodes from his military career became public controversies:
- In 2014, before entering Gaza, he issued a letter to his soldiers with religious language ("the head of the spear in fighting the Gazan terrorist enemy"); critics said it framed the operation in religious terms, and supporters saw it as a statement of faith and duty [64]. Winter said in 2024 that he had read out "Shema Yisrael" before battle and that "there was noise about it then" [62].
- He commanded the battle in Rafah on 1 August 2014, after Hamas broke a ceasefire and abducted Lt. Hadar Goldin; the party's own biography says he ordered the "Hannibal" procedure and the use of all means to recover him [4]. See [Legal or ethical matters](#legal) for the Military Advocate General's 2018 decision.
- He was passed over for promotion to major general, first in 2018 under Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot and again in May 2023 [61]. In May 2024 Chief of Staff Herzi Halevi informed him that he would be released from the IDF [62, 64]. In a campaign video on 1 October 2026, Winter said that five days before 7 October 2023 Halevi refused Netanyahu's request to appoint him as the prime minister's military secretary, saying his strategic thinking "does not suit the General Staff" [40].
- In 2024, after leaving the army, he said he was "not going into politics yet" while several right-wing parties tried to recruit him [64]. In June 2026 he led a "decisive victory" petition on the war in Gaza and Lebanon, which Srugim reported gathered more than 10,000 signatures in two and a half weeks, followed by home meetings around the country [66].

Winter has said he is entering politics to become defense minister: "I tried to influence within the system. I will work to become defense minister, and I will influence from that side too" [42, 28].

**Yoseph Haddad (No. 2).** An Arab Christian from Nazareth, Haddad volunteered for the IDF and served as a combatant and commander in the Golani Brigade; he was badly wounded in the Second Lebanon War and lost his foot [4]. He became a well-known pro-Israel public-diplomacy activist and former CEO of the organization "Together – Vouch for Each Other" (ביחד – ערבים זה לזה), lit a torch at the 2023 Independence Day ceremony and received an honorary doctorate from Ariel University in 2024 [4]. The party presents him as its candidate for public diplomacy minister, and he says he wants to be the first Arab member of the security cabinet [10, 11, 13]. See [Legal or ethical matters](#legal) for a pending criminal case.

**Netali Shem Tov (No. 3).** A journalist and news anchor and a reserve captain, born in Kiryat Shmona and raised in Metula, near the Lebanese border [4]. She held several roles at Channel 12 and anchored Channel 13's main Friday news edition; she hosted the national torch-lighting ceremony in 2024 and the Israel Prize ceremony in 2025 [4]. Her family was evacuated from Metula during the war [4].

**Eran Ben-Ari (No. 4).** A lawyer and partner in a law firm, a member of the Israel Bar Association's national council (where the party says he leads the opposition), and host of a legal podcast [4, 9]. He previously worked at the State Attorney's Office, as secretary of a committee headed by the late Supreme Court Justice Edmond Levy, and as the official responsible for settlement matters in the Defense Ministry's legal department [4]. The party says his public activity focuses on "balance in the justice system" and on applying Israeli sovereignty in Judea and Samaria [4]. He lives in Neve Ilan [4].

**Fleur Hassan-Nahoum (No. 5).** A lawyer who immigrated from Gibraltar, she served as a Jerusalem city council member and deputy mayor, working on the city's economy, technology industry and ties between communities [4]. Since the start of the war she has done public diplomacy in English and Spanish and works to strengthen ties with Diaspora Jewry [4].

**Other candidates on the CEC list** [1, 4]:
6. **Laly (Lali) Derai**, a public speaker from Eli who immigrated alone from France at 14; her son, Sgt. Maj. (res.) Saadia Yaakov Derai, was killed in combat in Gaza [4, 11].
7. **Ronen Bodnero**, an economist and former Finance Ministry health-budget official who later worked in investment; a reserve major who says he served more than 750 reserve days since the war began [4].
8. **Davidi Ben Zion**, deputy and acting head of the Samaria Regional Council and holder of its education portfolio; a reserve paratrooper officer who fought in Kfar Aza on 7 October 2023 [4, 11].
9. **Aviv Ezra**, founder of the reservists' movement "Dor HaNitzahon" (Generation of Victory), whose Knesset lobby, the party says, advanced laws giving reservists priority in university admission and public-service hiring [4].
10. **Col. (res.) Eli Gino**, who served about 27 years in the Givati Brigade and, the party says, more than 1,000 reserve days since the war began, including as Givati's chief of staff [4].
11. **Sigal Kraunik-Atia**, a member of Kibbutz Be'eri whose husband, Arik Kraunik, the kibbutz's security coordinator, was killed on 7 October 2023 [4, 11].
12–18. Eric Matthew Rubin, Alam Ibrahim, Moshe Har-Zion, Lilach Eidelberg, Moshe Toledano, Raz Malka and Dor Yitzhak Levi [1].

**Reported internal tension.** On 6 October 2026, Channel 14 reported that Davidi Ben Zion and two other candidates were considering leaving the list [57, 59]. Ben Zion denied it ("I'm not going anywhere") and the party called the report "complete fake news" [58, 59].

## Positions on the issues {#issues}

The party has never served in the Knesset, so there are **no votes, bills or government actions** to report. Its most formal source is its founding document [2] and its published proposal for an October 7 inquiry [3]. Everything else comes from statements by Winter and other candidates.

### Cost of living {#issue-cost-of-living}

- **Formal commitment:** One of the "ten first steps" in the founding document is a move to lower the cost of living "by breaking up centers of concentration and monopoly power, opening the markets to real competition, and removing import barriers and unnecessary regulation", in order to "return power from the concentrated systems to the Israeli consumer" [2].
- **Formal commitment:** The economic chapter says the party will "strive for a free market and the breaking of monopolies", remove regulatory and bureaucratic barriers holding back small and medium businesses, and reward work, service and contribution "through tax benefits" and incentives for businesses that employ and reward people who serve [2].
- No statements were found on VAT or income-tax cuts in general, price controls, or raising welfare allowances or the minimum wage.

**Closest option(s):** Open the market to more competition, cut import barriers and red tape, and break up dominant companies (`open-market-competition-imports`).

### Crime in Arab society {#issue-crime-in-arab-society}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document says a strong state "does not allow crime organizations or criminal elements to create spaces where the state does not govern". It promises to strengthen enforcement and punishment, "with an emphasis on protection rackets, illegal weapons and arms smuggling", and to set up "a national coordinating body for the fight against crime that will combine intelligence, enforcement and economic sanctions under one picture" [2]. Its "ten first steps" include passing "the legislative package against crime organizations, after which a national enforcement operation will begin" [2]. These commitments apply to crime nationally, not only in Arab communities.
- **Statement:** At the launch, Winter spoke of fighting organized crime and protection rackets "in the Negev and the Galilee" and of strengthening the partnership between Jews and Arabs [14, 8].
- **Statement:** Yoseph Haddad has said that responsibility for crime in Arab society lies with three actors: the state, the police and Arab community leaders [33]. On 2 September 2026 he said fighting crime in Arab communities would be one of his priorities [18].
- **Statement:** At a press conference on 5 October 2026 Winter also warned about "nationalist Bedouins who are arming themselves as if they were preparing for war" [49].
- No statement was found on the July 2026 government plan to involve the Shin Bet in fighting crime organizations, on holding suspects without trial, or on the five-year development plan for Arab communities.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence points to tougher enforcement and a new anti-crime law package, but does not say whether the party supports Shin Bet involvement or special detention powers. It sits between `emergency-powers-shin-bet` and `more-police-resources-normal-law`, and gives no support to putting investment in Arab communities first (`invest-in-arab-communities-first`).

### Gaza Strip policy {#issue-gaza}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document's security doctrine rests on "quick decisive victory, exacting a territorial price from the enemy, initiative and the continuous removal of capabilities" [2]. On Gaza it says: "We will demand to lead a plan for voluntary emigration for those who wish to leave the Strip and rebuild their lives, and we will act within international agreements and in cooperation with countries that agree to absorb migrants" [2]. The first of its "ten first steps" is to receive "all the powers to lead the voluntary emigration of the populations of the Gaza Strip residents" [2].
- **Statement:** At the launch on 25 August 2026, Winter said: "There will never be a Palestinian state here. There is one solution in Gaza: migration," and that "an enemy that launches a war against Israel will pay a heavy territorial price" [10, 11]. He also said: "We won't end a war while the enemy has the ability to recover… An enemy that opens a war against Israel will never return to the situation it was in on the eve of the war" [9].
- **Statement:** In a Channel 12 interview reported on 4 October 2026, Winter called mass emigration of Palestinians from Gaza "in the national interest" and backed Jewish settlement in Gaza afterwards [41]. At a public event in Tel Aviv on 5 October 2026 he said Israel should "destroy Hamas and facilitate voluntary emigration from Gaza" and that "Trump gave us the OK". He said then-Defense Minister Yoav Gallant had asked him to prepare a plan, which the government did not take forward: providing food and aid in the parts of Gaza Israel controls, drawing the population out of the Hamas-controlled zone, and letting Gazans who wish to leave do so [54]. He has also described his plan as including a siege and separating civilians from Hamas, and said "our soldiers come before enemy civilians" [28].
- **Statement:** Winter opposes the continued entry of aid trucks into Gaza. He cited "the entry of hundreds of aid trucks into Gaza" among the government's failures in August 2026 [16] and said on 4 October 2026: "no trucks will enter Gaza" [44, 45]. On 16 September 2026 he said Defense Minister Israel Katz had asked him to set up an emigration directorate, that he had demanded real powers and presented a detailed plan, and that no emigration had been carried out [27].
- **Statement:** In a July 2024 article, Winter wrote that Israel "must achieve a clear and decisive outcome in Gaza" and called for removing Hamas's ability to govern, not only its military capabilities [65].
- **Statement:** On 29 September 2026, after the prime minister and defense minister warned of possible attacks, Winter said: "Enough containment, enough rounds… Take them apart now, don't wait… We will empty the pool and bring about emigration, we will bring a decision" [38]. On 23 September 2026 he told Katz: "Stop making threats if you don't intend to follow through" [31].
- **Disputed report about his past advice.** In early September 2026 Channel 12 reported, citing an unnamed former military official, that in the days after 7 October 2023 Winter met Netanyahu at a secret location and urged a total military blockade instead of a full ground offensive, warning of very heavy losses [23]. Winter called the casualty figure attributed to him "a complete lie". He did not deny proposing an alternative, and described it as "a powerful, short and smart war that leads to a decisive victory, and not to a three-year campaign without any resolution" [23]. Shaul Meridor of the rival Yashar party made a similar claim on 18 September 2026 [30]. The party says Winter pushed "to push the Gazan population southward and impose a smart siege" rather than "raid after raid" [29]. No official record of the meeting or of what was proposed has been published.

**Closest option(s):** Go back to war and take control of all of Gaza, encourage residents to leave voluntarily, and allow Israeli communities to be rebuilt in Gaza (`resume-fighting-full-control`).

### Haredi military enlistment {#issue-haredi-enlistment}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document says "everyone will be partners in the circle of responsibility and contribution — in military service for those suited to it, and meaningful national service for those who cannot serve in the IDF… no one will stand outside the circle of shared commitment" [2]. It promises "differential rewards" by type, length and risk of service: "Whoever gives more will receive much more" [2]. It recognizes Torah study as "a foundational value" and keeps "the option of unique tracks for Torah excellence", but says shared service "will be the rule and not the exception" [2].
- **Formal commitment:** The second of its "ten first steps" is to pass a **"Basic Law: Service"** (a law with constitutional status) under which every citizen serves the state, to set up a service administration, and to build a screening system for outstanding students, "including the parameters for objectively examining quotas of Torah learners" [2].
- **Statement (coalition condition):** On 2 September 2026 Winter said: "We will not enter a government that does not pass, before it is formed, a law that puts an end to [non-enlistment] and requires everyone to report and contribute… Not a promise, not a clause in a coalition agreement, and not 'after we form a government'" [17, 20]. He also said: "We intend to bring about the integration of the Haredi public into the IDF without compromises and without delays" [19, 21].
- **Statement:** On 27 August 2026 he said Haredim "will be integrated into the IDF, with or without Haredi political activists" [15]. On 7 September 2026, replying to Agudat Yisrael leader Yitzhak Goldknopf's demand for a full exemption for yeshiva students, he said "not everyone who wears black and white studies Torah", that the current situation "harms first and foremost the true Torah students", and that "those who do not serve, do not study and do not contribute will not enjoy benefits" [25].
- **Statement:** In September 2026 he said his plan includes greater benefits for those who serve, Defense Ministry oversight of yeshivas, and religious accommodations for Haredi soldiers [28]. In August 2026 Haredi protesters confronted him after he spoke at a Jerusalem conference for young Haredi men interested in military service [67].
- **Statement:** In a Channel 12 interview reported on 4 October 2026 he said he would still sit in a coalition with Haredi parties such as United Torah Judaism, and claimed his party would effectively hold a veto, as the Haredi parties do [41]. See [Coalition stance](#coalition).

**Closest option(s):** Require everyone, including Haredim, to serve in the army or civilian national service, with exemptions only for a limited number of outstanding Torah scholars, and cut benefits for those who refuse (`universal-service-with-sanctions`).

### Housing {#issue-housing}

- **Formal commitment:** The economic chapter, titled "Economy, cost of living and housing", promises to "expand urban renewal in cities and towns at the edges of the country, combining an increase in the housing supply with protection [bomb shelters] and strengthening of infrastructure" [2].
- **Formal commitment:** The chapter on border and priority areas promises employment through tax incentives, quality education, efficient transport and governance in these areas, "strong Jewish settlement in the border areas and in threatened areas", and more powers and resources for local authorities [2]. It says the state "will give priority to those who contribute to its security and national resilience, including through the allocation of land and benefits" that strengthen settlement and attract people to border areas [2].
- **Formal commitment:** The tenth of its "first steps" is to develop physical infrastructure, including energy and public transport [2].
- No statement was found on building permits or planning in central Israel, the Israel Land Authority, affordable rental housing, rent regulation or general mortgage assistance.

**Closest option(s):** Mainly invest in transport, jobs and urban renewal outside the center (`develop-periphery-and-transport`), with an element of direct help for those who served, through land and benefits (`direct-help-for-buyers`).

### Iran, Hezbollah and the region {#issue-iran-and-regional-security}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document's security doctrine aims "to establish a reality in which our enemies lose the will to fight and the ability to arm, recover and threaten", and says Israel should no longer manage the conflict "from round to round" [2]. It calls for "quick decisive victory" partly so that reservists can be released quickly [2], and for "technological superiority and independence" [2]. It also calls for assertive public diplomacy to widen Israel's diplomatic and security freedom of action [2].
- **Statement:** At the launch Winter said: "We won't wait for the threat, we'll remove it. We won't end a war while the enemy has the ability to recover, we will deny it that… We will never again abandon the borders of our state" [9].
- **Statement:** In a June 2024 speech he asked "Who is this Hezbollah anyway?" and said of Hamas: "I'm not dismissive, but they can be taken apart" [62]. His June 2026 petition called for replacing "rounds of fighting" in Gaza and Lebanon with continuous offensive initiative aimed at a clear decision [66].
- **Statement:** Ynet's 8 September 2026 profile of possible defense ministers describes Winter as rejecting temporary truces as a substitute for military decision and demanding proactive operations against threats [26].
- **Statement:** Haddad says Israel should build ties with groups in Lebanon he says are "sick and tired of Hezbollah", and points to the UAE as a success story for outreach to the Arab world [32].
- No specific statement was found on the US–Iran talks, the Lebanon ceasefire arrangements, or handing areas in southern Lebanon to the Lebanese army.

**Closest option(s):** Keep attacking until the threats are removed, without interim deals (`military-pressure-until-threats-removed`). The evidence is about general doctrine more than specific Iran or Lebanon policy.

### Judea and Samaria {#issue-judea-samaria}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document states that the security doctrine "rests first and foremost on faith in the justice of our path and that the land of the Land of Israel belongs to the People of Israel" [2]. It calls for "strong Jewish settlement in the border areas and in threatened areas" as "an inseparable part of establishing sovereignty" [2]. The founding document published on the party's website, as fetched for this research, does not itself contain a sentence on a Palestinian state [2].
- **Statement (party's written answer):** In answers to Ynet and Yedioth Ahronoth published on 28 September 2026, the party said it supports establishing settlements "in Judea and Samaria and in all parts of the land", that the 7 October attack and the Gaza war proved that evacuating settlements is "an existential danger and not a solution", that "a Palestinian state will never be established alongside us", and that to end that idea "the state must deepen its hold on the land and apply sovereignty in Judea and Samaria and everywhere in the land". It said illegal Palestinian building in Area C must be demolished immediately, alongside carrying out the voluntary emigration plan, and that Israel should try to regularize every Israeli hold on the land while all citizens must obey the law [37].
- **Statement:** On 6 October 2026 the party told Arutz Sheva that it "firmly opposes a Palestinian state and supports applying sovereignty over Judea and Samaria", and promised to expand settlements and farms and strengthen governance and enforcement in Area C. In two answers it did not say whether it would work to cancel the Oslo Accords or support settling the open areas in Areas A and B (the areas run by the Palestinian Authority) [56].
- **Statement:** Winter: "There will never be a Palestinian state here" (25 August 2026) [10] and "There will never be a Palestinian state here, ever" (4 October 2026) [45, 46]. On 5 October 2026, asked about his candidates' past views, he said "there is no Palestinian nation" and that the idea of a Palestinian state is meant "to destroy Israel" [54]. At a press conference on 5 October 2026 he warned of a possible massacre "in Judea and Samaria" if the current approach continues [49]. In September 2026 the party published a video that included a TV commentator's warning that "the next October 7 will come from Judea and Samaria" [34].
- **Change over time:** Two of the party's top five candidates have spoken in the past in favor of a Palestinian state or Palestinian self-determination. Yoseph Haddad said in an interview with the Al Arabiya channel that "the final solution is the State of Israel alongside the State of Palestine" [44]. Reports date the interview to 2021 or to "more than five years" before October 2026, and Haddad himself placed it in 2020 [44, 51, 54]. He says he changed his mind during the May 2021 riots in mixed cities (Operation Guardian of the Walls) and now opposes a Palestinian state [44, 57]. Channel 14 reported that Fleur Hassan-Nahoum said she supports the idea of Palestinian self-determination in a debate with former US diplomat Dennis Ross, while saying a Palestinian state is not on the agenda and that autonomy may be the answer, and that as Jerusalem deputy mayor she wrote in 2020 that "the ultimate goal of the 'Deal of the Century' is indeed two states" [46]. The debate was held in New York on 16 July 2024 by Open to Debate and the Council on Foreign Relations; Hassan-Nahoum argued the side that the two-state solution is no longer viable [54, 75]. The party says both candidates oppose a Palestinian state, and Winter said he was proud of "a person's ability to change his views in the right direction" and that "whoever did not change their views after October 7 is ignorant and foolish" [45, 46]. Winter also said the 2020 statement described President Donald Trump's "Peace to Prosperity" plan, which the Israeli government had supported [54].

**Closest option(s):** Apply Israeli sovereignty and expand settlements, with no Palestinian state (`full-sovereignty`). The party's written answers speak of applying sovereignty "in Judea and Samaria and everywhere in the land" [37], but it has not said what should happen to the Palestinian Authority, the Oslo Accords or Areas A and B [56].

### Judicial system {#issue-judicial-system}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document calls for "checks and balances in the justice system, including a right of appeal on judicial decisions of the High Court of Justice through the establishment of a supreme court of appeal" [2]. It promises to "define in law the powers of the branches of government" and to build a justice system and prosecution that work "with transparency, measurement and accountability" [2].
- **Formal commitment:** The fourth of its "ten first steps" is to split the role of the attorney general into "an independent prosecutor general and legal advice to the government, which will help it advance its policy within the law", and to allow citizens and authorities a right of appeal on High Court of Justice decisions, "as is customary in the vast majority of legal systems" [2]. The party says a detailed plan "to restore trust in the justice system" will be published [2].
- **Statement:** At the launch Winter called for "significant change" in the justice system [8]. The party's No. 4, Eran Ben-Ari, focuses on "balance in the justice system" [4].
- No statement was found on the 2025 law on choosing judges, on the government's attempt to fire the attorney general, or on the Supreme Court's power to strike down laws.

**Closest option(s):** Give elected officials more power relative to the courts and the attorney general (`continue-judicial-changes`). The party's own proposals (a court of appeal over the High Court and splitting the attorney general's role) differ in detail from the changes passed since 2023, and its call to define each branch's powers in law also touches on a clear constitutional framework (`broad-agreed-constitutional-framework`).

### October 7 inquiry {#issue-october-7-inquiry}

- **Formal commitment:** The sixth of the party's "ten first steps" is to set up "an independent commission of inquiry into the October 7 massacre, which will be given all the powers of a state commission of inquiry, and alongside them the power to enforce its conclusions" [2].
- **Formal commitment:** On 5 October 2026 the party published a detailed proposal [3, 50]. Under it, any group of ten Knesset members could nominate a candidate to chair the inquiry. All 120 members would vote by secret ballot; the candidate with the most votes would chair it and the runner-up would be deputy chair. **Both must be judges** [3]. The party says this method, modelled on how the Knesset elects the President, ensures "the government does not appoint those who are supposed to investigate it" and that the inquiry is not "a distribution of appointments between political camps" [3].
- **Formal commitment:** The body would have nine members: the two judges; three professionals in security, intelligence and oversight; one representative chosen by the local authorities hit on 7 October; and three family representatives (of bereaved soldiers' families, bereaved civilians' families and hostages' families) [3]. It would have full powers to summon witnesses and demand documents, with no limit on whom or what it examines, including the government, the Knesset, the security services, intelligence and the justice system, and the lessons since the 2014 Gaza operation [3]. If the two judges disagree, the local authorities' representative decides. It would start within 60 days of the new government being sworn in and publish initial findings within six months [3].
- **Statement:** The party says that no commission having been set up is "a shared disgrace for both the coalition and the opposition" [50]. Winter said on 2 September 2026: "Whoever failed for three years to establish a commission of inquiry that would enjoy broad public trust… will not succeed in establishing one in the future either" [22]. Haddad has described the same model [33].

**Closest option(s):** A neutral method picks the members, such as judges elected by the Knesset in a secret vote, and the inquiry covers the government, the army and the legal system alike (`commission-chosen-by-neutral-mechanism`).

### Religion and state {#issue-religion-and-state}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document's first principle is "the Jewish nation-state": a state "whose identity is clear: Jewish, Zionist and sovereign. A state in which Judaism invites and does not coerce, shares and does not dictate", where disagreement takes place "around a shared table" with the understanding that "Judaism is a family and not a battlefield" [2]. It promises "unity that is not uniformity" [2]. In education, it calls for Jewish values, Zionism and civic responsibility alongside quality core studies, and says the party will not fund schools that teach "anti-Zionist" values or programs [2].
- **Statement:** Winter is religious, and the party's messaging uses Jewish and faith-based language [7, 47]. At the same time the party says most of its candidates are secular [12]. On 4 October 2026 the religious news site Srugim reported that No. 3 Netali Shem Tov, who does not present a religious lifestyle, had posted on social media on Shabbat, and said this could be an image problem for a party courting religious voters [47].
- No position was found on civil marriage, the Chief Rabbinate, conversion, public transport on Shabbat, or the July 2026 basic law on Torah study.

**Closest option(s):** The evidence is too general to match one option firmly. The founding document's "invites and does not coerce" language does not fit enforcing Jewish religious law by statute (`strengthen-jewish-character`), and the party does not call for separating religion and state (`separate-religion-and-state`). The party's position appears to fall between `keep-status-quo` and `more-choice-local-decisions`, but it has not said which.

## Other notable positions {#other-positions}

### Security doctrine: from containment to decisive victory {#other-security-doctrine}

- **Formal commitment:** The party's central theme is changing Israel's security doctrine "from containment to decisive victory": quick decision, a territorial price from any enemy that attacks, initiative, continuous removal of enemy capabilities, and technological superiority and independence [2]. It says the state's "independence of decision is not up for debate and decisions on its fate are made in Jerusalem" [2].
- **Statement:** Winter blames a "conception" in the security establishment that dismissed warnings, and says: "If we do not substantially change our security doctrine, then October 7 will come again, from Judea and Samaria or from other borders" [9]. He says the "distorted conception" that "our soldiers come after enemy civilians" must change, and that he wants "to see generals stamping their feet like in the Six-Day War" [42]. At a press conference near Ben Gurion Airport on 5 October 2026, after a suspected attack on an Israel-bound flydubai flight, he said "the conception is still here in full" and that "whoever does not want to investigate will not want to fix" [49].

### Rewards for soldiers, reservists and those who serve {#other-service-rewards}

- **Formal commitment:** The party promises a "differential rewards" system based on the type, length and risk of service, so that combat soldiers, combat-support soldiers and reservists with long service "will be rewarded according to their contribution" [2]. It promises tax benefits for those who serve and incentives for businesses that employ them, and priority in land allocation and benefits for those who contribute to security [2]. It says a detailed plan for "regulating the status of those who serve" will be published [2].
- **Statement:** Winter: "The more you serve, the more you receive" [28]. Several candidates are reservists, and No. 9 Aviv Ezra heads a reservists' movement [4]. Winter's slogan is "we are determined to serve those who serve" [21].

### Public diplomacy and antisemitism {#other-public-diplomacy}

- **Formal commitment:** The third of the "ten first steps" is "an initiating, national and professional international diplomacy and public-diplomacy system" that will tell "the Israeli story" and confront "rising antisemitism" [2].
- **Statement:** Haddad, the party's candidate for public diplomacy minister, proposes weekly press conferences in Hebrew, Arabic and English and direct engagement with foreign and Arabic-language media [32, 33]. The party wants to revive the Public Diplomacy Ministry with substantial funding [18]. Winter has promised "true public diplomacy for the State of Israel and not just a public diplomacy ministry" [15] and has spoken of making Israel "a home for all the Jews of the world" [14].

### Arab citizens and Jewish–Arab partnership {#other-arab-citizens}

- **Formal commitment:** The founding document describes a state expressing the Jewish people's right to self-determination, "alongside a partnership of fate between those who serve from all sectors and full commitment to the security, dignity and rights of all its citizens" [2].
- **Statement:** At the launch Winter said the party seeks to include Arab citizens who support the state [8]. Haddad says Arab citizens need representatives "who represent them, not Gaza, Jenin and Ramallah" [11], says his "mission is to throw [the Arab parties] out of the Knesset", and prefers the word "partnership" to "coexistence" [33]. On 2 September 2026 he said he wants to strengthen Arab citizens' Israeli identity in schools [18]. Winter said in October 2026 that his party would encourage both Haredi and Arab high-school students to consider service [54].

### Periphery, border communities and local government {#other-periphery-local-government}

- **Formal commitment:** The party calls strengthening border areas "a national and security goal" and promises tax incentives for employment, quality education, efficient transport and governance there [2]. It promises to give local authorities more powers and resources while keeping regulation and oversight with the central government [2], and to devolve the running of schools to local authorities and schools, while the Education Ministry keeps the core curriculum, regulation and measurement [2]. It says local government "proved its ability to deliver in a time of crisis" [2].

### Education {#other-education}

- **Formal commitment:** The party wants every child in Israel to receive quality core studies alongside Jewish, Zionist and civic values, to narrow gaps between the different school systems with a shared basic standard, and to teach "the full story of Israel's communities" [2]. It would give school principals more independence over hiring, staff and programs, with measured targets [2], and would not fund schools that teach anti-Zionist values or programs [2].

### Infrastructure, artificial intelligence and government performance {#other-infrastructure-ai}

- **Formal commitment:** The party calls for a multi-year national plan for energy, water, transport, communications and ports, with clear targets [2]. It says computing power, data and computing infrastructure are national infrastructure in the age of artificial intelligence, and that Israel should control critical systems and build "sovereign national computing power" to avoid strategic dependence on foreign infrastructure [2].
- **Formal commitment:** It promises that every national task will get measurable targets, a timetable and one responsible minister, with a yearly public implementation report on what was and was not done [2].

### Law and order and governance {#other-governance}

- **Formal commitment:** "Sovereignty does not end at the edge of the community": the party promises to enforce the law everywhere and to demand transparency and accountability from elected officials [2].
- **Statement:** On 6 October 2026, after what he called "extremist Haredi rioters" broke into a building in Modi'in to prevent the arrest of a deserter, Winter attacked the coalition for not condemning it: "What a disgrace, what a loss of control" [52].

## Coalition stance {#coalition}

**Bloc and recommendation.**
- **Statement (25 August 2026):** Winter said the party is "a clear and unequivocal part of the right-wing camp" and will seek "the broadest possible right-wing government with as many Zionist partners as possible" [7, 10].
- **Statement (27 August 2026):** "We don't work for anyone, only for the people of Israel. We are in the right-wing camp, the leader of the right-wing camp is Netanyahu and we will recommend only him. We won't dance to anyone's tune" [15]. He has repeated this since, including on 4 October 2026: "I have criticism of Netanyahu over October 7… but that doesn't mean there is a much better alternative" [41]. Jewish Insider reported in October 2026 that he has said the party will not sit with Arab parties or the left-wing Democrats [54].

**Who he won't sit with.**
- **Statement (2 September 2026):** Winter said any alliance would be only with "new and clean people", and ruled out joining Naftali Bennett and Avigdor Liberman: "Of the existing alternatives, we're simply not there. We're on the right" [17, 18, 20].
- **Statement (17 September 2026):** In a Jerusalem Post interview Winter said Gadi Eisenkot could join a broad government but should not be defense minister, and welcomed Bennett and Liberman into such a coalition "as ministers" [28].
- **Statement (4 October 2026):** "I won't do what Bennett did. I won't say A and do B. I won't sit under Eisenkot, not under Bennett and not under Liberman" [42, 43]. Yisrael Beytenu MK Yulia Malinovsky had said Winter would join Liberman after the election; Eran Ben-Ari replied "that's a lie" [42]. Earlier, Haddad had said Liberman "could be a worthy partner", which Likud used against the party on 29 September 2026 [39].
- **Statement (26 September 2026):** Winter said Goldknopf is "not a Zionist" and that he did not want him as "the 61st finger" (the vote that gives a government its majority) [35].

**Conditions.**
- **Statement (2 September 2026):** The party will not join any government unless a law requiring universal service passes **before the government is sworn in** [17, 19, 20].
- **Statement (4 October 2026):** In a Channel 12 interview reported that day, Winter said he would still sit in a coalition with Haredi parties such as United Torah Judaism, and claimed his party would effectively hold a veto in government, as the Haredi parties do [41].
- **Statement (reported 6 October 2026):** At a home meeting that week, Eran Ben-Ari said: "We did not say we would sit only with Netanyahu. We said we would recommend Netanyahu", and "If we cannot change the situation for those who serve, we will not be in the government. There are other options." No. 8 Davidi Ben Zion said of Gadi Eisenkot joining a government: "Why shouldn't Eisenkot join? That would be great." Ben-Ari ruled out sitting with Yair Golan's Democrats [55]. These remarks, and Winter's own 17 September comments [28], are consistent with his pledge not to sit *under* Eisenkot, Bennett or Liberman, but they leave room for a broader government.

**Relations with the right-wing parties.** Netanyahu urged Winter in August 2026 to unite with existing parties or withdraw, warning the party could "bring down" the bloc [8, 16]. Smotrich called Winter's run "an irresponsible move" [36]. Winter said Netanyahu had "decided to eliminate" his party [27, 35]. On 6 October 2026 Likud and the Religious Zionist Party signed a surplus-vote agreement with each other, leaving Otzma Yehudit to choose between an agreement with Amcha Yisrael or none; the Hendel–Zelekha list had also offered Winter an agreement, but he did not respond [53]. A senior Likud official said Likud did not trust Winter to go only with Netanyahu [53].

**Track record of keeping pledges.** The party is new and has never been through an election or coalition talks, so there is no record of keeping or breaking coalition pledges.

## Track record {#track-record}

**The party has never been in the Knesset or in government.** It was founded in August 2026 and has no voting record, bills, budgets or coalition agreements [7, 8].

**Its candidates' prior public roles** are the only relevant record:
- **Ofer Winter** served in the IDF from 1990 to 2024, including as military secretary to defense ministers and as a division commander, and has never held elected office [4, 7, 61, 64]. As Givati commander he prepared the brigade for the tunnel threat and led it in Operation Protective Edge in 2014 [4]. After leaving the army he led a public petition and home meetings calling for decisive victory (June 2026) [66]. Winter says he presented a detailed emigration plan to Defense Minister Katz and that it was not carried out [27].
- **Fleur Hassan-Nahoum** was a Jerusalem city council member and deputy mayor [4].
- **Davidi Ben Zion** is deputy and acting head of the Samaria Regional Council [4, 11].
- **Aviv Ezra**'s "Dor HaNitzahon" movement, according to the party, set up a Knesset lobby that advanced laws on priority for reservists in university admissions and public-service hiring and the expansion of a study-grant law for soldiers [4].
- **Eran Ben-Ari** worked at the State Attorney's Office and in the Defense Ministry's legal department on settlement matters [4].

## Legal or ethical matters involving leaders {#legal}

**Ofer Winter.**
- **Military Advocate General decision (2018).** On 15 August 2018 the Military Advocate General, Maj. Gen. Sharon Afek, announced that no criminal investigation would be opened into the battle in Rafah on 1 August 2014, which followed Hamas's breach of a ceasefire, the killing of two soldiers and the abduction of Lt. Hadar Goldin. The decision found that ground fighting and fire were based on operational considerations, that there was no basis for claims that the IDF acted out of revenge, and no factual basis for claims that using the "Hannibal" procedure led to disproportionate or unrestrained force; it also pointed to operational lessons [60]. The decision does not name Winter; Srugim reported it under the headline that Winter "acted as required" [60]. Times of Israel says Winter commanded the forces in that battle and that the army's own investigation "identified failures in how the battle was waged, but no criminal acts" [61]. **Status:** closed with no criminal investigation.
- **Disciplinary reprimand (2021).** On 9 June 2021 Chief of Staff Aviv Kochavi reprimanded Winter, then commander of the 98th Division, for a personal conversation with Yamina chairman Naftali Bennett, then a Knesset member, held without permission through proper channels; IDF rules forbid officers from meeting elected officials without approval [72, 64]. This was an internal disciplinary step, not a criminal matter.
- **2024 Military Advocate's letter.** In June 2024, while Winter was still on pre-retirement leave, the Military Advocate's Office wrote to his lawyer that he could not take part, without prior approval, in a public conference where his presence could be seen as representing the military [63]. Winter did not speak at the event [63].
- No indictment, conviction or State Comptroller finding involving Winter was found.

**Yoseph Haddad (No. 2).**
- **Indictment (2025).** On 7 August 2025 the Tel Aviv District Prosecutor's Office filed an indictment against Haddad in the Tel Aviv Magistrate's Court for a reckless and negligent act with a firearm [69, 70, 73]. According to the indictment, during a road argument in Jaffa with a scooter rider, Haddad drew his licensed pistol and held it near his feet, "without any need having arisen"; the rider spat at him through the car window and began to drive off, and Haddad then fired one shot toward the street, which the indictment calls hasty and negligent. No one was hurt [69, 70, 73]. The scooter rider was indicted for assault [70, 73]. Before the indictment, the Tel Aviv Magistrate's Court had extended Haddad's house arrest by three days on 4 August 2025 [68, 70]. Haddad's lawyer called the indictment political rather than criminal and said that, according to the indictment itself, Haddad was the victim [69]. Haddad says he was threatened and that the shot was accidental [54, 68]. **Status:** pending. N12 reported on 28 August 2026 that one hearing had been held before Judge Yaron Gat, who suggested criminal mediation; that Haddad had asked to cancel the indictment, saying his right to a pre-indictment hearing had been violated; that later hearings were postponed at the parties' request; that the two sides were in advanced plea negotiations; and that the next hearing was set for early October 2026 [74]. No ruling, plea agreement or closure had been reported as of 7 October 2026.
- **As a victim:** In September 2026 Haddad received death threats and his campaign poster near Shfaram was defaced with the word "traitor"; Winter condemned the incitement [24].

**Other candidates.** No indictments, convictions or official inquiries were found involving Netali Shem Tov, Eran Ben-Ari or Fleur Hassan-Nahoum.

The courts' own case records were not available to confirm the current status of Haddad's case.

## Information availability {#information-availability}

Public information on Amcha Yisrael is **moderate but uneven**. The party is about six weeks old and has **no Knesset record**: no votes, bills, budgets or coalition agreements, so every position here is either a formal commitment from its published founding document and October 7 inquiry proposal, or a statement by its leader and candidates. There is no record of keeping or breaking coalition pledges.

The party has published a founding document with eight principles and "ten first steps" [2], and a detailed inquiry proposal [3]. It says more detailed plans on service, decisive victory and emigration, and the justice system will be published later [2]; none were found as of 6 October 2026. News coverage is extensive on security, Gaza, Haredi enlistment and coalition questions, but thin on religion and state, housing, the cost of living beyond the founding document, and specific Iran and Lebanon policy. Nothing was found about the history of the registered party "Ami Chai La'ad" beyond the CEC filing [1].

Items not fully verified: the current status of Yoseph Haddad's 2025 criminal case beyond N12's report of 28 August 2026 (court records were not available, and no outcome of the hearing set for early October 2026 had been reported); and the details of the Channel 12 report on Winter's advice to Netanyahu in October 2023, which rests on an unnamed source. Several Haaretz and Kan items could not be opened and were not used.
